# News Briefing — 18 August 2026

State capacity is the thread today: Ceuta's migration response, Germany's security and industrial weak spots, Berlin's administrative resilience and voting access, and a world economy still being reorganised by conflict and technology.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Madrid and Ceuta have finally agreed on new reception sites**

After more than two weeks of deadlock over the enclave's migration surge, La Moncloa and Ceuta agreed on four sites for 1,500 reception places, turning a humanitarian emergency into a sharper test of whether Spain's centre and periphery can still coordinate under far-right pressure. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **A judge linked to the Villarejo-BBVA case is being suspended**

Spain's judicial council is set to suspend Eduardo Ortega Martín, the magistrate due to stand trial over alleged illegal work for ex-police fixer José Manuel Villarejo when he was at BBVA, deepening the sense that the scandal still reaches into core state institutions. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Spain's heat is now disrupting sleep as well as daytime health**

Night-time minimum temperatures above 20C and increasingly above 25C are multiplying across Spain, turning what used to be summer discomfort into a compounding public-health problem because bodies no longer recover after extreme daytime heat. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany still looks poorly defended against hostile drones**

After the recent drone incident near Leipzig, pressure is rising on interior minister Alexander Dobrindt because Germany's promised anti-drone build-out still runs into legal and operational gaps, leaving critical sites exposed faster than the federal response is maturing. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Volkswagen is still paying for a Herbert Diess-era bet**

Handelsblatt says a strategy launched under former chief executive Herbert Diess continues to burden Volkswagen and could yet force painful consequences at a Lower Saxony plant, showing how Germany's auto groups remain trapped by expensive decisions made before today's demand slowdown. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Standing sections are returning to Germany internationals**

For the first time in more than 25 years, the German football federation will again sell standing-room tickets for national-team matches, a small but notable shift in how one of the country's biggest institutions is rebalancing safety culture, fan access and match-day atmosphere. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A cyberattack has hit Berlin's state network**

Hackers penetrated the Berliner Landesnetz and sensitive data was exfiltrated, prompting two senate administrations to go offline as the capital confronts the fact that routine public administration now depends on digital resilience it does not fully control. ([rbb24][7])

* **Pankow is testing a way to bring voting closer to homeless Berliners**

With tens of thousands of people in Berlin lacking stable housing despite having the right to vote, a pilot project will take election-office support directly to them, treating disenfranchisement as an administrative-design problem rather than just a social one. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin is looking at reeds as climate infrastructure**

Engineers and ecologists are arguing that reed beds, willows and modular habitat systems could cool waterways and restore life to Berlin's canal-like concrete banks, recasting urban renaturation as part of the city's heat-adaptation toolkit rather than an optional beautification project. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Google plans to move Pixel manufacturing out of China**

Nikkei Asia reports that Google aims to stop making Pixel devices in China in 2027, a supply-chain shift that shows even US technology groups with modest hardware volumes still want more geopolitical distance from Chinese assembly. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Airlines are resisting fare cuts even as jet fuel eases**

After oil pressures tied to the Iran conflict started to soften, carriers have entered a stand-off over whether cheaper fuel should mean cheaper tickets, suggesting wartime pricing power may linger longer for travellers than for the industry's cost base. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Zimbabwe's Lake Kariba disaster keeps getting worse**

Police say 22 more bodies have been recovered after the overloaded ferry capsized on Lake Kariba, taking the death toll to 68 and underlining how basic transport safety failures can become mass-casualty events in underregulated systems. ([The Guardian][12])
