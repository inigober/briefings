# News Briefing — 12 July 2026

Franco-era reckoning and hardened anti-immigrant rhetoric in Spain, telecom layoffs and pension inequality in Germany, and a resolved Berlin hostage standoff frame a day when labour-market experiments with AI and counter-insurgency violence in Nigeria and Canada show how domestic stress travels globally.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Government strips Franco psychiatrist's top honour over republican-prisoner experiments**

eldiario.es reports Madrid withdrew Antonio Vallejo-Nájera's Gran Cruz de Sanidad after documenting how the dictator's official psychiatrist used concentration camps and women's prisons to test eugenic theories linking Marxism to biology. Officials say the experiments, authorised by Franco himself, sought to "take advantage of human material" for ideological research. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When a democratic government formally revokes a fascist-era medical honour, Spain's memory politics move from symbolic debate to institutional disavowal of science abused for repression.

* **EL PAÍS traces how PP and Vox rhetoric is hardening social climate against immigration**

A year-long investigation finds Spain locked in a feedback loop of tougher right-wing messaging, darker social-media hostility and rising street tension, with activists warning the atmosphere resembles a "pressure cooker." The piece documents how mainstream conservatives increasingly echo Vox framing on "national priority" and border control. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Javier Negre built a multinational disinformation business selling access to Trump and Milei circles**

EL PAÍS profiles how the controversial broadcaster — godfather to agitator Vito Quiles — turned political propaganda into an international consultancy pitching proximity to Washington and Buenos Aires. Editors argue the network shows how Spanish-language media entrepreneurs monetise polarisation across the Atlantic. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **O2 plans to cut roughly one in six jobs as unions accuse management of planlessness**

Handelsblatt says the Telefónica-owned carrier will shed a large share of its workforce as disappointing results force a restructuring, with the ver.di union attacking the lack of a credible growth strategy. The cuts land on a German telecom sector already squeezed by price competition and heavy network-investment bills. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Bundestag data show average pensions lag civil-service payouts by a wide margin**

Handelsblatt analyses new parliamentary figures documenting how statutory retirement benefits trail Beamten pensions, while noting career length, contribution histories and early-retirement rules explain part of the gap. The comparison feeds a broader debate on whether Germany's dual pension system entrenches inequality between public and private-sector workers. ([Handelsblatt][5])

💡 When official numbers quantify the Rente–Pension divide, retirement reform stops being an abstract fairness argument and becomes a measurable distributional fault line inside the labour market.

* **Study estimates nationwide motorway speed limit could prevent up to 90 deaths a year**

Tagesspiegel reports researchers modelled how a general Autobahn cap would cut fatal crashes, reviving a politically frozen dispute that splits the traffic-light coalition. Proponents say the toll is modest relative to Germany's annual road-fatality count; opponents argue local limits already target the riskiest corridors. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **SEK ends 11-hour supermarket hostage crisis; prosecutors detail suspect**

rbb24 reports special forces freed a woman held overnight in a Marienfelde Rewe store Saturday morning after Friday-evening negotiations failed, with the state attorney's office now releasing background on the barricaded man. Investigators are reconstructing motive and prior police contact as the case enters formal proceedings. ([rbb24][7])

* **Summer day at Columbiabad belies its reputation as Berlin's trouble-prone pool**

rbb24 visits Neukölln's Columbiabad at the start of school holidays and finds families, chips and bird song rather than the brawls that earned the site a violent reputation. The report asks whether extra security and holiday crowds are temporarily masking structural safety problems at one of the capital's most debated Freibäder. ([rbb24][8])

* **Serious accident at Wannsee lido draws rescue helicopter**

rbb24 says emergency services airlifted an injured bather from the capital's busiest Strandbad after a serious incident on Saturday, mobilising police and medical crews at the lakeside site. Authorities had released few clinical details by evening as the investigation continued. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Honda and other Japanese firms pay staff bonuses to drive workplace AI adoption**

Nikkei Asia says manufacturers are offering workers financial incentives to experiment with generative tools on the factory floor and in back offices, treating AI fluency as a productivity metric rather than a top-down IT mandate. The approach contrasts with Western layoff narratives by tying adoption to employee pay rather than headcount cuts alone. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Nigeria says troops killed more than 300 bandits in two-day Zamfara operation**

The Guardian reports soldiers and vigilantes targeted kidnapping gangs in Gummi district during a fight that raged through the night, according to Zamfara's information commissioner. The claimed toll underscores how northwestern Nigeria's cattle-rustling and abduction economy continues to absorb large-scale military responses with contested casualty accounting. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Toronto festival shooting kills two after gunmen exchange fire near crowded street event**

The Guardian says police recovered two firearms after Saturday's attack beside the Salsa on St Clair festival, which the mayor condemned as an irresponsible act of violence in a family-oriented neighbourhood. Investigators believe the assailants were targeting each other rather than the crowd, though four bystanders were wounded. ([The Guardian][12])
