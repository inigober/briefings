# News Briefing — 03 November 2026

Washington's midterm vote, Madrid's rail-safety reckoning after Urda and the strain of coalition-building in Berlin frame an edition in which institutions are tested on trust and competence.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Transport minister Óscar Puente takes a rail-safety package to parliament, as cabinet approves emergency aid for Urda crash victims**

After the Urda collision between two high-speed trains, Puente is due to present changes to the protocols of Adif (the state rail-infrastructure manager), innovation measures and extra staffing, while the Council of Ministers fast-tracks help for those affected. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Spain's Guardia Civil says the alleged leader of the "Blocus" looting network had been to Paris and Turkey and had money to flee**

Investigators say the man, arrested in Picanya (Valencia) on 30 October, ran the Telegram and Signal channels used to call the looting that hit Spanish cities, and had registered one account with a Turkish phone. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **The judicial inquiry into the Leire Díez case turns up new evidence pointing to a PP-linked fake-news operation, eldiario.es reports**

The case before judge Pedraz concerns an alleged plot to destabilise judicial proceedings against PSOE leaders and their relatives; the paper says the file now shows fabricated stories in conservative media and investigators suspect Koldo García's lawyer of collaborating in the network. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Economy minister Katherina Reiche turns a demand from the last coalition committee into a bill that probably misses its goal but avoids economic tangles**

Handelsblatt's morning briefing calls it the best law she has yet produced: it makes concrete what the governing parties asked for, even though the intended target will likely be missed. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Germany's Dax needs just 19 points to reach 26,000 as Bayer, Zalando and Lufthansa report results before the US midterms**

The benchmark index of the 40 largest listed German companies starts November near a record, with investors watching quarterly numbers and the American vote. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **A father asks how to respond when his twelve-year-old requests explicit images from ChatGPT**

The Tagesspiegel advice column treats it as a puberty-and-parenting question, showing how chatbots have entered conversations about sex and pornography that families used to have only with each other. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Almost 1,200 SPD members nationwide sign an appeal against a coalition with the Linke while Berlin's three-party talks run**

Berlin's Social Democrats, Greens and the Left Party (Linke) are negotiating a red-green-red government, and the signatories include prominent regional and national figures. ([rbb24][7])

* **Police data seen by rbb24 show 24 deaths from domestic violence in Berlin in 2025, more than before**

The figures come from the police crime statistics, which rbb24's investigative team obtained exclusively. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin asks what a central library would do for Alexanderplatz, the square's shoppers and traders weigh in**

Plans for a central library aim to revive the square; people who pass through daily mix hope with scepticism and worry for the department store there. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Japan's Aisin develops an AI chip it says cuts energy use by 98%**

Aisin, the Japanese auto-parts maker in the Toyota orbit, says its new chip design could sharply reduce the power demands of artificial intelligence, according to Nikkei Asia. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Scientists report fossilised red blood cells and traces of heme in a 115m-year-old Brazilian dinosaur bone**

Chemical tests on the leg bones of Aratasaurus, a small meat-eating dinosaur from what is now north-eastern Brazil, found heme, the compound that lets haemoglobin carry oxygen, which the researchers say points to preserved blood cells. ([The Guardian][11])

* **As Americans vote in the midterms, the FT asks whether a Democratic "blue wave" will sweep Congress and leave Donald Trump a lame duck**

The midterms elect all of the House and a third of the Senate; the paper's guide identifies the key races that will decide control. ([Financial Times][12])
