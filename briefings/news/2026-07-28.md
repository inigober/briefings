# News Briefing — 28 July 2026

Spain's megafire emergency turns into a blame game over climate politics and regional leadership, Germany debates electoral rules and airline restructuring, and extreme weather from Madrid to Afghanistan exposes how fast adaptation windows are closing.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Madrid president Ayuso steps back from fire command but keeps the photo ops**

eldiario.es reports Isabel Díaz Ayuso withdrew from political management of the capital-region emergency the day crews conceded the blaze was beyond local containment, even as she maintained a frenetic public schedule appearing as the face of the response. The piece argues she wants credit without accountability as Madrid and Castilla-La Mancha still battle uncontrollable fronts. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Podcast asks what blocks a cross-party pact on wildfires**

EL PAÍS's daily podcast examines why the PP calls for joint firefighting work yet refuses to sign a formal state agreement, with populares questioning climate change's role in blaze intensity while demanding cooperation on suppression. The stand-off prolongs a truce that briefly held as evacuations mounted across central Spain. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Crews have 48 hours to contain central Spain blaze before new heatwave**

La Vanguardia says firefighters are racing to stabilise the enormous Madrid–Castilla corridor fire before a fourth summer heatwave due Wednesday, when temperatures could erase overnight gains. The deadline frames how Spain's overlapping crises — megafires, drought and incoming scorching weather — compress decision time for aerial and ground teams. ([La Vanguardia][3])

💡 When operational windows shrink to two-day intervals, fire policy stops being about budgets alone and becomes a sequencing problem — control perimeters before the next temperature spike, or lose the season.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Lufthansa job cuts move from announcement to concrete restructuring**

Handelsblatt's morning briefing says Europe's largest airline group is now detailing which posts will go as it digests post-pandemic overcapacity and rising labour costs, even as European governments dispatch firefighting aid across the continent. The dual headline underscores how climate shocks and corporate retrenchment are hitting German workers simultaneously. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Former constitutional court president says five-percent threshold violates equality**

Tagesspiegel quotes Hans-Jürgen Papier arguing 6.8 million second votes were wasted at the last Bundestag election because of the Sperrklausel, calling the bar no longer fit for a fragmented party system. His intervention revives a constitutional debate Merz's coalition has little incentive to reopen before the next federal vote. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Guide examines how online pharmacies work and how to spot fraud**

Handelsblatt walks through how digital dispensaries fulfil prescriptions and over-the-counter orders, and which credentials signal a legitimate operator. The explainer matters as statutory insurers and brick-and-mortar pharmacists press Berlin to tighten e-health rules and cross-border sales. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Six police vans crash in chain collision on the A115**

rbb24 reports six Polizeitransporter collided on the Avus approach road in southwestern Berlin, injuring officers and blocking traffic during the morning rush. The pile-up adds pressure on a force already stretched by CSD security and summer staffing. ([rbb24][7])

* **Berlin ecologist plants reed "islands" to cool waterways and shelter species**

Tagesspiegel profiles Ralf Steeg's project to thicken underwater vegetation in urban lakes, creating shade against heat stress and habitat for fish and birds, with Kreuzberg sites next on the list. The work offers a low-tech countermeasure as Berlin's heat plans focus on buildings and transport. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Toy tractor maker Rolly Toys files for insolvency, threatening 110 jobs**

Berliner Zeitung says the Bavarian manufacturer of yellow pedal tractors will shut by end-2026 after six decades, leaving more than 110 staff facing redundancy. The collapse of a niche exporter highlights how mid-sized German producers are losing ground to cheaper imports and weak garden-retail demand. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **Extreme rainfall from Afghanistan to Taiwan triggers floods and typhoons**

The Guardian documents ten-day deluges destroying mud-brick villages in eastern Afghanistan, alongside flash floods and typhoon alerts across South and East Asia, as residents say storms arrive heavier and earlier each year. The reporting ties disparate disasters to a single pattern of wet-bulb extremes overwhelming poor-country infrastructure. ([The Guardian][10])

* **AI chip boom shifts bottleneck to advanced packaging, AT&S CEO says**

Nikkei Asia reports Andreas Gerstenmayer, head of Austrian substrate maker AT&S, argues fabs can expand wafer output faster than the industry can package chips for AI servers, making back-end capacity the binding constraint. The shift matters for anyone tracking whether memory IPOs and foundry subsidies actually shorten lead times. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **India's ethanol-fuel protesters look to student-movement playbook**

The Financial Times says opponents of the BJP's push for higher ethanol blends in petrol are organising street campaigns modelled on recent campus-led protests, testing whether rural fuel prices can become a national wedge issue. The dispute sits alongside New Delhi's struggle to secure Gulf oil as Middle East shipping stays disrupted. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 When fuel-standard fights borrow student-protest tactics, India's energy-security debate becomes a retail-politics problem — not just a refinery logistics one.
