# News Briefing — 23 July 2026

Valencia enshrines anti-migrant "national priority" in law, Madrid pushes tobacco and housing decrees through a summer parliamentary sweep, Germany's auto sector navigates US-China tech rules and a sales rebound, and Berlin's Gaza diaspora turns to the courts as Washington and Riyadh sign a contested nuclear pact.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **PP and Vox encode "national priority" in Valencia's first joint budget**

eldiario.es reports the alliance's inaugural regional accounts make "prioridad nacional" legally binding, funding an Office of Settlement and Return and studies linking immigration to crime. The text is the first time Vox's euphemism for denying migrants public aid appears in Spanish law and is being read as a template for future PP coalitions. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When a regional budget turns a campaign slogan into statute, anti-migrant policy stops being rhetoric and becomes a fiscal baseline other conservative governments can copy without fresh legislation.

* **PSOE and Sumar agree to extend rent controls in housing decree until July 2028**

Government sources tell eldiario.es the crisis-housing package due before the Council of Ministers next Tuesday will prolong regulated-lease protections for two more years as Congress closes its summer session. The pact aims to smooth September's parliamentary ratification even as the stability path faces another likely defeat. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Government targets tobacco as cigarette and vape tax revenue hits record highs**

EL PAÍS says Treasury collected more than €7.3 billion from tobacco sales in 2025 despite falling consumption, as ministers prepare a new anti-smoking law extending outdoor bans and tightening vape rules. Health minister Mónica García argues party politics should not block legislation she says could prevent 140 deaths a day. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **US Senate plan on connected cars with Chinese ownership could hit Mercedes**

Tagesspiegel says bipartisan legislation to restrict vehicles linked to Chinese shareholders may ensnare Mercedes-Benz because of its Geely stake, potentially barring sales in America's largest luxury market. The bill widens Washington's tech-security campaign from semiconductors to automotive supply chains. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Study finds Europe rich in household wealth but poor in productive investment**

Handelsblatt cites research showing global net worth rose to $1,800 trillion in 2025 while European households hoard assets instead of channelling capital into growth projects. The gap helps explain why the continent's equity markets lag US peers even as savings rates stay high. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **European car registrations rise in first half as EV sales accelerate**

Handelsblatt reports new vehicle approvals climbed across the EU in the January–June period, with battery models gaining share but still below industry targets. Manufacturers warn the recovery remains fragile while Chinese rivals expand and US trade rules tighten. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin mechanic sues foreign ministry after wife trapped in Gaza for 1,000 days**

rbb24 says Faruk Doghmosh married in the strip in July 2023 and has been unable to secure family reunification since the 7 October attacks; the couple is now challenging the Auswärtiges Amt in court. The case tests how consular discretion applies when German residents have spouses in active conflict zones. ([rbb24][7])

* **Middle East conflict splits Berlin's left-wing youth centre in Hellersdorf**

rbb24 reports residents are leaving the "La Casa" project after clashes between pro-Palestinian and other left groups escalated into an internal stand-off. The dispute shows how Gaza politics is fracturing autonomous housing collectives that once treated foreign policy as secondary to local welfare. ([rbb24][8])

* **Deutsche Bank quality-of-life index ranks Berlin 28th as Munich takes fourth**

Berliner Zeitung says Luxembourg tops the 2026 league table ahead of Copenhagen and Amsterdam, while Berlin slips behind smaller German cities on infrastructure and affordability scores. The ranking lands as mayoral candidates trade competing visions for housing, transport and fiscal consolidation. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **US and Saudi Arabia sign landmark civil-nuclear cooperation agreement**

Financial Times reports the pact clears Riyadh to buy American reactor technology but omits "gold standard" bans on domestic uranium enrichment and reprocessing that non-proliferation advocates demand. Critics warn the deal could eventually give the kingdom a pathway to weapons-grade material. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 A civil-nuclear accord without enrichment limits trades non-proliferation guardrails for Gulf alignment — and sets a precedent other middle powers may cite when shopping for reactors.

* **WHO says fastest-recorded Ebola outbreak in DRC is outpacing response**

Foreign Policy describes how the Kasai epidemic is spreading faster than previous Central African waves, straining contact tracing and vaccine deployment. Health officials warn the strain could spill into neighbouring states without a scaled-up international mission. ([Foreign Policy][11])

* **Australia warns China it will not accept "provocative" military actions**

The Guardian says foreign minister Penny Wong will use a Manila speech to condemn Beijing's recent submarine-launched missile test and urge neighbours to resist coercion. The address comes as Canberra accelerates its own nuclear-submarine and long-range strike programmes. ([The Guardian][12])
