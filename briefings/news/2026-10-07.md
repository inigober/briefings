# News Briefing — 7 October 2026

Spain is rewriting housing rules after dissolving parliament, Magdeburg has put the AfD in a speaker's chair, and AI's next cheque is a $40bn chip loan.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The vulture-fund ban now runs to 2030 — with more exceptions**

Cabinet has put the fallen housing decrees back on the table and stretched the ban on speculative home purchases by vulture funds — large institutional buyers — from 2028 to 2030, while opening exceptions for "social" use and a future code of practice. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The calendar looks tighter; the exceptions are where the ban can leak.

* **The Constitutional Court has cleared the path for Puigdemont's return**

Spain's Constitutional Court has upheld the amnesty for embezzlement in the Catalan independence process, which brings Carles Puigdemont, the exiled former Catalan president, closer to a legal homecoming — though the Supreme Court still has to apply the ruling to his case. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Most Spanish homes still belong to small owners**

Spain's property register shows people with one home, or between two and five, own more than 90% of the stock; large landlords still hold more than a million flats. ([EL PAÍS][3])

💡 The political fight is about funds; the cadastre says this is still a country of small owners.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Saxony-Anhalt has given the AfD a Landtag presidency**

The new state parliament in Magdeburg has put an AfD politician in the speaker's chair — the first time Germany's far-right party has taken the presidency of a Landtag — and the morning-after reactions are being read as a test of how the republic answers. ([Berliner Zeitung][4])

💡 A Landtag president sets the chamber's agenda, not only the photograph.

* **A Harvard economist is asking whether Germany can still afford a euro-crisis shock**

Ludwig Straub, speaking ahead of a Germany summit, says jumpy bond yields are a warning that Germany can no longer absorb the kind of shock it managed last time the euro area cracked. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Some Kitas are still using a "penalty room"**

Tagesspiegel reports nursery staff who lock children in a Strafraum — a punishment room — and asks what parents can do when the local Kita, Germany's daycare that is also a labour-market institution, crosses that line. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Die Linke wants Hendrikje Klein as Berlin's parliament president**

Die Linke, the left party that won the city's House election, has nominated MP Hendrikje Klein for president of the Abgeordnetenhaus — the speaker's job in Berlin's parliament. ([rbb24][7])

* **A mural is selling the central library inside Galeria at Alexanderplatz**

A giant wall painting on the Galeria department store at Alexanderplatz is being unveiled to show how Berlin's central library, the ZLB, could look if it moved into that emptying retail hulk. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Another ten "100 Köpfe" researchers are being asked what community means**

A new slice of Tagesspiegel's annual 100-Köpfe science list turns from last week's life-sciences names to historians, philosophers and cultural scholars asked what holds a community together. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **SpaceX wants $40bn of debt to buy Nvidia chips**

SpaceX is trying to raise $40bn, in an Apollo-led financing, to buy Nvidia chips — a loan the size of a small country's budget for the processors that train AI models. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The AI boom is no longer just equity: it is borrowing against chips.

* **Kenya has recorded its first Ebola death**

Kenya's health minister confirmed the country's first Ebola death after a traveller arrived from the Democratic Republic of Congo, where a Bundibugyo-strain outbreak that began in May has already killed more than 4,000 people. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Samsung is putting $5bn into higher-value chips in Vietnam**

Samsung is investing $5bn to move Vietnam further up the semiconductor value chain, past assembly and toward higher-value chip work. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
