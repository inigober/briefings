# News Briefing — 09 November 2026

Spain's campaign tightens as unions test the street over housing, Germany weighs trust in democracy and medical cannabis, and a spy-tinged scandal at Nvidia meets a China-US trade pause on the day's world stage.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A 40dB poll for EL PAÍS and the SER radio network shows the right-wing bloc still ahead for the 29 November election, but the Socialists (PSOE) closing on the People's Party (PP)**

The PSOE is gaining despite the Urda rail crash, while the poll warns of a collapse for the forces to its left. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Spain's two big unions, CCOO and UGT, expect mass demonstrations in a general strike but a patchy turnout that varies by region, sector and age**

The unions aim to halt activity but expect "asymmetric" backing that depends on how hard the housing crisis and the conflict already under way affect each group. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Demand for private health insurance in Spain has jumped 36% as the public health system deteriorates**

As the public system deteriorates, more households are paying for private cover, a shift that favours those who can afford it. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **An ARD poll finds most Germans back democracy as a system of government but are widely dissatisfied with how it works in practice**

The survey, published on 9 November, the date of the 1938 pogroms and the 1989 fall of the Berlin Wall, also flags further warning signs about trust in institutions. ([rbb24][4])

* **Medical cannabis flowers have not been available on public health-insurance prescription since July, and the reform that ended them is drawing criticism**

The government says the change prevents abuse; other cannabis preparations remain available, but patients and doctors argue the rule hurts those with a genuine medical need. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **A large genetic study suggests that genes help explain why the new weight-loss injections barely work for some patients**

The research, reported by Tagesspiegel, also offers an expert's pointers on how to tell early whether treatment is likely to succeed. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin and Brandenburg mark 9 November with events remembering the 1938 Reichspogromnacht (the Nazi-organised attacks on Jews), the 1989 peaceful revolution and the fall of the Wall**

The date is one of the most loaded in German history, and commemorations are planned across the region in the coming days. ([rbb24][7])

* **The Tagesspiegel Checkpoint newsletter reports that a former SPD building senator calls the current interior senator "embarrassing" and that Deutsche Bahn views Berlin as its "headquarters of madness"**

The roundup also carries the Left party leader's announcement of rent rises and a ranking in which Mitte leads the district "rubbish championships". ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **A 13-year-old girl reported missing in Berlin-Marzahn has been found dead in Brandenburg, and the suspect is in a clinic**

According to prosecutors the body is that of the missing girl, and the alleged perpetrator is being treated in a clinic. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Nvidia, the world's most valuable company, has opened an investigation after chief executive Jensen Huang confirmed an extramarital relationship with a woman US lawmakers suspect had links to Chinese intelligence**

Huang admitted the liaison in a message to staff, and congressional members have asked him for a confidential briefing, putting the chip giant's China exposure under a new security spotlight. ([Financial Times][10])

* **China has extended its suspension of some retaliatory tariffs on US goods just before a deadline and a major meeting between the two governments**

Beijing's move in the long-running trade dispute is read as a conciliatory gesture ahead of talks. ([Handelsblatt][11])

* **Banana growers in Latin America are demanding better pay as extreme weather from this year's "super El Niño" hits output**

The Fairtrade Foundation says high winds in Colombia, a key supplier to the UK, and heavy rain in Peru and Ecuador are already cutting production, with a drought forecast in some regions. ([The Guardian][12])
