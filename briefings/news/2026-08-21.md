# News Briefing — 21 August 2026

State capacity and hard power frame today's edition: Spain is testing courts and fire readiness, Germany its trade and food resilience, Berlin its basic civic machinery, and the world the harsher edges of migration, militia pressure and Chinese capital.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain is still unpicking a billion-euro hydrocarbons VAT mess**

The tax agency says 35 investigations remain open in the fuels sector after the Aldama affair, with more than EUR 1 billion owed by companies to Hacienda, turning tax enforcement in hydrocarbons into a live test of how far Spain can clean up a politically exposed fraud market. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Spain's Supreme Court will let investigators keep tracing Alvise's crypto trail**

Judge Julián Sánchez Melgar rejected far-right MEP Alvise Pérez's attempt to stop inquiries into the EUR 100,000 cash payment allegedly tied to his European campaign, keeping a financing probe centred on opaque digital assets and off-book money moving. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Aragón's wildfire dependence on state aircraft is exposing uneven fire capacity**

EL PAÍS reports that Aragón, already Spain's weakest-equipped region per forest hectare, has logged more than 2,000 flight hours from state firefighting aircraft, showing how summer fire response still depends heavily on emergency support from Madrid rather than balanced regional capacity. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany's exporters are still living with the limits of the EU-US tariff truce**

One year after Brussels and Washington announced their trade deal, German coverage is treating it less as a reset than as an uneasy armistice, with key disputes unresolved and Europe's export model still exposed to Donald Trump's tariff politics. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Robot football is starting to look less like a gimmick**

Researchers trained a robot in simulation to track moving balls and score against a human goalkeeper, a small but concrete marker of how machine vision and motor control are moving from lab demos toward coordinated physical tasks. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Germany's drought is becoming a food-price story**

Weeks of heat and dryness are now threatening feed supplies and harvests, turning Germany's summer drought from an agricultural problem into a likely supermarket-inflation pressure as farmers and food systems absorb lower yields. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's cyberattack is now slowing ordinary citizen services**

After the hit on the city's IT systems, several borough offices cannot fully process applications such as housing-eligibility certificates, making the breach a broader public-administration failure rather than only an election-security scare. ([rbb24][7])

* **A vacant former fire station has become a small Berlin planning indictment**

A Kaiser-era firehouse in Charlottenburg has stood empty for more than a year since the Malteser left, turning a prominent heritage building into another example of how slowly the city reuses valuable urban space. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **An armed robbery in Lankwitz showed how exposed routine cash logistics remain**

Attackers ambushed a cash transporter outside a supermarket in south Berlin, leaving one employee in shock and underlining that even ordinary retail infrastructure can become a public-safety weak point in the capital. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **The Houthis are pushing Yemen's old coffee port back into frontline geography**

Mocha, once a trading hub that gave its name to the coffee trade, is now exposed to Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, showing how regional militia warfare keeps remapping commercial sites that once connected the Red Sea economy. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Trump's Liberia deal is extending deportation policy into west Africa**

Twenty people arrived in Liberia under an agreement that could send up to 1,200 deportees there, making one of the administration's largest third-country arrangements a test of how far migration enforcement can be externalised onto poorer states. ([The Guardian][11])

* **China's tech boom is again bypassing Hong Kong**

The Star 50 index in Shanghai has surged ahead of Hong Kong this year, suggesting Beijing's current technology frenzy is strengthening mainland capital markets more than the city's older role as China's main financial gateway. ([Financial Times][12])
