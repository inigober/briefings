# News Briefing — 19 October 2026

Spain's social budget and a Vox-era aid cut, Merz's pitch to BlackRock and Nvidia, and Berlin's Monday coalition vote sit beside a hack that warmed supermarket fridges.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Madrid's health budget does not cover the staff it already authorised**

An internal Community of Madrid health paper shows the personnel line is set below last year's spend and does not even fund the authorised headcount — and that Madrid doctors have the lowest purchasing power in Spain — a day after regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso blamed teacher and doctor strikes on the left's "hate." ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The hole is in the staffing budget, not in the campaign speech.

* **Andalusia's PP–Vox deal has started cutting NGO beds**

After signing a "national priority" pact with Vox, Juanma Moreno's Andalusian government has suspended grants to the Red Cross, SAMU and Inserta that paid for shelter flats and job programmes for homeless people, about 80% of them foreigners, who are due back on the street on 20 December. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The clause is no longer a programme line; it is a winter date.

* **Every Tuesday until the vote is now a social decree**

The caretaker Sánchez government will use remaining Cabinets before 29 November as "social Tuesdays," opening with a decree to raise widowhood, disability, and terrorism- and gender-violence benefits, and still intending to pass the minimum-wage rise trailed last week. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Merz is selling Germany to BlackRock and Nvidia**

At the "Invest in Germany" summit in Berlin, Chancellor Friedrich Merz is courting foreign capital — including BlackRock's Larry Fink and Nvidia's Jensen Huang — a Standort pitch while the black-red coalition at home can barely keep a television peace. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **The Union calls socialization the stupidest idea going**

On *Caren Miosga*, Union floor leader Thorsten Frei and SPD counterpart Matthias Miersch tried to talk federal coalition peace and instead argued over Berlin's red–green–red housing plan; Frei called *Vergesellschaftung* — taking large landlords into public ownership — the stupidest thing one could do. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Booking next summer in October is being sold as a mood drug**

A consumer-psychology note says locking in a summer holiday while the days shorten is a mental anchor — and a price tactic — for a country walking into winter. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Today is the day the SPD and Greens say yes or no**

Two days after exploratory talks closed, Berlin's SPD state board and a Green party meeting are due to decide whether to open formal coalition talks with Die Linke — the Monday vote already flagged last week, now with public doubts inside the Social Democrats. ([rbb24][7])

* **The SED commissioner wants the next generation, not only the files**

Linda Teuteberg, the new federal commissioner for the consequences of the communist dictatorship, says her Berlin-based office will put youth education about SED injustice beside the usual counselling work. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **The BR Volleys nicked the Ligacup**

Berlin's professional volleyball club, the BR Volleys, squeezed through the German Ligacup final against Giesen. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Hackers turned up the fridges in French supermarkets**

Attackers remotely raised the temperature of supermarket refrigeration in France; investigators still do not know the motive, but the method is ordinary industrial-control intrusion, not science fiction — a food cold-chain story dressed as cyber. ([Politico Europe][10])

💡 The vulnerability is the warehouse controller, not the chatbot.

* **The human brain may have evolved into Alzheimer's**

Genetic changes that enlarged the human brain may also be what made our species uniquely vulnerable to Alzheimer's — a health cost of the organ we selected for. ([The Atlantic][11])

* **Russia wants a price for a Japanese journalist**

A former detainee tells Nikkei that Moscow will not free a Japanese journalist without something in return — hostage diplomacy as a Japan–Russia channel, not a consular delay. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
