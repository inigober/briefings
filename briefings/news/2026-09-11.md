# News Briefing — 11 September 2026

Ceuta's reception collapse, a budget fight that could unseat Germany's chancellor, and Beijing's pause on new battery plants set the frame as Berlin remakes Checkpoint Charlie and an AI lab reports blocked bioweapons work.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Women and children have been left at the doors of two Ceuta reception centres**

Spain's North African enclave is still refusing peninsula transfers; dozens of migrant women — some pregnant — and children are camped outside two government shelters with little food or water and no medical help. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The no-transfer rule is no longer only a cabinet line: it is an open-air queue at the state's own doors.

* **Isabel Perelló opened the judicial year by warning politicians not to attack rulings**

The president of the CGPJ — Spain's judicial council — and of the Supreme Court told the solemn opening that criticism of judgments by office-holders weakens trust in the courts, a speech eldiario.es reads as ignoring the bench's own failings. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Catalonia's president asked for convivencia as management problems and the far right pile up**

Salvador Illa, the Socialist head of the Generalitat, called for unity to fix "crucial areas for our prosperity" rather than let day-to-day failures and a rising extremist vote set the tone. ([La Vanguardia][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **If the federal budget fails, Handelsblatt says the chancellor fails with it**

Frustration with Friedrich Merz inside the Union — the CDU/CSU bloc he leads — is now being aimed at the Bundeshaushalt, the annual federal spending law, as the power test that could break him. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 A routine budget vote is being treated as a kill switch for the chancellorship, not only a numbers fight.

* **Another ECB rate rise is weighing on the Dax**

The European Central Bank has raised rates again, and Germany's main index is soft, with Friday's activity data the next hook for investors. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Klingbeil wants to “open doors” for an Expo in Berlin**

Finance minister Lars Klingbeil, who is also preparing a record €555 billion federal budget, is using the morning briefing to back a world-fair bid in the capital. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A winning design is in for Checkpoint Charlie's rebuild**

The former Cold War border crossing — still a souvenir strip more than a museum — now has a chosen scheme to turn it into a place for education and memory. ([rbb24][7])

* **Emirates has been cleared to fly BER–Dubai**

The Dubai long-haul carrier may now serve Berlin Brandenburg Airport, adding a Gulf connection the city has lacked as a scheduled Emirates route. ([rbb24][8])

* **Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg is heading for a Greens–Left duel for district mayor**

Both parties have put up a gay candidate for Bezirksbürgermeister in the inner-east district, and both are campaigning on what they would do for its large queer community before 20 September. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **China has halted new battery-factory projects**

Beijing has temporarily stopped construction of new plants for power and energy-storage cells until a year-end review of capacity, Caixin reported — a pause in the industry that already dominates global supply. ([Bloomberg][10])

💡 Treating overcapacity as a risk to be frozen, not a trophy, is industrial policy, not just a factory notice.

* **Anthropic says it stopped work that could have helped build bioweapons**

The US AI lab disclosed five cases in which users circumvented its controls and hid the purpose of research that might have fed biological weapons — a safety-failure report, not a model launch. ([Financial Times][11])

* **A $15.5m fund aims to build “unstoppable momentum” against FGM**

Her Horizon Fund is the largest single philanthropic pot yet for work against female genital mutilation, which has harmed more than 230 million women and girls, and is launching as donor money for survivor-led groups dries up. ([The Guardian][12])
