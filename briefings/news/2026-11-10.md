# News Briefing — 10 November 2026

Spain's far-right fringe and its secret services come under judicial scrutiny on the eve of a housing-driven general strike, while Merz's no-show at the Antalya climate summit and fears over AI valuations frame a jittery week.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Members of Núcleo Nacional, a Spanish neo-Nazi group, received paramilitary training with rifles in Ukraine, according to the national police**

The group, whose leaders face a criminal-organisation investigation, allegedly planned "an armed uprising" and met the Azov Special Forces Brigade. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Investigators have finished their inquiry into a case at Spain's intelligence service, the CNI, in which a former head of its Russia desk and another agent are headed for trial**

The former Russia chief, allegedly recruited by the United States, is accused of leaking nearly 300 files, and the trial at the Madrid Audiencia has only one precedent in Spain. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Spain's first general strike called by the major unions in 14 years takes place on Wednesday 11 November, called by the Tenants' Union after the eviction of a woman named Maricarmen**

The strike lands days before the 29 November election and pits the government's boast of the eurozone's fastest growth against stagnant wages, speculation and a shortage of public housing. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Chancellor Friedrich Merz is skipping the UN climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, a decision that has drawn criticism**

A Handelsblatt podcast argues that critics should first weigh two basic facts about the track record of past climate summits. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **German children's-rights expert Jörg Maywald explains which warning signs parents should take seriously when childcare centres (Kitas) cross a child's boundaries**

Even in settings meant to keep children safe, violations can occur, and he argues the key is for providers not to sweep mistakes under the carpet. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **The DAX, Germany's blue-chip share index, is rising at the start of the week after heavy losses last week**

Investors are waiting for US consumer-price data on Tuesday, a reading that shapes interest-rate expectations. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The criminal trial of Patricia Schlesinger, the former head of the public broadcaster rbb, opens on Tuesday with three other defendants**

She is accused of criminal conduct during her time as director-general of Rundfunk Berlin-Brandenburg, and the court has scheduled 56 hearing days. ([rbb24][7])

* **Police carried out searches across Berlin in an operation against firearms crime**

The searches target suspected gun offences in the capital; rbb24 gives few further details so far. ([rbb24][8])

* **The Jewish Culture Days Berlin run from 12 to 22 November under the title "The World Needs Righteous People"**

The festival, which builds on a legend of righteous individuals, follows this week's 9 November commemorations in the city. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Australia plans a new regulator for artificial intelligence after a hack at OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT**

The move shows governments starting to hold AI developers to account after a security incident at one of the field's leading firms. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Deadly flash floods struck northern Iraq on Saturday as storms swept across the region, including neighbouring Iran**

The city of Erbil was among the worst hit, with about 80mm of rain, against a forecast of 30mm, damaging homes and businesses and killing two people. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Investors are moving into defensive shares such as consumer staples and healthcare amid fears that the AI-driven stock-market boom may be ending**

The shift shows worry that big technology companies' future earnings may not justify their valuations. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 Money rotating out of the trade that has driven global indices is a market signal, not just a one-day mood swing.

## Other Headlines Today 📋

* **Climate talks:** Some 96% of oil and gas producers still plan to grow output, a new database finds, with host Turkey also in focus at COP31.
* **Spain's defence budget:** The ministry signed contracts for 15 new weapons programmes, including anti-drone systems, missiles and corvettes, to keep spending at 2% of GDP.
* **Nvidia scandal:** Spanish press says US intelligence warned Donald Trump a year ago of suspicions around Jensen Huang, whom he counts as a friend.
* **Iran and Hormuz:** Tehran reports destroyed navigation aids in the Strait of Hormuz while oil falls on hopes for a diplomatic resolution.
* **Markets:** Gold edged lower as rate-hike bets outweighed falling oil prices.
* **New Zealand:** Coalition talks after the too-close-to-call election are expected to take weeks.
* **Spain's campaign:** Feijóo called the general strike "a nonsense", and the PP accepted that the Academia de Televisión will organise election debates.
