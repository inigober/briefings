# News Briefing — 24 August 2026

Spain's housing lock-in, Germany's import and energy-security gaps, Berlin's ageing civic buildings and the rising cost of the AI race set today's frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spanish house prices have now doubled in a 12-year climb**

Since the 2014 trough, Spanish home prices have risen for 48 consecutive quarters and have doubled, overtaking the old bubble peak in 16 of the country's 17 regions and turning housing from a recovery story into a long freeze-out for buyers. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Spain's courts have shut the door on Baltasar Garzón's UN-backed appeal**

Baltasar Garzón, the former investigating judge convicted of prevarication, has now been turned away by the Supreme Court, the Audiencia Nacional and the judicial council after a UN committee said he was denied a fair trial and should be compensated. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 A UN finding of an unfair trial has no practical effect if Spain's own courts treat it as non-binding commentary.

* **Feijóo is recasting Ceuta as a public-health emergency**

Alberto Núñez Feijóo, the conservative opposition leader, is pressing a confinement response to the enclave's migrant surge; the health ministry calls that demand unjustified and says transmission risk is low for residents and only moderate among new arrivals. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany still has no answer to a record flood of cheap Chinese imports**

Europe's goods deficit with China is at a record, and German industry is absorbing the hit, but Handelsblatt says the government still lacks a response — a policy blank where industrial strategy should be. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Germany's micro-car boom is running ahead of its safety rules**

Tiny low-speed cars that 15-year-olds may already drive are becoming more common as a cheap urban option, but their crash protection still lags ordinary cars, turning a youth-mobility workaround into a regulation problem. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **A sea drone off Romania is forcing a harder look at German gas terminals**

The discovery of a sea drone near Romania's Neptun Alpha gas platform has pushed attention onto Germany's LNG terminals, which the air-drone debate has mostly ignored as a target. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

💡 Germany's drone debate has focused on airports and airspace; an underwater weapon near a gas platform points the risk at energy import points instead.

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's school-building drive has added 62,000 places — and little shared credit**

Ten years and about €7 billion into the Schulbauoffensive, the city has 160 new buildings and hundreds of refurbished schools, but the new school year is being marked separately rather than as a common civic achievement. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The FEZ in Wuhlheide is showing its age behind a still-busy façade**

The former East German Pioneer Palace, Berlin's big youth and recreation centre in Treptow-Köpenick, is almost 50 years old: much of it still runs on 1970s systems, and nobody has a clear owner for the growing renovation bill. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Berlin's zoo hid a fatal accident in its flagship wild-horse project**

The Tierpark, the city's eastern zoo, is part of a high-profile scheme to return Przewalski's horses to the Kazakh steppe, but it concealed the death of a mare, Uschi, from a broken neck before she was due to travel. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Alibaba is making investors pay up front for its AI build-out**

Alibaba, the Chinese tech group, saw its shares fall after a $10bn new share sale to fund AI spending, a reminder that the next phase of China's AI race is being financed by dilution as much as by profits. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **A promised clean-up came too late for a Conakry waste dump**

A landslide at a huge dump in Guinea's capital killed 30 people after heavy rain, days after the government said it would close and move the site because of rainy-season risk to nearby shacks. ([The Guardian][11])

* **America is widening the AI investment gap with Europe**

The United States is pulling further ahead of Europe in spending on high-tech equipment and facilities, so the transatlantic AI contest is now as much about factories and kit as about models. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 The gap is less about who has the better chatbot than about who is actually pouring concrete and buying the machines.
