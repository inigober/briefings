# News Briefing — 3 August 2026

A corruption probe relaunching against a former finance minister, Berlin's parties staking out housing platforms, and Taiwan's warning on Chinese hybrid warfare set today's frame as Ceuta's death toll rises and Wall Street experiments with blockchain settlement.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Judge seeks to revive Montoro probe over alleged gas-sector kickbacks**

Judge Rubén Rus in Tarragona has extended the instruction against former finance minister Cristóbal Montoro, arguing the corruption case over favours to gas distributors in exchange for commissions remains too complex to close after secrecy was lifted in June 2025. Defence lawyers say the file is riddled with procedural flaws and expect Madrid courts to take over in September. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Sánchez government bets August housing decree on its last parliamentary window**

Pedro Sánchez's minority coalition has until the end of August to pass a decree regulating short-term lets and room rentals — measures blocked since 2024 — after Junts and Podemos rejected an earlier draft and forced a September delay. The squeeze turns the summer recess into a final attempt to deliver on the government's signature "housing legislature" before the window closes. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Morocco acknowledges 11 deaths on its side of Ceuta border crisis**

Rabat's government admitted for the first time that 11 people died on Moroccan territory during last week's mass crossing into the Spanish exclave, even as it blamed "misinterpretations" of Spain's Supreme Court ruling on swim-back returns and portrayed itself as a "reliable partner." The partial disclosure widens the gap between Madrid's count of 72 fatalities and Ceuta authorities' tally of 88. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **SPD figures back Bavarian conservative Ilse Aigner as next federal president**

After CSU leader Markus Söder proposed Landtag president Ilse Aigner to succeed Frank-Walter Steinmeier, senior Social Democrats including parliamentary vice-president Katja Mast are publicly endorsing the Bavarian conservative as a "binding" cross-party choice for Germany's largely ceremonial head of state. The early bipartisan signalling suggests Berlin wants to avoid a bruising succession fight ahead of next year's election cycle. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Court tightens rules on handwritten wills after illegible signature dispute**

Germany's Federal Court of Justice has clarified that a scrawled or wave-shaped signature on a holographic testament does not meet legal requirements, in a ruling that could invalidate home-written wills where testators rushed the final line. The decision matters as ageing households increasingly draft documents without notaries to save costs. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Police launch nationwide speed-camera marathon after summer road deaths**

Authorities in several federal states are mounting a coordinated enforcement blitz against excessive speeding, citing recent fatal crashes on rural roads as summer holiday traffic peaks. The campaign is part of a broader attempt to cut serious accidents before the autumn return-to-school commute. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin parties draw contrasting housing lines ahead of state election**

rbb24 compares how the capital's parliamentary parties plan to expand supply and regulate rents in their Abgeordnetenhaus election programmes, with all promising more construction but diverging sharply on how far the state should intervene in private leases. The policy gap will shape coalition talks if the current government loses its majority in September. ([rbb24][7])

* **Berlin and Brandenburg mark Roma and Sinti Holocaust remembrance**

Commemorations across the capital region on Sunday honoured the hundreds of thousands of Roma and Sinti murdered under National Socialism, with survivors and officials warning that anti-minority rhetoric is resurfacing in European politics. The dual-city ceremony keeps Berlin's public memory calendar tied to Brandenburg's parallel events. ([rbb24][8])

* **Public votes on Germany's 2026 youth word of the year**

The Berlin-based "Jugendwort" competition has opened voting on shortlisted slang terms for 2026, continuing an annual barometer of how teenagers repurpose language on social media. The light-hearted poll nonetheless tracks how mainstream culture absorbs — or mocks — Gen-Z vocabulary each summer. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Taiwan security chief warns China's hybrid warfare is testing the whole region**

National Security Bureau director-general Tsai Ming-yen told Nikkei Asia that Beijing is combining cyber operations, disinformation and economic coercion in ways that pressure not only Taipei but neighbouring democracies watching for escalation templates. The interview frames Taiwan as an early-warning system for grey-zone tactics that stop short of open invasion. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **West African governments tighten anti-LGBTQ laws as politicians scapegoat minorities**

Campaigners say Senegal's decision in March to double prison terms for gay sex and Niger's first criminalisation of same-sex intimacy in February are part of a regional wave of legislation targeting LGBTQ people for electoral gain. Activists argue politicians are choosing the easiest minority to attack when budgets and jobs fail to deliver. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Wall Street accelerates blockchain experiments despite systemic-risk warnings**

Financial Times reporting says major banks and exchanges are piloting distributed-ledger settlement for bonds and funds even as regulators warn that tying critical market infrastructure to crypto-style technology could amplify contagion in a stress event. The push shows incumbents trying to own the plumbing before fintech rivals or foreign venues set new standards. ([Financial Times][12])
