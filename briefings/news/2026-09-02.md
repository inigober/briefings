# News Briefing — 2 September 2026

The UN now treats 1.5°C overshoot as inevitable, Germany has named Russia for Leipzig, Berlin is shutting the Russian House, and China's robot push is on the factory floor.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A UN report says crossing 1.5°C is now inevitable**

The UN's climate science process now expects the planet to exceed the 1.5°C safety limit — the Paris threshold meant to avoid the worst impacts — in the coming years; the same report says temperatures can still be brought back if emissions are cut hard, and it again flags Spain's heat, fire and drought mix as critical vulnerability. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Overshoot is no longer a warning line; it is the baseline a tourism-and-outdoor economy will be measured against.

* **Teenagers are walking out of Ceuta on their own**

A month after the enclave's migration surge, adolescents are leaving the Morocco border in a daily drip — "I can't take any more, I'm going" — after what they call the worst month of their lives, a reversal that does not wait for Madrid's returns plan. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Ninety years on, Spain is still counting the scientists Franco's side shot**

Sadí de Buen, the epidemiologist whose 1920–36 campaign came close to erasing malaria, was executed in Córdoba on 2 September 1936; the disease returned after the Civil War wrecked the anti-malaria service, and a CSIC archive is now reconstructing about 500 purges of scientists. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Berlin has named Russia for the Leipzig drone attack**

Germany now attributes the failed bomb-drone strike at Leipzig airport to Moscow, and police have found explosives and homemade rockets at a substation that feeds Jänschwalde, Brandenburg's largest coal plant. ([EL PAÍS][4])

* **Forklift-maker Jungheinrich is launching a €100 million startup fund**

Jungheinrich, the Hamburg-listed warehouse-equipment group, is putting venture capital into a new €100 million pot run by subsidiary Uplift Ventures under Christian Noske — a manufacturer buying optionality rather than waiting on its core machines. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **The AfD is sending its own observers into polling stations**

The party and the far-right network Ein Prozent are calling on supporters to watch polling stations and especially the count, a turnout of "election observers" that other civic groups are also matching ahead of this month's Landtag votes. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Russian House on Friedrichstraße is being closed**

After the Leipzig drone, the federal government has cancelled its contract with the Russisches Haus — the Soviet-era cultural centre on Friedrichstraße — in the hardest package of Russia measures since 2022. ([Berliner Zeitung][7])

💡 Shutting a Friedrichstraße venue is the visible city price of a Leipzig attribution.

* **Florida Eis is leaving a too-small Spandau factory for Magdeburg**

The Berlin ice-cream brand, already in supermarket freezers nationwide, has outgrown its plant on an old airfield at the city's western edge and is expanding production west into Saxony-Anhalt. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Union's women have signed Werder captain Lina Hausicke**

1. FC Union, in its first Bundesliga season for the women's team, has taken Werder Bremen's captain to strengthen a side that opened the campaign with a heavy loss to Bayern. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **China's robot race is on the factory floor, not the humanoid stage**

The useful Chinese advantage is scaling industrial arms and the supply chains behind them — a manufacturing superpower applied to robots — rather than the bipedal demos that fill trade-fair halls. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The industrial contest is motors, wrists and suppliers, not the humanoid that photographs well.

* **Climate risk is becoming a line on Indian property values**

From Mumbai to Bengaluru, developers are spending millions to flood-proof buildings, and that spend is starting to show up as a measure of value, not as corporate-social aftercare. ([Bloomberg][11])

* **Mark Carney has told Washington to start being serious**

Days before Ottawa is due to answer Donald Trump's tariffs with duties of its own, Canada's prime minister rebuked US officials for insulting his country and told them to treat the trade fight as a government-to-government problem. ([The Guardian][12])
