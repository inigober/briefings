# News Briefing — 17 July 2026

Luxembourg's amnesty verdict and Madrid's jury-trial order frame Spain's judicial summer, Germany weighs consent-law reform and Lebanon troop options as green-power storage lags, Berlin trims universal school transport, and Beijing and New Delhi race to own the AI build-out while London slashes African aid.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **CJEU backs Spain's amnesty law as a route to "national reconciliation"**

eldiario.es reports Luxembourg judges upheld the bulk of the procés amnesty statute, rejecting claims it breaches EU anti-terror rules or financial-interest protections. Puigdemont and Junqueras still await a Constitutional Court ruling this autumn before the Supreme Court must apply the law to their cases. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When Europe's top court blesses a politically divisive pardon statute, Spain's autumn constitutional fight shifts from legality to whether Madrid's judiciary will obey a reconciliation mandate it long resisted.

* **Madrid appeals court trims Peinado's case but orders Begoña Gómez to face a citizen jury**

eldiario.es says provincial judges struck two of four alleged offences and lifted passport restrictions yet upheld sending the prime minister's wife to a Tribunal del Jurado on influence-trafficking and embezzlement charges. The mixed ruling keeps Spain's most politically charged criminal file alive while narrowing the judge's original indictment. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Andalucía's Vox vice-president taps former Falange leadership candidate as deputy minister**

eldiario.es profiles Rafael Sánchez Saus, a medieval-history professor who once sought to lead Falange Española, as Manuel Gavira's number two for tourism and justice in Juan Manuel Moreno's coalition government. The appointment tests how far the PP–Vox pact will embed hard-right intellectuals inside regional executive power. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Poll finds younger Germans strongly back "only yes means yes" sexual-offence reform after Bundesrat vote**

Handelsblatt cites a survey showing 46% of respondents favour affirmative-consent standards in criminal law, with under-35s the most supportive after the upper house backed the government's package. Older voters remain sceptical, suggesting generational friction as Berlin tries to modernise Sexualstrafrecht. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Foreign minister Wadephul proposes EU stabilisation mission for Lebanon after UNIFIL winds down**

Tagesspiegel says Johann Wadephul wants European troops to help secure the border with Israel once the UN mission ends, framing the idea as crisis prevention rather than combat deployment. The pitch lands as Berlin debates how to project influence in the Middle East without joining US strikes on Iran. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Missing grid storage emerges as the bottleneck holding back Germany's renewable power surge**

Tagesspiegel explains that wind and solar already cover large shares of generation yet output and demand still misalign for lack of batteries and pumped hydro at scale. Utilities are expanding storage projects, but analysts warn the build-out pace may leave surplus green electricity stranded for years. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Transport senator Bonde wants to end free school tickets for higher-earning Berlin families**

Tagesspiegel reports Ute Bonde is pressing to means-test the popular zero-fare pupil pass as the city faces a €1bn budget gap, arguing well-off households should pay again. The proposal pits fiscal consolidation against a signature mobility benefit ahead of September's state election. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Berlin and Brandenburg oppose federal plan to shorten child-maintenance advance payments**

rbb24 says CDU family senator Katharina Günther-Wünsch criticises Bund-level proposals to stop Unterhaltsvorschuss at age 14 instead of 16, while still wanting tougher enforcement against absent parents. The split shows city conservatives resisting welfare cuts that their federal party is championing. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin court sentences man to twelve years for fatal stabbing attack**

rbb24 reports judges convicted the defendant of killing a victim with a knife in the capital, imposing a long custodial term after a trial that examined intent and self-defence claims. The verdict closes a high-profile street-violence case that had unsettled the neighbourhood where the attack occurred. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Xi lays out China's ambition to lead global AI standards and deployment**

Financial Times says the president used a Shanghai forum to call for "positive" use of artificial intelligence while Beijing pushes influence over technical norms and export markets. The speech pairs domestic champion-building with diplomatic outreach to Southeast Asian leaders at the same venue. ([Financial Times][10])

* **India races to build giant AI data centres as critics warn coastal cities will pay the water and power bill**

The New York Times reports officials are courting hyperscale projects to close a technology gap, yet residents near proposed sites fear megawatt loads will drain scarce resources without creating durable local jobs. The tension mirrors debates from Virginia to Singapore over who bears the infrastructure cost of the AI boom. ([The New York Times][11])

* **UK aid cuts could slash bilateral support to some African countries by up to 90%**

The Guardian cites newly published Foreign Office tables showing Labour's reduced overseas budget will hit bilateral programmes hardest in several African states over the next three years. Critics say the figures signal Britain retreating from development diplomacy just as climate and conflict pressures intensify across the continent. ([The Guardian][12])
