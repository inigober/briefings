# News Briefing — 15 November 2026

Spain's campaign narrows to vote arithmetic and Junts's veto on Sánchez, Germany and Berlin grind through weapons ambitions and slow local politics, and a $4.2tn rout in AI-infrastructure stocks unsettles markets worldwide.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The Socialist party (PSOE) is gaining among centrist and left-leaning voters but risks losing about a million votes on its conservative flank, according to the third campaign tracking poll**

EL PAÍS's latest tracking, one of the daily readings it publishes before the 29 November general election, suggests the PSOE's gains in the centre do not yet offset defections to the right. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **The PSOE has filed a public complaint with the tax agency against Dos Mil Palabras, the publisher of the digital outlet Okdiario, asking it to review how the company declared €242,000 paid for the legal defence of Koldo García**

García is a former aide to ex-minister José Luis Ábalos and a figure in the corruption case around him; the businessman Aldama accuses Okdiario's director of blackmailing Ábalos in exchange for advertising. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Miriam Nogueras, who heads the list of the Catalan pro-independence party Junts, says she is "not running to make or unmake Spanish prime ministers" while ruling out backing Pedro Sánchez for re-election**

She claims credit for the gains that pressure on Sánchez has won Junts, yet vetoes him for any re-election. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany is developing hypersonic weapons in Schrobenhausen, Bavaria, with a range of more than 2,500 kilometres and speeds above ten times the speed of sound, to strengthen deterrence**

Handelsblatt, citing the development work in Schrobenhausen, presents the long-range precision weapons as a way to strengthen deterrence. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Own-brand Christmas sweets such as chocolate Santas and gingerbread are noticeably cheaper in German supermarkets this year than last, while branded products often cost more**

A price comparison reported by the dpa news agency finds that shoppers who choose supermarket own labels pay perceptibly less than a year ago. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Voyager 1, the probe launched in 1977, is now one light-day from Earth, farther than any other human-made object, and a NASA researcher calls it "a milestone for humanity"**

In an interview she discusses what the craft is still sending back from space, along with Jupiter's moons and the search for extraterrestrial life. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Eight weeks after Berlin's borough-assembly (BVV) elections, only two borough mayors have been chosen and negotiations are still running in most of the city's twelve districts**

The Linke could end up supplying almost half the mayors, and talks in Neukölln and Marzahn-Hellersdorf are expected to take longer than elsewhere. ([rbb24][7])

* **Berlin has recorded eight diphtheria cases, a new high, according to rbb24**

Diphtheria is a bacterial infection of the throat that vaccination has made rare in Germany, so a cluster of this size is a notable public-health signal. ([rbb24][8])

* **Eleven months after the attack-triggered blackout in Berlin's south-west, repairs at the site are still under way and the construction zone will grow, disrupting traffic for years**

The Tagesspiegel quotes an official conceding "we know the situation is extremely unsatisfactory" as the work site keeps growing. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Shares in the "picks and shovels" companies of the AI boom, the firms that build data centres, have lost $4.2tn in value as a wider tech sell-off unravels the AI trade**

The Financial Times reports that the firms building the physical capacity behind AI have been hit hard by the sell-off. ([Financial Times][10])

* **South Sudan's first national election since independence in 2011, originally set for December, will be delayed**

Analysts had warned that the vote, in a country still without an elected national government, faced a legitimacy crisis. ([The New York Times][11])

* **Javier Milei, Argentina's "chainsaw" president, says he will run for a second term in the 2027 election, citing the economic upswing under his government**

His programme of deep spending cuts remains contested and has imposed considerable social hardship. ([Handelsblatt][12])
