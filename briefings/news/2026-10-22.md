# News Briefing — 22 October 2026

Javier Milei's intrusion into Spain's campaign and the PP's gamble on the Constitutional Court dominate Madrid, while Berlin's rail works and a mild-winter forecast, Taiwan's first chip strike and a Peruvian basic-income trial fill out the day.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Milei's visit hands Sánchez a campaign gift and leaves the PP and Vox uneasy**

Argentina's far-right president Javier Milei, on a visit to Spain, called prime minister Pedro Sánchez an "impresentable dictador" and declared himself a friend of Madrid's Isabel Díaz Ayuso and Vox's Santiago Abascal, giving the PSOE a foil and the PP and Vox an embarrassment ahead of the 29 November general election. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **The PP is betting the election will let it take control of the Constitutional Court**

The PP has blocked renewal of Spain's Constitutional Court — the body that decides whether laws respect the constitution — for ten months, waiting for a Senate majority large enough to impose the four justices that chamber appoints. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The Senate result, not the Congress one, decides who controls the court.

* **Spain's builders agree more homes are needed but not how many they can deliver**

Developers, contractors and experts agree that raising supply is essential to ease the housing crisis, but they disagree about the sector's real capacity to build. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **E-cigarettes could end up untaxed by accident under Germany's new tobacco-tax law**

The law, meant to raise billions for the federal budget by lifting rates on nicotine products, may by mistake leave e-cigarettes untaxed, Handelsblatt reports — a further twist in the drafting error that brought it into force early. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **What a flu, Covid or RSV self-test actually tells you as the cold season starts**

Handelsblatt asks whether knowing which virus caused a sore throat or fever changes anything for patients, and how useful the home tests that became familiar during the pandemic really are. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Board games boom as loneliness grows and social cohesion weakens**

Tagesspiegel, citing dpa, notes that card and dice games are booming just as many Germans report loneliness, and asks what sitting around a table does for young and old players alike. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Works at Westkreuz disrupt five S-Bahn lines, including one to the airport**

Construction at the Westkreuz junction in western Berlin brings cancellations and delays across five lines of the city's suburban rail network, so passengers must plan for changes. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The UN weather agency warns of a strong El Niño that could bring Berlin a wet, mild winter**

The World Meteorological Organization expects an especially heavy El Niño, a periodic warming of the tropical Pacific that shifts weather worldwide, and rbb24 reports it could tend to mean a wet, mild winter for Berlin and Brandenburg. ([rbb24][8])

* **Schöneberg's Begine, Berlin's longest-standing women's meeting place, reinvents itself as a Flinta space**

The venue is opening to Flinta people (women, lesbian, intersex, non-binary, trans and agender), and its operators describe the conflicts that have followed. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Taiwan sees its first-ever chip workers' strike, at a Micron plant**

Workers at the US memory-chip maker Micron's plant in Taiwan have walked out in the first strike by chip workers on the island, a labour test for an industry that anchors the global semiconductor supply chain. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **A £2-a-day basic income for Amazon communities helped protect the rainforest**

A University of Bath study of a pilot in Peru found that almost 90% of Indigenous participants given an unconditional basic income adopted environmentally beneficial practices, feeding a debate over paying communities that maintain globally important ecosystems. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Tesla trims its sales target as Musk explores merging it with SpaceX**

The carmaker reported flat revenue and falling profits while Elon Musk, its chief executive, pivots it from electric vehicles toward AI and sets a lower sales target, as Tesla explores a combination with his rocket company SpaceX. ([Financial Times][12])
