# News Briefing — 6 October 2026

Spain is now in a snap-election fight, the Bundestag faces named tax votes, and Qualcomm is preparing to pay Huawei for 5G and AI patents.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Sánchez decided this Congress was useless — and called the election**

Pedro Sánchez and his inner circle were already talking about dissolving parliament on Thursday afternoon, once they saw Junts heading to no on the housing decrees; the doubt was the state of the left, but they saw no option except to go to the country now. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 Yesterday Moncloa was still drafting an election; today the campaign is the fact.

* **Podemos and the Frente Amplio have ten days to decide whether they are one list**

The parties to the left of the Socialists — the rump of the old Sumar space — must pick a candidate in days: Mónica García is the name gaining ground, IU says that is not settled, Más Madrid is cold on a deal with Podemos, and Podemos wants open primaries on 14–15 October. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **The grandchildren law and the 29 November census are now on a collision course**

Spain's electoral roll for 29 November closed on 1 August, before the Supreme Court provisionally limited the vote of descendants of Franco-era exiles under the "ley de nietos"; the foreign ministry had already warned how hard it would be to check 168,896 files now in doubt. ([eldiario.es][3])

💡 The snap date freezes a court fight the census calendar was not built to absorb.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The Greens have put seven tax laws to a named vote**

On Thursday morning the Bundestag will hold a rare cluster of recorded votes: the Greens have tabled seven namentliche Abstimmungen on tax bills, forcing every MP to put a name next to a rate rather than hide in a show of hands. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Germany has already had more sun this year than a normal year gets**

By the end of September the DWD, Germany's weather service, had counted 1,783 hours of sunshine — more than a typical full year — and the 2022 record is in reach, a climate shift the paper says is not only good news. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Kleinanzeigen's chatbot is reading private sales chats**

The AI on Kleinanzeigen.de, Germany's main classifieds site, reads buyer–seller messages to suggest replies; there was no consent prompt, though the setting can be switched off. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Four plant islands are being floated on Berlin's grey canals**

Water engineer Ralf Steeg is putting miniature biotopes — planted rafts meant to cool the water and work as a "high-rise for fish" — onto canals in Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg that have been little more than concrete ditches. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Tear gas was set off in a Kreuzberg school**

Several people were injured in a Reizgas attack inside a school in Kreuzberg, a local shock that lands on a city still arguing about how safe its classrooms are. ([rbb24][8])

* **A 65-year-old died after an apartment fire in Neukölln**

A man of 65 has died following a flat fire in Berlin-Neukölln. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Qualcomm will pay Huawei for patents — for the first time**

Qualcomm, the US chip designer, has agreed to pay Huawei for 5G and AI patents, the first time the American firm will write that cheque in a licensing breakthrough between two companies the chip war was supposed to keep apart. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

💡 A royalty payment is doing what export controls did not: put a price on using Huawei's portfolio.

* **Reliance has shut every 7-Eleven in India**

Reliance Industries, Mukesh Ambani's conglomerate, has closed all 7-Eleven stores in India — an abrupt end to the Japanese convenience brand's bet on the Indian high street. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Turkey and Pakistan are sending troops after Houthi strikes on Medina**

After attacks by Yemen's Houthi militia on Medina and the Saudi kingdom, Turkey and Pakistan — allies that signed a defence pact in August — are sending soldiers to help. ([Tagesspiegel][12])
