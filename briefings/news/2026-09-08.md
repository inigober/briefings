# News Briefing — 8 September 2026

Spain's new state housing portal, a German winter-gas warning, and China's AI-fed export jump set the frame as Berlin's young voters and a Russia–North Korea road bridge fill out the map.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Madrid opened a booking site for the first 800 state flats**

Pedro Sánchez, Spain's prime minister, launched Casa 47 — a public portal meant to let people reserve the first 800 state-owned homes the way they would a hotel — and used the event to attack Madrid's PP premier Isabel Díaz Ayuso over a €6 million penthouse. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The rent-extension decree is still frozen; the government is substituting a booking site for legislation.

* **A 3% tax on hundred-millionaires could fund aid that saves millions of lives**

ISGlobal, a Barcelona global-health institute, says a levy at that rate on the ultra-rich could raise enough to cover much of the humanitarian aid still missing worldwide — and, in its tally, save up to 30 million lives by 2030. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Attacks on journalists have become routine in Ceuta's protest week**

Insults and threats — from "traitors" to rape wishes — are now a regular part of covering the North African enclave, as rallies multiply and reporters are treated as enemies of the street. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **New storage figures say a very cold winter could leave Germany short of gas**

INES, the association that tracks the country's caverns, says reserves look adequate only in a normal winter; in a deep freeze, households and firms would feel a shortage. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The story has moved from "stores are historically low" to a customer-level risk if the weather turns.

* **Heat has already killed at least 16,000 people in Germany this year**

Researchers put the 2026 heat death toll at that floor and say many such deaths still go uncounted — a public-health number, not only a weather note. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Wagenknecht and the AfD's Chrupalla both ruled out governing together**

On Markus Lanz's talk show, BSW founder Sahra Wagenknecht and AfD leader Tino Chrupalla each stepped back from a joint government and spent the hour accusing the other of ducking responsibility. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Young Berliners say city politics does not speak for them — and back Die Linke**

A new BerlinTrend poll finds 16- to 34-year-olds feel poorly represented; among them Die Linke, the left party already leading several city-wide surveys, is on 38% with the 20 September Abgeordnetenhaus election twelve days away. ([rbb24][7])

* **An Olympics swimming venue at the Olympiastadion is now costed at €106 million**

If Berlin wins a future Games, summer races are meant to be staged at the open-air pool by the 1936 stadium — a rebuild that only makes sense if the bid does. ([rbb24][8])

* **A Pride memorial tree has been torn out, days after a rainbow bench was wrecked**

The tree marked victims of an attack on Berlin's CSD, the city's Pride march; the bench in the same memory was vandalised only days earlier. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **China's August exports jumped 25%, a lift the FT ties to the AI build-out**

The customs print lands just before a Trump–Xi meeting later this month and reads as real-economy demand for the kit behind data centres, not only another stimulus headline. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 Yesterday's China story was a bank recap; this is the trade number.

* **Anthropic and OpenAI's bankers want investment-grade ratings after the IPOs**

An IG stamp would let the AI labs — and the infrastructure partners building their compute — borrow more cheaply, as if they were utilities rather than venture bets. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Russia and North Korea have opened their first road bridge**

The one-kilometre crossing of the Tumen River, between Khasan in Russia's far east and the North, is another physical tie since the Ukraine war. ([The Guardian][12])
