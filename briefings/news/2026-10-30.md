# News Briefing — 30 October 2026

Spain's rail inquiry turns from the crash itself to the oversight system around it, while a reported ECB leadership change, Berlin's new parliament and a possible resumption of US strikes on Iran set the tone for institutions and markets under strain.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **First official report puts the Urda collision speed at 160–200 km/h**

The authority investigating the Urda crash, in which an AVE high-speed train hit a stationary Iryo and 69 people died, says in its first report that impact came at "between 160 and 200 km/h", that the black boxes are recovered, and that it is examining organisational and procedural factors that could have worsened the failure. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Atocha controllers reportedly ordered the wrong train-control mode before the crash**

Supervisors at Adif's Atocha control centre (Adif runs Spain's rail infrastructure) selected the LZB 4 mode of the German-designed train-protection system on the Madrid–Seville line, which allows maximum speed, when LZB 3, which caps speed so a train can stop for an obstacle, applied; one is quoted saying "I was getting confused". ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Spain's rail safety agency has missed its audit plans for Adif for six years**

The state rail safety agency AESF, which regulates and inspects the network, has fallen short of its annual plans to audit infrastructure managers like Adif since 2020, and has long warned of heavy workload and "considerable staff turnover". ([eldiario.es][3])

💡 The body meant to check Adif's safety systems has itself been short-staffed, so the inquiry now covers the regulator as well as the operator.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Christine Lagarde is reported to be stepping down as ECB president**

Handelsblatt's morning podcast says Lagarde, who heads the European Central Bank (the Frankfurt-based body that sets euro-area interest rates), is resigning, leaving her successor to face inflation control and a bond-market strain, with first candidates emerging. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **A private-equity owner plans a paywall and a broker pivot at Finanzen.net**

Inflexion, the investor that took over the investing portal from publisher Springer, wants to introduce a paywall and shift the business towards being a neobroker (an app-based online broker), a change that will affect many of the site's active traders. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **A didactics professor argues creativity belongs in every school subject, not just art and music**

Anja Mohr, who teaches subject didactics, cites Germany's PISA results (the OECD's international school tests) in calling for more freedom and a different style of teaching across all subjects. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Left Party's Klein becomes president of Berlin's new state parliament**

At the Abgeordnetenhaus's first sitting, nearly six weeks after the Berlin election, deputies chose Linke (Left Party) politician Klein as parliamentary president. ([rbb24][7])

* **Berlin police see no targeted calls for violence ahead of the 31 October protest day**

Despite warnings from some politicians, police say they have no intelligence of deliberate calls for violence around the nationwide protest day organised by the "Initiative Gen Z regelt". ([rbb24][8])

* **The Berliner's autumn guide picks the city's best upcoming opera, dance and theatre**

The English-language city magazine lists must-see opera, dance, theatre and performance art for the coming weeks. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Narendra Modi's 25 years in power show that fast growth is not enough without jobs**

The Economist marks a quarter-century of the Indian prime minister's rule, arguing that he is learning rapid economic growth will not placate voters if it fails to produce employment. ([The Economist][10])

* **Oil rises on signs the US may resume strikes on Iran**

Bloomberg's oil roundup says crude climbed as Washington appeared ready to restart military action against Iran, risking fresh turbulence in a region central to global energy flows. ([Bloomberg][11])

* **Vietnam tightens airport safety after drone intrusions**

Nikkei Asia reports that drone incursions have prompted a nationwide airport safety response in Vietnam. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
