# News Briefing — 23 September 2026

Spain's prime minister wants a global AI rulebook for children, German family firms are opening to outside capital, Berlin asks whether Die Linke can govern, and British Columbia is suing OpenAI over a school shooting.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Sánchez wants a global alliance against AI's "techno-oligarchs"**

At the UN, Spain's prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, called for a binding international pact on artificial intelligence — with clear limits and cooperation — so the technology serves children and the public rather than a handful of firms. ([La Vanguardia][1])

* **Spain's judicial council has closed Bolaños's complaints against judge Peinado**

The CGPJ, the body that governs Spanish judges, has definitively archived Justice Minister Félix Bolaños's complaints against Juan Carlos Peinado, the investigating judge in the case against the prime minister's wife, after conservative members of the standing committee plus one progressive vote. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Venezuela's Delcy Rodríguez asked Sánchez to keep a channel open**

On the UN sidelines, Venezuela's number two, Delcy Rodríguez, greeted Pedro Sánchez and asked to keep talking about Venezuela; the Spanish prime minister said Spain remains committed to the country's future. ([La Vanguardia][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **German family firms are opening to outside capital**

A new generation of Mittelstand owners — the privately held firms that still dominate much of German industry — is bringing in external investors for professional structures, not rescue money, a shift that loosens the old family-equals-control model. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The classic German firm is becoming a managed company that happens to have a family name.

* **The Union's new labour deputy wants the pension reform untouched — and an end to retiring at 63**

Johannes Winkel, the CDU/CSU group's new deputy for work and social policy, says the coalition's Rentenreform should pass without cuts and that the Rente mit 63 early-retirement track should go, putting pressure on labour minister Bärbel Bas to deliver. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Public patients are being asked if they will pay privately for a slot**

In Germany's two-tier system, people on statutory insurance can wait six months for a gastroscopy while specialists ask whether they would rather pay out of pocket — and many of the doctors still taking them are retiring. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin is asking whether Die Linke can — and wants to — govern**

Two days after the Left's historic Abgeordnetenhaus win, the question has moved from the result to coalition talks: whether the party has enough governing experience, and how it handles antisemitism accusations, before it can lead the city. ([rbb24][7])

* **More people drowned in Berlin waters this season, DLRG says**

Berlin's water-rescue service reports more fatal accidents in city lakes and rivers than last year, with men and recklessness the main pattern — a local safety failure, not a weather note. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **The person behind BerlinClubMemes lives half on Instagram, half in the club**

The anonymous feed that jokes about Berlin nightlife is run by someone who treats a 72-hour party stretch as fieldwork — a portrait of how club culture now circulates as memes as much as nights out. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **British Columbia is suing OpenAI over the Tumbler Ridge school shooting**

The Canadian province has filed in San Francisco against OpenAI and Sam Altman, arguing the February mass shooting could have been stopped if the company had told police that the attacker used ChatGPT to plan it — a bid for damages and product changes. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 The claim is not that a chatbot pulled a trigger; it is that a duty to warn the police now sits on the model-maker.

* **China is inventorying Broadcom kit in its state AI data centres**

Beijing is counting how much switching gear from the US chip firm Broadcom sits inside state-backed data centres, as it tries to build a domestic AI stack and reduce exposure to American hardware. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 The bottleneck is no longer only power; it is whether state compute still runs on US switches.

* **Sharp wants a dish that talks to any satellite, not only Starlink**

Japan's Sharp is building a universal satellite antenna meant to work across networks, a hardware bid to loosen SpaceX's Starlink hold on the ground terminal. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
