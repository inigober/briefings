# News Briefing — 27 September 2026

Spain's housing fight is now a Sol camp and a Tuesday pact, Berlin's SPD has agreed to talk to the Left, and South Korea is treating AI as a public utility.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The eviction has forced a coalition turn before Tuesday**

Pedro Sánchez's minority government and its partners now say they must lock a housing pact before Tuesday's vote, and Sumar — the junior coalition party — has threatened to walk if the decree stays thin, after Saturday's protests over the eviction of Maricarmen Abascal. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The deadline is no longer only Junts arithmetic; it is whether the coalition holds.

* **A UCO file is being spun into a false PSOE passport story**

Spain's Audiencia Nacional is investigating Leire Díez, a former Socialist activist, over an alleged parallel network; some outlets used a Civil Guard (UCO) report to claim Justice officials backed a passport for Venezuelan ex-minister Nervis Villalobos — a request eldiario.es says those officials refused. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Galicia's PP is floating a village Trumpist for Ourense**

Ana Vázquez, a long-serving PP deputy from Ourense known as Ana de Bande, spent years on the back benches under Rajoy, rose under Casado, and is now being talked up by Galicia's president Rueda as a possible mayoral name. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Thuringia's premier wants the Länder to rewrite the CDU**

After the party's state-election losses, Voigt — the CDU minister-president of Thuringia — is using a closed session to demand a programme reset and says the federal states, not only Berlin headquarters, should drive it. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Care funds say a coalition freeze would empty the pot**

Germany's planned Pflegereform — the rewrite of long-term-care insurance — has become a fresh Union–SPD fight; the Pflegekassen, the funds that pay for care, warn that if the reform is blocked the money runs out. ([Handelsblatt][5])

💡 A delay is not a pause in the accounts; it is a solvency event for the care system.

* **A start-up wants aircraft to make their own lift**

"Blown lift" is a wing trick that uses directed airflow to generate lift at low speed; Tagesspiegel asks whether that physics can open a niche for electric flight that today's batteries cannot. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The SPD will talk to Die Linke and the Greens**

Berlin's Social Democrats, third after the Abgeordnetenhaus vote, had their state board decide on Saturday to open exploratory talks with Die Linke and the Greens — the same pairing the Left endorsed at Friday's congress. ([rbb24][7])

* **Brandenburg will not extend BER's night-flight ban**

Berlin Brandenburg Airport already bars flights from midnight to 5am; Die Linke wants that curfew lengthened, and Brandenburg's transport minister, Crumbach, has said no. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Union Berlin won a send-off for Kreilach**

The Köpenick club beat Rijeka in a friendly that doubled as a farewell for long-time player Kreilach. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **South Korea is betting the state can make AI a public good**

The deputy prime minister is selling a national "AI for all" push — state-backed deployment across the economy — even as some at home still treat frontier models as a catastrophic risk. ([Financial Times][10])

* **US private-equity landlords are now a midterm target**

Institutional buyers of American homes, blamed for pushing up housing costs, have become a campaign issue ahead of the midterms, according to an FT analysis of the crunch. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 Spain's fight with housing funds and America's fight with PE landlords are the same political argument in two markets.

* **The Pacific hurricane season is crowding Hawaii**

Hawaii is on its third close call of the season, in a Pacific basin producing overlapping major storms. ([The Atlantic][12])
