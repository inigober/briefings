# News Briefing — 10 August 2026

Ceuta's civic backlash, a Chinese industrial plant beside a Spanish naval arsenal, and Iran's security reshuffle during Hormuz talks frame a day when Berlin's mayoral race tightens, Germany accelerates airport drone defences, and Beijing opens an Arctic shipping lane.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Chinese factory beside Ferrol naval base triggers espionage alarms**

Spain's defence ministry and national intelligence service are scrutinising plans for a Chinese-owned industrial plant adjacent to the Ferrol naval base and Navantia shipyard, which builds advanced combat systems alongside US technology. Madrid fears the installation could enable surveillance of a strategic Atlantic arsenal at a moment when Europe is rearming and Washington is tightening export controls on dual-use infrastructure. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Thousands march in Ceuta demanding government response to migrant crisis**

Residents mobilised by Ceuta's four religious communities marched through the exclave chanting "Ceuta does not surrender," demanding concrete help after the 30 July mass border crossing overwhelmed local services. The protest turns a weeks-long migration emergency into a test of whether Pedro Sánchez's government can reassure a city of 84,000 that feels abandoned while Madrid negotiates with Rome and Rabat. ([La Vanguardia][2])

* **Meloni attacks Spain's regularisation while Italy recruits migrant workers**

Giorgia Meloni's government has publicly condemned Madrid's plan to regularise undocumented migrants even as Rome expands Italy's "Decreto Flujos" work-permit quotas for non-EU labour — a programme migrant-rights groups say is dysfunctional and often leaves recruits in irregular status. The contradiction sharpens the Italy–Spain feud that already produced reciprocal Schengen border checks and gives Sánchez a counter-narrative ahead of an emergency EU interior-ministers meeting. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Transport minister vows faster airport drone defences after Leipzig scare**

Federal transport minister Patrick Bilger says he will accelerate equipping German airports with drone-detection and interception systems after the explosive-laden unmanned aircraft found beside a Ukrainian cargo plane at Leipzig/Halle triggered a national security prosecution. The pledge moves hybrid aerial threats from a Saxony police case to a nationwide aviation-security procurement timeline weeks before the autumn legislative session. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Tunnel magnate Herrenknecht backs Deutsche Bahn chief amid punctuality crisis**

Engineering entrepreneur Martin Herrenknecht has publicly defended Deutsche Bahn CEO Evelyn Palla and demanded politicians give her political cover to push through long-delayed corridor upgrades, as punctuality on flagship routes such as Berlin–Hamburg remains near one in four. The rare private-sector endorsement matters because infrastructure contractors and the state-owned operator usually blame each other when voters punish the government for rail unreliability. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **June and July were warmest on record for western Europe**

New climate data show western Europe's average temperature for June and July reached 21.62°C — the highest since records began — amid heatwaves, low river levels and wildfire alerts across the continent. The reading turns this summer's drought and fire-risk headlines into a measurable regional benchmark rather than anecdotal weather reporting. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **CDU mayoral candidate Evers leads personal poll ahead of Left rival**

A Berlin state-election survey puts conservative Spitzenkandidat Kai Wegner's running mate Martin Evers well ahead of Die Linke challenger Ferat Eralp in direct comparisons, even as both trail their parties' headline numbers and many voters say they would not back any named candidate for mayor. The gap suggests personality and governing experience may matter more than headline party strength in a fragmented race where no bloc is near a majority. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Karls adventure park tightens rules on outside food**

The Karls Erlebnisdorf chain, popular with families in eastern Brandenburg near Berlin, now allows only half-litre water bottles, baby food, biscuits and rice cakes — banning larger picnics that visitors say make a day out affordable. The policy has drawn local outrage and feeds a wider debate about how theme parks price captive audiences as household budgets stay squeezed. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **Regional express evacuates 500 passengers after Hannover football match**

A Berlin–Cottbus RE2 service carrying roughly 500 supporters evacuated all passengers on Sunday evening after an incident following a Hannover 96 fixture, disrupting one of the capital region's main east–west rail links. The disruption highlights how major-event traffic still strains Deutsche Bahn's contingency planning on corridors where punctuality is already under political scrutiny. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **China opens regular Arctic container route to bypass maritime chokepoints**

Beijing has launched its first scheduled container service through the Arctic "Ice Silk Road," cutting Europe–Far East transit to about 20 days by sailing north of Russia instead of through the Suez Canal or Strait of Malacca. The route gives Chinese exporters a climate-change-enabled alternative to Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian shipping lanes that Washington and its allies can more easily interdict during crises. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 If Arctic ice retreat makes the northern passage commercially routine, European ports and insurers must price a permanent shift in Asia trade flows — not a seasonal curiosity.

* **Iran replaces security council chief as Hormuz talks intensify**

Tehran appointed former Revolutionary Guard commander Mohsen Rezaei to head its Supreme National Security Council after Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr stepped down, reshuffling the team negotiating with Washington over reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The leadership change arrives as Gulf mediators seek to prevent a new escalation that would choke roughly one-fifth of global oil transit. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Sony and TSMC commit $6.3 billion to advanced image-sensor plant in Japan**

Sony Group and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company will jointly invest $6.3 billion in a Kumamoto factory producing next-generation image sensors for smartphones, automotive cameras and industrial robotics. The deal extends Japan's semiconductor renaissance beyond memory chips into the optics stack that AI-enabled devices need at scale. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
