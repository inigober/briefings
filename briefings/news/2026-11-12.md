# News Briefing — 12 November 2026

Spain's housing strike, Germany's shelved taxes and Berlin's hospital walkout show public bodies squeezed by living costs, while Brazil and South-East Asia look for ways to cut their dependence on foreign tech and fuel.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's first general strike in 14 years, called by unions over housing costs and low wages, ended with uneven turnout, large street protests and dozens of arrests**

The stoppage was more visible on the streets than in workplaces, with stronger participation in industry, carmaking and ports, and the conservative PP clashed openly with the unions ahead of the 29 November general election. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **EL PAÍS's election model, which simulates Spain's 29 November general election 20,000 times, finds 150 of the 350 seats almost decided and another 150 probable**

About fifty seats remain genuinely open, and the paper publishes province-by-province poll results for each of them. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Spain's government has pursued a ban on TikTok after a report alleged the social-media platform is lax about child sexual abuse material**

Politico Europe reports that Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's government is pushing the ban just weeks before the general election. ([Politico Europe][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany's governing coalition enters the decisive budget day with a compromise: the promised electricity-tax cut is dropped, but so are the proposed sugar and plastic levies**

Handelsblatt's morning newsletter notes that one problem remains unresolved, despite the deal. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **The DAX, Germany's blue-chip share index, is close to a new record before results from Siemens, Merck and other groups**

A leadership crisis at the chipmaker Nvidia is also returning to investors' attention. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **A Tagesspiegel columnist warns homeowners who must refinance loans taken out ten years ago against signing new Bausparverträge, building-society savings contracts that pair saving with a later loan**

The column calls them far too expensive and sets out alternatives. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Verdi union has called staff at all three Charité sites, the Berlin university hospital, to a two-day strike in a wage dispute**

Verdi is demanding that planned operations on patients be postponed, which raises the question of how heavily daily hospital work will be affected. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The 22-year-old suspect in the killing of 13-year-old Lisa from Marzahn has died in hospital, where he was in intensive care with severe head injuries**

Investigators say their inquiries will continue despite the suspect's death. ([rbb24][8])

* **Tagesspiegel revealed in 2024 that social-housing flats in Neukölln were being sold as expensive owner-occupied apartments, and an insolvency procedure could now stop the resulting fines from being paid**

The fines were imposed over the sales, which Tagesspiegel first exposed. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

💡 A fine only deters if the offender remains solvent, so the penalty meant to protect Berlin's subsidised housing stock may fail where it matters most.

## World 🌐

* **Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines are to set up shared oil reserves**

Nikkei Asia reports the plan, in which three South-East Asian economies would hold fuel stocks jointly. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **AI has been used to prove that a theory underpinning fundamental physics is trustworthy, though the key insights appear to have come from human physicists**

The Economist notes that the credit for the central ideas still appears to belong to people. ([The Economist][11])

* **Foreign Policy argues that Brazil's election shows the danger of digital chokepoints, since President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's campaign had to break Big Tech's monopoly**

The article frames dependence on a handful of platforms as a political vulnerability for democracies. ([Foreign Policy][12])
