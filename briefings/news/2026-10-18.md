# News Briefing — 18 October 2026

Spain's Senate fight tightened and the rental listings did not flee, Berlin's left closed a socialization deal, and a $10tn AI build-out ran into steel, power and nickel.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Vox may stand aside so the PP can lock the Senate**

Spain's Senate is the upper house that can still block or slow a government; Vox is considering not running candidates in some provinces and backing the People's Party, a tactical withdrawal aimed at stopping the left's new joint Senate list. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The chamber the left treated as insurance last week is the one the right may now try to lock.

* **Landlords have not fled the rental market — they have paused**

Property portals and landlord groups say there has been no mass withdrawal of listings after the 7 October housing decrees, only a temporary 2–5% dip in ads while the Permanent Deputation and the 29 November vote decide whether the rules survive. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **The PNV says deeds, not fear, are how it keeps Vox out**

Aitor Esteban, the Basque Nationalist Party's president, argues that no party has done more in practice to keep Vox from power, while refusing a "fear vote" even as he warns that people not yet worried will be if the ultras govern. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The navy is looking for more coast**

Germany's navy needs more berths, people and kit; planning is already under way at North Sea and Baltic stations for where to build and where to spend — and what is still open. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **An expert walks through whether weather really hurts**

A Tagesspiegel explainer tests the folk claim that scars and headaches forecast a weather front — *Wetterfühligkeit* — and what, if anything, the body is actually doing. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **A dying phone battery is enough to cancel the evening**

A survey finds some smartphone users so uneasy when the charge runs low that they drop plans, even though a power bank would do — a small measure of how far the handset has become the day's infrastructure. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Red–green–red talks are over — formal coalition talks can start**

Die Linke, the Greens and the SPD have finished exploratory talks on a Berlin government, including a compromise on *Vergesellschaftung* — the socialization of large housing stocks — and formal negotiations could open in early November. ([rbb24][7])

💡 The city's most legally exposed housing idea is now inside the coalition draft, not only on the protest signs.

* **Universities are opening lecture halls to the city again**

Several Berlin Hochschulen are running public lecture series this term — AI in art, family history and literature, food waste — for anyone who wants a seat. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Pupils marched in Kleistpark for better schools**

School students protested in Schöneberg's Kleistpark for better education. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **America's $10tn AI build-out is starting to fray**

The United States is in the middle of a multi-trillion-dollar bet on data centres, power and chips; the FT says that boom is now hitting the unforgiving economics of the physical world — sites, steel and electricity, not only share prices. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 After two days of stressed AI debt, the constraint is concrete and kilowatts.

* **VW, Ford and Tesla batteries are tied to Indonesian forest loss**

An analysis links nickel mines in the supply chains of western carmakers to clearance of primary rainforest on Tolaki land in Indonesia — the climate case for the electric car colliding with the woods that produce the metal. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Venezuela is shopping for $76bn of oil money**

Acting President Delcy Rodríguez said the country expects almost $76 billion of oil and gas investment over five years, a bid to pull private capital back into a wrecked energy industry. ([Bloomberg][12])
