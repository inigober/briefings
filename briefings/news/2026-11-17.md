# News Briefing — 17 November 2026

Spain's campaign turns to who will pay for housing, wages and rural subsidies, Germany and Berlin face carmaker anxiety and years of rail disruption, and Washington's tightening grip on AI chips reverberates from Asia to Africa's health systems.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Conservative leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo now denies ever promising to build a million homes, although the pledge is point 144 of his Partido Popular's (PP) manifesto and a staple of his rallies**

Feijóo told Politico that housing is something Spain "needs", a retreat that matters because affordability is one of the sharpest issues ahead of the 29 November general election. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **The governing Socialists (PSOE) propose higher taxes on listed companies whose chief executive earns more than 100 times the median employee, a threshold crossed at Santander, BBVA, Telefónica, Inditex and Ferrovial**

The manifesto idea targets pay inequality at the flagship firms of the Ibex 35, Spain's main stock index. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Regional governments run by the PP and the far-right Vox are raising hunting subsidies, from cartridge aid to free licences and a plan to cull wolves, and signal unprecedented backing if the right wins a majority in Congress**

Hunting is a rural-identity issue where the two parties already govern together, so these regional measures preview what a national coalition could do. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Elon Musk's announcement of a multibillion-euro investment, at a time when other carmakers are shrinking, prompts Handelsblatt to ask what the Tesla boss sees in the location that German manufacturers do not**

The newsletter frames it as a test of whether the established industry is misjudging the future of car-making. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **North Rhine-Westphalia's interior ministry has banned the Mönchengladbach chapter of the Hells Angels motorcycle club and arrested a leading member in a police raid**

It is the latest in a run of bans against the club in the state, whose authorities have pursued it chapter by chapter. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **A new climate-impact index, published by researchers who rank countries by storm, flood and heatwave damage, finds that Germany is far from spared even though the poorest states suffer most**

Losses from extreme weather now run to billions of euros, a reminder that Germany's own exposure is growing. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Berlin Ringbahn, the S-Bahn orbital line, must be closed on one of its most important sections for more than a year**

The Tagesspiegel warns that construction and closures will weigh on commuters for years, with the S-Bahn hit hardest. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Vanessa Emde, a controversial Berlin MP, says she wants to stay in the Left Party's (Linke) parliamentary group and rejects the accusations against her, as the group's leadership prepares a motion to expel her on Tuesday**

The row complicates the Linke's role in the coalition talks in the city. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Cycling groups including Changing Cities and the ADFC cycling club call for a redesign of Berlin's streets after an eight-year-old girl on a bicycle was run over by a bus in Mitte and died**

A local politician joined their demand for safer street layouts. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **The United States is moving to restrict China's remote access to advanced AI chips, closing a route through which Chinese firms could use American hardware without buying it, after the Nvidia scandal over the chipmaker's China ties**

The step follows earlier export controls and sent Asian stock markets lower on Tuesday. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **South Africa is rolling out a groundbreaking HIV drug in what Foreign Policy calls an early test of whether the country can fight the epidemic without US funding, after Washington withdrew from PEPFAR, its flagship global AIDS programme**

The result will show whether African health systems can fill the gap left by American aid cuts. ([Foreign Policy][11])

* **A small laboratory in Ghana, Minohealth, is warning that AI models can lower the barrier to designing biological weapons and is pressing for regulation of DNA synthesis**

Its engineers build their own AI model, Moremi, which gives the warning a practitioner's basis. ([Rest of World][12])
