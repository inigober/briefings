# News Briefing — 30 July 2026

Ayuso's publicly funded luxury flat, Sánchez's bid to host an EU firefighting hub, and Deutsche Bahn's return to core profit land as AI euphoria cracks in Seoul — while Berlin cancels another pride parade and Washington–Beijing tech decoupling reaches SpaceX's factory floor.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Madrid planning rules block Ayuso government's "temporary office" in €multi-million Chamberí attic**

EL PAÍS reports municipal zoning for the 485-square-metre penthouse the regional executive bought with public money permits only residential use, undermining the president's claim the property is a professional workspace during renovations at Sol. The finding intensifies scrutiny of opaque procurement and whether Ayuso's administration misrepresented the purchase to taxpayers. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Sánchez asks Brussels to base new Mediterranean wildfire hub at Mallorca airfield**

eldiario.es says Pedro Sánchez has written Ursula von der Leyen urging the EU to locate its western-Mediterranean firefighting coordination centre at Son Sant Joan, after the Commission announced the hub concept amid Spain's record blaze season. The lobbying turns disaster response into a regional economic prize as Balearic leaders court federal infrastructure. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Inspector who investigated PP "caja B" to testify on parallel dirty war against Podemos**

eldiario.es reports judge Santiago Pedraz will let police chief Manuel Morocho tell internal-affairs investigators about pressure tactics he witnessed while sidelined from Gürtel probes, including workloads designed to stop him monitoring PP financing cases. The testimony could widen the Audiencia Nacional's look at institutional harassment of opposition parties. ([eldiario.es][3])

💡 When the same investigator links caja B and Podemos cases, Spain's corruption fights stop looking like isolated scandals and start resembling a pattern of using police deployment as political containment.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Deutsche Bahn reportedly back in profit on core rail operations in first half**

Handelsblatt says the state-owned group is set to record a surplus on its main passenger and freight business despite punctuality woes and crumbling track, citing an operational rebound even as long-distance reliability remains politically toxic. The numbers matter for Berlin's debate over whether to fund network modernisation through fare rises or fresh federal injections. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Autonomous driving advances on motorways but urban approval still lags**

Handelsblatt surveys how German carmakers are close to hands-free highway systems while city regulators and Brussels still limit robotaxi roll-outs, with the piece contrasting EU caution against faster Chinese deployment. The gap shapes whether domestic OEMs can monetise software or cede urban mobility to foreign platforms. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Former Berliner trades capital flat for Mecklenburg village house renovation**

Tagesspiegel profiles Carmen Leidereiter, who left Berlin for a 25-person lakeside community and has spent two years restoring a dilapidated home she calls a life dream. The portrait illustrates how remote-work flexibility and housing costs are redistributing eastern Germany's demographic map beyond the capital's commuter belt. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Organisers cancel "Zug der Liebe" parade after Christopher Street Day attack**

rbb24 says Berlin's annual love train demonstration will not run this year as organisers cite security concerns following the deadly vehicle attack that struck revellers after CSD celebrations. The cancellation extends the capital's reckoning over how to stage open-air queer events without normalising a permanent militarised perimeter. ([rbb24][7])

* **Rock singer Tamara Danz honoured with commemorative silver coin**

rbb24 reports the East German frontwoman of Silly, who died in 1996, will feature on a limited Bundesmint collectors' edition celebrating influential women in German music. The minting keeps GDR cultural memory in official circulation as reunification-era artists gain belated state recognition. ([rbb24][8])

* **Plagiarism complaint against constitutional judge Brosius-Gersdorf not substantiated**

rbb24 says a Potsdam court review found no evidence supporting claims that Brandenburg constitutional judge Doris Brosius-Gersdorf copied academic work, closing a case that had threatened to embroil the state's highest bench. The ruling removes one reputational cloud over a court that frequently rules on Berlin–Brandenburg federal disputes. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **ByteDance bets TikTok profits on an all-in AI expansion**

The Financial Times reports the Chinese owner of TikTok is redirecting cash and talent toward large-language-model development, treating generative AI as its next growth engine despite regulatory headwinds in Washington and Brussels. Investors question whether an app-centric conglomerate can compete with dedicated model labs without diluting its core advertising business. ([Financial Times][10])

* **SpaceX moves to purge Chinese nationals and components from its supply chain**

Nikkei Asia says Elon Musk's rocket company is tightening vendor rules and personnel screening to exclude Chinese-linked parts and staff, mirroring Pentagon pressure on defence-adjacent manufacturers. The policy shows how export-control politics is reaching private space contractors that straddle commercial launch and national-security contracts. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Retail investors in Seoul face steep losses as AI-linked chip stocks slide**

The Financial Times describes Korean households that levered into Samsung and SK Hynix shares now confronting paper wipeouts as memory makers' earnings disappoint lofty AI demand forecasts. The selloff turns Asia's retail brokerage boom into a political risk for a government betting chip exports will fund the next growth cycle. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 When mom-and-pop traders in Seoul move in sync with hyperscaler capex cycles, AI optimism stops being a Silicon Valley story and becomes a household balance-sheet shock across export Asia.
