# News Briefing — 18 September 2026

A national-court green light for Ceuta's port camp, a 25-cent fuel offer in Berlin's last campaign weekend, and AI slipping from chips into batteries and rival-lab hacks set the frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's national court has unblocked a migrant camp in Ceuta's port**

The Audiencia Nacional, Spain's national high court, has refused to freeze a reception camp in Ceuta's port, so the government can start moving people off the beaches of the North African enclave still holding July's crossing. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 A police-union injunction was the last procedural brake on the "exit plan"; the court declined to pull it.

* **Spain's nutrition agency has withdrawn a menopause study built on data from men**

AESAN, the Spanish food-safety and nutrition agency, has pulled a menopause-nutrition paper after it emerged the evidence came from men, and has asked its scientific committee to start again. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Internal Affairs has authenticated the Kitchen tapes that implicate Rajoy's PP**

Spain's police Internal Affairs unit has, for the first time, validated Villarejo recordings that put Mariano Rajoy's PP in the picture on Operation Kitchen — the 2013 police job to steal evidence from Luis Bárcenas, then the party's treasurer. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Merz and the Länder are talking a 25-cent cut at the pump**

Chancellor Friedrich Merz and the vice-chancellor held a special session with the Länder on Thursday over record petrol prices, with relief of up to 25 cents a litre on the table. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 Two days after "state before market" on energy, the coalition is putting a number on the subsidy.

* **Handelsblatt's three theses: AI can already do damage**

The morning briefing argues that documented AI "outbreaks" already show what the technology can break — even if extinction talk is overdone — unless two governments actually intervene. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **German investors are watching Destatis and a 31-year Japanese rate high**

Friday's Destatis releases and the Bank of Japan's lift to the highest policy rate in 31 years are the two markers Handelsblatt flags for German markets into the weekend. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin is in line for an extra €5 billion for culture and security**

The capital is being lined up for additional federal money earmarked to make the city safer and more attractive — a late cheque with the Abgeordnetenhaus, Berlin's state parliament, voting on Sunday. ([The Berliner][7])

* **The interior department will not say if the Berlin AfD is under surveillance until after the vote**

Officials will only answer whether the local AfD is under Verfassungsschutz — Germany's domestic intelligence service — observation once a new Senate is in place, citing neutrality until Sunday. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

💡 Voters will elect a House without an official answer on whether the Berlin AfD is already a surveillance case.

* **Affordable housing will weigh on the ballot for two-thirds of Berliners**

A new survey finds rents and building failures will help decide the vote for two-thirds of the city, who hand the Senate failing grades on housing — the campaign's rent talk as a number, two days out. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Panasonic Energy is chasing the AI power boom**

Panasonic Energy, the battery arm of the Osaka group, is moving into the same AI build-out that is straining grids and chip lines — selling cells into the boom, not another accelerator. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Almost 50 people have died in Nigeria after drinking methanol-laced alcohol**

At least 48 people have died in Ondo state, in southern Nigeria, after a locally brewed drink thought to contain methanol, with about 100 more in treatment — a poison that tracks cheap, barely regulated village stills. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Researchers used Anthropic models to break into OpenAI**

A security firm hacked the ChatGPT-maker using tools from Anthropic, the Claude lab next door — a lab-on-lab breach that treats frontier systems as attack tools, not only as products. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 The competitive question is no longer only whose model is smarter; it is whose model can break the other's house.
