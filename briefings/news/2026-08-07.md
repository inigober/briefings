# News Briefing — 7 August 2026

Federal prosecutors take over the Leipzig drone probe, Madrid's Planifica scandal deepens, and Berlin's election rivals draw coalition red lines as drought costs hit Rhine industry and Nigeria records its largest hostage rescue.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Ex-Civil Guard officer killed ex-partner after filing 45 harassment complaints against her**

A former Guardia Civil agent shot Laura Cruz dead in southern Spain, leaving three orphaned children, after years of stalking that included 45 police complaints prosecutors say he filed purely to harass her — and after his latest expulsion from the force. The case exposes how Spain's gender-violence protection system can fail even when victims repeatedly seek state intervention. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Madrid's Planifica public firm traces roots to Púnica corruption scandal**

Planifica Madrid, the regional public company Isabel Díaz Ayuso's government used to buy a €4.3 million Chamberí penthouse, is the successor to Nuevo Arpegio — a land-planning vehicle at the centre of the Púnica urban-corruption case that ensnared dozens of People's Party officials. eldiario.es reports senior executives at the firm that purchased the disputed attic earned at least €162,690 from contracts linked to a principal Púnica defendant, reviving questions about whether Ayuso's luxury office deal sits atop an unreformed patronage network. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Summer remote work from holiday homes can void labour protections, lawyers warn**

Spanish employment lawyers say workers logging on from poolsides or second residences outside their registered municipality risk breaching contract terms on workplace location, confidentiality and occupational safety — grounds employers have used to justify dismissals. The warning matters as August telework-from-the-beach culture collides with post-pandemic rules that never fully standardised where remote work legally happens. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Federal prosecutor opens terrorism probe into Leipzig airport drone**

Germany's federal prosecutor has taken over investigations into the explosive-laden drone found beside a Ukrainian military cargo plane at Leipzig/Halle airport, treating the incident as a potential attack on transport and logistics infrastructure. The escalation from a Saxony police case to a national security prosecution signals Berlin now classifies hybrid drone threats against allied supply hubs as a federal counter-terrorism matter, not local sabotage. ([Frankfurter Allgemeine][4])

💡 When the Bundesanwaltschaft steps in, intelligence agencies gain wider surveillance powers and the case becomes a template for how NATO-adjacent sabotage is prosecuted inside Germany.

* **Rhine drought threatens €913 million in industrial output this summer**

Record low water on the Rhine is forcing partially loaded barges and pushing freight onto road and rail, with economists warning the Rhine basin alone could lose roughly €913 million in production if dry conditions persist through August. The figure turns Germany's familiar summer low-water story into a measurable industrial-policy risk for chemicals and manufacturing clusters that depend on cheap inland shipping. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **China's July export surge to Germany widens bilateral trade gap**

Chinese firms sold sharply more goods to Germany in July while buying less there, widening an already lopsided trade relationship that Berlin's industry lobby has flagged as a competitiveness threat. The imbalance complicates Chancellor Friedrich Merz's pitch that German manufacturers can compete in open markets without resorting to the tariff walls Washington is erecting. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Left party draws coalition red lines as Berlin election polls lift**

Berlin's Left party is publicly setting conditions for post-election coalition talks — including rent caps and higher social spending — before September's Abgeordnetenhaus vote, irritating Social Democrat leaders who say premature demands expose deep trenches in a campaign where polls show the Left gaining. The friction previews how a fragmented result could stall government formation if progressive partners cannot agree on price controls before negotiating with conservatives. ([rbb24][7])

* **Wildcat sighting confirmed again in Brandenburg nature park**

Naturalists have documented another European wildcat in the Dahme-Heideseen nature park southeast of Berlin, adding to evidence that the shy predator is recolonising forests around the capital after decades of absence. The find feeds into Brandenburg's debate over balancing rewilding with agriculture as the state updates its species-protection monitoring. ([rbb24][8])

* **NASA measurement may confirm 90-year-old quantum prediction on vacuum structure**

Berlin researchers are among physicists parsing data from NASA's IXPE satellite suggesting magnetars could make vacuum double-refraction — a quantum effect predicted in the 1930s — visible for the first time, though sceptics say alternative explanations remain. If confirmed, the finding would tighten the link between astrophysical observation and fundamental particle theory tested in capital-region labs. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **Nigeria frees more than 300 hostages in largest single-day rescue**

Nigerian military, police and counter-terrorism units recovered more than 300 abducted people from forests inside Kainji Lake national park, which President Bola Ahmed Tinubu called the country's largest single-day hostage release on record. The operation highlights both improved joint-security capacity in Nigeria's north-central bandit zones and the scale of kidnapping economies that still hold thousands captive beyond this raid. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Japan's Kirin buys Canadian vitamin maker for $1.3 billion**

Japanese beverage and pharma group Kirin has agreed to acquire Canadian health-science firm Kyowa Hakko Bio's vitamin operations for $1.3 billion, betting that fortified foods and supplements can offset stagnating beer demand at home. The deal shows how Japan's consumer giants are pivoting toward regulated health markets as domestic demographics shrink and overseas nutrition brands offer faster growth. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Jane Street seeks to offload $11 billion debt pile to fund AI bets**

Secretive US trading firm Jane Street is in talks to shift roughly $11 billion of its debt to private-credit investors including Pimco, freeing balance-sheet room for further artificial-intelligence-related investments, the Financial Times reports. The structure matters because it reveals how even elite market-makers are tapping non-bank lenders to keep pace with AI capital demands without diluting equity. ([Financial Times][12])
