# News Briefing — 2 October 2026

Junts has already doomed Spain's housing vote, Merz is warning of hybrid war as Berlin recounts seats, and the US is staging forces while Trump weighs strikes on Iran.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Junts will sink both housing decrees — and wants them pulled before the vote**

Junts, the Catalan party whose votes Pedro Sánchez's minority government needs in Congress, will vote down both emergency housing decrees on Friday and has asked for the texts to be withdrawn first, calling them too weak on access and likely to make the shortage worse. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The housing week ends as a floor vote the government already knows it will lose.

* **Voters want rent intervention — including more than half of PP and Vox supporters**

A 40dB poll for EL PAÍS finds more than two-thirds of Spaniards want measures to hold down rents even at the landlord's expense, including over 55% of PP and Vox voters, in the week after 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal's eviction. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Vox is selling deportation as a housing policy**

Like other European far-right parties, Santiago Abascal's Vox has no supply or tenure programme for the shortage; it points at migrants and proposes mass removals — a line distilled in a party post that if Maricarmen had been "Morocarmen" she would have a home. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Prien has ruled out a duty of service for everyone**

Education minister Karin Prien has said a compulsory service for all young people is "not up for debate"; what she is designing instead is a "Gesellschaftsdienst" — a civic-service scheme — on top of today's purely voluntary year, not a return of Wehrpflicht and Zivildienst. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 A named civic service is how a CDU minister parks conscription without restoring the draft.

* **Merz says Europe should expect severe Russian hybrid attacks**

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has warned that Russia will intensify hybrid attacks on Europe — sabotage and disruption short of open war — and called the escalation a sign of "desperation" over the war in Ukraine. ([Financial Times][5])

* **Germany has had fewer tornadoes this year — and the weather service knows why**

The Deutscher Wetterdienst, Germany's national weather service, has counted 31 tornadoes in 2026 so far, below the recent average, and says it can explain the drop. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Exploratory talks paused until Saturday — and a recount just unseated five MPs**

Die Linke, the Greens and the SPD — the three parties that could form Berlin's next Senate after this month's House election — have adjourned their first exploratory round until Saturday; a recount has already cost five members their Abgeordnetenhaus seats. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **A house for street children has opened in Lichtenberg**

The charity Straßenkinder has opened "Butze", a residential and support house in Lichtenberg for minors who are homeless or have no fixed address — a group the city's visible homelessness numbers usually hide. ([rbb24][8])

* **Twenty founding professors were honoured — without their cabinet patrons**

Berlin awarded Gründerpreise to twenty professors who have started companies; the federal ministers who sponsor the scheme, Dorothee Bär and Katherina Reiche, did not attend — Bär was at the Oktoberfest — and the absences landed badly in the capital. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **The US is moving thousands of troops as Trump weighs strikes on Iran**

Washington is deploying thousands of additional troops to the Middle East, with the carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt due in the region by the end of November, while President Trump considers strikes on Iran. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The carrier calendar is the policy: force is being staged while a strike decision is still framed as pending.

* **Brazil's attorney general has told Washington to stay out of the election**

Attorney-general Jorge Messias said foreign meddling "cannot be tolerated" after a Guardian report that the Trump administration was pushing money toward a campaign against Brazil's supreme court, days before Sunday's presidential first round. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Singapore wants a larger stake in nuclear fusion**

Singapore is positioning itself for a bigger role in nuclear fusion, the still-uncommercial technology that would generate power by fusing atomic nuclei rather than splitting them. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
