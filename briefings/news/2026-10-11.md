# News Briefing — 11 October 2026

Spain's closing legislature is being scored on growth versus housing, Germany is trying to freeze health-insurance rates, and a Houthi strike on Riyadh plus a pause in AI borrowing set the wider frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The legislature's scorecard is growth and jobs — and a housing failure**

Since 2018 Spain's economy has grown about 15% and employment is at a record, but the Sánchez governments never fixed the shortage of homes or the level of rents — the drag eldiario.es puts at the centre of a legislature now heading into a snap election. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The campaign opens on the one failure the growth numbers cannot hide.

* **The Constitutional Court president says attacking the court is attacking democracy**

Cándido Conde-Pumpido, whose term as president of Spain's Constitutional Court has expired, told EL PAÍS in the week the court ordered Catalonia's *procés* embezzlement amnestied that there is no *lawfare* — political abuse of the courts — but there is a problem of justice being pulled into politics. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **A debate over whether to remove Franco-era monuments or convert them**

Spain still has statues and memorials from Franco's dictatorship; some historians now argue that taking them down erases the crime scene, and that the better path is to recut them so they condemn what they once praised. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The coalition is trying to lock health-insurance rates for 2027**

Germany's statutory health funds — the *Krankenkassen* that most workers pay into — were headed for another contribution rise; the coalition has passed a savings package meant to hold rates in 2027, and is still amending the sums. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **CDU leaders are meeting on how to get out of an election crisis**

The Christian Democrats, Germany's main centre-right party and still the senior partner in government, are holding a closed leadership session after a run of disastrous polls — billed internally as one of the worst crises in the party's history. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Germany's intelligence chief says Putin can no longer end the war**

BND-Chef Jäger, the head of Germany's foreign intelligence service, says Vladimir Putin can no longer stop the war he started; Berliner Zeitung sets that line beside MI6 and other services on whether Russia is stuck. ([Berliner Zeitung][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Thousands marched through the government quarter for Palestine**

A pro-Palestine demonstration took thousands through Berlin's Regierungsviertel, the strip of federal ministries and the Reichstag. ([rbb24][7])

* **Christmas trees will cost more this year**

The Christmas-tree season is about to start, and the festive green will be more expensive in many places — Nordmann fir versus a cheaper species, a street stand versus cutting your own. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Alba lost on the road in Trier**

Berlin's basketball side lost to the Gladiators in Trier in the Basketball-Bundesliga, the top German league. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Investors are cooling on the AI debt binge**

After months of record bond sales to fund data centres and other AI infrastructure, lenders are becoming more cautious — a test of whether the buildout can still be financed at the same pace. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The constraint has moved from chips to the credit window.

* **A missile strike on Riyadh airport killed 12 and wounded hundreds**

Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen hit King Khalid airport in the Saudi capital, days after a run of attacks — a jump from Red Sea shipping and Medina to a Gulf capital's main airport. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 The war is no longer only a maritime and holy-city problem for Riyadh.

* **Robot baristas and K-pop dancers are the face of Korea's physical AI**

South Korea's AI story is moving onto the shop floor: robots that make coffee and dance in K-pop shows are being sold as the commercial edge of "physical AI" — machines that act in the real world, not only in chat windows. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
