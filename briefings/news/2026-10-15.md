# News Briefing — 15 October 2026

Spain's campaign is now about pensions and whether the CIS can be believed, advertised rents jumped in Germany, and Asia's AI trade just hit a trading halt.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The right says the pension system is about to explode — Brussels and AIReF do not**

Spain's public pension system is the pay-as-you-go scheme that pays today's retirees from today's contributions; the People's Party and Vox are calling it "broken," a "zombie," or about to *petar*, but the scare does not match a recent reform the European Commission backed or the two spending-rule tests the fiscal watchdog AIReF says the system has already passed. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Collapse talk is doing campaign work that the official scorekeepers will not sign.

* **Sociologists do not believe the CIS, because the right will not pick up**

The CIS is Spain's official public pollster; sociologists say its latest campaign survey over-represents the left because many right-wing voters refuse the interview, a sample bias the agency denies. ([EL PAÍS][2])

💡 The number may be measuring who still answers the phone, not who will vote.

* **A single Senate list could give the left 40 extra seats**

Spain's Senate is the upper house that can still block or slow institutional change; an internal PSOE paper says a united left list there is close and could add about 40 seats — enough for an absolute majority that would stop a PP–Vox steamroller even if the right wins the Congress. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Advertised rents are rising faster, especially in the big cities**

The Kiel Institute tracks asking rents in Germany's 30 largest cities; that increase has accelerated, with existing flats the ones in demand and Berlin and Düsseldorf among the sharpest markets. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **AI can already design new viruses; Germany is not ready for that**

Models can now sketch novel viruses, but turning a design into a usable bioweapon still depends on labs and delivery; what the experts agree on is narrower: Germany's civil preparation for that emergency is thin. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

💡 The constraint is wet-lab capacity and state readiness, not only whether a chatbot can draft a genome.

* **A report asks what Kiesewetter knew before Nord Stream was blown**

Roderich Kiesewetter, a CDU lawmaker who long blamed Russia for the 2022 Nord Stream attack, now faces a report of concrete warnings beforehand — and a question about why the Chancellery has stayed silent. ([Berliner Zeitung][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The French Gymnasium has already had its own blockades**

The Französisches Gymnasium is Berlin's state French–German school in Mitte; after the pupil protests in France, two students there say they joined blockades because "the state does not listen to us pupils." ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **A Neukölln app asks: are you still alive?**

Bastian Matzen built a check-in tool so people who live alone — first his mother — can send a regular sign of life; younger users are now on it too. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Union thinks the money for a bigger Alte Försterei is coming**

1. FC Union Berlin, the Köpenick club, says financing for the Alte Försterei stadium expansion now looks close enough to sign. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **The AI sell-off halted trading in Seoul and sank the Nikkei 5%**

A deeper global dump of AI-linked stocks, after Wall Street fell, triggered a trading halt on South Korea's Kospi and dropped Japan's Nikkei 225 by 5%. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The "casino" line from earlier this week is now a session stop in East Asia, not only a Nasdaq mood.

* **A Caine Prize winner denies his story was written by a machine**

The Caine Prize is the main award for African short fiction; days after Zimbabwean writer Simbarashe Kundizeza won it for *The Blue Fighter*, *The Atlantic* said software had flagged the story as almost entirely AI-generated, and he has denied using a model. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Florida is fighting nine-inch snails that eat houses and carry brain worms**

Giant African snails block roads, scrape stucco and can spread a parasite that infects the brain; the piece argues that only Florida has learned how to run that eradication as an operations problem. ([The Atlantic][12])
