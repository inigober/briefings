# News Briefing — 05 November 2026

Washington's post-midterm blame game, Madrid's summit diplomacy and an election campaign shadowed by a rail disaster, and the sudden death of a Bavarian deputy premier in Berlin frame an edition about political leaders absorbing shocks.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Ibero-American summit opens in Madrid with Milei set to meet Sánchez for the first time since calling him a "dictator"**

King Felipe opened the 30th Ibero-American Summit, the annual meeting of Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking countries, praising the bloc as a route to "greater freedom and prosperity", while Argentina's Javier Milei is due to face Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez after branding him an "unpresentable dictator" on his last visit. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Polls after the Urda train crash get their own data podcast as the 29 November election nears**

EL PAÍS launched "Entre datos", a podcast with election analyst Kiko Llaneras, to track how voting intentions for the 29 November general election are shifting after the Urda rail accident that forced transport minister Óscar Puente out. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Guatemala's Bernardo Arévalo uses the Madrid summit to warn against "normalising authoritarianism"**

Arévalo, the only progressive head of state in Central America, told EL PAÍS that the region must resist the drift towards strongman rule while reviewing his country's political challenges. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Bavaria's deputy premier Hubert Aiwanger found dead in a Berlin hotel room**

Aiwanger, 55, leader of the Free Voters party and Bavaria's economy minister and deputy minister-president, was found lifeless in a Berlin hotel room, prosecutors said; circumstances of his death have not yet been detailed. ([rbb24][4])

* **Jochen Flasbarth, Germany's long-time "shadow climate minister", leaves the civil service saying nature policy has grown harder under the CDU–SPD coalition**

The veteran state secretary, who shaped environmental policy across several governments, told Tagesspiegel that criticism of the Greens' building-energy law was a "smear campaign" and that conservation work has become more difficult under Chancellor Friedrich Merz. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Handelsblatt says Trump's midterm loss turned on affordability, the same issue now pressing European governments**

The business daily's morning newsletter argues that Trump lost his core voters mainly over living costs, a theme it says is becoming just as urgent in Europe. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Demolition of the Lindentunnel in Mitte begins, with traffic restrictions until the end of next year**

The former tram tunnel under Unter den Linden, out of service since 1951, is being removed from Wednesday, disrupting traffic on the boulevard until late 2027. ([rbb24][7])

* **Tesla opens a research centre in Berlin-Köpenick**

The carmaker, which already runs its Gigafactory outside the city in Brandenburg, is adding a development site in the south-east district. ([rbb24][8])

* **Tierpark welcomes Kibo, a new bull elephant from Erfurt**

The zoo in Friedrichsfelde has taken in the African elephant bull as its newest arrival from Thuringia. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump's party enters the post-midterm era split, with JD Vance already blaming the Iran war**

After Democrats took Congress on Tuesday, the vice-president is pinning the defeat on the war with Iran while Trump faces what eldiario.es calls an internal schism in the final stretch of his term. ([eldiario.es][10])

* **Russia deepened its military backing of Iran with a missile-launcher deal**

Moscow has increased arms aid to Tehran, the Financial Times reports, even as the war in Ukraine strains its own resources. ([Financial Times][11])

* **China's export curbs on tungsten used in drill bits squeeze the AI supply chain**

Beijing's restrictions on the metal, essential for the bits that drill the hard rock behind chip and data-centre supply chains, are tightening a bottleneck for AI hardware, Nikkei Asia reports. ([Nikkei Asia][12])

## Other Headlines Today 📋

* **Post-midterm markets:** Asian chipmakers slid and China and Australia gained as investors rotated; gold rose above $4,100 as the dollar and Treasury yields fell.
* **Europe cyberattacks:** Suspected hackers who tampered with refrigeration at hundreds of French supermarkets in October are now reported to have hit shops in other countries.
* **Great-power summitry:** China confirmed talks with Washington and Moscow on a possible Trump–Putin–Xi meeting in Shenzhen; nothing is yet binding.
* **Japan:** Defence minister Koizumi wants an open debate on the country's non-nuclear principles as the government rewrites its security doctrine.
* **German coalition:** Merz says an anti-expropriation law is nearly finished; the government is postponing its funded-pension ("Kapitalrente") plan.
* **Urda crash:** EL PAÍS published profiles of the victims after a week of identifications.
