# News Briefing — 17 October 2026

Spain's leftover left talks collapsed and took a housing hit at the top of the PSOE, Germany is pricing 2027 through the grid charge, and the AI boom is now a story of dear chip loans and rival blocs.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The leftover left failed to copy the Canary deal**

Frente Amplio — the Sumar leftover — and Podemos closed a single Congress ballot only in the Canary Islands and Valencia; everywhere else the vote to the left of the Socialists will be split, after the talks turned into a public auction of list places. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 Yesterday's Canary signature was the exception, not a national template.

* **The PSOE's spokesperson resigned over her partner's rental portfolio**

Montse Mínguez, the Socialists' voice in Congress, stepped down after reporting that her partner holds dozens of homes in the rental business — a housing-campaign wound Pedro Sánchez said he had asked her not to inflict. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The street fight over rent is now landing on the government's own spokespeople.

* **Medicinal cannabis is arriving in hospital pharmacies**

Specialist-prescribed cannabis preparations will be dispensed only by hospital pharmacies, mainly for chronic pain that other drugs have not touched — Spain's legal path out of the unlicensed market. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Grid charges are set to rise in 2027**

*Netzentgelte* are the network fees baked into every German electricity and gas bill; a new look-ahead says those charges go up next year, so the 2027 price is being set in the pipes as much as in the wholesale market. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **The Bundestag passed an early-start pension — and MPs still want their own scheme**

The federal day-book records the *Frühstartrente*, an early-start top-up to the statutory pension, passing the Bundestag; Hendrik Wüst, North Rhine-Westphalia's premier, was elected president of the Bundesrat; and Hoffmann is opposing a plan to put politicians into the public pension insurance. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Four younger politicians sketch a Germany of 2050**

Ricarda Lang, Catarina dos Santos-Wintz, Ines Schwerdtner and Daniel Rinkert — a Green, a Christian Democrat, a Left and an SPD name — write their utopias for mid-century, a hope piece from a coalition that has spent the week looking unworkable. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **GP waiting rooms are spilling onto the street**

A Wilmersdorf practice had patients queued outside as respiratory bugs, stomach flu and Covid cases rose together — the city's ordinary winter load, with coronavirus back in the mix. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Prenzlauer Berg is no longer Pregnancy Hill**

The district that once meant full playgrounds and buggies has lost the *Kinderhochburg* reputation that made it a national punchline; the piece asks what replaced the young families. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Berlin table tennis took European mixed-doubles gold**

Nina Mittelham, who plays for the city's TTC Eastside, won a five-set European Championship mixed-doubles final with her partner — a rare Berlin sports result that is not another football scoreline. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Southeast Asia is being asked to pick an AI bloc**

Governments and firms in the region are caught between competing US and Chinese artificial-intelligence stacks — a technology-alignment choice, not only a chip-price story. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Amazon deforestation has fallen to the lowest level on record**

Clearance in the Brazilian Amazon is at the smallest total since records began in 1988, a Lula-era result that the 25 October presidential run-off could lock in or reverse. ([The Guardian][11])

💡 The climate number is now on the ballot.

* **Anthropic is paying Blackstone a double-digit yield for chips**

The AI lab is offering the private-capital group a double-digit return to finance processors — a borrowing cost that sits awkwardly next to its aim of investment-grade status. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 Yesterday's dumped data-centre bonds are today's pricing problem for the labs themselves.
