# News Briefing — 26 October 2026

Lula's win in Brazil and Vučić's concession in Serbia show incumbents' fortunes turning at the ballot box, while Spain's campaign enters its final registration day and Berlin's coalition talks await a party vote.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Left bloc narrows the gap, but PP and Vox still hold a majority**

A poll by the firm Ateneo del Dato for eldiario.es, a month before the 29 November general election, finds the conservative People's Party (PP) still the most-voted force and, together with the far-right Vox, would reach an absolute majority in Congress, even as the left closes some distance and more than 19% of Alberto Núñez Feijóo's 2023 voters would now pick Vox's Santiago Abascal. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **A podcaster with two employees lands Sánchez, Feijóo, Abascal and Colau in his home studio**

Jordi Wild's *The Wild Project*, a podcast with millions of followers and a strong pull among young men, has won agreement from the leaders of the Socialists (PSOE), PP, Vox and the left-wing alliance to appear, a first in Spanish political communication and a sign of where campaigns now go to reach young voters. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Madrid's government delegate resigns after being left off the PSOE's lists**

Francisco Martín Aguirre, the central government's representative in Madrid since 2023, quit saying the conditions to continue no longer exist, and the Council of Ministers is due to name his successor on Tuesday, probably his deputy Pilar Trinidad Núñez. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **A track worker is killed by an ICE high-speed train in Essen**

One worker died and another was rescued seriously injured after being struck by a Deutsche Bahn ICE, the long-distance flagship train; the cause is unclear and the line stays closed for now. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Cruise ship "Mein Schiff 3" collides with two container ships in Bremerhaven**

The cruise liner, about to leave on a 20-day voyage, hit two container vessels in the port of Bremerhaven, and video of the accident has since emerged. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Starting a new job before the written contract arrives: what German labour law says**

Handelsblatt's employment-law explainer answers a common worry for new hires — whether you must show up on the agreed first day when the written contract is missing or late. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin SPD members vote on the coalition as secret Senate scenarios circulate**

The Tagesspiegel's morning Checkpoint newsletter reports that Social Democrat (SPD) members decide on the red-green-red coalition, that behind-the-scenes scenarios for the next Senate are in play, and that Franziska Giffey warns of an "erosion of our democracy". ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Füchse Berlin beat Wetzlar comfortably as Wiede returns**

The handball club won its Bundesliga home game convincingly on the day Wiede made his comeback. ([rbb24][8])

* **A day trip from Berlin: Schwerin's architecture**

The Berliner magazine pitches the Mecklenburg-Vorpommern state capital, a short trip away, for its mix of history, nature and architecture. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Lula wins a fourth term in Brazil; Bolsonaro refuses to accept it**

Brazil's electoral court declared the leftist president Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva winner of the runoff by about four million votes, but his rival Flávio Bolsonaro, son of the former president, is demanding the election be annulled and has promised a legal fight. ([EL PAÍS][10])

* **Serbia's Aleksandar Vučić concedes defeat**

The president who has dominated Serbia for 12 years said "it is clear that we have lost these elections" in a surprisingly early speech congratulating his opponents, after a student-led movement challenged his rule. ([The New York Times][11])

* **China increasingly relies on copying American AI**

Model distillation — training a cheaper model on the outputs of a stronger one — infuriates Washington, The Economist reports, but may end up holding China back. ([The Economist][12])

* **Israel:** Voters elect a new parliament tomorrow, with Netanyahu's heartland towns such as Sderot still firmly behind him and the outcome seen as pivotal for the region's conflicts.

* **North Korea:** Kim Jong-un crossed the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge into China and spoke of wider cooperation.
* **Venezuela:** Opposition leader María Corina Machado returned from exile to crowds in the streets.
* **Spanish campaign:** The deadline to register candidate lists ends tonight, with PP and Vox haggling over Senate lists and the left's split already settled.
* **Markets:** Moody's is reviewing France's credit rating, with a downgrade a risk for markets.
* **Berlin left:** Berlin's Linksjugend clashes with its parent party over motions on Israel ahead of a national congress.
