# News Briefing — 2 August 2026

Pedro Sánchez's emergency appeal to Brussels, a deepening Plus Ultra probe, and Trump's Iran climbdown frame a day when Berlin's Volksbühne reopens with a swimming pool and west African legislatures tighten anti-LGBTQ laws.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Sánchez accuses fellow EU governments of attacking Spain and demands emergency interior-ministers meeting**

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez wrote EU leaders on Saturday accusing several member states of choosing to "attack" Madrid over the Ceuta surge rather than help, and called for an urgent meeting of interior ministers after roughly 50,000 people crossed from Morocco. The letter turns a border crisis into a test of whether Brussels can broker solidarity when far-right governments criticise Spain's handling of returns. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Judge keeps Zapatero at centre of Plus Ultra probe as UDEF wins access to 60-plus bank accounts**

Ten weeks after José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero's imputation in the state airline rescue scandal, National Court judge Santiago Pedraz's latest ruling still places the former socialist premier at the "direction" of an alleged influence-trafficking network and authorises financial police to audit more than 60 accounts linked to him, his family and related companies. The widening forensic scope signals the case is moving from political theatre toward asset tracing. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Trump praises Morocco's king while keeping Spain at arm's length over Ceuta**

EL PAÍS contrasts Donald Trump's warm rhetoric toward King Mohammed VI — cemented by Washington's Western Sahara recognition — with cooler treatment of Pedro Sánchez as the Ceuta crisis strains Madrid's Atlantic alliances. The asymmetry leaves Spain negotiating border pressure from Rabat while its key security partner signals tacit sympathy for Morocco. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **SPD leadership under quiet scrutiny as Bas and Klingbeil face pre-election pressure**

Handelsblatt reports rumblings inside Germany's struggling Social Democrats over whether parliamentary leader Bärbel Bas and co-chair Lars Klingbeil can steady the party before autumn state elections and the next round of black-red reforms. Summer recess has not silenced questions about whether the SPD's top duo can deliver a coherent economic message while the chancellor's coalition polls weakly. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Environmental association to challenge new heating law in court**

Deutsche Umwelthilfe says it will sue in September to overturn the Bundestag's revised Heizungsgesetz, arguing constitutional flaws that were flagged before the vote were never fixed. The challenge keeps Germany's building-decarbonisation rules in legal limbo just as households and installers are meant to plan replacements. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Ceuta surge sharpens Berlin's demands for tougher EU border protection**

Tagesspiegel says the Spanish exclave's migrant influx has reignited Germany's debate over Frontex capacity and return rules, with Berlin joining calls for EU-level fixes as calm slowly returns to the city. For the traffic-light government, the crisis is a domestic security issue as much as a Spanish one — and a preview of how member states fracture when Schengen neighbours disagree on solidarity. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Volksbühne opens ticket lottery for its pop-up swimming pool**

New intendant Matthias Lilienthal's summer project turns part of the historic theatre into a public pool, and Tagesspiegel explains how Berliners can secure scarce admission slots when bookings open Friday. The stunt is part of a broader reset at a venue that has spent years in political and artistic turmoil. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **BFC Dynamo and FC Stahnsdorf open Regionalliga season with wins**

rbb24 reports both Berlin-area clubs started the Regionalliga Nordost campaign with victories on Saturday, giving local supporters early momentum in Germany's fourth tier. The results matter little nationally but keep neighbourhood football visible as the capital's professional sides remain absent from the top flight. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin rowers contribute to Germany's medal haul at European championships**

rbb24 says German crews collected hardware on day two of the European rowing championships in Varese, including performances from Berlin-based athletes. The capital's clubs remain a feeder for national crews even as funding debates continue across Olympic sports. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump calls off fresh Iran strikes, claiming allies helped secure "perimeters of a deal"**

The US president said he cancelled planned attacks after Middle Eastern partners intervened, asserting that outlines of an agreement are now in place even as Tehran's response remains unclear. The reversal averts an immediate escalation but leaves open how Washington will enforce any terms without renewed military pressure. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Tourist plane crashes near Peru's Nazca Lines, killing 13**

A Cessna carrying sightseers went down shortly after departing Pisco, with local police reporting 11 foreign tourists and two pilots among the dead roughly six kilometres from the UNESCO geoglyphs. The disaster highlights how thin-air tourism routes over remote archaeological sites still depend on ageing single-engine aircraft. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Trump's tariff diplomacy is pushing Thailand closer to Beijing, analysts warn**

Nikkei Asia argues Washington's transactional trade policy is eroding Thai confidence in US reliability and accelerating hedging toward China on investment and security. Southeast Asia's second-largest economy is trying to avoid choosing sides, but tariff threats are narrowing its room for manoeuvre. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
