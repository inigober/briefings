# News Briefing — 21 November 2026

Spain's campaign turns to Catalan kingmakers and disputed ballots, Germany's energy and welfare bills and youth protests test the coalition, and a US tech sell-off shapes Trump's AI trade politics.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Carles Puigdemont, the Catalan separatist leader in exile in Waterloo, received what La Vanguardia calls a "certificate of good conduct" from People's Party (PP) leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo in Thursday's debate**

The warm words signal that Junts, Puigdemont's party, could matter in coalition arithmetic after the 29 November general election. ([La Vanguardia][1])

* **Spain's Supreme Court has barred people naturalised under the "grandchildren law", which grants citizenship to descendants of Spanish exiles, from voting on 29 November even if they live in Spain**

At the request of the far-right party Vox, the judges told the electoral authorities that the suspension covers all affected people, not only those on the overseas voter roll. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **The nuclear revival and fracking, the controversial gas-extraction method banned in Spain, form the energy platform that the PP and Vox promote in this campaign**

Vox wants new reactors and openly backs fracking, which Feijóo said he was willing to study soon after taking over the PP. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Some 333 local gas suppliers will raise tariffs on 1 January, passing on the high wholesale prices caused by the Iran war**

For families the extra cost can run to several hundred euros a year. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Germany's statutory long-term care insurers, whose spending keeps rising, warn of fresh financial risks at the start of 2027**

The coalition's bill to stabilise the system for the new year is now in parliament, but the insurers question whether it goes far enough. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Tens of thousands of young people took part in nationwide "Gen Z regelt" protests demanding more money for schools and opposing a return of conscription**

Most marches were peaceful, though there was later violence in Hamburg and Leipzig. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin has recorded 38 road deaths so far this year, more than in all of last year, although annual figures fluctuate**

The count underlines the pressure on the city's traffic-safety plans. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Merieme Benali-Jockers is set to become co-leader of Berlin's Greens, a choice the party's centrist "Realo" wing regards as an alarm signal**

The dispute raises the prospect of a hard fight over whether the party moves further left. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Galeria, the insolvent department-store chain, is closing its flagship branch on the Kurfürstendamm, and the store stayed shut on Friday**

Longtime customers describe the loss of a Berlin institution. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **President Donald Trump has threatened a "tremendous" tax on data centres built outside the United States, framing the fight as a way to stop China taking the lead in artificial intelligence**

The threat would turn AI infrastructure location into a tariff question for allies hosting US companies' computing capacity. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Japan is weighing early funding for the International Criminal Court (ICC) before a US sanctions cutoff**

Tokyo, one of the court's largest donors, wants to pay before Washington's penalties on the tribunal take effect. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **The European Union has warned that the draft text under discussion at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, would be "a substantive step backwards" and risks turning it into a "fossil fuel summit"**

EU leaders said the current draft decision would weaken existing climate commitments. ([Politico Europe][12])
