# News Briefing — 01 November 2026

Spain's rail inquiry moves from the Urda crash itself to control-room staffing and political fallout, while Berlin's Left opens coalition talks, a North Korea-linked crypto theft and Iranian threats set the global tone.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Transport minister Óscar Puente will take a high-speed rail safety package to cabinet on Tuesday**

Puente, who offered to resign after the Urda collision that killed 69 people, is backed by Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, although some ministers see his position as untenable and the election campaign has changed course. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Investigators' first report on the Urda crash leaves five questions unanswered**

Among them: why the LZB train-protection system failed, why the AVE driver was told to proceed at normal speed, and why he obeyed when his own system showed the track ahead occupied. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Atocha's high-speed control centre has doubled its train load per controller since rail liberalisation**

Since private operators Iryo and Ouigo entered the market, a lone controller at the centre that directs Spain's AVE network handles 30 to 40 trains per shift rather than 10 to 20, with no increase in staff. ([eldiario.es][3])

💡 Staffing now joins individual decisions in the causal chain, a systemic question that liberalisation made harder to ignore.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany's gas storage is filled below the required targets heading into winter**

The country's gas stores are comparatively low and statutory fill levels have been missed, prompting questions about what that means for winter supply. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Deutsche Bahn extends its weekend last-minute fares and youth BahnCard for another year**

The state-owned rail operator, which since May has sold cheap tickets at weekends for travel in the following week, is prolonging the offer by twelve months. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Millions of Germans eat vegetarian or vegan, and experts say the plate can be a climate lever**

Animal welfare and climate both play a role in the shift, and specialists argue that diet change can deliver real emissions savings. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's Left party votes to open coalition negotiations with the Greens and the SPD**

A majority at a special party congress backed talks on a possible three-party Senate despite sometimes fierce internal criticism. ([rbb24][7])

* **Police end the "GenZ hat genug!" protest in Berlin-Mitte after stones, bottles and fireworks**

After a calmer rally at the Brandenburg Gate, protest activity moved to Mitte, where the mood heated up and the demonstration was ended early. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin's winter outreach buses for homeless people are back on the road**

As temperatures fall, teams in the Kältehilfe buses hand out hot drinks and sleeping bags or drive people to warm shelter. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Binance says it has replaced 90% of the $6.3bn stolen in a record hack the FBI blames on North Korea**

The crypto exchange expects withdrawals to resume on Sunday, while the FBI attributes the theft to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking group. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 Crypto theft is estimated to supply about half of North Korea's foreign-currency income, making raids like this a core funding channel for a sanctioned state.

* **Iran's supreme leader warns of an "immediate" response without "geographical limitations" if attacked**

Ayatollah Khamenei told military commanders this as Qatari mediators try to avert a feared US-Israeli bombing campaign. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Pakistan's refineries report a 1,860% jump in profit amid a war windfall**

Nikkei Asia reports the surge in earnings at the South Asian country's refiners as a windfall linked to the conflict. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
