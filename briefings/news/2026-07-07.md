# News Briefing — 7 July 2026

Spanish courts issue conflicting travel bans on the prime minister's wife, Germany lands a multibillion-euro submarine contract as health insurers rebuke sick-note reforms, and Havana's third blackout shows how energy crises are splitting the Atlantic.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Substitute judge allows Begoña Gómez to travel to London but blocks NATO summit trip**

eldiario.es reports acting magistrate Antonio Viejo, standing in for Juan Carlos Peinado, authorised Pedro Sánchez's wife to collect her passport and fly to her daughter's graduation between 8 and 10 July while barring travel to Turkey for this week's NATO summit. The split ruling deepens a row over judicial treatment of the first lady as Madrid's Audiencia Provincial prepares to review Peinado's wider case within a week. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Extreme heat and human negligence fuel a wave of forest fires across Spain**

eldiario.es describes how record temperatures and careless behaviour have combined to ignite blazes that have already destroyed thousands of hectares — a 19% jump above the decade's seasonal average. Authorities warn the pattern mirrors wider European infrastructure stress as climate volatility meets under-resourced prevention. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 When negligence sparks fires faster than crews can contain them, the policy debate shifts from abstract climate targets to enforceable land-use and emergency capacity.

* **PSOE ministers face questions over campaigning from government desks**

eldiario.es asks whether regional victories are realistic when sitting ministers such as Diana Morant delay leaving their portfolios to focus on Valencia, citing poor precedents for Montero and Alegría. With May 2027 autonomic elections approaching, the party must balance cabinet continuity against on-the-ground organisation. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Health insurers say long absences — not short sick notes — drive costs**

Handelsblatt cites statutory-fund data showing chronic and extended illnesses account for the largest share of sickness-benefit spending, challenging the coalition's push to tighten day-one Krankschreibung rules. Funds argue prevention and rehabilitation deserve priority over policing brief GP visits. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 If the cost curve sits in long-term cases, tightening first-day paperwork targets political symbolism more than the balance sheet insurers must defend.

* **Canada selects TKMS to build a dozen next-generation submarines**

Handelsblatt reports Ottawa chose Thyssenkrupp Marine Systems over a South Korean rival for one of the country's largest defence contracts, deepening transatlantic industrial ties before this week's NATO summit. The order extends a German shipyard pipeline that had been hunting export orders to offset domestic budget uncertainty. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **AI boom fuels record German start-up creation in first half of 2026**

Handelsblatt notes more than 3,000 new ventures were founded in the first six months — an all-time high — as generative-AI tooling lowers entry barriers and laid-off tech workers seek alternatives. Analysts caution that volume does not guarantee durable scale without follow-on capital and revenue. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Postbank closures leave Pankow without a single branch offering postal services**

Tagesspiegel reports two Postbank outlets have shut in Berlin's most populous district, leaving residents without counter service for letters and parcels. The district mayor and a local shopping-centre manager warn basic financial infrastructure is thinning in dense residential areas. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **New rent index unlocks increases — but not every landlord may use it**

rbb24 explains Berlin's May 2026 Mietspiegel sets reference rents that can justify hikes, yet legal caps and documentation rules still block many landlords from passing costs through. Tenant advisers say confusion over who qualifies is already triggering disputes. ([rbb24][8])

* **Police warn of burglars posing as home-care workers to target seniors**

Berliner Zeitung details a rising scam in which criminals in fake Pflegedienst uniforms gain apartment access and steal cash and valuables from elderly residents. Officers urge verification through official care agencies before admitting strangers. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **Cuba suffers third nationwide blackout in six months**

The Guardian reports Monday's grid collapse left the island in darkness again as fuel stocks dwindle under a US oil blockade imposed in January. Residents describe days-long outages compounding medicine and food shortages in an economy already squeezed before the embargo tightened. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Samsung posts nineteenfold profit jump but shares fall on oversupply fears**

Nikkei Asia says record semiconductor earnings failed to reassure investors worried that memory-chip supply is outpacing AI demand, sending Seoul-listed shares down 9%. The reaction highlights how AI hardware booms can still punish suppliers when inventory builds faster than orders. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Japan's ultra-fast maglev line wins key construction approval**

Nikkei Asia reports regulators cleared a critical segment of the Chuo Shinkansen project linking Tokyo and Nagoya, keeping alive a decades-long bet on magnetic-levitation speeds above 500 km/h. The go-ahead arrives as conventional bullet-train operators face ageing networks and rising construction costs. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
