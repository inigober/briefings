# News Briefing — 25 July 2026

Megafire data exposes Spain's structural wildfire risk, Trump's tariff toolkit and a tense EU-US trade anniversary test Brussels, oil above $100 meets scepticism over AI debt, and Berlin opens Pride weekend with a democracy rally at the Brandenburg Gate.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **July's megafire surge ranks among the worst two-week runs in four decades**

EL PAÍS analyses satellite and EFFIS data showing the past fortnight is the fourth-worst stretch for large fires since 1983, even though Spain now records half as many major blazes as 40 years ago — each burns more than twice the average area. The pattern underscores how suppression-heavy policy is losing ground to faster, hotter fires. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 When total fire counts fall but burned hectares rise, prevention funding becomes the binding constraint — not aerial tanker capacity.

* **EU-US trade pact marks one year as Trump threatens digital and green rules**

eldiario.es notes Monday's Turnberry anniversary arrives with bilateral trade up 4.5% to €1.8 trillion despite a 15% US levy on European goods, while Brussels presses Washington to honour tariff caps and grant exemptions. Trump has countered with threats over EU fines on US tech platforms and climate regulation. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Washington hunts obscure statutes to sustain tariff wall after Supreme Court setback**

eldiario.es reports the administration is dusting off decades-old trade laws never used at this scale after the Supreme Court struck down blanket duties, even as Brent crude returns to $100 and Hormuz tensions complicate supply chains. Madrid and other EU capitals face renewed uncertainty on which legal basis will govern the next tariff round. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **DIW economist says European oil supply remains secure despite $100 Brent**

Handelsblatt quotes a German Institute for Economic Research expert arguing storage, diversified sourcing and demand destruction can buffer the Middle East shock even with prices above $100 a barrel. The assessment matters for Berlin as industry lobbies warn energy costs could erase the margin gains from any tariff relief. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **DHL proposes industry-wide two-person delivery for heavy parcels**

Handelsblatt says Europe's largest courier wants carriers to team up on doorstep drops for items such as dumbbells and bulk cat litter, citing rising injury claims among solo drivers. If adopted, the shift would raise last-mile costs but could become a template for how German logistics firms manage e-commerce's weight creep. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Photo essay documents 21st-century destruction of UNESCO heritage sites**

Tagesspiegel surveys war and terror damage from Palmyra to Kharkiv, arguing each strike erases civic identity beyond the immediate casualty toll. German museums and reconstruction funds are increasingly asked to prioritise which ruins can be stabilised before looters and weather finish the job. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Christopher Street Day opens with democracy rally at Brandenburg Gate**

rbb24 reports Pride weekend began with a "Democracy Evening" at the Gate — music, speeches and politics ahead of Saturday's parade — as organisers frame LGBTQ+ rights as part of a broader defence of open society. The programming reflects Berlin's habit of pairing cultural spectacle with explicit civic messaging. ([rbb24][7])

* **RE1 regional express suspended between Berlin and Frankfurt (Oder) for three weeks**

rbb24 says all trains on the Berlin-Ostbahnhof to Frankfurt (Oder) stretch are cancelled from 24 July while tracks are repaired, with replacement buses including an express link to BER. Commuters and cross-border workers face the latest in a summer of piecemeal rail disruptions around the capital. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin court sentences ex-officer to three years for fake police car dealership raid**

rbb24 reports a Landgericht convicted a suspended policeman who posed as law enforcement during an armed robbery at a car dealership. The case feeds ongoing debate over vetting and oversight in a force already under scrutiny for misconduct cases. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **BlackRock sees soft demand for Meta-linked AI data-centre bond sale**

Bloomberg says investors balked at a $12.3 billion debt package backing a Texas data-centre project for Meta, after weeks of concern that AI infrastructure spending is outpacing returns. Weak appetite from the world's largest asset manager signals credit markets may start pricing a capex correction. ([Bloomberg][10])

💡 When BlackRock struggles to place AI-linked paper, the debate shifts from chip shortages to whether hyperscaler balance sheets can fund the build-out without a repricing event.

* **Indigenous leaders say organised crime is holding Amazon villages hostage**

The Guardian reports armed groups from a Brazilian-Peruvian border territory invaded homes in Apiwtxa, intimidating residents and trafficking drugs while destroying forest. A delegation travelled to Brasília after communities said state presence has collapsed along the western Amazon frontier. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Banned Sikh activist film draws underground audiences in Punjab villages**

Nikkei Asia says screenings of a proscribed documentary on Sikh separatism are spreading in rural Punjab despite government bans, testing New Delhi's ability to control nationalist narratives outside major cities. The dispute sits alongside broader crackdowns on dissent ahead of state elections. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
