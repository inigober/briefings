# News Briefing — 9 October 2026

Spain's first post-dissolution poll has not lifted the left, Germany's east is fighting Brussels over farm cash, and OpenAI's run-rate is $20bn short of the figure the market had priced.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The first poll after the snap election does not show a left rebound**

The first survey since Pedro Sánchez dissolved parliament finds the left still needing to mobilise, win back undecideds and stop leaks to the right — and so far the poll only sees the first of those, and only halfway. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The dissolution was sold as a reset; the first print says the race has not moved.

* **Sánchez: parliament should look like the street, not the penthouses**

In his first interview since calling the 29 November election, the Socialist prime minister told eldiario.es that the next Cortes should resemble the street rather than people in *aticazos* — penthouse flats — and that Maricarmen Abascal's death should be answered with policy, not a fight over who owns the grief. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **A judge is investigating three teachers at a Madrid concertado for abusing babies**

A Madrid court is examining three staff at Colegio Alborada, a state-subsidised private school, after colleagues say infants had their noses covered to force them to swallow; two teachers went to the police when an internal complaint produced no response. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Eastern Länder are lobbying the Chancellery against the EU farm reform**

Five east German states are pressing the federal chancellery to fight the planned rewrite of EU farm subsidies — the Common Agricultural Policy that still pays most of what large eastern estates receive — and Brandenburg alone puts the hit at about €80m a year in direct payments. ([Berliner Zeitung][4])

💡 The argument in the east is that Brussels is cutting the farm structure those Länder still run on.

* **Breeding dogs for the look is being filed as cruelty**

Short snouts, rare coat colours and skin folds are still sold as cute; a German animal-welfare feature lays out how those traits are often a medical burden the dog cannot opt out of. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **A dooring case says the person who opened the door is not always solely liable**

When a car door swings into a motorcycle — *Dooring* — a German court has treated fault as shareable, not automatic on the driver who opened it. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Jusos are campaigning for a red–green–red city government**

Berlin's SPD youth, the Jusos, say they share the most with the Greens and Die Linke and want a left coalition — Rot-Grün-Rot — as the party's state delegates meet, a youth push after a week of adult talks about expulsion rules and a speaker. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The Spree exit at Jannowitzbrücke is shut over load-bearing doubts**

The riverside way out of Jannowitzbrücke station in Mitte has been closed because engineers are not sure the structure can still take the load. ([rbb24][8])

* **The western Ringbahn is closed this weekend**

Berlin's circular S-Bahn will lose its western Ringbahn arc over the weekend for engineering work. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **OpenAI's annualised revenue is $20bn short of the number the market had used**

The AI lab has told investors the figure that matters — revenue run-rate — was nearing $50bn in September, about $20bn below the $70bn that had been widely reported. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 A lot of AI equity has been marked to a $70bn story the company is not telling.

* **AI is slowing hiring in India's offshore tech hubs**

Multinationals that parked software and support work in Indian captive centres — the offshore hubs that sold India as the back office — are slowing recruitment as they put more of that work through AI. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

💡 The labour shock is landing in the offshore model, not only in California headcount.

* **Ethiopia has drone-struck Eritrean troops who entered Tigray**

Eritrean soldiers crossed into Ethiopia's Tigray region as far as Adigrat; federal drones then hit them, a step that turns a renewed civil war into a risk of a state-to-state fight between neighbours. ([The Guardian][12])
