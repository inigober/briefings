# News Briefing — 2 July 2026

Andalucía moves toward Spain's first post-election PP–Vox regional government, German regulators force open long-distance rail while Mercedes struggles to deliver its breakout electric SUV, and Berlin's Senate votes on the country's first rent registry as polling turns against the CDU — against a backdrop of rights crackdowns in Niger, memory-crunch iPhone strategy, and H-1B uncertainty reshaping US tech immigration.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Vox negotiates entry into Moreno's Andalucía government ahead of second investiture vote**

eldiario.es reports that PP premier Juan Manuel Moreno is finalising a coalition pact with Vox in Spain's most populous region after the far-right party blocked his first investiture and set a 48-hour deadline for concessions. Negotiators are haggling over portfolio scope and the meaning of PP's abandoned "national priority" clause — a template that could prefigure national arrangements after the next general election. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Andalucía ceases to be the exception: a PP–Vox executive in the south would normalise power-sharing that Feijóo once ruled out nationally.

🧩 If portfolios grant Vox control over education or culture, Madrid's minority government may face copycat demands from its own right flank before autumn budget votes.

* **Spanish utilities offload renewable capacity worth twelve nuclear plants amid price squeeze**

eldiario.es calculates that Iberdrola, Endesa, Naturgy and Repsol have sold more than 12 gigawatts of wind, solar and hydro assets in five years — roughly a dozen reactor equivalents — raising over €15 billion as wholesale power prices collapse. Sellers frame the deals as de-risking and debt reduction; critics see a strategic retreat from the energy transition just as Spain pitches green-industry investment. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 Asset recycling turns renewables from growth engines into balance-sheet releases — a structural shift when merchant power revenues no longer justify holding full portfolios.

🧩 If generators keep shedding operating plants while retaining development pipelines, regulators may scrutinise whether consumers still benefit from earlier subsidy regimes.

* **Police probe alleged abuse at award-winning Catalonia addiction clinic**

eldiario.es reports that investigators are examining Zeus, a private detox centre in Catalonia that won industry prizes and advertises widely, after former patients described coercive punishments, psychological manipulation and blocked outside contact. Complaints echo allegations at a Basque facility, raising questions about oversight of Spain's booming private addiction-treatment market. ([eldiario.es][3])

💡 A high-profile clinic under criminal investigation exposes regulatory gaps in a sector that expanded faster than inspection capacity after pandemic-era demand surges.

🧩 Regional health departments may face pressure to publish accreditation standards and incident reporting for centres that market directly to families in crisis.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Mercedes' electric GLC wins demand but buyers face long waits**

Handelsblatt reports that Daimler has designed a battery GLC customers actually want — yet supply bottlenecks mean showrooms cannot fulfil orders. The contrast with earlier German EV launches that missed market taste underscores how production constraints, not demand scepticism, now gate the country's electrification narrative. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 A "good product, no units" story reframes Germany's EV debate from consumer rejection to industrial delivery — with implications for 2035 fleet targets.

🧩 If Mercedes prioritises export markets or high-margin trims, domestic waitlists could become a political talking point alongside charging-infrastructure gaps.

* **Rail regulator orders Deutsche Bahn to open long-distance tracks to rivals**

Tagesspiegel reports that the Bundesnetzagentur is requiring DB to grant competitors greater access to Fernverkehr corridors, intensifying pressure on the state-owned operator after years of punctuality failures and capacity disputes. The ruling tests whether market opening can improve service without fragmenting timetabling on congested arteries. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

💡 Structural transport reform is moving from rhetoric to network governance — with independent regulators willing to override incumbent privileges.

🧩 If private operators enter key Berlin–Hamburg or Rhine corridors, fare integration and delay liability will become the next coalition battleground.

* **Stuttgart court questions whether social networks are liable for third-party tracking**

Handelsblatt covers an Oberlandesgericht Stuttgart ruling that social-media platforms may bear responsibility when their tools enable cross-site tracking — while signalling the question may need Bundesverfassungsgericht clarification. The case sits at the intersection of GDPR enforcement and platform business models built on data brokerage. ([Handelsblatt][6])

💡 A regional court is pushing liability upstream from publishers to platforms — a material precedent as EU digital rules harden.

🧩 If Karlsruhe takes the case, Germany could set a federal standard that ripples through ad-tech contracts across the single market.

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Senate votes on Germany's first citywide rent registry**

Tagesspiegel reports that Berlin's governing coalition is set to adopt the Mietenkataster on Thursday — a nationwide first requiring landlords to register contract rents so authorities can identify Wuchermiete gouging. Campaigners call it a breakthrough for affordability enforcement; property lobbyists warn of bureaucracy and litigation risk. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

💡 Material shift from announcing the registry to a binding vote: Berlin is converting rent-cap politics into an institutional data tool.

🧩 Implementation hinges on compliance rates and court appetite to act on flagged outliers — tests the Senate will face as autumn lease renewals arrive.

* **BerlinTrend poll shows Linke leading and CDU support sliding**

Tagesspiegel's BerlinTrend survey puts Die Linke ahead as the strongest force while Kai Wegner's CDU loses ground — feeding speculation about whether the governing mayor can recover before scheduled 2026 elections. Analysts link the slide to blackout communications, coalition fatigue and competition from both left and AfD. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

💡 Fresh polling data turns Wegner's summer from administrative drift into an electoral early-warning system for the Senat.

🧩 If CDU continues to bleed centrist voters, Senate partners may recalculate whether housing and climate concessions are worth another year in a weakening alliance.

* **Constitutional protection data show political violence rising in Berlin**

Berliner Zeitung reports that Verfassungsschutz statistics document higher politically motivated violence in the capital, part of a nationwide uptick with distinct left- and right-wing patterns across eastern states. Berlin's figures stand out in absolute terms given demonstration density and symbolic federal targets. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

💡 Rising violence statistics force local security policy beyond headline crime rates — especially as summer protest calendars fill.

🧩 Senate interior officials may face demands for targeted prevention programmes without echoing federal debates on protest restrictions.

## World 🌐

* **Niger's military junta arrests dozens in anti-LGBTQ+ crackdown**

The Guardian reports that Niger's new penal code has triggered a "witch-hunt" with up to 40 arrests and 16 imprisonments, including military officials, amid fears that disrupted healthcare access will revive HIV transmission. Rights groups say the campaign marks a sharp reversal in one of West Africa's most fragile states. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 Criminalisation moves from statute books to mass detention — a material escalation with public-health externalities beyond the targeted community.

🧩 ECOWAS and EU partners must weigh human-rights conditionality against counter-terror cooperation with Niamey's junta.

* **Apple plans five new iPhone models to defend share amid memory shortage**

Nikkei Asia reports that Apple will broaden its 2026 handset lineup to five models, segmenting price points to capture demand while global memory supply tightens and raises component costs. The strategy signals that even premium vendors must trade margin for volume in a component-constrained cycle. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

💡 Hardware segmentation becomes a supply-chain coping mechanism — not just a marketing tier — when HBM and DRAM shortages bite.

🧩 If Apple absorbs memory inflation through model proliferation, Android rivals with thinner supply contracts may face sharper margin hits.

* **US tech immigrants weigh exit as H-1B rules keep shifting**

Rest of World reports that although courts struck down Donald Trump's $100,000 H-1B fee, years of policy whiplash have many foreign engineers considering Canada, the UK or Gulf hubs — an "uncertainty tax" on Silicon Valley talent pipelines. Employers say visa unpredictability now rivals compensation in retention calculations. ([Rest of World][12])

💡 A court win does not restore confidence: structural US immigration volatility is reallocating human capital without a single legislative settlement.

🧩 If mid-tier firms cannot plan multi-year hiring, startup ecosystems outside the US may capture teams that would once have defaulted to California.
