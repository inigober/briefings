# News Briefing — 8 August 2026

Spain reimposes Schengen checks on Italy, Berlin's Left faces an expropriation trap before the state election, and Washington backs Colombia's new hard-right president as German rivers force freight onto roads and Chinese families turn to AI tutors for the summer.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain reinstates border controls on travellers from Italy**

Pedro Sánchez's government will screen arrivals from Italy at ports and airports from midnight Saturday until 7 September, retaliating after Giorgia Meloni refused to lift Rome's own checks on Spanish travellers in the latest round of a migration feud inside the Schengen zone. The move escalates a bilateral spat that began with Ceuta but now tests whether EU free-movement rules can survive when Mediterranean governments weaponise internal borders against each other. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Moroccan social-media networks keep recruiting migrants underground**

A sweep of Moroccan Facebook groups shows organisers of attempted Ceuta crossings have migrated to less visible channels after platforms shut larger groups that once gathered 250,000 users sharing maps, routes and wetsuit sellers. EL PAÍS reports the shift suggests Rabat and Madrid are winning a visibility battle without dismantling the recruitment infrastructure that fuelled last week's mass entry. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Compromís files Supreme Court complaint against Vox MP who called to "hunt" Ceuta migrants**

Compromís-Sumar deputy Alberto Ibáñez has asked Spain's Supreme Court prosecutor to investigate Vox parliamentarian José María Figaredo for telling television viewers authorities should "hunt immigrants one by one" after the Ceuta surge. The complaint turns a far-right soundbite into a test of whether dehumanising rhetoric against asylum seekers can trigger criminal liability for elected officials. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Environmentalists attack relaxation of truck bans on drought-hit rivers**

Germany's BUND federation says the federal transport ministry misunderstood low Rhine and Elbe water levels as a temporary logistics problem rather than a climate signal when it eased lorry restrictions meant to keep freight off fragile embankments. The dispute matters because inland shipping bottlenecks are already pushing cargo onto roads, and activists argue short-term truck waivers undermine long-term adaptation planning. ([Frankfurter Allgemeine][4])

* **Industry and policymakers debate whether AI systems need a tighter leash**

Handelsblatt examines calls to restrict autonomous hacking capabilities that US tech firms have admitted their large language models can perform, weighing stricter liability rules against fears that over-regulation could cede AI safety leadership to China. The debate lands as Berlin drafts its implementation of the EU AI Act and manufacturers plead for carve-outs on security research. ([Handelsblatt][5])

💡 If Washington and Brussels diverge on how much autonomy to grant frontier models, German firms building industrial AI could face compliance regimes stricter than those binding their US competitors.

* **WHO flags more than 240 West Nile fever cases across Europe this season**

European health authorities have logged more than 240 West Nile virus infections since the mosquito season began, with Italy reporting the highest burden and Germany monitoring imported cases as warmer summers extend the insects' range northward. The outbreak turns a Mediterranean nuisance disease into a continental surveillance test for public-health systems still calibrated to tropical latitudes. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's Left faces expropriation dilemma on path to city hall**

Polls showing Die Linke leading September's Abgeordnetenhaus race have collided with the party's pledge to force large landlords to sell housing stock, a demand conservative and liberal rivals say makes coalition talks impossible if the Left insists on expropriation votes before forming government. The stand-off previews how a fragmented result could paralyse Berlin's next senate even before ballots are cast. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Sister testifies as Fabian murder trial questions reconstructed timeline**

In the eleventh week of Berlin's high-profile Fabian murder trial, the defendant's sister told the court she no longer trusts the prosecution's account of how the student died, while defence lawyers challenged forensic reconstructions of the night in question. The hearing underscores how Germany's most watched capital criminal case may hinge on family testimony rather than new physical evidence. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **Union Berlin president questions German hypocrisy on private football investors**

Union president Dirk Zingler criticised domestic outrage over FIFA president Gianni Infantino's plan to sell commercial rights to private investors while German clubs quietly court sovereign-wealth and buyout money, arguing the debate ignores Bundesliga teams' own dependence on outside capital. The intervention matters because Berlin's fan-owned club is often cast as the moral counterweight to leveraged takeovers elsewhere in European football. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **US pledges $1 billion security package as Colombia's hard-right president takes office**

Donald Trump's administration announced $1 billion in security assistance for Abelardo De La Espriella hours after the Trump-aligned lawyer was sworn in as Colombia's president, pledging austerity and a militarised crackdown on drug trafficking and armed groups. The package signals Washington is betting on a rightward pivot in Bogotá after years of tense relations with Gustavo Petro's leftist government. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Chinese families hire AI tutors to drill children through the summer break**

Parents across China are subscribing to artificial-intelligence tutoring apps to keep children studying during school holidays, extending Beijing's push to embed AI in education from classrooms into household routines. The trend shows how China's consumer-tech giants are monetising exam pressure even as regulators tighten oversight of algorithms that shape minors' learning. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Moonshot AI reshuffles leadership to secure Beijing approval for Hong Kong IPO**

Chinese start-up Moonshot AI is reorganising its management team as it prepares a Hong Kong stock-market listing to fund its next development phase, according to the Financial Times, in a move seen as smoothing regulatory review by mainland authorities. The listing race matters because several domestic AI champions are testing whether investors will finance model builders before they turn a profit. ([Financial Times][12])
