# News Briefing — 5 October 2026

Spain is readying a snap election, Brazil is headed to a runoff, and a German minister is already in court over what she said after Berlin voted.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The snap election is ready — and so is a second try at the housing decrees**

After Friday's defeat in Congress, Pedro Sánchez's Socialists and government have the early-election call prepared and are drafting ways to pass the housing decrees again and have them validated by the Permanent Deputation, the rump of the chamber that sits once parliament is dissolved. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 Dissolution is being treated as a route to pass the texts the floor just killed, not only as an exit.

* **A judge has used Almeida's Maricarmen rescue to block other city evictions**

A Madrid judge has treated city hall's rapid help for 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal — evicted after 70 years in her home — as a "notorious fact" and ordered the municipal housing firm EMVS to guarantee alternatives before it evicts other vulnerable families. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The mayor's showpiece case is now a standard the same administration has to meet.

* **Vox is the only gainer in a poll that still has the right at 50%**

A 40dB barometer for EL PAÍS, taken before Friday's housing-vote collapse, puts the PP at 31.6% (down 0.8 points) and the PSOE still second; only Vox is rising, and the right-wing bloc remains at half the electorate. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Die Linke has taken Reiche to the Constitutional Court**

After Berlin's election, economics minister Katherina Reiche said antisemitism and expropriation were a danger to Germany; Die Linke, the left party that won the capital, has asked the Federal Constitutional Court to rule on those remarks. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 A post-election soundbite is being turned into a constitutional case.

* **Gen Z's labour-market swagger is running into a thinner jobs list**

Younger workers could lately demand four-day weeks and home office; the entry-level market has shrunk so sharply that, as Tagesspiegel puts it, humility is returning — "but not for everyone." ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Handelsblatt has already written off Merz at home — and left him Ukraine**

The paper's morning note says Friedrich Merz's chancellorship is likely to end as a domestic misunderstanding, and that Ukraine is the one mission on which the CDU chancellor can still earn the country real credit. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **AI is moving into Berlin's clinics — the question is who is watching it**

Hospitals and practices in the city are folding AI into diagnosis and admin; The Berliner asks whether the systems are in good hands, not whether they are arriving. ([The Berliner][7])

* **A person fell 15 metres down a lift shaft at Potsdamer Platz**

Someone fell about 15 metres into an elevator shaft on Sunday evening at Potsdamer Platz, was critically injured, and was flown to hospital by rescue helicopter. ([rbb24][8])

* **The Eisbären ground out a win against Nürnberg**

Berlin's DEL ice-hockey side beat Nürnberg after a slow start, a result that steadies the week more than it dazzles. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Flávio Bolsonaro leads Lula after the first round**

Flávio Bolsonaro, the senator son of jailed former president Jair Bolsonaro, finished ahead of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and is the unexpected favourite for the 25 October runoff. ([Financial Times][10])

* **A booming US economy is not helping Trump**

Anxiety about AI and the cost of living has turned President Trump's populist party into a political target even as the official numbers look strong. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 The midterm problem is prices and job fear, not the growth rate.

* **Egypt charged a journalist with terrorism after detaining a whole newsroom**

Authorities arrested six staff from Matsadaash, an independent fact-checking site whose name means "don't believe," and have charged one with joining a terrorist group; the other five are still unaccounted for. ([The Guardian][12])
