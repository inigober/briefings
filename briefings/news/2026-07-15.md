# News Briefing — 15 July 2026

A court ruling on Pedro Sánchez's brother and a World Cup final berth collide with Spanish diplomacy, Germany's auto-industry reckoning and Merz's summer reset, Berlin's post-Wegner transition, and Washington's bid to politicise US science grants as Tokyo chases air-gapped clouds.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Badajoz court convicts David Sánchez for prevaricación but absolves him of tráfico de influencias**

eldiario.es says three provincial judges sentenced the prime minister's brother to nine years' disqualification from public office for improperly renaming a square, yet found no solid proof he traded on family influence to win a regional job. The split verdict leaves eleven officials ten working days to appeal before the Extremadura high court. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When prosecutors fail to prove the influence-peddling narrative but still secure a prevaricación conviction, Spain's judiciary delivers political damage without the criminal theory that fuelled two years of headlines.

* **La Roja dismantles France 2–0 to reach its second World Cup final**

eldiario.es credits a collective display led by Rodri and Dani Olmo for overpowering a star-studded French side on Bastille Day eve, with Mikel Oyarzabal and Dani Carvajal sealing a rematch 16 years after Spain's first final. The win lands amid a diplomatic row over Mariano Rajoy's column questioning the diversity of Les Bleus. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Nearly half of US World Cup viewers watch in Spanish as English-only fans switch to Telemundo**

eldiario.es cites Nielsen data showing Fox and Telemundo splitting first-round audiences almost evenly, with one in five Telemundo viewers speaking only English and drawn to Spanish commentary's energy. The shift suggests Hispanic broadcasters are capturing mainstream sports audiences beyond Latino households. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Merz faces summer press conference with reforms done but polls still bleak**

Handelsblatt previews the chancellor's Berlin appearance after a bumpy first year, noting he feels buoyed by the coalition's agreed growth package even as surveys show voters unconvinced. The set-piece will test whether Schwarz-Rot can sell structural bills as a turnaround narrative before the recess. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **ZF crisis forces debate on whether auto workers must surrender decades-old perks**

Handelsblatt's morning briefing asks when suppliers in structural decline can demand wage sacrifices from staff still enjoying allowances and bonus schemes inherited from boom years. The supplier's restructuring has become a proxy fight over how Germany shares industrial pain. ([Handelsblatt][5])

💡 When a components giant negotiates over "moon salaries" rather than plant closures alone, Germany's auto transition becomes a distributional conflict inside the factory gate, not just a China-competition story.

* **North Sea rocket-launch ship hunts berths as offshore spaceports advance**

Handelsblatt reports engineers are scouting where a floating Startrampe can moor to fire small satellites from the sea, with sites beyond the German coast now under review. The project tests whether Europe can replicate mobile launch economics without Cape Canaveral-scale infrastructure. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin police to open new forensic institute in Johannisthal**

rbb24 says the capital is building a Kriminaltechnisches Institut to centralise DNA, trace and digital evidence work that is now scattered across ageing facilities. The investment aims to shorten case backlogs as prosecutors lean harder on scientific proof in major investigations. ([rbb24][7])

* **Berliner Zeitung chronicles Kai Wegner's political unravelling after the blackout crisis**

The paper retraces how Berlin's mayor lost control of the narrative after January's citywide power failure, with months of fumbled crisis messaging culminating in the CDU naming Stefan Evers as his successor. The timeline portrays a leader who could not convert emergency management into electoral credibility. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **DDR Museum marks 20 years beside the Spree with Trabi simulators and a Plattenbau flat**

Tagesspiegel notes the Mitte attraction celebrating two decades of interactive GDR history, from a driving simulator to a faithfully furnished socialist apartment. Curators pitch it as both tourist draw and living archive as Ostalgie competes with newer memory sites. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump plan would let political appointees vet science grants for presidential priorities**

Financial Times reports proposed reforms requiring agency leaders to screen research funding to ensure projects "advance the President's priorities." Critics say the shift would politicise peer review and accelerate brain drain from US laboratories already facing budget cuts. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 When grant panels answer to political commissars, the world's largest public-research funder risks exporting talent to rivals just as Beijing and Brussels ramp industrial-science spending.

* **Oracle proposes air-gapped cloud for Japan's most classified data**

Financial Times says Larry Ellison's group leads bidding to host top-secret workloads for Tokyo as Washington presses allies to harden sensitive networks against Chinese intrusion. An isolated architecture would let Japan keep intelligence and defence material off shared hyperscaler infrastructure. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Delhi pushes electric vehicles to cut severe urban air pollution**

Nikkei Asia reports India's capital is expanding EV incentives and charging infrastructure as particulate levels routinely breach safe limits. Officials hope electrifying buses and two-wheelers can bend the pollution curve faster than coal-plant retirements alone. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
