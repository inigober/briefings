# News Briefing — 23 October 2026

A global tech sell-off, strain at Mercedes and rising power bills, and Spain's courts and a new virus set the frame for a day in which institutions are tested by money and risk.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A Madrid judge refuses an eviction, citing the "collapse of the housing market"**

A judge blocked the eviction of a family with five children from a Community of Madrid flat, arguing that "social reality" and a severe housing crisis outweigh leaving a public home empty and boarded up. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Courts weighing the housing market itself, not just the contract, could open a route for other judges facing similar cases.

* **An animal virus has jumped to a human for the first time, killing a man in France**

The shamonda virus, until now known to infect only animals, has infected a man in France who died, surprising experts, and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control is monitoring the situation. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Spain wins a landmark ruling in its decades-long fight over cut energy subsidies**

Madrid, which is resisting investors' attempts to freeze about €2bn of Spanish state assets to enforce awards over subsidies it withdrew, has won a landmark victory in the long-running dispute. ([Politico Europe][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Mercedes is losing sales and margin, and its workforce is growing restless**

Handelsblatt's Morning Briefing asks whether the carmaker can stay a global company or shrinks into a regional brand, a question that now hinges on a rescue plan. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Electricity prices rose 10% in a month, but switching supplier saves €341 on average**

A Berliner Zeitung analysis of more than 6,300 places shows where households pay most, which federal states are hit hardest and how much a change of provider saves. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

* **OnlyFans turns ten, and creators and campaigners still disagree about what it offers women**

Tagesspiegel, citing dpa, sets an erotic creator's account of freedom against a women's-rights campaigner's warning about dependence on the subscription platform, alongside what the company and the federal criminal police (BKA) say. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Hundreds of thousands of speeders are caught each year, but the worst are hard to stop**

Berlin police catch huge numbers of speeders annually, yet some drive so fast and recklessly that they would face driving bans, and unmarked patrols still find it difficult to catch them. ([rbb24][7])

* **The RE5 regional line north out of Berlin is interrupted again**

Rail replacement buses are running on the RE5 in Oberhavel after a further interruption to the line, which links Berlin with the north. ([rbb24][8])

* **Tagesspiegel's Checkpoint asks whether Stefan Evers has issued an invitation**

As Linke, Greens and SPD look ahead to formal coalition talks, the morning newsletter asks whether the CDU finance senator has actually invited anyone, and whether to hold exploratory talks at all. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Japan's yen rallies as a global tech sell-off sends investors to safe havens**

The yen, a currency investors buy in turmoil, gained against the dollar as Asian stock markets followed Wall Street lower. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Japan's probe to the moons of Mars has launched**

The mission, from Japan's space agency, will collect samples from Mars's moons and search for signs of ancient life. ([The Economist][11])

* **AI-driven disinformation may be less likely to disrupt the US midterms than feared**

Foreign Policy assesses the risk to America's midterm elections, looking at the steps taken by AI firms such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft, and finds the answers more encouraging than expected. ([Foreign Policy][12])
