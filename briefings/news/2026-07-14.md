# News Briefing — 14 July 2026

Congress votes on Spain's largest care overhaul in years, Germany records a widening China trade gap and rising conscientious-objector filings, Berlin's CDU names its mayoral challenger as justice IT fails, and Tokyo confronts a spy scandal while Mexico pursues Washington over migrant deaths.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Congress votes €6.2bn dependency overhaul shifting care from institutions to ageing at home**

eldiario.es says lawmakers will approve twin reforms Tuesday granting universal access as a subjective right, letting neighbours and friends qualify as carers and raising central-government cost-sharing despite PP opposition to the 50% funding formula it long demanded. Officials frame the package as a paradigm shift away from residential warehousing for elderly and disabled Spaniards. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When Madrid commits billions to universal home care while the opposition blocks the funding share it once championed, Spain's welfare state becomes a parliamentary stress test, not just a social-policy upgrade.

* **Government braces for budget-path defeat but vows to govern through 2027**

eldiario.es reports Junts, PP and Vox will almost certainly reject Tuesday's stability-path vote, yet Moncloa insists it will exhaust the legislature by advancing dependency reform and other bills before summer recess. Sánchez's team treats the setback as predictable arithmetic rather than a trigger for early elections. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **One in ten practising doctors in Spain is foreign-born, mostly from Latin America**

EL PAÍS finds more than 30,000 colegiados hail from abroad, many working without homologated specialties as regions lean on immigrant clinicians to sustain public health. The ratio underscores how migration is quietly staffing hospitals even as political debate hardens over regularisation and border control. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Chinese exports to Germany jump sharply in June as German shipments rise only marginally**

Handelsblatt says the growth gap in bilateral trade widened again last month, with Chinese goods flooding in faster than Bundesrepublik sales to the PRC. The imbalance adds pressure on Berlin's industrial-policy toolkit as manufacturers already compete with subsidised Chinese rivals. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Conscientious-objector applications surge after new military-service law takes effect**

Tagesspiegel reports filings for Kriegsdienstverweigerung are running well above last year's pace since the revamped Wehrdienst framework launched. The trend suggests compulsory-service debates are mobilising pacifist refuseniks faster than recruiters. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Unified Pflegefachassistenz training from 2027 opens a formal Quereinstieg path into nursing**

Handelsblatt explains a nationwide assistant-nursing qualification from 2027 will standardise lateral entry into a sector desperate for staff. Employers hope the route can channel mid-career switchers without waiting for full Pflegefachkraft apprenticeships. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Finance senator Stefan Evers elected CDU lead candidate for September's state election**

rbb24 says the party executive chose Evers Monday to head its Abgeordnetenhaus campaign, installing a fiscal hawk to succeed Mayor Kai Wegner on the ticket. The decision locks in the conservatives' austerity-leaning message months before voters decide whether to extend CDU rule. ([rbb24][7])

* **BVG breaks ground on Waisentunnel rebuild after eight-year closure**

rbb24 reports transit officials celebrated a Spatenstich for replacing the century-old tube near Jannowitzbrücke, aiming to end the practice of trucking U-Bahn carriages across town for repairs. Four lines should gain maintenance resilience once the new bore opens. ([rbb24][8])

* **Severe IT outage leaves Berlin judges without access to digital case files**

rbb24 says courts have lacked electronic dossiers since Monday morning as a long-running justice digitisation drive hits operational failure. The paralysis exposes how Berlin's paperless ambitions can stall routine hearings when infrastructure buckles. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Tokyo vows tougher counter-espionage after report that Russia turned Japan into a spy hub**

The Guardian says officials pledged greater rigour following a New York Times investigation alleging weak laws let Moscow use Japan to gather intelligence and procure dual-use components for its Ukraine war. The admission marks a rare public reckoning for a country that prizes low-profile security policy. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Mexico to file US criminal complaints over migrant deaths in ICE operations**

The Guardian reports President Claudia Sheinbaum will pursue legal action over more than a dozen Mexican nationals killed in detention or enforcement raids, including Lorenzo Salgado Araujo's shooting in Houston last week. The move escalates sovereignty friction as Washington intensifies deportation operations. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Ad tech and roaming data used to track US military personnel during Iran conflict**

Financial Times says Tehran-linked actors exploited commercial phone-location systems to pinpoint American forces as fighting spread across the Gulf. The campaign shows how consumer surveillance infrastructure has become a battlefield targeting tool. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 When ad-tech pipelines built for marketing can geolocate troops, export controls on chips look quaint next to the data brokers already inside every pocket.
