# News Briefing — 31 August 2026

Spain's heat is now a GDP line, Germany's sick-pay tab is an ignored industrial file, Berlin is fighting over who gets on the air, and the first US-Iran shots in a month have reopened Hormuz.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A Triodos study says this summer will cost Spain more than €20 billion**

Triodos Bank, the Dutch ethical lender, estimates extreme heat will take about 1% of Spanish output this year, with tourism already wobbling in the hottest destinations and farming, construction and transport next in line. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The climate file is no longer a mortality count; it is a one-point hit on GDP in a tourism-and-outdoor economy.

* **Public universities are rationing the degrees that feed the private boom**

A report finds about 17,000 students a year miss a public place on high-demand courses — including medicine, where a 12.8 school mark may not be enough — and blames rectorates and internal interests, not only a lack of money. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Spain's football federation is close to paying for players to freeze their eggs**

The RFEF, which runs the national teams, is finishing a deal so women in the squad can bank fertility while peak performance still overlaps with peak reproductive age. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Bärbel Bas is ignoring an €85 billion sick-leave bill**

Sickness costs at German firms have hit a record, and labour minister Bärbel Bas — the SPD politician who holds the social-affairs brief — is treating the new figures as something not to discuss. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 Once sick pay is an €85 billion location cost, refusing the number is an industrial-policy choice, not a briefing oversight.

* **VW's last works meetings are asking whether plants will close**

Thousands of Volkswagen staff are using the final Betriebsversammlung round — the company's shop-floor assemblies — to ask if their jobs and factories survive, with the supervisory board due to speak this week. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Klopp watched Augsburg's Kade — and has not called**

Jürgen Klopp, in his first stretch as Germany's national coach, saw Augsburg's U21 midfielder Anton Kade play a strong home game; there has been no contact, and Klopp's first home match in the job is also in Augsburg. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **BSW is suing rbb to get into the Berlin election debate**

Sahra Wagenknecht's BSW, the left-conservative party she founded after leaving Die Linke, has gone to court after rbb, the city-state's public broadcaster, kept it off the Wahlarena — the televised candidates' arena — weeks before the 20 September vote. ([Berliner Zeitung][7])

* **A xenophobic attack in Berlin has a Grey Wolves lead**

Investigators say an assault on an Indian man in the city may be linked to the Grey Wolves, the Turkish ultra-nationalist movement, turning a street crime into a far-right network question. ([rbb24][8])

* **Night trains on the RE1 will be cancelled from Monday**

ODEG will not run overnight services on the RE1, the regional line between Berlin and Frankfurt (Oder), a commuter cut that lands on the city's eastern rail spine. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **The US and Iran have exchanged fire for the first time in a month**

American forces struck an island in the Strait of Hormuz, the oil chokepoint between the Gulf and the Arabian Sea; Tehran answered with an attack on Jordan, and crude rose on the exchange. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Wall Street wants robots to save US factories; unions see the wage cut**

Investors and Silicon Valley are betting that physical AI — machines that work on a shop floor, not a chat window — can rebuild American manufacturing; unions and economists say the first effect is on jobs and pay. ([Financial Times][11])

* **An Nvidia supplier is being probed for relabelling China-made parts**

Unimicron, the Taiwanese firm that makes circuit boards and chip substrates for the AI-chip champion, is under investigation for allegedly marking China-origin components as something else. ([Nikkei Asia][12])

💡 If the probe sticks, export-control theatre around Nvidia's stack has a paperwork leak, not just a tariff.
