# News Briefing — 16 November 2026

Spain's campaign shifts to who can be trusted to audit the budget and count the ballots, Germany and Berlin turn to household savings and the city's grey-market taxis, and a Starship catch and a Korean chip race show where AI's money is going.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The conservative Partido Popular (PP) questions the independent fiscal watchdog AIReF and the Bank of Spain, promising instead an "independent audit" of the state's accounts if it wins on 29 November**

Candidate Alberto Núñez Feijóo says he does not trust the official numbers, which lets him avoid saying whether defence spending would rise to 5% of GDP or whether his promised tax cuts are affordable. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Discrediting the institutions that cost party manifestos leaves voters with no neutral yardstick for the PP's spending and tax promises.

* **1,261 migrants reached the Canary island of El Hierro in seven boats on Sunday, and the minister Ángel Víctor Torres, visiting the island, announced further reinforcements**

El Hierro is the landing point of the Atlantic route from West Africa, which the headline calls the deadliest migration route, and the central government has now declared a migration emergency in the Canaries. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Polls suggest turnout in the 29 November general election could top 75%, a level the Socialists (PSOE) treat as key to their chances**

EL PAÍS looks at what a high turnout would mean for each bloc, with the right-wing parties currently favoured in most polls. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Plural, a London-based venture fund invested in defence start-up Helsing and fusion developer Proxima Fusion, is doubling down on Europe-only investments even as another well-known investor pulls out of the continent**

Handelsblatt asks why the fund is betting that European defence and deep-tech companies can grow without American money. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Only about half of people in Germany can put money aside, according to a dpa survey, and many of those who do choose savings products that pay little or no interest**

High living costs are the main constraint, and the survey points to a clear motive behind the cautious choice of savings vehicles. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Employees in Germany who quit soon after receiving their Christmas bonus may have to pay it back, depending on the wording of their contract**

Handelsblatt's weekly labour-law Q&A sets out which contract details decide whether a Weihnachtsgeld repayment clause holds. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Prosecutors are investigating operators of Uber and Bolt-type ride-hailing services in Berlin and officials in Brandenburg over suspected bribery**

Ever more cheap ride-hailing cars in Berlin carry Brandenburg licence plates, where their number has recently soared, and investigators suspect bribes may have helped. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The former Moabit freight yard, from which more than 32,000 Jewish Berliners were deported, is a Holocaust memorial whose surviving remains have been decaying for years**

Who should secure the remains permanently, and how, has been argued over for years without a resolution. ([rbb24][8])

* **Police arrested 20 people and filed 28 criminal complaints after Saturday's "Gen Z regelt" protest march along the Ku'damm in Charlottenburg turned violent**

The youth initiative's demonstration targeted dilapidated schools, conscription and cuts to universities and social spending. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **SK Hynix, the South Korean memory-chip maker whose products are central to AI hardware, is working around the clock to finish a colossal new chip plant**

Nikkei Asia describes a 24/7 construction race as the company tries to bring the factory online. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **SpaceX caught the second stage of its Starship rocket, the upper section that carries payloads into orbit, a first for the company**

Catching the stage rather than letting it fall into the sea is a step towards making the whole vehicle reusable, which SpaceX argues is the key to cheap spaceflight. ([The Economist][11])

* **Six months into the Democratic Republic of Congo's largest Ebola outbreak, containing the virus increasingly depends on tackling hunger, armed conflict, mistrust and a health system buckling under the strain**

Bloomberg calls it a record crisis that is defying control. ([Bloomberg][12])
