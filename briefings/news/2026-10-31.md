# News Briefing — 31 October 2026

Spain's rail inquiry widens from one crash to the reliability of the safety system and the politics around it, while AI-valuation jitters, an Ebola outbreak that keeps growing and Berlin's protest day frame the rest of the edition.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's train-protection system has been down almost every other day this year**

Transport minister Óscar Puente says the LZB, the German-designed system that stops two trains occupying the same stretch of track, was out of service at least 170 times in 2026 before the Urda collision that killed 69 people. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The question is shifting from what one control room did wrong to why a known-fragile safety layer stayed in service.

* **The Urda crash strains the PSOE's alliance with Ada Colau's Frente Amplio a month before the election**

The Frente Amplio, the left alliance running jointly with Sánchez's Socialists in most districts at the 29 November general election, attacks the PSOE's liberalised "neoliberal" rail model, while Podemos stops short of demanding Puente's resignation after Sánchez refused his offer to step down. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Forensic teams carried out 69 autopsies in three days to identify the Urda victims**

Behind the families' ordeal is a national protocol, in place since 2009, that governs medical-forensic and police-science work after incidents with many casualties. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The AfD's leaked internal emails are a bigger problem for the party than it admits**

The far-right Alternative für Deutschland calls its data leak "a few gigabytes of unimportant stuff", but the mails show officials recognised the party's radicalisation early and could carry legal weight. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Germany's birth rate is unlikely to recover soon, a study finds**

The number of women aged 25 to 39, the main childbearing years, is set to fall markedly by 2035, with east and west developing differently, and the authors call for a policy response. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Many Germans are open to shares but keep postponing retirement saving**

A survey for the private banks' federation finds interest in equities even among young people, yet old prejudices and inertia leave pension provision deferred, with serious consequences later. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A possible Red-Green-Left Senate plans a federal push to cap commercial rents**

If Berlin's Linke, Greens and SPD form a coalition, they want a Bundesrat (the states' chamber) initiative to limit rising rents for shops and workshops, and Tagesspiegel asks how realistic that is. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Some Ku'damm shops board up before the nationwide "Gen Z regelt" protest day**

Police are preparing a large operation for the protest day organised by the "Initiative Gen Z regelt" but say they do not expect serious violence. ([rbb24][8])

* **Halloween walking tours take in Berlin's cemeteries, bat caves and bunkers**

With the dark season setting in, rbb24 rounds up eerie places in Berlin and the surrounding Brandenburg commuter belt to visit around Halloween. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Canada and the EU launch an "Alliance for the Future" open to other countries**

At a summit in Montreal, Prime Minister Mark Carney and Commission president Ursula von der Leyen unveiled a partnership of like-minded states meant as a hedge against unpredictable geopolitics, particularly from Washington. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Congo's Ebola outbreak has passed 5,000 deaths, days after reaching 10,000 cases**

The toll in the Democratic Republic of Congo crossed another threshold with no end to the epidemic in sight, according to dpa. ([Handelsblatt][11])

* **OpenAI cuts its funding target by $10bn as worries about AI valuations grow**

The ChatGPT maker is now seeking about $20bn at a $1.25tn valuation, tempering earlier ambitions, the Financial Times reports. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 A scaled-back raise at a still-huge valuation suggests investors are rationing capital for AI labs, not abandoning them.
