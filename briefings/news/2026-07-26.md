# News Briefing — 26 July 2026

Castellón's out-of-control blaze and Madrid's firefighter standoff expose Spain's summer crisis management, Berlin Pride ends in a fatal vehicle attack, and a US–Korea AI pact tests who will fund the next chip build-out.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Castellón wildfire declared "beyond extinction capacity" as 15,000 flee 16 municipalities**

eldiario.es reports authorities evacuated roughly 15,000 residents across La Vall d'Uixó and 15 neighbouring towns as flames overwhelmed local crews, with more than 300 personnel and 14 aircraft deployed. Six people suffered burns or smoke inhalation as the blaze spread through Sierra de Espadán park, where investigators suspect arson. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When regional command posts themselves must evacuate, the bottleneck is coordination capacity — not tanker availability.

* **Madrid forest firefighters accuse regional government of "blackmail" amid national emergency**

EL PAÍS says roughly 60 Madrid wildland crews are locked in a labour dispute as the regional executive denounces a work stoppage during the capital's megafire crisis, while firefighters counter they are idled on irregular contracts. The standoff lands as Ayuso and Sánchez trade blame over who controls prevention spending. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Sánchez enters final mandate stretch without a budget and with courts setting the pace**

eldiario.es argues the government has passed 34 laws and 40 decrees in three years despite right-wing obstruction, yet faces a barren autumn on regional financing reform, 2027 budgets and pending corruption trials. With Congress adjourned until September, judicial calendars may matter more than parliamentary bargaining for the rest of the term. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Households face higher 2027 grid fees as Berlin weighs cutting transmission subsidies**

Handelsblatt says the federal government may trim its contribution to long-distance network costs as it hunts savings, pushing more of the Energiewende bill onto retail power prices from next year. Industry groups warn the shift could erase recent relief on industrial tariffs just as chemical and metals plants plead for cheaper electrons. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Eastern chemical firms warn they are pausing investment as Germany loses appeal**

A Handelsblatt survey finds companies in eastern states increasingly question whether to expand local capacity, citing energy costs, bureaucracy and weak demand. If the trend hardens, it would deepen the east-west productivity gap Merz's coalition has promised to close. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Denmark's state-funded AI news project sparks backlash over public journalism subsidies**

Tagesspiegel reports "Projekt Y" plans to generate stories without traditional reporting, financed partly by Copenhagen, drawing fierce criticism from established outlets. The fight previews how European governments will draw — and police — the line between innovation subsidies and propaganda. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Vehicle drives into crowd after Christopher Street Day, killing one woman**

rbb24 says a car struck people near Tiergarten late Saturday as Pride celebrations wound down, leaving one woman dead and several injured; police were searching for the driver. Organisers had already cut the programme short, turning a weekend framed as a democracy festival into a security and mourning operation. ([rbb24][7])

* **Fire breaks out on former military site in Jüterbog, south of Berlin**

rbb24 reports crews battled a blaze on disused Bundeswehr terrain in Teltow-Fläming district, adding to a summer of wildfire alerts across Brandenburg's pine forests. The incident revives debate over whether abandoned defence estates near the capital need clearer hazard management. ([rbb24][8])

* **Brandenburg agriculture minister says wolf cull plan will stay limited**

Tagesspiegel quotes state minister Hanka Mittelstädt insisting planned shootings will not reach "hundreds" of animals, seeking to calm farmers and conservationists deadlocked over livestock losses. Berlin commuters and weekenders still encounter the predators in surrounding woodlands as federal rules tighten. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Samsung, SK Hynix and Nvidia join $700bn US–Korea AI investment push**

Nikkei Asia says Seoul and Washington unveiled a package tying chipmakers to data-centre and semiconductor spending as both capitals race China on AI infrastructure. The pact formalises supply-chain alignment that could redirect Korean fab capacity toward American cloud customers. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

💡 When industrial policy is negotiated as a bilateral investment headline, fabs become diplomatic assets — not just commercial bets.

* **Iran says Ukrainian strike on Caspian vessel killed a sailor**

The Financial Times reports Tehran accused Kyiv of attacking a cargo ship in the Caspian Sea and urged the EU to stop the Middle East war spreading beyond the Gulf. If confirmed, the incident would mark a new geographic vector for a conflict that has mostly stayed in Ukraine and the Hormuz corridor. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Britain cancels Kenya army exercise after Nairobi demands prosecution rights**

The Guardian says the UK scrapped September training when Kenyan lawmakers refused to ratify a defence pact without stronger jurisdiction over offences by British troops. The dispute underscores how post-colonial basing agreements are being renegotiated across Africa as public scrutiny of foreign soldiers rises. ([The Guardian][12])
