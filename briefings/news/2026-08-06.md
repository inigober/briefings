# News Briefing — 6 August 2026

A bomb-laden drone at Leipzig airport, Vox taking control of Andalusia's gender-violence units, and Iran's Hormuz shipping deal sketch today's frame as Berlin's mayoral race reopens the Tempelhofer Feld and courts probe who knew what before Ceuta's deadliest crossing.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Vox gains levers over Andalusia's gender-violence assessment units**

Spain's far-right Vox party, junior partner in Juanma Moreno's Andalusian government, now runs integrated violence-against-women evaluation units from the regional justice ministry — and while national law bars abolishing victim-support services, the coalition can reshape protocols, budgets and leadership appointments. Madrid's central government is questioning the People's Party's loyalty to the national gender-violence pact after conservatives handed Vox control of services feminists call ideological battlegrounds. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Semen microbiome may shape IVF outcomes, Spanish researchers find**

A new analysis suggests bacterial communities in male semen correlate with miscarriage rates and lower live births after fertility treatment, challenging clinics that still treat infertility as overwhelmingly a women's-health problem. The finding could push Spanish and European reproductive medicine to screen male partners systematically rather than defaulting interventions to egg quality and uterine factors alone. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **National Court judge demands data on Guardia Civil warnings before Ceuta deaths**

Audiencia Nacional magistrate María Tardón has asked the Civil Guard whether it received any advance alert before last week's mass crossing into Ceuta, as she investigates the causes of 79 confirmed fatalities on the Spanish side of the border. The request turns a humanitarian crisis into a judicial test of whether Spanish security services had intelligence they failed to act on. ([La Vanguardia][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Explosive-laden drone found beside Ukrainian transport plane at Leipzig airport**

Police discovered and defused a drone carrying explosives next to a Ukrainian military cargo aircraft at Leipzig/Halle airport, prompting interior minister Alexander Dobrindt to describe a new tier of hybrid sabotage against critical infrastructure. The incident disrupted holiday flights in Saxony and raised questions about whether European airports are prepared for weaponised unmanned aircraft targeting allied supply hubs. ([Süddeutsche Zeitung][4])

💡 Attacking a Ukrainian logistics node inside Germany would signal that the war's sabotage front has moved from Baltic cables and rail lines to NATO-adjacent aviation hubs.

* **Higher track-access fees threaten to push up Deutschlandticket price**

Germany's 16 states are demanding more federal cash after Deutsche Bahn sharply raised regional-rail network charges, warning the popular €49 monthly Deutschlandticket could become unaffordable without a subsidy fix. The dispute pits Berlin's flagship public-transport bargain against a rail operator trying to repair finances after years of under-investment. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Kiel Institute sees German house-price rally losing steam**

The Kiel Institute for the World Economy reports that owner-occupied apartment prices are rising more slowly nationwide, with nominal declines in Stuttgart and Frankfurt, as higher mortgage rates finally cool a market that rebounded quickly after the 2022 energy shock. The data suggests Germany's housing affordability crisis may be easing at the margin even as rents in major cities stay elevated. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **SPD mayoral hopeful proposes artificial surf wave on Tempelhofer Feld**

Steffen Krach, the Social Democrats' candidate for Berlin mayor, has ruled out building on the former airport's open field and instead pitched an artificial surfing lagoon as the centrepiece of a "century park" vision for Tempelhof. The idea tests whether the city's most symbolic undeveloped space can host spectacle infrastructure without reviving the housing battles that have blocked earlier development plans. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Berlin museums revisit colonial objects in new collection display**

A exhibition opening across Berlin state museums asks visitors to confront how amber game boards, royal garments and everyday artefacts entered collections through empire, trade and confiscation, part of a wider European reckoning with provenance. Curators frame the show as a pilot for how German institutions narrate difficult ownership histories without removing objects from public view. ([The Berliner][8])

* **Brandenburg interior minister seeks tougher drone-defence powers after Leipzig scare**

Brandenburg's interior minister, Michael Stromer, says the explosive drone found at Leipzig airport marks a "fundamentally changed" security environment and wants expanded police powers to intercept unmanned aircraft over critical sites near Berlin. The push could accelerate drone-defence legislation in Germany's most populous eastern state ahead of September's Abgeordnetenhaus election. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Google DeepMind chief Demis Hassabis steps back in AI lab reshuffle**

Demis Hassabis is moving from day-to-day leadership of Google DeepMind as Alphabet reorganises its artificial-intelligence units, while long-serving chief scientist Jeff Dean departs to launch a start-up. The shake-up comes as Silicon Valley giants race to integrate frontier models into search, cloud and robotics products without losing the researchers who built them. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Peruvian communities win $150m settlement over childhood lead poisoning**

A US mining company's subsidiary has agreed to pay roughly $150 million to more than 1,300 Peruvians allegedly poisoned as children by emissions from a Andean smelter, capping a two-decade campaign by Cardinal Pedro Barreto that brought death threats and Vatican lobbying. Activists call the deal a rare victory for poor communities against transnational extractive firms in Latin America. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Suzuki bets on flex-fuel cars as Modi pushes ethanol over imported oil**

Japan's Suzuki Motor is launching flex-fuel vehicles in India that can run on petrol or high-ethanol blends, aligning with Prime Minister Narendra Modi's drive to cut crude imports by expanding domestic biofuel production. The move could reshape India's mass-market car fleet and pressure rivals to follow in the world's third-largest auto market. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
