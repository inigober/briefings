# News Briefing — 13 October 2026

Spain's Supreme Court is locking 163,000 new citizens out of the snap-election roll, Germany is arguing over AI jobs and the power-tax bill, and chip investors just heard the word casino.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The Supreme Court is taking the vote from 163,020 'nietos'**

Spain's *ley de nietos* is the 2022 law that let descendants of exiles become citizens; the Supreme Court has now ordered the government to suspend voting rights for 163,020 of them before 29 November, a step ministers say is "materially impossible" and a dissenting judge called an irreversible loss of the franchise. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The electorate is being rewritten by judges with six weeks to polling day.

* **Doctors and teachers are on the street too — not only housing**

Housing is the axis of the snap-election campaign, but physicians and school staff have been striking for months — at different ministries — and will still be out next month, so the street that will vote on 29 November is not only a rent camp. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **A third of Spain's self-published bestsellers are now mostly AI**

EL PAÍS ran 175 popular titles through an AI detector: in 2019 the same test found nothing, but a third of today's self-published Spanish bestsellers are mostly machine-written, including Amazon romance and a hit by Alvise Pérez, the far-right influencer-turned-author. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The digital minister wants retraining — and Gulf money for compute**

Karsten Wildberger, the cabinet minister for digital policy, says Germany must retrain workers for an AI labour market and keep "deep tech" from becoming a foreign test lab — while still talking about Gulf capital for German data centres. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 Technological sovereignty is being pitched as a training programme that still needs someone else's money for the chips.

* **The 2027 Bahn timetable adds a Munich–Rome train**

Deutsche Bahn's next timetable puts more international services on the map, including a direct Munich–Rome run, even as punctuality remains what the paper calls a *Zumutung* — an imposition — for passengers. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Klingbeil is already doubting the electricity-tax cut**

After a rough Monday in the black-red coalition, SPD finance minister and vice-chancellor Lars Klingbeil is publicly questioning whether the promised *Stromsteuer* cut can be funded, while saying he will keep both jobs; defence minister Boris Pistorius ducked a question about leading the party. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The law says keep a blackout kit; the boroughs are not ready**

German disaster law already treats a stocked household as part of the plan for a blackout; rbb24 says Berliners still cannot fully rely on the *Bezirke*, whose emergency "lighthouse" points are incomplete. ([rbb24][7])

💡 The city is asking residents to be the backup because the boroughs are not.

* **Camps are cleared and the tents come back a block later**

Boroughs keep dismantling homeless encampments, and people return or shift a corner away — in part because a large share are homeless EU citizens for whom the path from a tent to a help desk is especially hard. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin is spending millions to teach primary pupils to swim**

The city is putting large sums into water training so fewer children leave Grundschule unable to swim; trainers say cultural fear of the water still sits behind the *Seepferdchen* badge numbers. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Chip stocks fell after Broadcom's boss said 'casino'**

Samsung and SK Hynix led a sharp Kospi drop after Broadcom's chief called parts of the AI investment wave a "casino" — a sudden loss of nerve about how much the chip supply chain should spend. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The AI trade is being repriced on a CEO's word, not a missed earnings print.

* **Bolivia is investigating Evo Morales over 2009 killings**

Bolivia's attorney-general has opened a case against former president Evo Morales over a 2009 Santa Cruz hotel raid in which three Europeans were shot and two others tortured. ([The Guardian][11])

* **AI fakes are turning hiring into a cybersecurity job**

Fraudulent AI applications are forcing HR departments to work with cybersecurity teams — so the people who hire you may now also be the people who assume you are a fake. ([Financial Times][12])
