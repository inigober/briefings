# News Briefing — 25 August 2026

Uncontested Spanish business leadership and vanishing soil sit beside a German cabinet on a growth retreat, Berlin districts spending a federal pot, and a world redrawing chip, cyber and migration lines.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Antonio Garamendi will keep the employers' federation without a vote**

Antonio Garamendi, the Basque businessman who leads CEOE, Spain's main employers' association, is heading for a third term unopposed after no rival filed by the 24 August deadline, so the country's peak business lobby will be proclaimed rather than contested. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 An unopposed third term means Spanish capital's peak lobby now reproduces itself without an internal argument about the country's economic course.

* **Spain is arriving at a UN land summit as Europe's most desertified country**

While 196 countries meet in Ulaanbaatar to slow global soil degradation, more than half of Spain's land is already classed as desertified, and in Murcia, Albacete, Almería and Las Palmas the share exceeds 80%. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **A wave pool is splitting a Cantabrian surf coast over what kind of tourism to build**

Cantabria's regional government is backing an artificial-wave complex it says could draw 200,000 visitors and that would rezone 100,000 square metres in a working surf area, a land-use fight neighbours say cements the wrong tourism model. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **School stress is now a mass condition for German teenagers, especially girls**

A new study finds one in three young people heavily burdened by school pressure, including 42% of girls, with family stress and loneliness stacking on top of the classroom load. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Merz's cabinet is going on retreat to learn growth**

Chancellor Friedrich Merz's black-red cabinet starts a closed session putting growth and jobs first; Handelsblatt says the more useful lesson would come from five people brought in to teach the government economics. ([Handelsblatt][5])

💡 A cabinet that needs outside tutors for growth is advertising a competence gap more than a programme.

* **Annalena Baerbock is heading to Columbia instead of home**

After her term as president of the UN General Assembly ends in September, the Green politician is expected to take a post at Columbia University in New York rather than return to German politics for now. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's districts are spending a federal special fund on roads, disaster kit — and some odd extras**

Each Berlin borough is getting almost €20 million from the federal special fund, much of it for streets and civil protection, but some of the chosen projects are already drawing criticism from economists. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

💡 A federal emergency pot is already doubling as a second municipal budget, including projects economists call questionable.

* **Berlin's games studios showed up in Cologne, but Gamescom is not moving east**

Gamescom in Cologne sold out its stand space in advance for the first time, with Berlin studios in the mix, while the fair is more political this year and is still not relocating to the capital. ([rbb24][8])

* **A new musical is using 1920s queer Berlin to talk about the present**

"Burning Berlin" follows lesbian subculture in the 1920s, a world the Nazis later destroyed, but the production is being framed as a story about now rather than a period piece. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **A TSMC supplier is bringing chipmaking 'tubes' to Taiwan**

Nichias, a supplier to chip foundry TSMC, plans to build key chipmaking tubes in Taiwan, pulling another specialised fab input closer to the plants that use it. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **The UAE is answering an AI-era cyber wave with a homegrown AI security industry**

Attacks on the country's banks, aviation and energy systems have climbed since the war with Iran began, and its response is to build AI-on-AI defences at home rather than only import them. ([Rest of World][11])

💡 The Iran war is creating a Gulf market for AI-on-AI cyber defence, not just missiles.

* **Colombia's new far-right president is opening with a Trump-style immigration raid**

Abelardo de la Espriella, a millionaire former lawyer in office just over two weeks, has ordered authorities to detain and deport people who are in the country irregularly or have committed crimes, even as Colombia is still dealing with a 7.4-magnitude earthquake that killed more than 300. ([The Guardian][12])
