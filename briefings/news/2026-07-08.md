# News Briefing — 8 July 2026

Spain locks in a record spending ceiling while labour and ethics fights sharpen, Germany's Bundesbank bids to manage a capital-pension overhaul as markets reel from Hormuz, and fresh US–Iran energy sanctions sit alongside AI hardware shifts and deadly storms in China.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Ayuso government doubled payments to Quirón since 2021, official accounts show**

eldiario.es reports Madrid regional payments to the private health group roughly doubled after the PP took sole power in 2021, according to the Comunidad's audited accounts. A separate tax inspection alleges the president's partner invoiced Quirón Prevención €4.4m between 2021 and 2023 as the company's public contracts expanded. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When audited payments and family billing move in parallel, anti-corruption scrutiny stops being partisan noise and becomes a procurement-governance test for Spain's largest opposition-led region.

* **Feijóo pledges pay and benefit cuts for workers on sick leave**

Speaking to Basque business leaders, the PP leader called workplace absenteeism a "cancer" costing €30bn annually and vowed to impose reforms with or without union agreement. He did not distinguish medical leave from other absences, sharpening a pre-election labour agenda that targets statutory sick pay. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Cabinet approves record €226bn spending ceiling for 2027 budget**

The government set a non-financial spending cap of €226.032bn — roughly double the last Rajoy-era budget — ahead of September draft accounts. Ministers frame the ceiling as a health-and-education investment record, but parliamentary partners Podemos and Junts have yet to signal support for the wider budget path. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Bundesbank offers to manage state's €140bn capital-pension pot**

Tagesspiegel quotes vice-president Sabine Mauderer saying the central bank could invest the planned Kapitalrente assets "from a standing start," opening a bidding race against private managers. Berlin's coalition is designing a funded pension pillar meant to shore up statutory pensions without further contribution hikes. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 If the Bundesbank wins the mandate, Berlin keeps pension capital inside a public balance sheet — a political choice about state capacity, not just asset returns.

* **Dax loses momentum as fresh US strikes on Iran lift oil prices**

Handelsblatt notes Germany's benchmark index cooled after three record sessions as Middle East escalation pushed crude higher and tech shares slipped. Traders are repricing inflation and rate-cut expectations before the NATO summit in Ankara. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Handelsblatt warns judicial battles can backfire against populist challengers**

The paper's morning briefing argues Marine Le Pen's decision to run for president despite her embezzlement conviction shows how legal disqualification fights can energise, not contain, Europe's far right. Editors frame the lesson for German politics as courts alone cannot substitute for competitive centre-right offerings. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Verdict expected Wednesday in palliative doctor murder trial**

rbb24 reports Berlin's Landgericht will rule on a physician accused of killing at least 15 patients in palliative care, capping a case that has shaken trust in end-of-life medicine. Prosecutors sought a life sentence after a multi-year investigation into deaths across several facilities. ([rbb24][7])

* **DWD forecasts return of summer heat after mild spell**

Tagesspiegel says Germany's weather service expects rising temperatures across Berlin in coming days following a cooler interlude. Municipal services are watching demand on transport and open-air facilities as the capital heads back toward seasonal highs. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Berlin Music Video Awards draw eclectic red-carpet turnout**

The Berliner profiles artists and directors at the BMVAs, highlighting the city's continuing role as a low-budget visual-music hub. Organisers pitched the ceremony as a showcase for experimental work outside major-label pipelines. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **US revokes waiver on Iranian oil sales after Hormuz tanker strikes**

The Financial Times reports Washington ended a sanctions exemption for Tehran's crude exports following attacks on vessels near Oman, including Qatari and Saudi tankers. The move tightens energy pressure as Gulf shipping insurers reassess war-risk premiums. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 Revoking the waiver links maritime security directly to Iran's fiscal lifeline — raising the cost of escalation for Tehran and importers alike.

* **Severe storms in China kill 15 as tornadoes and landslides spread inland**

The Guardian says thunderstorms and gale-force winds killed at least 11 people in Hubei province alone, with additional deaths from landslides in western China; Xi Jinping ordered "all-out" rescue efforts. State media reported tornado damage in multiple cities as seasonal convection intensifies. ([The Guardian][11])

* **CPUs move to centre of AI race as inference costs bite**

Nikkei Asia argues semiconductor strategists are re-rating central processors as cloud providers seek cheaper ways to run AI workloads beyond GPU clusters. The shift matters for chipmakers balancing data-centre capex against enterprise demand for affordable inference. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
