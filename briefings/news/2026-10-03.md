# News Briefing — 3 October 2026

Failed housing votes in Madrid, another department-store collapse in Germany, Berlin's still-missing S-Bahn, and a fight over who reserved Japan's power grid set today's frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Both housing decrees are dead — and five million tenants are back on the clock**

Pedro Sánchez's emergency housing decrees fell in Congress on Friday, killed by Junts, the PP and Vox three days after Cabinet passed them, leaving about five million tenants due a renewal before 2028 without the automatic extensions the texts would have locked in. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 A lost floor vote is no longer a bargaining text; it is the event Sánchez is now testing as an election trigger.

* **Sánchez will use the weekend to decide whether to call an election**

Spain's Socialist prime minister is reading polls and waiting on Saturday's housing march before deciding whether to dissolve parliament as early as Monday — treating the defeat as a possible campaign opening, not only a legislative one. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **A housing general strike now polls as a majority idea — including on the right**

Seventy-three percent of Spaniards, and more than 60% of PP and Vox voters, would support a general strike over housing, the step the tenants' union and the big labour federations CCOO and UGT are now weighing. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Galeria is in insolvency again — and the surviving map is smaller**

Galeria, the department-store chain that has already been through insolvency, is in another; Heilbronn retail analyst Gerrit Kortum says it can have a future only with fewer stores, and others doubt the latest crisis is survivable. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **GLP-1 shots are being tried on children when ordinary obesity care fails**

For some children with extreme obesity, the usual diet-and-clinic programmes do not work; GLP-1 drugs — the Ozempic-class injections that suppress appetite in adults — can slow their hunger, though the long-term effects are still unknown. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Thousands spent two days at the Brandenburg Gate against bringing back the draft**

Several thousand people joined a rally and concert against Wehrpflicht, the military conscription Germany suspended in 2011 and which the coalition has been circling again. ([rbb24][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Unity promised the 1961 rail map — seven S-Bahn stretches are still missing**

When Germany reunified, the Bund said it would restore Berlin's urban rail to its 1961 network; 36 years later seven S-Bahn stretches have not been rebuilt. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

💡 The unpaid unity promise is a timetable of tracks, not a speech.

* **Kauf Dich Glücklich is opening shop number 28**

Andrea Dahmen and Christoph Munier started with a tiny vintage store in the cheap-rent 2000s; the chain they built is opening its 28th branch, a survivor of the years the founders say you had to "bleed for" before a shop felt like yours. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Tag der Clubkultur is the city's annual argument that nightlife is infrastructure**

Workshops, panels, club nights and live sets fill a Berlin-wide day devoted to club culture — a calendar reminder that the city still treats clubs as something to defend, not only to visit. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Tokyo's utility is going after firms that book the grid and then sit on it**

TEPCO, the power company for the Tokyo region, is moving against "capacity squatters" — companies that reserve scarce grid connections for AI data centres and do not use them — so the computing boom cannot hoard electricity it has not yet drawn. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

💡 The constraint is no longer only chips; it is a reservation on the wire.

* **The US boarded a fuel ship bound for Cuba**

The US Coast Guard intercepted a cargo vessel it says was using "dark fleet" tactics to carry fuel to Cuba and took it to Mexico — a physical enforcement of the Trump administration's oil embargo, not only a paper one. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Starbucks has opened in Xinjiang — and Washington wants the shops shut**

The US coffee chain has opened its first cafés in Xinjiang, the Chinese region where Washington accuses Beijing of mass repression of Uyghurs; a US congressman has told the company to close them. ([Handelsblatt][12])
