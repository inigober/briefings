# News Briefing — 12 August 2026

Spain's peninsular eclipse, fresh Ceuta deterrence messaging, and a BSW breach of the AfD firewall in Saxony-Anhalt share the day with Berlin's CSD case closure, China's gig-economy jobs squeeze, and Colombia's deadliest quake in decades.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Foreign minister warns Moroccans tempted to cross: "You will be returned"**

After visiting Ceuta on Tuesday, José Manuel Albares told audiences on the Moroccan side of the fence that anyone encouraged to enter Spain's North African enclaves is "doomed to fail" and will be sent back — sharpening deterrence rhetoric while praising Rabat's cooperation and offering no criticism of Morocco's border management. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **ILO says AI and geopolitical shocks push global youth unemployment to 20%**

The UN labour agency reports the share of young people neither in work nor education has climbed to one in five worldwide, blaming automation, conflict and weak hiring in sectors that once absorbed school-leavers. Spain's own NEET rate remains below the global average, but Madrid's services-heavy economy is not insulated from the structural squeeze. ([La Vanguardia][2])

* **Cloud maps show where eclipse viewers may lose totality**

On the day Spain witnesses its first peninsular total solar eclipse since 1905, meteorologists are publishing municipality-level forecasts for cloud cover and horizon visibility so millions of spectators know whether they will see full darkness or only a partial show. Authorities are bracing for traffic jams along the totality corridor as hotels and campsites fill. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **BSW says it would back an AfD premier in Saxony-Anhalt if rivals refuse coalition**

The Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance, which entered several eastern state parliaments on a left-conservative ticket, declares the "firewall" against the far-right AfD has failed and says it would accept an AfD minister-president if other parties block its preferred government model. The statement shatters a taboo that mainstream conservatives have defended since the AfD's rise and could reshape coalition arithmetic after autumn elections. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 If BSW follows through, Berlin's cordon sanitaire becomes harder to sell in other eastern Länder where the AfD already leads polls.

* **DAX closes above 26,500 for the first time**

Germany's benchmark equity index crossed a new record on Tuesday even as investors await US inflation data and watch oil prices climb on Middle East shipping risks. The rally highlights how export-heavy listings can decouple from domestic manufacturing gloom when global tech and defence orders hold up. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Men who suffer intimate-partner violence struggle to be believed, Berlin case shows**

A Berlin man describes how an ex-partner threatened to injure herself and blame him — a pattern support workers say is under-reported because male victims fear ridicule or automatic suspicion. The account feeds a wider debate on whether Germany's protection system still treats domestic abuse as a women's issue. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Federal prosecutor closes case against CSD attacker Abdul Ballout**

Germany's federal prosecutor has ended proceedings against the man who drove into crowds after Berlin's Pride parade in June, while investigations into possible accomplices continue. The closure formalises the end of the main criminal track against the perpetrator and keeps security policy in focus weeks before the state election. ([Berliner Zeitung][7])

* **Wall anniversary: eyewitness recalls "living silence" of 13 August 1961**

East Berliner Dieter Dombrowski, who was ten when the border closed, tells rbb how the day that divided his city set him on a path to prison after a failed escape — on the 65th anniversary of construction starting. His testimony lands as Berlin debates how to teach division-era history to pupils with no living memory of the Wall. ([rbb24][8])

* **From factory floors to the Zoo: Berlin notables recall first summer jobs**

Singers, authors and politicians describe teenage stints on assembly lines, in care homes and at the city zoo — a seasonal portrait of how the capital's elite learned manual work before fame. The vignettes underline how Berlin's low-wage service sector still absorbs students even as manufacturing jobs thin out. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **China's gig platforms now employ 53 million low-paid workers**

As construction contracts and factory hiring slow, tens of millions of Chinese have moved into food delivery and ride-hailing apps that offer flexible hours but little security — a shift the Financial Times calls the country's "great jobs squeeze." The migration shows how automation and property slump are reshaping labour markets in the world's second-largest economy. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 Beijing's growth model once promised factory careers; if gig work becomes the default safety valve, consumer demand and social stability both weaken.

* **Colombia searches for more than 3,000 missing after magnitude-7.4 quake**

Regional authorities put the death toll above 250 after the tremor struck impoverished Chocó province, with thousands still unaccounted for as rescuers comb collapsed buildings. President Abelardo de la Espriella, sworn in days earlier, has declared a national disaster and appealed for international help. ([La Vanguardia][11])

* **China's Comac C919 jet prepares first international flight to Mongolia**

State-owned planemaker Comac will fly its C919 narrow-body — billed as a domestic rival to Boeing and Airbus — from Beijing to Ulaanbaatar on Wednesday, a symbolic step in Beijing's decade-long push to break Western duopoly in commercial aviation. Industry analysts say certification and supply-chain gaps mean a global challenge remains years away. ([The Guardian][12])
