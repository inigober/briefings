# News Briefing — 11 August 2026

A Ceuta accountability fight in Madrid, new German rail and EV policies, and Berlin's pre-election frictions frame a day when Colombia's earthquake, China's typhoon evacuations, and India's AI disruption dominate the wider world.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Shell company tied to Ayuso partner reports zero revenue for second year**

The Madrid firm through which Alberto González Amador, partner of regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso, allegedly paid €500,000 to a Quirón executive's wife has logged no turnover for two consecutive years while prosecutors investigate money-laundering and corruption links. The accounting gap deepens scrutiny of Masterman & Whitaker, Ayuso's consultancy that declared €710,000 in revenue and €1.2 million in dividend distributions in the same period. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Government and Senate head for institutional clash over Ceuta hearings**

Pedro Sánchez's executive and the opposition-controlled upper chamber are on a collision course after the PP reformed Senate rules to make ministerial appearances on the Ceuta crisis mandatory — and the government has already refused similar summons over past scandals. The stand-off could reach the Constitutional Court and turns migration management into a test of which branch controls executive accountability. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Researchers trace Ceuta surge to viral videos promising an open border**

A Moroccan academic's real-time study of the July crossing found that early videos showing successful entries — some with millions of views — pulled thousands of young men to the frontier, while Rabat's anti-disinformation agency later flagged foreign accounts and organised networks amplifying the message "the door is open." The finding suggests social-media contagion, not only border-policy shifts, drove Europe's largest single-day entry since 2021. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Deutsche Bahn to offer paid onboard childcare for children travelling alone**

From this autumn parents can book supervised travel for unaccompanied children as young as six on selected long-distance routes, paying an extra fee for staff who escort minors between stations. The product targets families who rely on rail to send children to grandparents but cannot accompany them, and arrives as punctuality and safety on regional corridors remain under political fire. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Export-control agency chief defends revived electric-car purchase subsidy**

Mandy Pastohr, president of the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control, says Berlin's reinstated EV buyer premium is already steering demand toward eligible models and releases early data on which manufacturers benefit. The defence matters because coalition critics argue subsidising private cars diverts climate spending from public transport even as German automakers struggle with Chinese competition. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Apple's Vision Pro finds a niche in operating theatres despite consumer flop**

Surgeons in German and international hospitals are adopting Apple's mixed-reality headset to overlay scans and vital signs during procedures, even as the €3,500 consumer version sells poorly in living rooms. The split illustrates how immersive hardware vendors may need medical and industrial buyers to justify development costs while waiting for a mass-market breakthrough. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Secret neo-Nazi concert draws 250 people to Köpenick**

Roughly 250 attendees gathered on Saturday for a "summer festival" featuring far-right bands including Lunikoff Verschwörung at a venue in Berlin-Köpenick, with some arriving in Ku Klux Klan masks and on Harley-Davidson motorcycles. Police eventually raided the AfD-linked "Heimat" party headquarters nearby, highlighting how underground music networks still stage shows in the capital despite bans on several participating groups. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Assaults on train conductors rise across Berlin and Brandenburg**

Deutsche Bahn reports a growing number of attacks on onboard staff across the capital region, adding pressure on a operator already criticised for overcrowding and delays on RE and S-Bahn services. The trend feeds security debates ahead of September's state election, when parties are promising both more visible policing and better working conditions for frontline rail workers. ([rbb24][8])

* **Ballot misprint becomes first mishap of Berlin state election**

Authorities discovered Greens mayoral hopeful Tobias B. Graf's name misspelled on sample postal ballots, forcing corrections before hundreds of thousands of votes are cast in September's Abgeordnetenhaus race. The error is minor but symbolic in a campaign where administrative competence and coalition arithmetic already dominate headlines. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Generative AI threatens India's IT-services employment model**

India built its economy on back-office coding and business-process outsourcing for Western firms, but Financial Times reporting warns large-language models can now automate tasks that once required armies of graduate engineers — putting millions of middle-class jobs at risk. The shift matters globally because Bangalore and Hyderabad supply the remote labour that keeps multinational IT budgets lean. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 If Indian IT giants cannot pivot from body-shopping to AI product work fast enough, the world's default offshore talent pool could shrink just as Western companies need cheaper automation partners.

* **Typhoon Dolphin forces more than a million evacuations in eastern China**

The strongest tropical cyclone to hit China this year made landfall on the east coast after dumping record rainfall on Shanghai and relocating more than 900,000 people in Wenzhou alone, with authorities warning of flooding and landslides as the storm moves inland. Six people were injured and 50,000 buildings lost power when the typhoon crossed Japan's Okinawa prefecture beforehand. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Singapore lifts 2026 growth forecast after 5.9% second-quarter expansion**

The city-state's trade-dependent economy grew 5.9% year on year in the April–June quarter, prompting officials to raise their full-year outlook despite global tariff uncertainty. The revision suggests Southeast Asia's financial hub is riding electronics and services demand even as China's property slump and US trade policy cloud the wider region. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
