# News Briefing — 02 November 2026

Accountability pressure runs through the day: Spain's parliament turns on its transport minister, Chancellor Merz faces his own party's base, and on the eve of US midterms voters weigh the price of fuel.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A majority of Congress's standing committee demands transport minister Óscar Puente's resignation over the Urda rail crash**

PP, Vox, Junts and PNV, together holding a majority in the Diputación Permanente (the committee that stands in for parliament while it is not sitting), will press for immediate political responsibility, while the minister and government argue that previous accidents drew far fewer explanations. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **A fresh fight over the judiciary's governing council freezes the filling of 15 Supreme Court vacancies**

After the president of the Consejo General del Poder Judicial (CGPJ, the body that appoints senior judges) and a Sumar-nominated member voted with the conservative bloc on committee allocations, the progressive bloc accuses them of handing the council to the right, and a deal on the empty seats recedes. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The vacancies show how a procedural split over committee seats can stall appointments to Spain's highest court.

* **Ten senior Valencian regional officials earn extra pay for teaching, and the government will not say how much**

Directors-general and secretaries on salaries of €68,000–96,000 sign only a declaration promising not to earn more than 30% of their pay from teaching, according to a parliamentary answer to the opposition PSPV. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Friedrich Merz meets the CDU's grassroots in Berlin on Monday as some members openly discuss replacing him as party leader**

The chancellor, whose Christian Democrats lead the governing coalition, faces a rank-and-file gathering that some in the party believe would be better led by someone else. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **German industry plans its own raw-materials strategy, citing the government's "lack of capacity to act"**

Led by carmakers such as BMW, major companies are pursuing an initiative to secure supply as Chinese export controls and rising energy costs add pressure, while the federal government plays only a secondary role. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

* **Telekom chief Timotheus Höttges floats a return of "Deutschland AG" after the chancellor's Asia trip**

The idea of close ties between big companies and the state, the cross-shareholding model that defined postwar German capitalism, sounds dated, but Handelsblatt's morning podcast says there are reasons behind it. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Trains from Berlin to Hannover take up to 80 minutes longer, and are also running late on top of the diversion**

A detour around track renovation has lengthened the journey since October, and an exclusive analysis of the first three weeks shows significant extra delays. ([rbb24][7])

* **Preparations begin for a new bridge on Schönhauser Allee in Prenzlauer Berg**

Work to ready the ground for the replacement structure on one of the district's main arteries is starting. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin's nightclubs face scrutiny over their environmental footprint**

In an opinion piece, The Berliner's music editor argues that nightlife's climate impact deserves attention in light of the climate crisis. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **US petrol prices are set to hit a record high under President Trump on the eve of the midterm elections**

Average fuel costs in his second term have passed Biden-era levels, putting affordability at the centre of Tuesday's congressional polls. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Taiwan and Japan are weighing a trade deal modelled on Taipei's pact with London, sources say**

The two economies are exploring an agreement of the kind Taipei and London have already struck, according to Nikkei Asia's sources. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Russian strikes leave thousands without power in Ukraine, Zelenskyy says**

The Ukrainian president spoke as Moscow reported "significant damage" to an industrial facility in Russia's Nizhny Novgorod region. ([Politico Europe][12])
