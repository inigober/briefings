# News Briefing — 27 November 2026

Two days before Spain votes, a revived Sánchez and a fact-checked debate dominate the campaign, while new US tariffs on the EU, a tech-stock rout and Middle East attacks lifting oil make for a nervous end to November.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Fact-check of the four-way election debate finds Vox and PP leaders misstating immigration and housing figures**

In yesterday's debate, Vox leader Santiago Abascal repeated the claim that six million foreigners arrived in six years and that sexual assaults have "tripled", while Alberto Núñez Feijóo of the conservative Popular Party (PP) wrongly said 400,000 homes had left the rental market, leaning on Idealista figures that inflate official data. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **PP candidate Feijóo pledges to resign if he cannot become prime minister**

Feijóo, who leads the main opposition party, says he would give up his seat in Congress and renounce his parliamentary immunity (aforamiento) if he fails to take the premiership after Sunday's vote. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **A side-by-side comparison of party manifestos for the 29 November general election**

EL PAÍS sets out the main proposals of the PP, the governing Socialists (PSOE), Vox and Frente Amplio, the left-wing list fronted by former Barcelona mayor Ada Colau, for the next four-year term. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Trump announces new tariffs, including on the EU and China**

President Donald Trump has declared fresh duties on imports from the EU and China, a blow to export-reliant Germany that follows reports earlier this week that new EU tariffs were coming. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Gold, down about a third from its January record, is back on analysts' buy lists**

After a record high in January the metal has shed roughly a third of its value, and analysts are again recommending it to investors despite the slump. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Investors watch how markets handle AI-stock swings as Rheinmetall explains its strategy**

With Wall Street back from its Thanksgiving holiday, today's session shows whether the up-and-down in AI shares continues, while Rheinmetall, Germany's biggest arms maker, briefs investors on its strategy. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's conservatives win Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf but look set to lose its top posts to a left-green alliance**

A planned Green–SPD–Left working majority in the western borough would deny the CDU, which won the borough election, the mayor's office and the building portfolio, which would go to the SPD. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Almost two million square metres of Berlin office space stand empty while hundreds of thousands of homes are missing**

rbb24 examines five hurdles to converting offices into flats, using two projects, one finished within weeks and the other taking years. ([rbb24][8])

* **Students occupy a closed cafeteria in the Technical University's maths building to renovate it themselves**

After a general assembly, students at the TU Berlin took over the shuttered room, demanding investment in universities instead of budget cuts and opposing military research. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **New Zealand's ruling coalition falls short of a majority as final election results are announced**

The result follows a count that was too close to call earlier this month. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Oil heads for its first weekly gain this month as Middle East attacks on energy infrastructure and tankers threaten exports**

Bloomberg's market live blog says traders fear that escalating hostilities in the region could disrupt supplies. ([Bloomberg][11])

* **UN secretary-general hopeful Lana Nusseibeh tries to leave her Sudan record behind**

Nusseibeh, the candidate backed by the US and Russia, is lobbying in Britain to shift attention from her time as a United Arab Emirates diplomat, when she denied the country armed a militia accused of war crimes and genocide in Sudan's civil war; London's resistance is her main obstacle. ([The Guardian][12])
