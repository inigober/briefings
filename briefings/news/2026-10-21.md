# News Briefing — 21 October 2026

A Supreme Court amnesty ruling that attacks Spain's Constitutional Court and an election campaign preparing for deepfakes sit beside a tobacco-tax law that took effect by accident, a hacked Berlin administration, and Alberta's vote to stay in Canada.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The Supreme Court amnesties the procés leaders' embezzlement and accuses the Constitutional Court of interference**

Spain's Supreme Court criminal chamber has applied the amnesty law to the embezzlement convictions of the Catalan independence leaders, ordered by a Constitutional Court ruling it now publicly accuses of overreach — an institutional clash with few precedents. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The fight is no longer over the amnesty but over which court has the last word on how it is applied.

* **A fake Sánchez–Ábalos kiss previews an AI-driven 29-N campaign**

After the PP posted a doctored image of the prime minister and his ex-minister José Luis Ábalos on its profiles, leaders of the main parties are saying how they intend to use AI tools in the run-up to the 29 November general election. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Ayuso and her economy minister left a lunch with billionaire donors off the public agenda**

Madrid's regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso and finance minister Rocío Albert did not publish a January lunch with donors of the Civismo foundation, a lobby funded by ultra-wealthy Spaniards, on the eve of a regional 99% tax exemption for family firms. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **A tobacco-tax law took effect today by a ministry error**

A tax increase meant to start in 2027 is already in force because of a technical error at the federal ministry, according to dpa, and the tobacco industry is dismayed. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 A drafting slip, not a vote, has just moved a fiscal date by a year.

* **Carmakers are replacing the steering column with electronics**

Steer-by-wire systems, in which a computer rather than a mechanical shaft links steering wheel to wheels, are arriving in series cars, with Handelsblatt asking whether the end point is replacing the driver altogether. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Parliaments are getting more male, and Saxony-Anhalt's AfD is the extreme case**

The AfD's group in the Saxony-Anhalt state parliament is 37 men and two women, and the Tagesspiegel reports that women's share of seats is falling beyond that one state, with consequences for the tone of debate. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A hacked Berlin administration now has senator Bonde's ID copy in the dark web**

rbb research finds that the cyberattack on two Berlin Senate departments has exposed highly sensitive data, including a copy of senator Bonde's identity document — despite early warnings about weak IT security. ([rbb24][7])

💡 The warnings that came before the breach make this a governance failure, not only a crime story.

* **Berlin's second-oldest shopping centre reports it is fully let**

The Schloßstraße mall in Steglitz, opened in 1970, says its last retail unit is rented, going against the city's retail-vacancy trend. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **A planned S-Bahn link puts the east's only golf resort in the path of housing**

The Pankow golf resort, open since 2005 on state-owned land, faces an uncertain future because a planned S-Bahn connection has raised interest in building homes on it. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Alberta votes to stay in Canada by roughly three to one**

In a provincial referendum on starting a process towards independence, about 76% of the nearly 1.9m votes counted backed staying, a decisive win for prime minister Mark Carney that exposes divisions inside the separatist movement. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Police raid the Guinea-Bissau researchers RFK Jr has praised and seize hepatitis B vaccine doses**

Judicial police searched the offices of vaccine researchers behind a US-funded trial on African newborns — backed by $1.6m from the CDC, matched by the Pershing Square Foundation — that has been the subject of an ongoing dispute. ([The Guardian][11])

💡 The hepatitis B birth-dose trial that the US health secretary has touted is now a police matter in the country where it is being run.

* **Netanyahu is pressing to resume strikes on Iran**

Israel's military drew up plans for an extensive operation earlier this month but held off after Donald Trump ruled out an attack before the US midterm elections. ([Financial Times][12])
