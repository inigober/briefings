# News Briefing — 07 November 2026

Incumbents are under strain on every front today: South Africa's ANC loses ground at the ballot box, Trump reshuffles his team after the midterm rout, and Spain's government finds good economic data failing to buy it votes before 29 November.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A judge refuses to charge ex-prime minister Zapatero with embezzlement over seized jewels, weakening the case**

Judge Calama rejected Anticorruption prosecutors' bid to accuse the former Socialist premier José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero of malversación over three jewellery sets worth more than €1m, leaving a tax charge that hinges on proving the stones were cut after May 2021 and a smuggling charge resting on suspicion. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Inflation is blunting the electoral payoff of Spain's strong growth and record employment**

Although the government boasts of leading Europe on growth and of its economy minister, Carlos Cuerpo, being the best-rated cabinet member, voters' view of their situation and prospects is souring as prices rise ahead of the 29 November election. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **European trade unions break with the global union federation to back Yolanda Díaz for the top job at the International Labour Organization**

The European Trade Union Confederation is angry that the global ITUC endorsed the incumbent, Togo's Gilbert Houngbo, and is working to rally votes for Spain's candidate Díaz at the UN labour agency. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Ilse Aigner is all but confirmed as the conservatives' candidate for federal president, drawing Green criticism of Merz**

The Bavarian CSU politician looks set to become the first woman nominated for Germany's largely ceremonial head-of-state post, with an official announcement expected Monday and the Greens attacking how Chancellor Friedrich Merz handled the choice. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Amazon and Rewe are racing to deliver groceries faster as online food shopping grows**

Germany's online grocery market, long dominated by supermarkets' physical stores, is being pushed toward ultra-fast delivery by the e-commerce giant and the retail chain. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Photos of the May 2024 aurora may explain why some northern lights glow red**

Researchers analysing pictures taken during the geomagnetic storm that pushed polar lights far south have found clues to how the rarer red aurora forms. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's Linke demands that its new state MP Vanessa Emde give up her seat**

Emde has distanced herself from the party that elected her and acts on her own, a dispute that has been weighing on talks to form a new Berlin government, and the leadership has now moved to force the issue. ([rbb24][7])

* **A conveyor belt at Berlin's Moabit combined heat-and-power plant burns for the second time in a week**

Firefighters were deployed in large numbers and the extinguishing work dragged on at the plant that supplies heat to the city. ([rbb24][8])

* **Brandenburg tops up its road-salt reserves after a harsh, snowy winter**

Last winter's unusually heavy gritting use has led the neighbouring state's winter-service crews to buy more salt for the coming season; this is a regional item, as Berlin-proper coverage was thin today. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump names immigration hardliner Stephen Miller his chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles days after the Republican midterm defeat**

Miller, the architect of the president's restrictive immigration policy, takes over the White House's day-to-day running from Wiles in a pick that signals doubling down rather than moderation. ([Financial Times][10])

* **South Africa's ANC heads for a bruising local-election defeat, falling below a third of the national vote**

With more than 80% of ballots counted, the party of Nelson Mandela that has led every national government since apartheid ended in 1994 is nearly 14 percentage points below its 2021 result, hurt by unemployment, crime, corruption and poor local services. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Beijing vows to retaliate against US sanctions on two Chinese companies**

China's Commerce Ministry said it strongly opposes the penalties, imposed over the firms' dealings with a third party, and reserves the right to take "necessary measures" to protect its interests. ([Bloomberg][12])
