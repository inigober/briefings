# News Briefing — 11 July 2026

Extreme heat and predictable wildfire failures in Spain, China's industrial squeeze on German manufacturing, and an Apple–OpenAI lawsuit frame a day when climate stress, election demographics and AI governance collide across Europe and the Americas.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Bishops' conference president ruptures church–government détente**

eldiario.es reports Luis Argüello accused Pedro Sánchez's government of loading Pope Leo XIV's discourse and called ministers a "band of thieves," prompting Equality Minister Ana Redondo to say the Church had "loaded" the papal message. Negotiators on both sides had been trying to lower tensions informally before the outburst. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Fire engineer says Almería catastrophe was foreseeable, not freak**

EL PAÍS interviews Víctor Resco, who argues Europe now loses more lives to forest fires than to terrorism and that Almería's terrain and dry spring made this week's tragedy predictable. He says prevention and evacuation planning lag behind a new meteorological pattern of continuous heat after an abnormally dry season. ([EL PAÍS][2])

💡 When specialists frame wildfire deaths as a planning failure rather than an act of God, Spain's climate-adaptation debate shifts from emergency response to land-use liability.

* **Spain reach World Cup semifinals for first time in 16 years**

Mikel Merino scored in the 88th minute as La Roja beat Belgium 2–1 in Atlanta, setting up a Tuesday clash with France. eldiario.es notes the win came without Pedri in the starting lineup as coach Luis de la Fuente continued midfield experiments around Rodri. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **"China shock 2.0" hits autos, machinery and chemicals**

Handelsblatt warns the People's Republic is graduating from customer to rival across German export pillars, with BYD and other manufacturers now competing directly in Europe. Editors say Berlin spent decades profiting from China's rise and must now choose between protection and deeper industrial retooling. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Tens of thousands file complaints over damaged or late parcels**

Handelsblatt reports consumer agencies logged a surge in grievances as e-commerce volumes rise and shoppers receive broken electronics, kitchen goods and books. The volume adds pressure on Deutsche Post and rival carriers already facing scrutiny over delivery reliability. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **AI agent completes 60-hour medical data review in 40 minutes**

Tagesspiegel profiles a biomedical research tool that automates literature and dataset screening, letting scientists focus on hypothesis design rather than manual curation. Developers say the agent is being tested in university labs before wider clinical deployment. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Man holds woman hostage in south Berlin supermarket**

Tagesspiegel reports police mounted a large operation in southern Berlin from late Friday after a man barricaded himself with a female hostage inside a grocery store. Authorities had released few details on motive or injuries by Saturday morning as negotiators remained on scene. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **More than 1.4 million Berlin residents cannot vote in September election**

rbb24 says the capital will have its largest eligible electorate since 1990 yet also a record number of residents without voting rights, including non-citizens and disenfranchised EU nationals. Lowering the voting age to 16 will add roughly 50,000 new voters but barely shifts the non-voter share. ([rbb24][8])

💡 When one in three adult residents lacks a ballot, Berlin's September election will decide city policy for a population far larger than the electorate that chooses it.

* **Bundestag tightens e-scooter rental liability after accidents**

rbb24 reports parliament approved rules making hire firms share liability when riders cause injury or property damage, easing victims' path to compensation. Operators must carry insurance covering third-party harm rather than leaving claims solely with individual riders. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Apple sues OpenAI alleging theft of confidential product information**

The Financial Times says Apple filed suit claiming OpenAI recruited staff who took proprietary data about unreleased devices and software, marking a breakdown in a once-cordial Silicon Valley relationship. The case lands as both companies compete to embed generative AI in consumer hardware. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Venezuela quake death toll passes 4,000 as recovery scale becomes clear**

The Guardian reports authorities now count at least 4,118 dead and 16,740 injured from twin coastal earthquakes on 24 June, with thousands still missing. President Delcy Rodríguez and UN agencies are appealing for international financial help as entire districts remain flattened in La Guaira state. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Mexico probes whether FBI lied about role in Sinaloa cartel leader's capture**

The Guardian says prosecutors opened an inquiry after local reporting suggested US agents participated inside Mexican territory in the 2024 seizure of Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada García, contrary to Washington's public denials. If confirmed, the operation would amount to a sovereignty breach in a country already strained by cross-border drug enforcement. ([The Guardian][12])
