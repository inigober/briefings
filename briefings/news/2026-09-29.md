# News Briefing — 29 September 2026

Spain's housing text now has dates and a sunset, Curevac is closing plants the Bund helped fund, and the world file is AI money plus a Brazilian betting ban.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The housing draft now has a 2028 sunset and two-year extensions**

Spain's housing ministry has put numbers on the Tuesday decree: evictions of vulnerable tenants would be blocked only until 2028, existing leases would get a two-year extension, rent updates would be capped at 2%, and tenants in stressed zones would get an income-tax break — short of the indefinite contracts the Sol camp wants. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 A dated shield is easier to pass than an open-ended one; it is also easier to let expire.

* **The fund that evicted Maricarmen will let her return as a tenant**

Urbagestión, the landlord that put 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal on the street, has agreed with her representatives that she can go back to the Retiro flat on a new urban-leases contract, paying no more than 30% of her income; she still has to accept the deal. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Junts is threatening to walk over indefinite leases and small landlords**

Sumar is pushing Pedro Sánchez's government to write in the tenants' union's main demand — leases that roll on indefinitely — while Junts, the Catalan party whose votes the minority government needs, says it will break if the plan also covers small owners who evict. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Curevac is closing plants the state helped pay for**

Curevac, the Mainz vaccine firm, is shutting three sites and cutting about 1,800 jobs, with the Tübingen factory in the frame because hundreds of millions in public money went into it — a ledger of the Bund as industrial investor, not only a company story. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The question the briefing poses is whether the state can pick firms if it cannot keep the plants open.

* **The Bundestag president is threatening to throw out whole factions**

Julia Klöckner, who chairs the Bundestag, has said that if disruption by AfD and Left MPs escalates she will eject the entire parliamentary group, not only the member at the microphone. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Klingbeil wants the self-employed and civil servants in the statutory pension**

Finance minister Lars Klingbeil is arguing that Selbstständige and Beamte — groups now largely outside the pay-as-you-go pension — should be brought into the gesetzliche Rente, a widening of the system the coalition has so far treated as too hard. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Greens have lost the Kollwitzkiez after twenty years**

In the family streets of south-west Prenzlauer Berg, Die Linke took the second votes and ended a two-decade Green hold on a neighbourhood that had become a shorthand for the party's urban base. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Berlin is treating toilet queues as an equality problem**

Women wait longer at public WCs than men; rbb24 frames that as potty parity — a design and equality issue — and reports what the city says it will do about it. ([rbb24][8])

* **Autumn stink bugs are moving into flats for the winter**

Several shield-bug species are coming indoors with the colder nights and staying until spring; Berliner Zeitung walks through which they are and how to get them out without a can of spray. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **Samsung is putting $1bn into AI infrastructure via Helix**

Samsung and affiliates are joining the race to finance the buildings and kit that train and run models, with a $1bn deal in Helix — a US infrastructure vehicle — rather than another chip-fab headline. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Lula has banned fixed-odds sports betting a week before Brazil votes**

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has signed an order ending fixed-odds sports bets, a product he says has pushed families into debt, in a move polls suggest is popular days before the first round. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Anthropic's IPO filing warns of existential risk — and an $8bn loss**

The Claude-maker's long-awaited S1 tells investors the company lost $8bn last year on $4.6bn of revenue and, in the same prospectus, flags "existential risks to humanity" as a business disclosure. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 The safety case is now part of the capital-markets story, not a separate essay.
