# News Briefing — 9 August 2026

A century-waited solar eclipse, renewed Italy–Spain border friction, and Rheinmetall's chief under guard frame a day when Berlin probes Russian espionage at a cultural institute and Beijing's state-backed financiers fuel an AI listings rush.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Millions of Spaniards prepare for 12 August total solar eclipse**

Spain will witness its first peninsular total eclipse since 1905 on 12 August, when the Moon's shadow sweeps from Galicia to the Mediterranean at sunset — an event authorities expect to draw millions to inland viewing sites already booked solid months ahead. The phenomenon, dubbed "the day of two nights," poses a logistical test for towns with few hotel beds and little experience hosting astronomy tourists. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Greenland warns Trump-linked oil firm is drilling without a licence**

Greenland's government says a US oil company linked to Donald Trump has landed exploration equipment in a remote Arctic zone without authorisation, as the president renews threats to acquire the Danish territory. The stand-off turns Washington's Greenland rhetoric into a concrete sovereignty dispute over who controls mineral rights beneath the ice cap. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Three VOX-led regions refuse to host unaccompanied migrant minors from Ceuta**

Regional vice-presidents from Extremadura, Aragón and Castilla y León told Spain's youth minister they will skip an August 13 coordination meeting and will not take in unaccompanied minors evacuated from Ceuta, deepening a territorial split over who bears the cost of the exclave crisis. The refusal tests Madrid's ability to redistribute arrivals when conservative-led communities reject central-government appeals. ([La Vanguardia][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Rheinmetall chief operates under personal protection after assassination plots**

Armin Papperger, CEO of Germany's largest arms manufacturer, is living under heightened security after authorities uncovered alleged Russian murder plots and protesters gathered outside his home, yet he told Handelsblatt he will not step back from expanding Rheinmetall's role arming Ukraine. The case shows how defence-industry executives have become individual targets in Europe's hybrid-security environment. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Turkey seeks to widen new defence pact beyond Pakistan and Saudi Arabia**

Ankara is building a trilateral military alliance with Pakistan and Saudi Arabia modelled on NATO's mutual-defence logic and wants additional countries to join, arguing regional powers must solve security problems without relying on outside great powers. The initiative matters for Berlin because it could redraw Middle Eastern alignments just as Turkey remains a NATO member with strained ties to the alliance's core. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Psychologists probe why total eclipses leave lifelong emotional marks**

Researchers studying the 12 August eclipse say witnesses of totality often describe decades-later awe, suggesting rare celestial events trigger a distinct neurological response that science is only beginning to map. The finding feeds Germany's public-education push as millions prepare to travel south for a spectacle many will never see again in their lifetimes. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **French intelligence fuels calls to act against Russian House in Berlin**

New evidence from France's interior ministry that Moscow's "Russian House" cultural centre in Berlin serves as cover for intelligence operations has prompted CDU, SPD and Green politicians to demand consequences, while AfD and BSW resist closure. The dispute tests whether Berlin will treat a long-controversial Soviet-era institution as a diplomatic venue or a security liability. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Retinal implant lets blind Berlin mother read again after 20 years**

A mother who lost her sight two decades ago regained enough vision to read after surgeons implanted a chip on her retina linked to a camera-equipped headset, part of a trial comparing German and Chinese prosthetic programmes. The case illustrates how experimental neurotechnology is moving from laboratory consent forms into everyday family life in the capital's hospitals. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **Only one in four trains on Berlin–Hamburg line runs on time**

Despite months of renovation on the Berlin–Hamburg corridor, Deutsche Bahn data show just a quarter of services met punctuality targets last week, frustrating commuters on one of Germany's busiest intercity links. The figures sharpen a pre-election debate over whether the state-owned operator can deliver reliability before Berlin votes in September. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **State-backed CICC dominates China's AI IPO pipeline**

China International Capital Corporation has become the lead financier behind a wave of artificial-intelligence listings as Beijing races US capital markets to fund domestic model builders, the Financial Times reports. The bank's role shows how China's tech competition with Washington now runs through securities underwriting as much as through fabs and cloud capacity. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Ukrainian evangelicals court MAGA voters to sustain US aid**

Ukrainian religious activists are touring American churches to persuade evangelical conservatives — a core Donald Trump constituency — that supporting Kyiv aligns with their faith, according to Nikkei Asia. The outreach matters because waning Republican enthusiasm for Ukraine funding could outlast any single White House occupant. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Texas police officer arrested in Mexico over triple homicide at border**

Mexican authorities detained 35-year-old Texas officer Chad Eberle at the border on warrants accusing him of killing three people and critically wounding the mother of his child during a custody dispute in Saltillo, Coahuila. The case highlights how cross-border family violence can trigger simultaneous criminal proceedings in both countries. ([The Guardian][12])
