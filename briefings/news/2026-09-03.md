# News Briefing — 3 September 2026

Leaked files and reciprocal closures are how states are answering last week's crossings and drones; Berlin's fight is a hospital and a ballot, Asia's is chips and ore.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A police file for the Audiencia Nacional points at Morocco's gendarmes**

Spain's immigration-police unit, Cenif, has given investigating judge María Tardón a 51-page report that treats Moroccan security agents — not a smuggling mafia — as the organisers of the late-July rush into Ceuta, without naming Rabat's government or spy services. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The leak turns a street crisis into a document the cabinet says it does not accept.

* **Sánchez tells Congress Madrid still has no proof Morocco organised the crossing**

After the Interior leak, Pedro Sánchez appears in Parliament without allies and repeats that the government lacks evidence Rabat planned the jump — while the cabinet is at odds with its own police and the Audiencia Nacional judge. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Unpublished letters show Francis telling a Spanish bishop not to yield to Opus Dei**

EL PAÍS has two notes from the late Argentine pope to Barbastro's bishop, Ángel Pérez Pueyo, backing him in the fight over the Torreciudad shrine and warning against the "mafia intrigues" of Opus Dei, the conservative Catholic movement founded in Spain. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Gas-storage operators want a rule change to refill 'historically low' tanks**

Germany's underground gas stores — the winter buffer built after Russian pipeline swing disappeared — are at a historically low fill, and the operators want the regulator to force more injection; the economy ministry is holding back. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 Asking for a regulatory shove is an admission the winter buffer is no longer filling itself.

* **Lavrov says Russia will close the Goethe-Institut**

After Berlin named Moscow for the Leipzig drone and shut the Russian House, foreign minister Sergei Lavrov says Russia's branches of Germany's cultural institute will be closed, calling the German measures unfounded. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Almost half of users fail at the login for Germany's digital pension overview**

The Digitale Rentenübersicht, the federal portal meant to show workers their future pension in one place, is used by only about 6% of those eligible, and a study finds nearly one in two who try already fail at registration. ([Berliner Zeitung][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Charité nurses have started a three-day warning strike**

Nursing staff at the Charité, Berlin's university hospital, have begun a three-day warning strike — a short, announced stoppage short of an indefinite walkout. ([rbb24][7])

* **Berlin's election chief says disinformation has reached a 'new dimension'**

Stephan Bröchler, the official who runs the 20 September Abgeordnetenhaus vote, says coordinated falsehoods now threaten the legitimacy of the count and that he is talking to social-media platforms. ([Die Zeit][8])

* **A signal failure at Baumschulenweg is delaying S-Bahn lines, including the airport route**

A signal fault at Baumschulenweg, the Treptow junction on the southeastern S-Bahn, is forcing morning diversions and delays on several lines, including trains toward the airport. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Taiwan has spent six years hunting China's undercover chip labs**

Unpublished government data compiled by Rest of World show Taipei's long crackdown on Chinese firms accused of hiding their ownership while hiring Taiwanese chip engineers and chasing restricted process know-how. ([Rest of World][10])

💡 The useful number is not one raid; it is six years of covert recruitment treated as a supply-chain file.

* **Uber is allying with driver unions to slow the robotaxi rollout**

The ride-hailing firm, after years of fighting its own drivers, is now backing unions in a bid to delay robotaxis — driverless cabs that would erase the contractor model it built. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Malaysia is drawing critical-minerals interest as China's export curbs loom**

Buyers looking past Beijing's grip on refining are treating Malaysia as an alternative source of critical minerals, ahead of tighter Chinese export controls. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
