# News Briefing — 8 October 2026

Maricarmen Abascal's death has closed Spain's housing fortnight, Germany is counting tax breaks the budget did not admit, and China is pouring AI capacity into the energy hinterland.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Maricarmen died hours before the eight-year lease**

María del Carmen Abascal, the 87-year-old Madrid tenant whose stretcher eviction set off Spain's housing revolt, died on Wednesday evening at Hospital Gregorio Marañón — hours before she was due to sign an eight-year lease back into the Retiro flat Urbagestión had thrown her out of. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The landlord reversed the eviction; the calendar did not.

* **Her case is why Spain is going to the polls**

In a fortnight an anonymous Retiro neighbour became the name on an emergency housing decree and the reason Pedro Sánchez dissolved parliament after the texts fell — the path eldiario.es traces from Alcalde Sainz de Baranda to the 29 November vote. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Sol's midnight line: no more Maricarmens**

Before dawn the Puerta del Sol housing camp, the tent city that grew from her eviction, marched on the warning that hers must be the last stretcher case Spain accepts. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Tax breaks are €76.5bn — more than the government booked**

A new study of Germany's tax subsidies — the special rates and exemptions that never appear as spending — puts the bill at €76.5bn, higher than Berlin has admitted, and economists are using it to attack the Tankrabatt fuel-duty cut that took effect this month. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The cut at the pump is being filed with the hidden line-items, not with "relief".

* **Kahla porcelain is closing after 182 years**

Kahla, the Thuringian china works, will stop production at year-end after a search for a buyer failed — the end of a factory that has been firing porcelain since 1844. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

* **The BGH is hearing who pays for cookies you never accepted**

Germany's Federal Court of Justice is asking whether firms can be held liable for storing tracking cookies on other people's sites without consent — a case that sits under the EU's long fight over cookie walls. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The left talks now include a rule to expel MPs over antisemitism**

After three days of drafting, Die Linke, the Greens and the SPD — the parties exploring a Berlin government — are due to issue a joint statement that would let a parliamentary group expel a member over antisemitism, then test whether a left coalition can be built on anything else. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Wild boars are wrecking graves in Spandau**

Wild boars have been tearing up In den Kisseln, Berlin's largest cemetery in Spandau; mourners describe ruined family plots, and the district councillor says the mess has more authors than the pigs. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **The Eisbären are into the CHL last 16**

Berlin's DEL side beat Salzburg away and reached the last sixteen of the Champions Hockey League. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **China is building the AI boom in the energy hinterland**

The first part of an FT series follows how Inner Mongolia — coal- and wind-rich, far from the coast — has become the engine room for China's AI data-centre build. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The constraint is power and empty land, not another coastal cluster.

* **Canada will keep mental illness out of assisted dying**

Ottawa will legislate to stop people whose only condition is mental illness from using Maid, Canada's doctor-assisted death law — making permanent a ban that had been due to lapse. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Thai floods are now a planning assumption for carmakers**

Toyota, Honda and other producers are treating repeated flooding in Thailand as a "new normal" for plants that still anchor a large slice of Asian car output. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
