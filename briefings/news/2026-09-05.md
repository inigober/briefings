# News Briefing — 5 September 2026

Ceuta is still crowding out Spain's housing calendar, German firms are pricing hybrid attacks and a shrinking water table, and AI is showing up as Japanese power plants, American license-plate cameras, and a farm-poisoning toll.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The housing decree is frozen by Podemos, Junts — and Ceuta**

The government's draft housing decree — a social package that would revive two-year extensions on existing rents — is still blocked: Podemos and Junts have rejected it, and the Ceuta crisis has pushed the political autumn's big social announcements off the calendar. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The enclave is no longer only a border story; it is eating the legislative week.

* **Judge Tardón told police not to send the Ceuta file to Interior**

Investigating judge María Tardón, who is handling the late-July rush into the North African enclave, phoned police ten days after commissioning their analysis and told them not to pass the file to the Interior Ministry — another clash between the bench and Pedro Sánchez's cabinet over who may see the evidence. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Former staff have taken Julio Iglesias to the Audiencia Nacional**

Rebeca and Laura, who worked in the singer's Dominican and Bahamas houses in 2021, have filed a complaint at the Audiencia Nacional — Spain's court for complex and cross-border crime — alleging forced labour, sexual offences and degrading treatment. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **One in five German firms now reports a hybrid threat**

About one in five companies says it has felt cyberattacks, disinformation, espionage or sabotage, and researchers warn the pressure is rising on power and water systems while protection still lags. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Germany's water deficit is being blamed on heat, not missing rain**

Groundwater and surface reserves are still shrinking, and researchers say the driver is rising temperatures rather than a simple shortfall of rainfall. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

💡 The water story is a heat-balance problem, not a dry-spell anecdote.

* **The town that elected Germany's first AfD mayor now talks about 60%**

In Raguhn-Jessnitz, the Saxony-Anhalt town that chose the party's first mayor, residents treat a result above 60% in Sunday's Landtag vote as the local baseline. ([EL PAÍS][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The BSI has turned Berlin's state-network leak into a phishing warning**

After data escaped from the hacked Landesnetz, the BSI — the federal cyber-security office — has warned of targeted phishing and flagged the risk around the 20 September Abgeordnetenhaus election. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Berlin wants more foster homes after 2,001 children in 2025**

The city's youth office says 2,001 children lived with Pflegefamilien last year and that it still needs more families, especially for one age group. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **BFC Dynamo has done the required job in the Berlin cup**

The former East Berlin club has seen off Viktoria Mitte in the Landespokal's second round — a Pflichtaufgabe, the tie a higher-tier side is expected to win. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Japan's AI data centres are on course to quadruple by 2033**

The planned fleet is being backed by about $60 billion of investment — a power-and-land bet that treats compute as industrial policy, not a cloud sidebar. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Republican governors are turning on Flock's license-plate cameras**

US state executives are cracking down on Flock, an AI surveillance startup bankrolled by Trump donors, as a conservative revolt against automated street monitoring gathers. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 The pushback is coming from Republican statehouses, not only from privacy NGOs.

* **Almost half of the world's farmers are poisoned by pesticides each year**

A Pesticide Action Network study puts unintentional acute cases at roughly 400 million a year and deaths at about 11,000, with India accounting for nearly 60% of the fatalities. ([The Guardian][12])
