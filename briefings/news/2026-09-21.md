# News Briefing — 21 September 2026

Sunday's German results, Spain's amnesty docket at the Constitutional Court, and a sodium-battery race China already leads set the day's frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's Constitutional Court takes up the amnesty the Supreme Court blocked**

Spain's Constitutional Court starts this Wednesday on whether to override the Supreme Court's reading of the 2024 amnesty law that kept it from Carles Puigdemont and the rest of the 2017 Catalan independence leadership; Jordi Turull's challenge is the test case. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Investigating judge Pablo Llarena is still holding Puigdemont until the court speaks — the last lock on a two-year-old statute.

* **The 35-hour week is spreading through regional and local government**

A shorter public-sector week is moving through Spain's autonomous and local administrations, with Catalonia already negotiating the cut while Madrid and Galicia staff threaten action where it has not arrived. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Eating disorders in women over 50 are being missed**

Clinicians say eating disorders in women past 50 are under-diagnosed because the condition is still treated as an adolescent problem — a generation that quietly stopped eating dinner. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Sunday's votes leave Merz as a chancellor without a project**

Landtag results in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and the Berlin House vote, Handelsblatt argues, lock Chancellor Friedrich Merz into a declining chancellorship: he intends to carry on, and has no idea to offer. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The night is being read as the start of a holding operation, not a reset.

* **The Dax opens the week watching the election tape**

German stocks start the new trading week asking whether the Dax can recover, with Sunday's state-election results expected to move prices during the day — especially in one sector. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Hidden GDR street photos have surfaced in Schöneweide**

Stiftung Reinbeckhallen is showing 1970s East German street photography — everyday pictures by a forgotten artist from Karow that sat in a home for fifty years. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Die Linke has a historic win in Berlin — and a new face in Elif Eralp**

Berlin's Left, on the floor two years ago, has a historic result in the Abgeordnetenhaus vote; a Tagesspiegel analysis asks how Elif Eralp's campaign did it and what would follow if the party has to govern. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The Senate plans to fell 32 trees on Torstraße without a permit**

After Mitte's district office refused permission, the Senate said it does not need a permit to take down 32 trees on Torstraße. ([The Berliner][8])

* **Galleries are leaving as culture budgets shrink**

Berlin's art editor argues the city is still not funding its "daft, unpredictable" cultural life, with galleries exiting and cuts shaping what remains. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Western firms are losing the sodium-battery race to China**

Sodium-ion cells, which use abundant materials rather than scarce battery metals, are the route out of rare-earth dependence for EVs and storage — and Chinese producers are already ahead. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The cheap-metal battery is becoming another industrial race the West is late to.

* **Eighteen people have been arrested over Haiti's 2021 presidential assassination**

US officials say 18 people are in custody over the killing of Haitian president Jovenel Moïse, shot at home in July 2021 by Colombian mercenaries while his guards did not intervene — a murder that helped unravel the state. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Japan wants factory machines to generate physical AI data**

Tokyo plans to use industrial machinery as a source of real-world training data for "physical AI" — models that learn from plants and tools, not only from text. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
