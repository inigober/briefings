# News Briefing — 27 October 2026

Street unrest in Barcelona, a PP–Vox pact for Spain's Senate, and elections in Brazil and Israel frame an edition about how political rivalry is playing out beyond the ballot box.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Barcelona's riots were organised online, and police are now identifying youths ahead of repeat calls**

After Saturday's riots and looting in central Barcelona ended with 130 arrests, 76 of them minors, Catalonia's Mossos d'Esquadra police identified young people at plaça Espanya on Monday, where a new social-media call followed the same pattern, while prosecutors seek to intern six minors. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Vox trades its own Senate lists for "independent" slots on the PP's tickets**

The far-right Vox has given up running in 14 provinces for the 29 November Senate vote in exchange for placing "independent" candidates on the People's Party (PP) lists, among them a former press chief of Vox's Ortega-Smith and a former regional minister under Valencia's Carlos Mazón. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **A study maps how Spain's right and far right are fighting for control of education**

Parties, foundations, religious groups, media and online platforms, from Vox to the CEU San Pablo University and the conservative campaign group HazteOír, work jointly, if not centrally, to "control and steer education for their ends", according to a study mapping the movement. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Allianz Trade expects German insolvencies to stay near record levels through 2027**

The credit insurer Allianz Trade forecasts almost 26,000 company insolvencies in Germany both this year and next, though it sees a reversal of the trend taking shape. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Mercedes reportedly offers production staff six-figure severance packages**

Handelsblatt's morning podcast reports that Mercedes-Benz, already running a rescue plan after weaker sales and margins, is offering production workers severance payments in six figures. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Pistorius and four defence ministers call for a "stronger Europe for a stronger NATO"**

In a joint guest essay, Germany's defence minister Boris Pistorius and four European counterparts argue that Europe must take more responsibility for its own security, calling it a historic duty. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Theodor Seidel, judge in the Mielke trial, dies aged 95**

Seidel was the judge in the trial of Erich Mielke, the long-time head of East Germany's Stasi secret police, according to rbb24's obituary. ([rbb24][7])

* **Hertha and Union Berlin both have German Cup ties this week**

Hertha BSC travel 450 kilometres to fourth-tier Jeddeloh II with roughly half the stadium expected in Berlin colours, while Union Berlin meet 1. FC Köln for the second time in five days. ([rbb24][8], [rbb24][9])

* **Redmann warns of "French conditions" ahead of 31 October protests**

CDU politician Jan Redmann warned of "French conditions" before protests planned for 31 October, in a debate that rbb24 links to Halloween masks and crime. ([rbb24][10])

## World 🌐

* **Israel votes in a tight election that could again end in deadlock**

Voting began for the 120-seat Knesset in a contest billed as decisive for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's future, with a close race that risks another stalemate complicating coalition-building. ([Handelsblatt][11])

* **Bolsonaro's Liberal Party accepts defeat in Brazil as Trump calls the result "rigged"**

Jair Bolsonaro's party has conceded the presidential runoff won by Lula, while US President Donald Trump questioned the result's integrity over an extension of voting hours. ([Financial Times][12])

* **Sudan's paramilitary strikes five power stations with drones**

The Rapid Support Forces, fighting Sudan's army in a civil war, hit two power stations each in Al-Jazira and Gedaref and one in White Nile, causing mass blackouts across the army-held east, a government official said. ([The Guardian][13])
