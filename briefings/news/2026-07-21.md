# News Briefing — 21 July 2026

Spain pivots from World Cup euphoria to judicial fallout and patronage probes, Germany reopens ethics debates after Spahn's exit, Berlin records a drug-death peak ahead of autumn elections, and world markets weigh Chinese AI listings against Middle East shipping risks and Latin American democratic retreat.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Plus Ultra executives implicate Zapatero in rescue commission as they turn state's witness**

eldiario.es says Julio Martínez Martínez and fellow directors told a judge the former prime minister offered to influence the €53m public loan and that a 1% fee was paid through dozens of sham invoices from three shell companies. The collaboration deal marks a new phase in a scandal that has shadowed Sánchez's circle without yet producing charges against the ex-leader. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When bankrupt airline executives trade testimony for leniency and name a former premier at the centre of the rescue, the case stops being partisan noise and becomes a test of whether Spain's judiciary can pursue political proximity without a formal indictment.

* **Nacho Cano's bankrupt musical keeps touring on PP-backed public contracts**

eldiario.es reports Productions Malinche SL owes Ifema more than €1m and the social-security agency €230,000, yet an instrumental version of "Malinche Symphonic" has played in Málaga, Pozuelo, Salamanca and Extremadura on contracts awarded by conservative regional governments. The producer even opened a Mexican subsidiary in Florida while winding down the failed Madrid run, raising questions about how insolvent cultural ventures keep accessing public stages. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Madrid's new children's court opens with one judge facing 3,751 cases**

EL PAÍS spent a day in the capital's first dedicated youth-violence tribunal, created to handle abuse against minors but already overwhelmed from day one with understaffing and inherited backlogs. The snapshot illustrates how Spain legislates specialised justice faster than it funds the personnel to deliver it. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Spahn resignation reignites national debate over banning surrogacy**

Handelsblatt says Jens Spahn's departure as CDU/CSU parliamentary leader has pushed Leihmutterschaft back to the top of the political agenda, with the practice illegal in Germany but widely practised abroad. Public opinion is split on whether altruistic arrangements should be tolerated, turning a personal scandal into a reopening of bioethics law the coalition had hoped to avoid. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Dax investors face six data points as Middle East tension clouds Tuesday trading**

Handelsblatt's market preview says fresh macro releases could give Frankfurt momentum after Monday's stabilisation, even as Hormuz-related uncertainty keeps oil and risk assets on edge. The checklist matters because German portfolios remain heavily exposed to export industrials whose earnings assumptions still price a contained Gulf conflict. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Bestselling doctor's testosterone pitch omits contested research on female libido**

Tagesspiegel reviews Sheila de Liz's new book promoting hormone creams to restore desire, arguing the popular physician skips a fraught chapter of clinical history on off-label testosterone use in women. The dispute highlights how wellness publishing can outrun evidence as Germany's ageing demographics make sexual-health products a growth category. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Drug-death memorial marks record toll as harm-reduction groups warn of young victims**

Tagesspiegel says nationwide overdose fatalities have reached a new high, with Berlin sharing the trend as activists gather to remember the dead. Organisers argue many casualties are young and risk-taking, pressing the city to expand supervised consumption and outreach rather than treating the crisis as a marginal street issue. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Guide maps who can vote in Berlin's September elections beyond German passport holders**

rbb24 explains that EU citizens may cast ballots in district assemblies, while refugees and other residents without citizenship still have pathways to political participation depending on status and registration. With roughly 1.4 million adult residents barred from the state parliament vote, the explainer clarifies where legal voice remains ahead of a tight Abgeordnetenhaus race. ([rbb24][8])

* **Queer advocacy group logs 4,000 hate comments in two weeks after launching safety project**

rbb24 reports the Lesben- und Schwulenverband recorded a surge of online abuse as it rolled out "Queersafe", a digital-violence support programme, against a backdrop of rising federally documented queer-hostile offences. The spike suggests Berlin's public queer visibility continues to attract coordinated harassment even as institutions expand protection services. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Moonshot AI plans Hong Kong IPO as Kimi K3 model rattles Silicon Valley benchmarks**

Nikkei Asia says the Chinese start-up is preparing a listing after its latest large language model undercut US rivals on cost and performance, reviving investor debate over whether Washington's chip curbs can slow Beijing's application-layer champions. A successful float would test whether global capital still funds Chinese AI champions despite geopolitical friction. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

💡 When a Chinese lab ships a frontier model that spooks Valley incumbents and immediately files for Hong Kong equity, AI competition shifts from export-control chess to capital-markets arbitrage.

* **Nicaragua's Ortega says country will hold no further elections**

The Guardian reports the 80-year-old president told supporters he will work with his loyal congress to pass laws "building a wall" against the opposition, formalising a de facto one-party state after years of arrests and exiles. The announcement removes even the ritual of contested ballots in a country Washington once treated as a leftist experiment rather than an autocracy. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Houthis declare naval blockade on Saudi Arabia as Red Sea routes stay volatile**

Foreign Policy says the Yemeni movement's threat to interdict Saudi shipping adds another chokepoint beyond Hormuz, with energy traders already pricing prolonged disruption. Riyadh's ability to keep crude flowing now depends on escorts and redundancy routes as non-state actors exploit a widening Gulf war. ([Foreign Policy][12])
