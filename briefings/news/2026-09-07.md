# News Briefing — 7 September 2026

A first-time right-wing majority in Spanish polls, a second darknet dump from Berlin's state network, and a $54bn Chinese bank recap set the frame as labour shortages and US debt crowd the rest of the page.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **PP and Vox have broken 50% together for the first time**

A new poll puts the conservative PP and far-right Vox above half of the vote for the first time, in the middle of the Ceuta crisis — a modest swing that still lifts the right's ceiling, while PSOE, Sumar and Podemos together sit at 36.1%. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The enclave shock is now an electoral fact, not only a border story.

* **Catalonia's police say they have no file on who ordered a teachers' assembly to be spied on**

The Mossos d'Esquadra — Catalonia's police — have told a judge they keep no "documentation" on who ordered two information officers to infiltrate a Barcelona teachers' meeting that was preparing a strike, citing the agents' confidentiality. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **A Burgos PP president's firm has run weddings in a protected barn for seven years without a licence**

The Granero de San Francisco, tied to the PP president of Burgos province and its Diputación, was licensed only for farm work if a roof and walls were fixed; the works were never done, and since 2019 the site has hosted weddings and concerts in an environmentally protected zone. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **German firms could be short more than 700,000 skilled workers by 2029**

The IW, a Cologne employers' institute, says the labour gap will widen across sectors — from shop-floor sales to care — as firms already struggle to hire. ([Die Zeit][4])

* **A new PISA round is about to test whether Germany's schools have turned around**

Every three years the OECD tests 15-year-olds on whether they have learned "for life"; Germany last scored poorly, and a fresh release will show if the slide has stopped. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Heatwaves and fires are now an air-quality problem far from the flames**

Toxic smoke and rising ozone mean hot, fire-heavy spells are degrading the air even in places nowhere near a burn site. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A new Landesnetz dump has hit the Senate building department**

After the hack of Berlin's state network, more files appeared on the darknet on Friday; staff at the Senatsbauverwaltung — the Senate department that runs city construction — may now face day-to-day restrictions. ([rbb24][7])

💡 The leak has moved from phishing warnings to a second drop that interrupts a ministry's work.

* **The Rheinmetall plant in Wedding has become a rearmament protest site**

The arms maker's factory in the northern district is now a local flashpoint in Germany's debate over rebuilding its weapons industry. ([The Berliner][8])

* **Three children's accounts have put a hole in the Fabian murder timeline**

In the trial over the killing of Fabian, a young man from Güstrow, three children have described a sequence that does not match the prosecution's assumed course of events — and the gaps are being treated as more than a slip. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **Beijing is injecting about $54bn into banks and insurers**

China is recapitalising the financial sector — the biggest such shot in almost twenty years — so eight institutions can keep lending and, the state hopes, put more cash into a weak stock market as growth stays soft. ([The Guardian][10])

* **US long-term rates near 5% are being read as a threat to the AI boom**

America's debt binge is starting to matter in the bond market: if long-term yields break decisively through 5%, the argument goes, the cheap-money bet behind AI investment could wobble. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 The constraint on compute spending may be the Treasury market, not only chip supply.

* **ICE has been flying Latin American deportees to Liberia, a country they had never heard of**

Three migrants told EL PAÍS near Monrovia they were detained by US Immigration and Customs Enforcement and expelled to the West African state — with no tie to it — and kept in chains for the whole journey. ([EL PAÍS][12])
