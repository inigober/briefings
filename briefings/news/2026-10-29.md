# News Briefing — 29 October 2026

A fatal Spanish rail collision traced to a control-room order frames a day about institutions under strain, from Berlin's coalition-building legal threats to Brazil's fugitive candidate and Egypt's press clampdown.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Audio shows Atocha's control centre told the AVE to resume normal speed with an Iryo stalled ahead**

In Wednesday's high-speed collision at Urda, near Toledo, recordings obtained by EL PAÍS show the Adif controller (Adif runs Spain's rail infrastructure) telling the AVE driver to "reconnect and resume normal speed" after the signalling system failed, five minutes after the Iryo driver had reported she was stopped and could not restart. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Sánchez promises a forceful response after a second train disaster in a year**

Visiting the emergency command post, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said two rail accidents in one year are "not acceptable for Spain" and pledged to establish what happened and assign responsibility, as the toll reached at least 68 dead. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Experts say Spain spends too little maintaining its high-speed network**

Citing European Commission and civil-engineer data, specialists argue Spain devotes less to conserving its rail network than other European countries do and "could and should do more", putting the funding of upkeep, not just new lines, at the centre of the debate. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Army chief says Germany must move faster to defend the Baltics**

After more than 70 German and Dutch generals toured possible flashpoints in the Baltic states, army chief Freuding said the pace of preparing NATO's eastern defence still lags, and voiced a concern about it. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **The ECB decides interest rates today as the Dax awaits earnings**

The European Central Bank, which sets borrowing costs for the euro area, announces its rate decision on Thursday, alongside a batch of quarterly results and economic indicators that German investors are watching. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Menopause is becoming a workplace topic, and a columnist asks whether talking to bosses helps**

As public debate over menopause grows, a Tagesspiegel columnist weighs whether women should now raise sleep loss and incontinence with employers, or whether open discussion revives old prejudices about women at work. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **CDU/CSU parliamentary leaders threaten constitutional suits against Left- or AfD-involving governments**

Several Union group leaders say they could go to court against possible future governments in Berlin, where the Left Party (Die Linke) is in coalition talks, and in Saxony-Anhalt, where the far-right AfD could take power. ([Berliner Zeitung][7])

* **Students blockade the Free University's Otto Suhr Institute**

Students blocked the Otto Suhr Institute, the Free University's political-science department, in the latest campus disruption in the capital. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin's cold-weather shelters are almost full already**

Even in moderate October temperatures, the city's emergency shelter beds for homeless people are nearly all occupied, a warning sign for the colder months ahead. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Flávio Bolsonaro leaves Brazil a day after saying he never considered it**

The losing candidate in Brazil's presidential runoff, a son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, left the country while under a money-laundering investigation, according to the Financial Times. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Egypt convicts Mada Masr's editor and orders the independent outlet shut**

A Cairo court fined Lina Attalah, editor-in-chief of Mada Masr, an independent news outlet, for running an unlicensed website and ordered it closed, which press-freedom groups read as an escalating media clampdown. ([The Guardian][11])

* **A measles outbreak in Bangladesh is called a 'warning sign' of collapsing vaccination**

A new expert report says the outbreak signals a wider vaccination crisis in the South Asian country. ([Foreign Policy][12])
