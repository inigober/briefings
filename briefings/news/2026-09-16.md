# News Briefing — 16 September 2026

Madrid turns Ceuta's emergency into permanent ID halls, Merz's cabinet reaches for energy price controls, and Brazil's supreme court detonates on the eve of a vote.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Interior wants two permanent 800-place ID centres in Ceuta and Melilla**

Fernando Grande-Marlaska, Spain's interior minister, is preparing two standing migrant-identification centres — 800 places each — in the North African enclaves, and meets EU commissioner Magnus Brunner on Thursday over European funds before another trip to Ceuta on Monday. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 A rush sold as exceptional is being poured into concrete.

* **Madrid's waiting lists are being 'made up' — and it is not a crime**

A Madrid hospital downgraded more than 50 patients due for surgery to shorten the published queue; the doctors' union Amyts says the practice is widespread, and the national health minister notes that manipulating waiting lists is neither new nor penalised. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **The 1-O rubber-bullet case finally reaches a courtroom**

Four national-police officers go on trial over a foam round that cost activist Roger Español an eye during Catalonia's 2017 independence referendum; six international human-rights groups will attend a case that has taken nine years to get there. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Merz's cabinet is putting the state ahead of the market on energy**

With petrol expensive and gas stores thin, Chancellor Friedrich Merz and the economy minister are lining up state interventions on fuel and heating — a "state before market" turn Handelsblatt warns could get very expensive. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 Voter anger is pulling the coalition into the price mechanism, not only into storage.

* **Wagenknecht says she will not help the AfD — then floats working with them**

Sahra Wagenknecht, who leads the BSW, told Maischberger she does not want to hand Sachsen-Anhalt to the AfD, then brought a working arrangement back into play; a federal minister called the performance nauseating. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Fathers, as well as mothers, can come out of a birth damaged**

A Tagesspiegel feature follows a German father who missed the delivery room and for a long time wished the baby away — a perinatal-health note, not a hospital reform. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The capital's campaign is about rents and school buildings, not 'sexy'**

Four days before the 20 September Abgeordnetenhaus vote, Handelsblatt finds the talk of the town is extortionate rents and new schools — a shabby-not-sexy ledger, not the old city-marketing line. ([Handelsblatt][7])

* **A bank statement punches another hole in the Fabian murder timeline**

In the trial over the killing of eight-year-old Fabian, Gina H. again tangled herself in contradictions on the day of the crime; a VR-Bank record is now among the hardest to square. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **The Eisbären open the DEL season with a new coach and a new goalie pair**

Berlin's hockey club starts on Thursday; pundit Rick Goldmann talks title odds, the coaching change, the goaltending duo and André Rankel as the new national-team coach. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Brazil's supreme court explodes into a public feud on the eve of the presidential vote**

Two rival justices traded insults in a livestreamed hearing that experts call the court's worst crisis since the return of democracy in the 1980s — with polls still a dead heat in Latin America's largest election. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 When the referee is on the ballot in all but name, the count is already contaminated.

* **US billionaires are writing the Republicans' midterm cheques**

Ultra-wealthy donors are overwhelmingly backing Donald Trump's party over the Democrats as money floods into the US midterm race — a concentration of cash, not a grassroots surge. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Trump and Xi will not meet in the middle on AI safety**

Foreign Policy argues the two leaders start from incompatible ideas of what the technology is and who should police it — a diplomacy gap, not a drafting quarrel. ([Foreign Policy][12])
