# News Briefing — 12 October 2026

Spain hosts more US naval firepower, German courts are being rewritten against extremists, and Washington is making Ukraine's arms depend on leaving Russian refineries alone.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A sixth US missile destroyer is due at Rota**

Rota is the Spanish naval base near Cádiz that already hosts US warships; the *USS John Basilone* arrives Thursday as the sixth guided-missile destroyer, adding firepower in Spain while Washington trims forces elsewhere in Europe. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The extra hull is a reminder that Spain is still a place the US parks force, not a place it is leaving.

* **Two leak cases show the PP is not being investigated like the former attorney-general**

Spain's former attorney-general was pursued over a leak; an appeals court has now called it excessive to seize the Alcalá mayor's emails — she is on trial for passing a police note "to save" Madrid's Isabel Díaz Ayuso — and a Madrid judge has refused to question officers suspected of handing a journalist's identity to Ayuso's chief of staff. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Feijóo is telling PP barons to mobilise — and floating Albiol for Barcelona**

Alberto Núñez Feijóo, leader of the conservative People's Party, has told regional barons they need "maximum mobilisation" for the 29 November snap election, and the party is studying Xavier García Albiol as its Barcelona list-head to cut the gap with the Socialists in Catalonia. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Lay judges will face stricter rules aimed at extremists**

*Schöffen* are the lay judges who sit with professionals in German criminal courts; Justice Minister Stefanie Hubig wants tougher eligibility rules so the far right cannot use those seats to shape verdicts. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The fight is not only in parliaments; it is over who sits on the bench for ordinary crime.

* **Bavaria is in Karlsruhe arguing inheritance tax should be a state matter**

The Federal Constitutional Court is spending two days on inheritance tax; day one is Bavaria's claim that the levy — which hits heirs of firms and houses — should be set by the Länder, not the federation. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Brandenburg's new medical university is opening in Cottbus**

The Lausitz, the former coal region in Brandenburg, is starting teaching at a new medical university; Julius Gutsche, who came out of nursing, is among the first students on 12 October. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **An economist's answer to socialization: just buy Vonovia**

Berlin is still arguing over *Vergesellschaftung* — the campaign to put large landlords into public hands after a 2021 referendum; housing economist Matthias Günther says it would be simpler if the federal government bought Vonovia, Germany's biggest listed landlord, instead. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

💡 The expropriation fight is being recast as a balance-sheet question the Bund could settle with cash.

* **Comic Invasion Berlin is taking 2027 off**

After 15 years as the city's main comics gathering, the Comic Invasion team says the festival is wearing them out and will skip 2027, with a planned return in 2028. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Union's U19 coach has quit over discrimination allegations**

Jens Härtel has resigned as coach of Union Berlin's under-19 side after accusations of discrimination, a German FA matter at the club that sits in Köpenick. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Washington is threatening to block Ukraine arms over refinery attacks**

After Donald Trump's diesel deal with Vladimir Putin, US officials are lobbying European capitals and Kyiv: keep hitting Russian refineries and American weapons could stop. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The oil bargain is being enforced as a condition on Ukraine's arsenal, not only as a market story.

* **A former iPhone display plant in Japan is being turned into chip supply**

A factory that once made screens for iPhones is being retooled for the semiconductor supply chain — a recycle of old consumer-electronics kit into the chip build-out. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **The Cop31 host wants climate spending to outrun NATO defence targets**

Turkey's Murat Kurum, due to preside over the next UN climate summit, told a Pacific pre-Cop in Fiji that rich countries should spend far more on climate than the 5% of GDP NATO members have pledged for defence, because "the climate issue is the security issue." ([The Guardian][12])
