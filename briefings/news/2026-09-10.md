# News Briefing — 10 September 2026

Declassified Ceuta files, a Sahrawi nationality vote, and a bribery probe against Berlin's SPD lead set the frame as the IMF's hiring fight and an Ecuador gang listing fill out the map.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Congress is set to grant Spanish nationality to Sahrawis**

Congress is due to pass a Sumar bill giving nationality by royal charter to Sahrawis born in Spain's former Western Sahara colony before 29 September 1977, and to their children — a vote arriving in the middle of the Ceuta crisis, with Vox opposed and the PP leaning to abstain. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Expanding a colonial-era nationality route while Ceuta is still a migration emergency is a political signal, not a scheduling accident.

* **Ayuso is using Madrid's mega-fire to push a dual carriageway through protected land**

After a blaze that burned about 28,000 hectares, Isabel Díaz Ayuso, Madrid's regional premier, has revived a plan to dual the M-501 — the "reservoirs road" — through a high-protection zone, calling it recovery. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The fire is being used to dust off an infrastructure scheme that was not on the recovery list.

* **Forty declassified files show what Madrid knew before the Ceuta rush**

The papers, passed among about 30 agencies, map earlier irregular entries and resource pleas about a possible "pull effect" — the contents of the release previewed earlier this week, not another argument about whether to publish them. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **A running tally puts Germany's Ukraine course at about €100 billion**

Four years into the war, a Berliner Zeitung reckoning for Annalena Baerbock — foreign minister when the 2022 security turn, the Zeitenwende, was declared — also cites gas prices about 79% higher. ([Berliner Zeitung][4])

* **Oil is weighing on the Dax ahead of an ECB rate decision**

Germany's main index is under pressure from a rising oil price, with investors now watching the European Central Bank and a run of US data. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Germany's warning day arrives with Berlin's siren build-out still lagging**

The federal Warntag, the nationwide alert drill, is the hook for a morning briefing that says siren coverage in the capital is still being installed slowly. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Hannover prosecutors are investigating Berlin's SPD lead candidate for bribery**

Steffen Krach, the Social Democrats' top name for the 20 September Abgeordnetenhaus election, is under investigation by Hannover's public prosecutor for Bestechlichkeit — taking bribes — a charge he denies; rbb says it has seen transfer records. ([rbb24][7])

💡 A cash-and-transfers story ten days before polling day is now a campaign fact, not only a legal one.

* **A second indictment has been filed over an attack at the Holocaust memorial**

Prosecutors have brought a further charge of aiding attempted murder in the case of an assault at Berlin's Holocaust Mahnmal. ([rbb24][8])

* **The Jewish Museum marks 25 years — with its first programme director looking back**

Cilly Kugelmann, who helped open the museum, talks through its start, how it has handled antisemitism, and the strain of the Middle East war on a Berlin institution built to hold that history. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **The IMF dropped its leading chief-economist candidate over Trump tariff remarks**

Ricardo Reis, an LSE professor who had been the frontrunner, had said the US president's trade war would hurt American consumers — and that was enough to end the bid. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 Tariff orthodoxy is now a hiring filter at the Fund, not only a Washington talking point.

* **Washington has listed Ecuador's Los Tiguerones as a terrorist group**

The gang that stormed a television studio during a live broadcast in 2024 is being designated so the Trump administration can ask Congress for extra money to dismantle it over narcotics trafficking. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Vietnam's new hub airport is nearly ready — and airlines are slow to commit**

A specialist look at the next Southeast Asian mega-hub: the concrete is close, but carriers are not yet filling the slots the project was sold on. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
