# News Briefing — 30 September 2026

Spain's housing decrees are now Cabinet law with a rent freeze attached, Berlin is dealing with a burst main and a Left statute fight, and AI companies are buying cover before the US midterms.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The Cabinet has passed two housing decrees — and wants a Friday vote**

Pedro Sánchez's minority government and junior partner Sumar have agreed two emergency housing decrees in the Consejo de Ministros — Spain's cabinet — extending the ban on evicting vulnerable tenants to 2030, barring vulture funds from buying homes until 2028, and putting automatic lease extensions in a second text; they have asked Congress for an extraordinary vote this Friday. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 A Cabinet signature is not a parliamentary majority; the street got a text, not a locked statute.

* **Rents already above the official index will be frozen until 2028**

The final decree, due out Wednesday morning, adds a measure that was not in Monday night's draft: tenancies priced above the official reference index cannot rise at all until 2028, and those at the index may rise by only 2% until the end of 2027. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **The tenant tax break is now a €500 average — if the region plays along**

A new income-tax deduction for renters would save mid-income tenants about €500 a year, at a maximum budget cost of €820 million, but only for people earning up to €33,000 and only where the autonomous community has declared a zona tensionada — a legally "stressed" rental market. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **BSW is trying to block the FDP's Teuteberg from Brandenburg's SED post**

Linda Teuteberg, the Free Democrats' deputy leader, is in line to become Brandenburg's commissioner for confronting the SED dictatorship — the official charged with the East German communist party's legacy — and Sahra Wagenknecht's BSW has called her "unelectable" after the confirmation hearing. ([Berliner Zeitung][4])

💡 The fight is over who owns the East's official memory, not only a patronage job.

* **Deutsche Aircraft has opened a Leipzig line for the D328eco**

The German regional-aircraft maker has opened a final-assembly line in Leipzig sized for up to 48 of its D328eco turboprops a year, with about 250 direct jobs planned, according to the Saxon economics ministry. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

* **Flu season is here — and the shot is a GP, works doctor, or pharmacy visit**

Germany's autumn influenza wave is starting; the vaccine that cuts the risk of a severe course is available from the Hausarzt, the company doctor, or pharmacies, and Handelsblatt walks through who should get it and who can skip. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A burst water main flooded Siemensstadt and emptied a house**

A fresh-water pipe broke on Tuesday morning in Siemensstadt, the Spandau industrial quarter, dumping more than a million litres into the streets and undermining one house so badly that it had to be evacuated. ([rbb24][7])

* **Die Linke's House group wants antisemitism written into its statutes**

Berlin's Left parliamentary group — now the largest party in the Abgeordnetenhaus after this month's election — wants a position against antisemitism locked into its own rulebook. ([rbb24][8])

* **The Landespokal weekend has an East–West Berlin tie**

The next round of the Berlin and Brandenburg regional football cups is this weekend, with a pointed East–West pairing in the capital and a Brandenburg upstart against the title holder. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Big Tech is trying to defuse a data-centre backlash before the midterms**

The AI Infrastructure Coalition — Google, Meta, Microsoft and peers — has published pledges to cover energy costs and cut water use, a political-insurance package aimed at communities fighting new data centres ahead of the US midterms. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The constraint is no longer only chips and debt; it is local power, water, and votes.

* **An Indian clean-energy firm has filed for a $1bn IPO**

Inox Clean Energy has lodged a draft prospectus to raise up to 100 billion rupees, about $1 billion — a listed-market bet on India's power-and-renewables build, not another hyperscaler bond. ([Bloomberg][11])

* **Hanoi is accused of abducting anti-trafficking activists**

Vietnam has detained three campaigners, including Vietnamese-Australian Tran Hiep, missing since 18 September, and a Norwegian; Hanoi says they confessed to membership of Viet Tan, a pro-democracy group it calls terrorist, on charges their supporters call fabricated. ([The Guardian][12])
