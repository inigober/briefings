# News Briefing — 4 September 2026

Prosecutors want Ceuta in Spain's top criminal court, VW's board has a rescue plan, and money is moving — from a $2tn fund's Treasuries to a Buenos Aires mansion and Tokyo rooftops.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Prosecutors want the Audiencia Nacional to take the Ceuta case**

Spain's public prosecutor will tell investigating judge María Tardón that the late-July rush into the North African enclave is a transnational offence — it started in Morocco, landed in Spain, and hit state institutions nationwide — and that the Audiencia Nacional, not a local court, should hear it. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 A leaked police file is becoming a case the cabinet still says it cannot prove.

* **Strasbourg has thrown out Marchena's 'ambient violence' doctrine**

The European Court of Human Rights has condemned Spain for violating protest rights in the *Aturem el Parlament* case — the 2011 Catalan sit-in that blocked the regional parliament — and discredited the "violencia ambiental" theory former Supreme Court criminal-chamber president Manuel Marchena used to treat a crowd's atmosphere as violence. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **A pro-Israel digital network is recasting Ceuta as Gaza**

An opaque cluster of channels with hundreds of thousands of followers, aligned with Israel, is flooding the enclave crisis with falsehoods and war talk — "Ceuta is now Gaza" — in a line that pushes insurgency against the government. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **VW's supervisory board has agreed a restructuring plan**

Volkswagen's Aufsichtsrat, the worker-and-shareholder board that must sign off big factory decisions, has accepted a Sanierungsplan that takes almost every proposal from management — with one exception. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 Board consent is the start of the fight over plants, not the end of it.

* **A CDU deputy has called the firewall 'bekloppt' on Lanz**

Days before the 6 September Sachsen-Anhalt Landtag vote, a Christian Democrat told Markus Lanz she finds the Brandmauer — the party's pledge not to govern with the AfD — "so stupid," while the show showed how loosely the CDU now treats its own resolution on Die Linke. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **IFA's 'future' gadgets are being remembered as flops**

A recap of Europe's big Berlin electronics fair lists the innovations once sold as the next wave — 3D glasses, smart kettles — and how quickly they vanished. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **BER Terminal 1 has been cleared after a hazardous-substance alert**

Police emptied Terminal 1 at Berlin Brandenburg Airport after a woman reported a dangerous substance; after a search, the terminal was reopened. ([rbb24][7])

* **Union's Alte Försterei expansion is delayed — so the club stays at the Olympiastadion**

The rebuild of 1. FC Union's Köpenick ground will take longer than planned, so the men's Bundesliga side must keep using the 1936 Olympic stadium as a stand-in; a temporary ground for the women's team is still unset. ([rbb24][8])

* **A convicted wind-farm fraudster has walked off a prison leave**

Hendrik Holt, jailed for a wind-power investment fraud, failed to return from a Freigang — a short, supervised day release — and an investigator who has followed him for years is now treating the case as a manhunt. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Peter Thiel's Buenos Aires move is being read as a legislative project**

The US tech billionaire bought a $12m mansion in April and moved his family to Javier Milei's Argentina; protesters in white masks gathered at the gates after Congress debated laws opponents say would entrench US tech fortunes in the country. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 The fight is over a statute, not a celebrity relocation.

* **An Octopus-backed retailer is joining Japan's mini-solar rush**

A power retailer backed by Britain's Octopus Energy is entering Japan's boom in small rooftop and neighbourhood solar — a distributed-energy push in a country still rewriting its post-nuclear power mix. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Norway's $2tn oil fund wants to cut its US Treasury holdings**

The manager of the world's largest sovereign wealth fund has told the Finance Ministry to overhaul the bond book and slash Treasuries in a bid for higher returns. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 A $2tn fund proposing fewer Treasuries is a reserve-currency story, not a portfolio tweak.
