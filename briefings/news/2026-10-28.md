# News Briefing — 28 October 2026

An Israeli election with no clear winner, Spain's right negotiating over who runs where in the Senate, and Berlin's would-be left-led government weighing who gets which ministry show coalition arithmetic deciding outcomes more than headline votes.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A Barcelona judge frees looting suspects, citing roots in the community, but bars them from protests**

A duty judge reviewing adult suspects from Saturday's Barcelona looting found "social and family roots" for two of them, which lowers their flight risk, and only banned them from further protests, saying the Blocus-called march was "empty of content" and aimed at looting shops. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Abascal fears Feijóo is chasing Vox voters at the expense of the centre**

Inside the far-right Vox, there is concern that the conservative People's Party (PP) leader Alberto Feijóo's picks of candidates such as Albiol and Álvarez de Toledo fight for already-convinced voters instead of winning over the undecided. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **A map shows how each party will run for the Senate on 29 November**

The ruling PSOE and its left ally Frente Amplio will run together almost everywhere except the Canary Islands, while on the right Vox withdraws in six provinces and the PP in three under a Feijóo–Abascal deal, making the Senate ballot unusually complicated. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **A study finds Europe almost wholly dependent on foreign digital platforms**

For operating systems, search engines and cloud services, Europe relies almost entirely on non-European providers, mostly American and Chinese, according to a study reported by the business daily Handelsblatt. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Amazon sellers offer money or vouchers to get customers to delete negative reviews**

Suspected sellers on Amazon, Germany's largest online marketplace, approach buyers who left bad ratings and promise payment for removing them, a scheme consumer advocates warn can carry legal risks for the reviewer. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **A father who does not provide childcare loses the tax allowance for it**

Germany's BEA allowance for childcare costs goes only to a parent who is actually providing the care: a father who has the child two afternoons a week, while the mother does the rest, gets nothing unless he takes part himself. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's schools could be run by the Linke for the first time since reunification**

With coalition talks underway after the Berlin election, the successor party to East Germany's ruling SED could take the education portfolio after 36 years, while the SPD and Greens show little interest in claiming it. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Bavarian lawmakers court property groups and warn against socialising housing in Berlin**

Bavarian MPs are debating Berlin's plans to expropriate big landlords and pitching Bavaria to firms that might be affected, though the companies are tempering expectations of any move. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Berlin's Linke parliamentary group postpones a decision on member Vanessa Emde**

After meetings of the group's leadership and the full group, the left party's Berlin faction has still reached no decision on how to handle its deputy Vanessa Emde, and the internal debate continues. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Israel's election leaves Netanyahu's bloc and its rivals short of a majority**

Exit polls suggest neither side has the 61 seats needed in the 120-seat Knesset, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is urging rivals to form a Jewish-only coalition and shun Arab parties, which polls suggest had a strong result and could hold the key. ([Financial Times][10])

* **The Trump administration faces another day in court over its tariffs**

Having lost five trade cases in a row over its use of emergency economic powers (IEEPA) to impose tariffs, the US government is set to face a possible sixth loss. ([Foreign Policy][11])

* **Britain sanctions Sudanese commanders over sexual violence in El Fasher**

On the first anniversary of the fall of the Sudanese city of El Fasher, where tens of thousands of civilians were killed, London imposed travel bans and asset freezes on military leaders from both warring sides it suspects of a "campaign of rape". ([The Guardian][12])
