# News Briefing — 25 November 2026

A last burst of Spanish campaign promises, a fresh US tariff threat for German exporters and a Berlin coalition test frame the day, while Beijing's purge and Cuba's falling life expectancy shape the world beat.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Government uses its last cabinet meeting to stack social measures five days before the vote**

At the final Council of Ministers of the legislature, the coalition raised birth leave to 20 weeks, extended bereavement leave to 10 days, cut income tax for the self-employed by €465m and prolonged 2027 public-transport aid. The opposition People's Party (PP) calls the leave measures "papel mojado" (dead letter) and will not say whether it would keep them. ([eldiario.es][1], [eldiario.es][2])

* **PP revives the spectre of electoral fraud and concedes a tie with the Socialists is possible**

With a week to go, the conservative PP is again talking of a "pucherazo" (ballot rigging) while admitting that Sunday's result could be level with the ruling PSOE, as uncertainty pushes candidates towards hyperbole and unexpected allies. ([EL PAÍS][3])

* **Electoral Board action against journalist Silvia Intxaurrondo reopens the row over far-right voices on public TV**

Six analysts weigh in on remarks that earned the journalist a file from the Junta Electoral (the body that polices campaign rules) and ask whether the far right should have a place on public television. ([EL PAÍS][4])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Solar output passes 100 terawatt-hours in a year for the first time**

German solar plants have generated more electricity in 2026 than in any previous year, crossing the 100 TWh mark and leaving earlier records far behind. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Trump may announce new tariffs on the EU this week, putting the transatlantic trade deal back in dispute**

Handelsblatt's morning podcast says the US president could name fresh levies on the EU and other countries, officially over dumped imports, and that the newly elected Congress can hardly stop him. ([Handelsblatt][6])

* **Experts on how parents can protect girls from cybergrooming and deepfakes**

Two specialists explain the warning signs of online sexual harassment, from adults grooming minors to AI-faked images, and why bans on social media do little to help. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Linke caucus expels Vanessa Emde, removing a sticking point for a red-green-red coalition**

Berlin's Left Party group in the state parliament voted to throw out the MP for breaching caucus decisions, chiefly over her stance on Israel and the Middle East conflict, a step the Greens had called necessary before governing with the Left and the Social Democrats. ([rbb24][8])

* **Two months after the election, borough mayors are taking shape in some of the city's twelve districts**

Tagesspiegel's overview of the district councils shows new alliances and surprises where the first mayors and councillors are chosen, and deep rifts where the votes are still unresolved. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

* **More than 20,000 people in Berlin suffered domestic violence in 2025, and groups press for work with perpetrators**

Most victims were women, and organisations say shelters alone are not enough, arguing that violence against women is "a men's problem" that requires programmes for offenders. ([Tagesspiegel][10])

## World 🌐

* **Cuban life expectancy falls for the first time since 1950**

The World Health Organization's chief, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, said in Havana that life expectancy on the island dropped from 78.8 years in 2020 to 74.4 in 2025 amid a severe economic crisis. ([The Guardian][11])

💡 The figures predate the US fuel blockade, so they do not yet show its effect.

* **Shanghai's Communist Party boss Chen Jining is placed under investigation as Xi widens his purge**

The Financial Times reports that Chen, who runs China's biggest city and sits on the Politburo, is suspected of "severe violations of discipline and laws", the latest senior figure to fall. ([Financial Times][12])

* **Huawei convicted in a US trial that exposed corporate espionage and sanctions evasion**

The verdict is a win for federal prosecutors after an eight-year legal fight with the Chinese telecoms equipment maker. ([Financial Times][13])
