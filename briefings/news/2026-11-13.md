# News Briefing — 13 November 2026

Spain's campaign opens amid scrutiny of strike-day policing, while Germany's budget, Mercedes's investment freeze and a consensus presidential pick show a state bargaining over money and leadership as markets bet AI will survive Nvidia's turmoil.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's 29 November general election campaign opened with the first daily 40dB tracking poll for EL PAÍS and the SER radio network putting the right-wing bloc on course for about 190 of the 350 seats**

The poll confirms the strength of the far-right Vox, whose leader Santiago Abascal now rivals the conservative PP's Alberto Núñez Feijóo in popularity. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska has ordered police to stop using tear gas at demonstrations and opened specific investigations into 'incidents' during last week's general strike**

The order follows complaints from unions about heavy-handed police charges against strike pickets. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Vox, the far-right party, published its election manifesto, proposing a cap on pupils 'from other cultures' per classroom and extending 'national priority' in access to public services**

The 13-block programme also makes 'remigration' (large-scale return of immigrants) central to its migration policy and keeps its calls for education to be recentralised and for tax cuts. ([eldiario.es][3])

💡 Putting a cultural quota into school rules would move Vox's migration agenda from border policy into everyday public services, where regional governments hold most of the powers.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The Bundestag budget committee approved a record 2027 federal budget after an all-night session, with more borrowing and one open question: whether the electricity-tax cut for everyone will still come**

The committee ran far behind schedule, and the unresolved tax cut is the last sticking point between the coalition partners. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Mercedes-Benz, the world's oldest carmaker, is reported to be halting investment in Germany and threatening plant closures, a move the Handelsblatt newsletter says only sounds like blackmail**

The paper argues that the company has carefully calculated reasons for losing faith in its home market. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Ilse Aigner, a respected Bavarian conservative nominated by Chancellor Friedrich Merz for federal president, has won backing from the Social Democrats and the Greens**

Politico Europe says the cross-party support has interrupted months of infighting in Merz's coalition and makes Aigner all but certain to become the first woman in the ceremonial post. ([Politico Europe][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's Senate wants to use artificial intelligence to speed up analysis of seized data in child-abuse investigations after the killing of 13-year-old Lisa from Marzahn**

The suspect had been under investigation for child pornography for almost two years, yet his data had still not been evaluated. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Transport senator Ute Bonde, now only in caretaker office after the election, is pushing ahead with contested road redesigns on Torstraße, Kantstraße and Tempelhofer Damm**

Berlin's Greens are angry that she is creating facts on the ground before a new Senate takes over. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

💡 With a coalition still unformed, a caretaker senator's building orders can lock in transport policy that the next government would have to pay to reverse.

* **Fridays for Future, the youth climate movement, is demonstrating in Berlin and in Wittenberge in Brandenburg**

The protests are tied to the UN world climate conference. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **China's greenhouse-gas emissions are falling, The Economist argues, driven by self-interest rather than altruism, with Chinese technology also helping other countries cut their own emissions**

The leader article, the paper's editorial view, credits Beijing's own interests rather than outside pressure for the decline. ([The Economist][10])

* **Emerging-market stocks are heading for their first weekly gain since late September as investors return to technology shares, betting that upheaval at the chipmaker Nvidia will not derail the wider AI industry**

Nvidia, whose chief executive Jensen Huang is at the centre of the turmoil, supplies the processors that most AI systems depend on. ([Bloomberg][11])

* **Jared Kushner, a US envoy, says the United States cannot reach a deal to end the war in Ukraine without Europe**

His comments come as European leaders reject a Russian push to overhaul the continent's security arrangements. ([Financial Times][12])

## Other Headlines Today 📋

* **Spanish policing:** Interior spent €70,000 a month ago on 790 pepper-spray units like those sprayed on strike demonstrators, unions and Amnesty International say.
* **Spanish campaign:** Abascal walked out of an interview with the YouTuber Jordi Wild; a court fined MEP Alvise Pérez €20,000 for a false terrorism claim.
* **Spanish housing:** Parties split over building more versus regulating more as housing becomes the election's biggest everyday issue.
* **Franco-German defence:** Paris wants Berlin to set clear rules on conventional German strikes on Russia, fearing Moscow may misread them.
* **Ukraine war:** Statistical data suggest Russia has lost almost half a million lives, for gains on only a few small fronts.
* **Left-wing coalitions:** The Linke is governing-ready in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern but still struggling in Berlin, where it is pushing to expel a deputy from its group.
* **AI finance:** Nscale, the Nvidia-backed 'neocloud' planning a $35bn IPO, warns it needs more cash to fulfil its Anthropic contracts.
