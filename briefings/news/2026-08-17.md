# News Briefing — 17 August 2026

Unpaid labour, water scarcity and transport fragility shape the domestic sections today, while Brazil's campaign launch and new strain around Korea and global shipping show geopolitics feeding straight into economic systems.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain is still losing the equivalent of 68,000 jobs in unpaid overtime**

Despite years of labour-law tightening, Spain still records about 2.5 million unpaid overtime hours a week because weak time-tracking leaves sectors such as education struggling to prove work done off the clock. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Drought is pushing northern villages onto water trucks**

After weeks of unusually dry weather, towns in northern Spain are already relying on tanker deliveries as springs fail and summer pasture disappears, extending the country's water-stress story well beyond the south. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Madrid is preparing new sanctions over abusive temporary hiring in the public sector**

Spain's public-administration ministry is negotiating alerts and penalties to curb excessive use of interim staff, saying roughly 200,000 temporary public workers remain affected after years of legal and union pressure. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Low river levels are starting to hit German building projects**

Low water on major waterways is raising the cost of moving cement, gravel and other bulky materials, turning Germany's recurring river-shipping problem into a direct price and delay risk for builders. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Merz is facing pushback from his own camp over the debt course**

Within Friedrich Merz's governing bloc, Union politicians are openly doubting the chancellor's fiscal line as weak productivity narrows room for extra borrowing, making budget discipline an early test of coalition cohesion. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **A bomb scare was enough to snarl rail traffic in Franconia**

Police action over suspected explosives halted train services in Franconia, another reminder that Germany's already fragile rail system can seize up quickly when security incidents hit important junctions. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Wild boars are becoming a routine safety problem on Berlin's edge**

On the capital region's wooded fringe, wild boars are moving deeper into residential areas and forcing local authorities to treat human-wildlife encounters as a regular urban-management issue rather than a rural nuisance. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Mariendorf kept one of Berlin's older sporting institutions in view**

The 131st German Trotting Derby was won by Fragolino Rosso in Mariendorf, a reminder that Berlin's racecourse culture still survives as a niche but durable part of the city's sporting calendar. ([rbb24][8])

* **A Marienfelde Späti offered a small snapshot of outer-district Berlin**

rbb24's street-level reporting from a corner shop in Marienfelde captured a familiar pressure outside the Ring: work, family logistics and rising costs are shaping daily life more than the city-centre political conversation suggests. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Lula has launched a bid for a fourth term in Brazil**

Brazil's 80-year-old president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, has opened a new campaign against Flávio Bolsonaro, turning Latin America's biggest election into an early referendum on whether the country's far right can win back the presidency. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Trump says the US will scale back drills with South Korea**

Donald Trump says Washington will reduce joint military exercises with Seoul, cutting into a core deterrence ritual on the peninsula just as officials warn North Korea is absorbing battlefield lessons from Russia's war in Ukraine. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Trade through the world's chokepoints is getting more expensive**

From the Panama Canal and the Rhine to the Red Sea and Hormuz, war and climate stress are pushing up the cost of moving goods through bottlenecks that now look less like isolated regional disruptions than a single inflationary trade risk. ([Financial Times][12])
