# News Briefing — 20 November 2026

A restrained Sánchez after Spain's only head-to-head debate, Berlin hosting Ukraine's backers while its coalition talks and school building roll on, and a tech sell-off rattling markets from Alaska's ballot boxes to Asian currencies frame today's edition.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **After Thursday's only head-to-head debate with People's Party (PP) leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo, Spain's Socialist prime minister Pedro Sánchez is trying to win back moderate voters with an unexpectedly soft tone**

Ahead of the 29 November general election, Sánchez dropped the campaign attacks and the appeal to mobilise the left, a pivot towards the political centre. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **A Valencian left-wing alliance, Compromís-Front Valencià, is concentrating its campaign on the hardest districts, with Ada Colau and Gabriel Rufián of the Catalan party ERC joining it for the first time**

The Frente Amplio, the wider left-wing electoral alliance, is involved in the area hit by the 2024 flood (dana), where a rise of the far right is feared, while Rufián tours Castellón, where the alliance finds a seat hardest to win. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Tomás Guitarte, who heads the list of Teruel Existe, the regional party for Aragon's depopulated province, says it will vote "no" to any prime-ministerial investiture for now because neither side has delivered for Teruel**

The party lost its seat in Congress in 2023 and wants it back, arguing that the province must not become a "sacrifice territory". ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Berlin wants to support Ukraine economically as well as militarily, according to Zeit's daily overview of the war**

The shift from weapons deliveries towards economic backing comes as Ukraine's allies gather in the German capital this week. ([Die Zeit][4])

* **Researchers quoted by Handelsblatt expect a population explosion of machines rather than people, asking when humanoid robots for homes and services will outnumber humans**

The question reflects how quickly household and service robots are moving from labs towards mass production. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Actor Jan Josef Liefers, star of the long-running "Tatort" crime series, says politics makes too many bad decisions and that this strengthens extremists**

He argues that democracy has to be made "sexy" and sees possible solutions "outside the current party system". ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's Social Democrats (SPD) are pushing, in coalition talks with the Left Party and the Greens, to record cases of anti-Palestinian racism in the city, a term that remains highly contested**

Their demands also include a Gaza memorial day and a Middle East advisory council. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Kraftwerk Berlin, the former power plant turned event venue, has admitted mistakes after hosting a launch event for Helsing, the Munich-based AI weapons maker**

The admission follows criticism of an arms company using a cultural venue. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin's first "stacked school", a multi-storey building meant to fit more classrooms on small plots, has had its foundation stone laid in Lichtenberg**

It is the first such building in the city's school-construction drive. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **The Trump administration appears ready to stop contesting the Alaska midterm election, after two almost identical names on the ballot led it to attempt a drastic intervention that failed in court**

The episode shows courts again acting as a check on federal efforts to reshape a vote's outcome. ([Handelsblatt][10])

* **China is preparing to restart investment in US private-equity funds after a pullback, with its flagship sovereign wealth fund leading a push for special terms that protect against disaster scenarios**

The safeguards are the price Beijing wants for unlocking funding while US–China tensions persist. ([Financial Times][11])

* **About 20 Indigenous Awá people, who live in voluntary isolation in Brazil's Amazon, emerged from the forest and made contact with a government team, in what the NGO Survival International called a "historic encounter"**

Organisations say the contact raises the alarm over growing invasions of the group's territory. ([The Guardian][12])
