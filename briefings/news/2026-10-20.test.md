# News Briefing — 20 October 2026

Spain's courts and floods, Germany's pricier credit and slower doctors' appointments, and Berlin's coalition arithmetic play out against a world pricing in an AI debt scare and an Ebola outbreak widening past its borders.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **A court has found two former Vox leaders cheated the tax office on a luxury penthouse**

Madrid's regional high court (TSJM) rejected four of five appeals by Iván Espinosa de los Monteros and Rocío Monasterio against Tax Agency fines for using a false invoice in a high-end property deal first exposed by elDiario.es. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Spain is learning from the 2024 Valencia floods that alerts save lives**

Comparing this month's torrential *dana* — a cut-off low that stalls over Spain and dumps rain — with the deadly 2024 floods in Valencia, El País finds mass phone alerts and prevention are what changed, with some lessons still unlearned. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Housing costs now push 770,000 working Spaniards into poverty**

Oxfam Intermón calculates that once rent or mortgage payments are deducted, the working-poor rate rises to 15.49%, or about three million workers — a measure that shows how housing, not wages alone, defines poverty in the run-up to the 29 November election. ([EL PAÍS][3])

💡 Wages look adequate on paper; the squeeze only appears after housing is subtracted.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Mortgages are the dearest since 2009 as the Iran war feeds "Trumpflation"**

Handelsblatt says the Iran war begun by US President Donald Trump is pushing up inflation and, behind it, lending rates, so that property loans now cost more than at any time since 2009 and prospective home buyers and house building feel the damage. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 A Middle East war is reaching German housing through the cost of credit, not through energy bills.

* **Doctors' groups say longer waits for appointments are coming**

The statutory-insurance doctors' association (KBV) warns that a new rule obliging practices to report a fixed number of free slots — until now they set the figure themselves — will not speed up access, predicting that "appointment scarcity" will worsen. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **The Dax opens the week with heavy losses**

Germany's main share index started the week sharply lower, with investors hoping that fresh economic data and quarterly results on Tuesday can spark a recovery. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **SPD and Greens agree to open formal coalition talks**

Two days after exploratory talks with Die Linke ended, Berlin's SPD and Greens have each voted to start coalition negotiations; Die Linke will decide at a party congress at the end of October. ([rbb24][7])

* **The Greens will let a four-person panel pick their Senate team**

After criticism of lead candidates Werner Graf and Bettina Jarasch, the Greens are reacting to their weak election result by having a four-member commission choose potential members of government. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Alba Berlin beat the holders despite a depleted squad**

Berlin's basketball club beat cup holders Bamberg 83:71 in the BBL-Pokal last 16, turning around a poor first half to advance. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Kenya's Ebola cluster exposes how far the US has stepped back**

An eighth person in Kenya has tested positive for the Bundibugyo strain — the country's first-ever cluster — as the world's second-largest Ebola outbreak has killed more than 4,300 and infected over 9,000, while Washington, having cut funding and left the WHO, is building a contested quarantine centre in Kenya. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 The US has swapped health support for border-style containment, and the host country is paying the political cost.

* **Investors are buying insurance against a big-tech debt unwind**

Trading in credit default swaps — contracts that pay out if a borrower defaults — tied to large technology groups has surged as investors seek protection against the AI boom's debt-funded build-out unravelling. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Blocking the Internet Archive will not stop AI, argues The Atlantic**

Publishers are within their rights to challenge companies that exploit their work for AI, the magazine argues, but blocking the Wayback Machine — the Internet Archive's public record of old web pages — makes the web's memory collateral damage. ([The Atlantic][12])
