# News Briefing — 20 October 2026

AI financing anxiety threads through today's edition alongside Sudan's deadliest single strike of its war, French unrest spilling across borders, and Berlin's own debates over surveillance and public safety.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **An AI model reads cancer risk in patients' faces**

Spanish oncologists trained a facial-ageing algorithm on more than 40 million photographs and found that cancer patients who look older than their real age tend to have worse prognoses, a signal doctors could use alongside standard staging. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 Visual biomarkers like this could let hospitals triage patients faster than lab-based staging alone.

* **Consulates say they can't vet Spain's "grandchildren's law" voters in time**

Spanish diplomats processing citizenship under the *ley de nietos* — the law granting nationality to grandchildren of those exiled under Franco — warn that distinguishing which newly naturalised citizens may legally vote on 29 November will be "incomplete and arbitrary" before polling day. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Ayuso calls striking Madrid teachers "violent" as the walkout drags on**

Isabel Díaz Ayuso, the Popular Party president of the Madrid region, branded protesting teachers and students "violent" after they rallied outside one of her events, deepening a standoff in which her education chief still refuses to meet the committee behind Madrid's historic all-level school strike. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **German stocks open the week lower as Asian jitters and European unrest weigh**

Frankfurt's Dax, Germany's blue-chip stock index, fell at Monday's open as investors awaited fresh US earnings and watched unrest spreading across Europe, with traders looking to Tuesday's economic data for a rebound. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **A 25-year-old Italian coffee bar still charges 70 cents for an espresso**

An elderly couple running a bar in Italy have held their espresso price at 70 cents for a quarter-century, even as German café prices have climbed steadily — a local holdout against a continent-wide inflation trend. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Microsoft ends support for Publisher, pushing holdout users to free alternatives**

Microsoft has discontinued Publisher, its desktop-layout tool for newsletters and brochures, leaving regular users to migrate existing files to free programs like LibreOffice or Scribus for both simple and complex layouts. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A 28-year-old remains critical after a violent attack outside a Kreuzberg Späti**

Berlin homicide detectives are investigating after a 28-year-old man was severely injured in an attack outside a late-night corner shop (*Späti*) in Kreuzberg, with the victim still in life-threatening condition. ([rbb24][7])

* **BR Volleys rebuild around a coach billed as volleyball's Pep Guardiola**

Berlin's top volleyball club, the BR Volleys, enters the new Bundesliga season with a largely new roster, returning familiar faces and a coach, Daniele Anastasi, whom club chief Kaweh Niroomand has compared to Pep Guardiola. ([rbb24][8])

* **Brandenburg drafts a law to film crowds in public spaces**

A draft bill seen by rbb would let Brandenburg authorities record people and groups with audio and video in public under certain conditions and store the footage for four weeks — a camera-expansion debate that echoes Berlin's own recurring fights over public surveillance. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **A drone strike kills 80 at a Sudanese market, one of the war's deadliest single attacks**

A drone struck twice at a transport hub in al-Dibab, West Kordofan, killing roughly 80 people — mostly commuters catching rides home — in one of the deadliest single attacks of Sudan's two-year civil war. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 Sudan's war, already the world's largest displacement crisis, keeps escalating with little international mediation pressure attached.

* **Four die in a fire at one of Canada's busiest military bases**

A fast-moving fire tore through a training base in Alberta, killing four people and seriously injuring dozens more, in what Prime Minister Mark Carney called one of the worst peacetime tragedies for Canada's armed forces. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Trump envoys head to Norway for fresh Ukraine talks as US energy costs climb**

Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, two of President Trump's key foreign-policy envoys, are expected to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Norway as rising US household energy costs add political pressure ahead of the midterms. ([Financial Times][12])
