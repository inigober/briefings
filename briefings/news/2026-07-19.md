# News Briefing — 19 July 2026

Feijóo's rollback blueprint and Madrid's first open mayoral primary frame Spain's weekend politics, Berlin's cultural institutions reopen for summer, and Hormuz escalation meets Cuba's grid collapse and India's credit boom.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Feijóo's PP promises sweeping legal counter-reform rolling back social protections if it wins power**

eldiario.es reports the conservative leader's team is drafting plans to cut unemployment benefits, curb union representation, reverse trans-rights protections and end aid for Franco victims and migrants, with secretary general Miguel Tellado saying nearly 100 laws must be repealed. The platform marks the most explicit pre-election blueprint yet for dismantling Sánchez-era social legislation rather than incremental adjustment. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When an opposition party publishes a hundred-law demolition list before an election, the campaign stops debating competence and becomes a referendum on whether Spain's post-Franco welfare settlement still commands majority support.

* **Madrid PSOE holds its first-ever open primary to choose a challenger to Mayor Almeida**

eldiario.es says ex-minister Reyes Maroto and district councillor Enma López face members on Sunday in an unprecedented contest for the capital's top ballot line after decades of leadership appointments from party headquarters. The vote tests whether grassroots selection can rebuild credibility in a city the Socialists have not governed since 2011. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Ayuso defends imputed Móstoles mayor while attacking PSOE over sexual-harassment cases**

eldiario.es contrasts the Madrid regional president's continued backing of PP mayor Manuel Bautista, imputed for workplace and sexual harassment, with her past demands that Moncloa act against similar allegations involving socialists. The double standard is becoming a liability for the PP's "moral superiority" narrative as judges keep the Móstoles case alive. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Merz accelerates search for Spahn's successor before summer break**

Handelsblatt says the chancellor wants the CDU/CSU parliamentary group to settle on a new leader quickly after Jens Spahn's resignation, with coalition managers speculating over possible candidates before Merz leaves for holiday. The vacancy leaves the Union without its most visible health-policy voice at a moment when the government is selling a contested reform package to sceptical voters. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Apprenticeship numbers rise in the trades as industrial crisis redirects school leavers**

Handelsblatt reports the craft sector is attracting more trainees even as manufacturing employers cut jobs, with the chamber president citing better career visibility and stable local demand. The shift suggests Germany's vocational pipeline may absorb displaced industrial workers faster than white-collar services can. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **EU mandates in-car driver-monitoring cameras on all newly registered cars**

Handelsblatt explains the ADDW system uses interior cameras to detect drowsiness and distraction, with manufacturers required to fit the technology from this month. Data-protection officials say the rules can comply with privacy law if firms limit storage and access, but drivers face a new layer of surveillance in everyday mobility. ([Handelsblatt][6])

💡 Mandatory cabin cameras turn every new car into a compliance device — a quiet expansion of state-adjacent monitoring that will outlast the drowsiness headlines.

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Volksbühne intendant Lilienthal plans Prinzenbad reopening and generational reset**

Tagesspiegel profiles Matthias Lilienthal's ambition to reinvent the capital's most polarising theatre with a poolside summer stage and a younger creative team, pushing back against critics who say Berlin's cultural flagship lost its edge. The interview signals how the city's post-pandemic arts recovery hinges on institutions willing to break with their own mythologies. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Schöneberg's 32nd lesbian and gay city festival opens across the traditional Kiez**

rbb24 says the weekend street festival returns to Nollendorfplatz and surrounding blocks with political speeches, live music and community stalls drawing locals and visitors. Organisers frame the event as both celebration and assertion of visibility in a district that has anchored Berlin's queer public life for decades. ([rbb24][8])

* **Toxic fumes force evacuation of Schöneberg shop and adjoining flats**

rbb24 reports emergency services cleared a business and several apartments after hazardous vapours spread through the building, with residents temporarily barred from returning until air-quality tests pass. The incident highlights how ageing mixed-use blocks in inner Berlin still pose sudden environmental risks to tenants above street-level commerce. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **US launches reprisal strikes on Iran after two American troops killed in Jordan**

Financial Times says Washington escalated attacks on Iranian targets as Tehran's missiles and drones hit Gulf states, intensifying the battle for control of the Strait of Hormuz. The deaths of US personnel in Jordan mark a new threshold in a conflict that had largely spared American lives outside the Gulf. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Cuba edges toward breakdown as power cuts and US oil blockade fray public patience**

The Guardian describes a third national grid collapse in ten days and growing unrest across cities sweltering under a six-month US embargo on fuel imports. Havana's ageing infrastructure is struggling to recover between outages, raising questions about whether the communist government can stabilise daily life without external relief. ([The Guardian][11])

💡 An oil blockade that triggers repeated grid failures does not just squeeze the regime — it tests whether a one-party state can keep order when basic services become unreliable for millions.

* **ICICI Bank beats profit forecasts as India's second-largest private lender posts loan surge**

Bloomberg reports strong retail and corporate credit growth lifted first-quarter earnings above analyst estimates at ICICI Bank. The results underscore how India's private banks are capturing demand even as global investors watch Middle East turmoil and US rate policy for spillovers into emerging markets. ([Bloomberg][12])
