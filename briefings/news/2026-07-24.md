# News Briefing — 24 July 2026

Madrid's simultaneous wildfires force a national emergency takeover, Trump's revived tariff wall hits European exporters, Merz reshuffles his cabinet after Spahn's exit, and Berlin confronts legionella deaths and queer-family law gaps as Intel and Taiwan signal where the AI infrastructure race is heading next.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Government declares national emergency as Madrid and Ávila fires overwhelm regional crews**

eldiario.es reports Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska activated level-three command over blazes in Villa del Prado, San Martín de Valdeiglesias and Burgohondo after Ayuso requested federal takeover, citing adverse weather and stretched resources. More than 10,000 residents have been evacuated as simultaneous fronts test whether Spain can coordinate cross-regional firefighting without outside help. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When a regional premier asks Madrid to seize operational control, wildfire politics stops being a blame game and becomes a stress test of whether Spain's civil-protection hierarchy can scale faster than summer heat.

* **Congress finalises mutualista pension bridge to Social Security but excludes current retirees**

Lawmakers approved letting private mutual-insurance holders join the autónomo regime for public pensions, yet a PP Senate amendment bars already retired mutualistas — widows excepted — from backdating access. Trade unions say the carve-out leaves thousands who paid into private schemes for decades without the public top-up the reform promised. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Trump imposes fresh 10–12.5% tariffs on 60 partners including Spain as prior levies expire**

EL PAÍS says Washington replaced the Supreme Court–struck universal 10% duty with country-specific rates of 10–12.5% on more than 60 trading partners, including EU members, tied to anti-trafficking enforcement benchmarks. Brussels must now decide whether to negotiate sector carve-outs or prepare retaliation before the new wall takes effect Friday. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Merz reshuffles cabinet and CDU leadership after Spahn resignation**

Handelsblatt reports Chancellor Friedrich Merz installed Nina Warken as chancellery minister, Carsten Linnemann as health minister and several other loyalists in a post-Spahn reset that also rotates CDU parliamentary posts. The speed of the carousel suggests Merz is prioritising party control over a summer pause as coalition poll numbers stay weak. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Washington announces new import duties on 60 trade partners including the EU**

Tagesspiegel says the Trump administration unveiled replacement tariffs as the legal basis for April's global levies expires, with Canada already threatening counter-measures. German exporters face renewed uncertainty just as industry groups were lobbying for carve-outs on autos and machinery. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Handelsblatt urges Merz to copy Unicredit chief's clarity on economic reform**

The paper's morning briefing argues Merz filled health and chancellery posts competently after Jens Spahn's exit but still lacks a credible growth narrative, pointing to Andrea Orcel's direct investor communication as a model. The critique matters because markets and eastern voters are punishing vague coalition messaging even when personnel moves look orderly. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Eastern envoy says pandemic eroded democratic trust in Berlin debate on east-west divide**

Tagesspiegel quotes Eastern Commissioner Elisabeth Kaiser warning that Covid policy fallout is "poison for democracy" as a new YouGov survey shows persistent scepticism toward institutions among eastern residents. The intervention lands as Berlin parties compete for votes from citizens who commute across the former wall daily. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Queer couples say Germany's parentage law leaves non-biological mothers without legal status**

rbb24 follows a Berlin family where only one partner is recognised as mother despite both raising the child, forcing the second parent through costly adoption procedures. Advocates want automatic co-parent recognition at birth, arguing the current regime exposes families to custody and inheritance risks. ([rbb24][8])

* **Legionella outbreak at Tegel nursing home prompts review of resident deaths**

rbb24 reports contaminated water at a senior-care facility in Berlin-Tegel, with authorities examining whether several recent fatalities are linked to the bacteria. The case revives scrutiny of oversight at privately run homes serving Berlin's ageing population. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Intel posts fastest revenue growth in 15 years on AI data-centre demand**

Financial Times says the chipmaker's second-quarter sales jumped 25% as cloud operators accelerated orders for processors powering generative-AI workloads. The result offers a rare bright spot for a company that had been losing ground to Nvidia and custom silicon rivals. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Taiwan eases rules on foreign telecom satellites, opening door to Starlink**

Nikkei Asia reports Taipei streamlined licensing for non-domestic satellite operators, a move analysts read as preparation to welcome Elon Musk's Starlink despite cross-strait security sensitivities. The reform could harden Taiwan's communications resilience if undersea cables are disrupted. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Angolan court jails two Russians for terrorism and coup plot**

The Guardian says Lev Lakshtanov and Igor Ratchin received eight- and 11-year sentences over alleged plans to topple Luanda's government and organise deadly fuel-price protests last year. Moscow's expanding security footprint in Africa is increasingly colliding with local courts willing to convict foreign operatives. ([The Guardian][12])
