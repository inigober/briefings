# News Briefing — 23 November 2026

Spain's campaign narrows into a final week of tight polls, Germany's tax and pension fights test the coalition, Berlin's public services strain under demand, and an AI-driven sell-off keeps unsettling Asian markets.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **PSOE draws almost level with PP in the 40dB tracking poll as Vox slips**

EL PAÍS's daily tracking poll by the firm 40dB shows the Socialists (PSOE) close to a tie with the conservative PP a week before the 29 November election, while the far-right Vox has kept falling since the campaign began. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Rufián and Nogueras bring the Catalan independence rivalry into the campaign**

Gabriel Rufián of ERC and Míriam Nogueras of Junts, the congressional spokespeople of Catalonia's two big pro-independence parties, are fighting each other in the campaign in the bluntest terms yet, a feud that dates back to the procés, the Catalan secession push. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Galicia's BNG aims to unite the left vote for a historic result**

The nationalist Galician Bloc is trying to beat "dual voting", where Galicians back different parties in regional and general elections and which has historically cost it votes, and analysts and polls expect it to gain on 29 November. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Söder keeps pushing to offset "cold progression" despite resistance from the states**

CSU leader Markus Söder is pressing for compensation for cold progression, the effect by which inflation pushes wages into higher tax brackets, and is raising the pressure on fellow state premiers, with opposition coming from North Rhine-Westphalia and elsewhere. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Study: tightening part-time retirement rules would pay off for the treasury**

As Germany debates pension reform, a study finds that stricter rules on Altersteilzeit, the scheme that lets older employees cut their hours before retirement, would benefit the state budget and the labour market. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Housing shortage becomes a top fear for young Germans, but leaving home late is not the problem**

For the first time the lack of affordable housing ranks among young people's biggest worries about the future, though experts say the age at which they move out matters less than whether they are forced to stay. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Fifteen injured in Lichtenberg hospital fire, police suspect arson**

A patient allegedly set their bed alight in a Berlin hospital on Sunday evening, and smoke spread through other parts of the building, injuring 15 people. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Berlin's youth welfare offices say they are overstretched**

The Jugendämter, which support families and must act in cases of violence or neglect, are under heavy strain in Berlin, and two social workers tell rbb24 that missing resources leave them "with our backs to the wall". ([rbb24][8])

* **Associations again demand a firework ban in Berlin for New Year's Eve**

Dozens of people are treated in Berlin hospitals for severe injuries from firecrackers every New Year's Eve, and several associations are calling for a city-wide ban on fireworks. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Russian corvette sinks in Libya's Benghazi after suspected Ukrainian drone strike**

The Merkury, a Russian navy corvette that had stopped in the eastern Libyan port to refuel on its way back to Russia, was sunk in an early-morning explosion that eastern Libyan officials attribute to a sea drone, though no one has claimed responsibility. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Asian regulators rush to prop up markets as the AI trade falters**

Authorities across Asia are stepping in to support sagging stock markets, with the region's chipmakers and hardware suppliers especially vulnerable to the turn in sentiment around artificial intelligence. ([Financial Times][11])

* **India snaps up Venezuelan oil as US–India energy ties tighten**

New Delhi, one of the world's largest oil importers, is buying Venezuelan crude in a sign of closer energy ties with Washington. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
