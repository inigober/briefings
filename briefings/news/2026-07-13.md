# News Briefing — 13 July 2026

A looming European Court ruling on Catalonia's amnesty law, banking litigation over stranded Russia assets and a Berlin austerity election pitch frame a day when AI cost pressures push Indian firms toward Chinese models and a Bangkok nightclub fire exposes Asia's safety gaps.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **European Court amnesty ruling Thursday will shape Puigdemont's fate and Sánchez's legislature**

eldiario.es reports Luxembourg judges will decide whether Spain's 2023 procés amnesty complies with EU law, with the Constitutional Court waiting on the verdict before resolving Carles Puigdemont's appeals. Independentists hope the ruling clears prosecutions; conservatives argue a negative judgment could destabilise Pedro Sánchez's minority government. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When EU judges hold veto power over a law that bought Sánchez his majority, Catalonia's legal question becomes a countdown clock on the entire legislature.

* **Feijóo and PP leadership conceal party top-ups from mandatory congressional disclosures**

eldiario.es finds Alberto Núñez Feijóo and senior opposition figures have not declared possible PP stipends to Congress despite transparency rules requiring Permanent Commission members to report outside income. Voters will not see updated asset statements until after the 2027 general election under current filing calendars. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **PP mayor of Móstoles imputed for sexual and workplace harassment of former councillor**

EL PAÍS says a judge summoned Manuel Bautista for questioning on 9 October and five witnesses in the days after, formalising a probe into alleged coercion of an ex-party colleague. The case lands as the opposition leader faces separate scrutiny over undeclared party payments. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Deutsche Bank, HVB and Commerzbank sue Linde over hundreds of millions in Russia guarantees**

Handelsblatt reports three major lenders are demanding repayment after an industrial-gas project in Russia collapsed, triggering cross-default fights over who bears stranded exposure. The litigation shows how decade-old Russia bets still ripple through German balance sheets despite sanctions-era write-downs. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **SPD leaders break with coalition plan to gut freedom-of-information law**

Tagesspiegel says senior Social Democrats were never briefed on proposals to slash Germany's transparency statute and are now distancing themselves, raising doubts the reform can pass. Civil-society groups warn the cuts would shield ministerial correspondence and procurement decisions from routine scrutiny. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

💡 A transparency rollback that splits the governing parties signals how much administrative convenience the coalition will trade for open-government credibility.

* **Consumer agencies report surge in illegal telemarketing for gambling and energy tariffs**

Handelsblatt says thousands of Germans are complaining about cold calls pitching lotteries and power contracts despite bans, with regulators struggling to trace offshore diallers. The volume adds pressure on Berlin to tighten enforcement as households already face bill shocks. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Auditor probes corruption claims against head of Berlin's IT services agency**

Tagesspiegel reports Maria Borelli faces scrutiny after awarding multi-million-euro contracts without competitive tenders, prompting an external accountant to investigate "serious" allegations. The case tests whether the capital's digital modernisation drive can survive procurement controversies months before the Abgeordnetenhaus election. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **CDU candidate Evers opens campaign with austerity pitch; SPD rival Krach attacks school-meal cuts**

Berliner Zeitung says Kai Wegner's designated successor Kai Evers floated spending curbs including questioning subsidised school lunches, drawing a sharp rebuke from SPD lead candidate Raed Saleh. The exchange sets the fiscal tone for September's vote as both camps hunt undecided voters. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **Profile maps which Left candidates could enter a surging Berlin parliamentary caucus**

Tagesspiegel previews more than 30 possible Die Linke deputies after polls put the party ahead of a sliding CDU, describing internal debates over experience and Middle East positioning. Analysts say a strong Linke showing could force novel coalition arithmetic even if the CDU retains the mayoralty. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Indian firms trial Chinese large-language models as US AI bills climb**

Nikkei Asia reports companies across India are piloting DeepSeek and other PRC-built models to escape soaring OpenAI and Google API costs, treating geopolitical rivalry as a procurement problem. The shift tests Washington's export-control strategy as enterprises prioritise unit economics over vendor nationality. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

💡 When price-sensitive adopters route around US models via Chinese alternatives, AI decoupling becomes a margin calculation long before it becomes a security doctrine.

* **Bangkok nightclub fire kills 27 and leaves 22 critically injured**

The Guardian says smoke filled a popular tourist pub early Monday, trapping patrons near rear bathrooms as Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul visited the scene. Officials called it one of the deadliest leisure-venue blazes in the capital in years, reviving questions about fire-code enforcement in Thailand's nightlife districts. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Wall Street banks harvest record fees from SpaceX IPO pipeline and mega-mergers**

Financial Times reports top US lenders are riding a revival of large deals as AI optimism and resilient consumer spending fuel equity markets. Bankers say the fee bonanza contrasts with years of drought but depends on volatile tech listings holding their opening valuations. ([Financial Times][12])
