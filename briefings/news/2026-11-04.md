# News Briefing — 04 November 2026

A Democratic sweep of Congress that rattled AI-exposed markets, a Spanish transport minister quitting over two rail disasters, and Berlin's stalled coalition arithmetic make this an edition about leaders and institutions absorbing the cost of failure.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Transport minister Óscar Puente resigns over Spain's rail disasters and will not stand in the 29 November election, against Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's wishes**

Puente, who ran the ministry through two fatal rail accidents and more than 100 deaths in under a year (most recently the Urda collision), says he is stepping aside so the campaign does not "pivot" on his responsibility, though Sánchez had wanted him to stay and several ministers think the move could revive the left. ([EL PAÍS][1]; [eldiario.es][2])

💡 The exit turns a safety failure into an electoral calculation: the government is trying to take the crash out of the campaign rather than defend its record on it.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany faces one of its biggest defence-procurement choices of the coming years: who builds the next-generation fighter jet, and with which partners — even as drones reshape what air forces buy**

Handelsblatt's security podcast asks whether the country needs another crewed combat aircraft when almost every military is rushing to buy drones. ([Handelsblatt][3])

* **Demolition crews blow up the crumbling B 192 bridge in Waren (Müritz), clearing the way for its replacement**

The old south bridge on the federal road in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern was brought down in a controlled demolition on Sunday, secured by about 200 personnel — one more of Germany's ageing structures to be rebuilt. ([Berliner Zeitung][4])

* **The "cocaine sailor" trial in Rostock will run until February 2027 after the court asked for more evidence**

The case concerns a drug seizure at sea; judges want a second Portuguese investigator to testify and new detail on how the vessel was boarded. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's Greens and SPD reject the CDU's request for exploratory coalition talks**

The conservative CDU had invited both parties to explore a coalition it would lead; the refusal comes while the SPD, Greens and Linke are negotiating a red-green-red government of their own. ([rbb24][6])

* **Berlin HIV practices fear the health-insurance reform will leave PrEP counselling unfunded**

PrEP is the daily preventive medication that sharply cuts the risk of HIV infection; the reform of statutory health insurance changes how the required counselling is billed, and some Berlin practices have already stopped taking new PrEP patients. ([rbb24][7])

* **Conductor Christian Thielemann says Berlin's educated middle class, the Bildungsbürgertum, still exists but in ever smaller circles**

The music director of the Staatskapelle (the Berlin State Opera orchestra) talks about empty rows, Richard Strauss and an upcoming Asia tour. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

## World 🌐

* **Democrats win the US Senate and House in the midterms, a sharp rebuke to President Donald Trump two years into his term**

The result strips Trump's Republicans of control of Congress and, per EL PAÍS, includes the party's first statewide win in Texas in more than three decades, with state legislator James Talarico taking a Senate seat with 59% of the vote. ([EL PAÍS][9]; [Handelsblatt][10])

* **Trump says the US will stop its roughly $4bn in annual aid to Israel but keep selling it weapons**

The US president, speaking to Fox on election day, said Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had raised the idea, ending a funding stream that has been a recurring topic in the midterm campaign. ([eldiario.es][11]; [Financial Times][12])

* **Global stocks and the dollar slide, with Japan's Nikkei sinking, as Democratic gains revive fears of a slower US AI build-out**

Traders are betting a Democratic Congress could slow the data-centre and infrastructure spending that has underpinned growth. ([Financial Times][13]; [Bloomberg][14])
