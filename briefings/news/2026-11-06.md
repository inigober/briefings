# News Briefing — 06 November 2026

A bribery claim against the PP's campaign, Audi's escape clause in Georgia and a Paraguayan president felled by a bank collapse show institutions tested by money, while Washington's Democratic win keeps unsettling AI-linked markets.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Jailed Koldo García says the PP's lawyer offered him up to €1m to lie against Sánchez, and the PSOE is weighing a criminal complaint**

Koldo García, the imprisoned former aide to ex-transport minister José Luis Ábalos, claims the conservative Popular Party's lawyer, Alberto Durán, offered him up to €1m, a way to avoid prison and removal of his brother and partner from the case in return for false information about the government; Sánchez has demanded explanations from PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Spain will raise the minimum wage to €18,004 a year in 2027 and lift the tax-free deduction so those earners pay no income tax**

The Finance Ministry will update the income-tax (IRPF) deduction for minimum-wage earners to €1,030, so the 2027 rise in the statutory minimum wage (SMI) is not clawed back by tax. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **The Iryo train in the Urda crash passed a full inspection of its signalling equipment two days before the collision**

Staff of Hitachi Rail, the signalling supplier, checked the private operator Iryo's train at Renfe's Madrid workshops just before the accident that has since cost the transport minister his job. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **IG Metall widens warning strikes as pay talks for 3.7 million metal and electrical workers stall**

Germany's biggest industrial union says there has been no real movement since 27 October, so it is extending short stoppages before talks resume on Monday in Düsseldorf. ([Berliner Zeitung][4])

* **Audi can still walk away from its new Georgia plant after signing, contract papers show**

Documents seen by Handelsblatt reveal the Volkswagen luxury brand kept an exit option on its planned US factory while also reserving room to expand it, a hedge that shows how uncertain US trade policy remains for German carmakers. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Water demand of German data centres could more than double by 2030, an Öko-Institut study warns**

The study by the environmental research institute links the AI boom to rising water stress and puts alternatives to the heavy water use of new mega data centres on the agenda. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's red-green-red coalition agreement will not be ready before January**

The Linke had hoped to present the deal with the SPD and Greens before Christmas, but all three parties now accept that timetable was too ambitious given several remaining hurdles. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Investigations into SPD state chief Steffen Krach are paused now that he sits in the state parliament**

Krach became a member of the Abgeordnetenhaus last week, and the immunity that comes with the seat stops prosecutors from continuing their inquiries for now. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Firas Rammo, the clan boss's son who confronted Linke MP Ferat Koçak after the Berlin election, faces another criminal trial**

Rammo, son of clan figure Issa Rammo, became nationally known after the vote for putting Koçak under pressure and now faces a court case again. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Paraguay's president Santiago Peña resigns after a bank collapse freezes millions of savings**

Peña's rightwing government had let Ueno Bank, an obscure digital lender that claimed about 60% of Paraguayan adults as customers, expand drastically before it failed and dragged down the country's banking system. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Foreign Policy argues the Israeli election is not a deadlock and Gadi Eisenkot should take his chance to govern**

An opinion piece contends that Palestinian voters in Israel's election have handed Eisenkot a workable path to a coalition, rejecting the "deadlock" label after exit polls showed no majority for either bloc. ([Foreign Policy][11])

* **Silicon Valley's backing of Trump backfires in the midterms, and tech executives are plotting a new political strategy**

The Financial Times reports that AI executives who allied with the president are now recalibrating after Democrats' sweep, as new officials pledge to curb data-centre building. ([Financial Times][12])
