# News Briefing — 29 August 2026

Madrid is racing Ceuta returns, Germany is hiring spies at a games fair, Berlin will let teenagers vote, and Washington is reaching for Venezuelan oil while closing Iran's books in Dubai.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Madrid wants every migrant off Ceuta's streets in two weeks — and most of them sent back**

The government is compressing asylum rejections and returns into a fortnight, with police also processing files online from outside the enclave, so that people sleeping rough can be cleared and, in most cases, removed. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The reception fight is being solved as a paperwork surge: once the file is closed, the street is meant to empty.

* **Congress still has eight flagship laws stuck as the legislature's last year begins**

Spain's gag-law reform, the families law and the official-secrets statute are among bills stranded in the lower house; the government also wants to revive a penal-code change that would drop insults to the Crown and to religious feelings. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Albares says Italy's cold shoulder on Ceuta breaks the EU, and that a hoax lit the fuse**

Foreign minister José Manuel Albares has accused Italy — and Denmark — of a lack of solidarity over the late-July crossing, and blamed a social-media falsehood, amplified by what he calls a "reactionary international" around Elon Musk, for triggering it. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany's foreign intelligence service is recruiting at Gamescom — with a game**

The BND, Germany's overseas spy agency, is on the floor at Cologne's games fair pitching itself to a generation it cannot reach with a careers pamphlet, using a game as the bait. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **St Pauli's own fans have published abuse inside their scene**

A supporter group at FC St Pauli, the Hamburg club that sells itself as a left-wing moral brand, has released cases of sexual, physical and psychological violence in its own ranks. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **September brings station alcohol bans and new checkout labels**

From the turn of the month, drinking will be banned at the first railway stations, and shoppers will start seeing new product labels at the till — a consumer-rule changeover, not a new statute. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A fire in a Spandau refugee shelter has forced hundreds out**

Flames on the fourth floor emptied the building on Saturday morning; some residents now have to move into emergency housing. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Berliners under 18 will vote for the city parliament on 20 September**

For the first time, 16- and 17-year-olds may take part in the Abgeordnetenhaus election, a franchise change whose effect on the Red Town Hall is still an open question. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

💡 Lowering the voting age is a one-time electorate rewrite just weeks before the race, not a youth-politics gesture.

* **The Berlin–Hamburg line is shut again this weekend for leftover works**

After the main renovation, Deutsche Bahn is doing final optimisation; travellers face diversions and replacement buses. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump says the US will take majority control of Venezuela's oil**

Donald Trump announced an oil agreement with interim president Delcy Rodríguez under which the United States would have “majority” control of 65 billion barrels of proven reserves, brokered, he said, by his secretary of state and defence secretary through private business. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 After abducting Nicolás Maduro in January, the remaining prize is the reservoir, not the presidency.

* **Washington's isolation of Iran is closing in on Dubai**

For decades the Gulf city has been the offshore hub for Iranian finance, trade and access to global markets; that remaining valve is now the target of the US campaign. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 Sanctions that stop at the border leave a city-state loophole; this is an attempt to close the books, not just the strait.

* **Nvidia's numbers still look like 1980s Japan**

Foreign Policy argues that formidable quarterly earnings at the AI-chip champion do not settle whether the boom is an investment cycle or a bubble — and that Japan's asset mania is the better historical rhyme than a soft landing. ([Foreign Policy][12])
