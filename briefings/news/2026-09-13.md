# News Briefing — 13 September 2026

A timed pledge to clear Ceuta's overflow sites, a German asylum-rate twist, and a forced Tata listing set the frame on a thin Sunday in Berlin.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Madrid's Ceuta commander says the overflow sites will be eased within days**

Ángel Víctor Torres — the territorial-policy minister now running the single command in Spain's North African enclave — says the camps at El Trampolín, El Príncipe and Loma Colmenar will be relieved "in a matter of days," after a month of street chaos he called a miscalculation when 70,000 people were assumed gone. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 A date on clearance is the first operational pledge, not another tour of the fences.

* **Sumar is using Ceuta to delay naming a candidate and a brand**

The left alliance in Pedro Sánchez's coalition says it would be unseemly to unveil its next general-election ticket and label while the enclave is unresolved, and some voices now talk about slipping that announcement into October. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Sánchez's government says a “deep state” is trying to bring it down**

After another bruising week, the executive reads parts of the Supreme Court, the CNI intelligence service, the police and the Civil Guard as working to force its fall — and is treating a visible fix in Ceuta as the way to breathe. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Asylum applications are down — and the share winning protection is up**

Fewer people are filing for asylum in Germany, but a higher fraction receive protection status; Handelsblatt flags a European Court of Justice ruling as more important to that shift than a new criminal-law tweak. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The protection rate is moving on Luxembourg case law, not only on who arrives.

* **Lower Saxony votes locally under a VW crisis and AfD exclusions**

About six million people in the northern state — home to Volkswagen's main works — elect councils a year before the Landtag vote, with several AfD candidates already struck from the lists. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Nivea's boss does not want cosmetics to become the next car industry**

Vincent Warnery, who runs Beiersdorf — the Hamburg group behind Nivea — says EU product rules are squeezing the cosmetics trade the way regulation and China squeezed German cars, and wants relief for an industry that still employs at scale. ([Handelsblatt][6])

💡 He is asking for a carve-out before cosmetics inherits the auto playbook, not after the factories go.

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The police are holding an open day — including for children**

Berlin's force is opening stations to show how special-deployment units and service dogs work — a public look at the units that usually stay off the street until a raid. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Autumn bird traffic is back in the Stadtgebiet**

Cranes, geese and starlings are staging through Berlin and the belt around it; a Tagesspiegel field guide maps the watch sites inside the city, not only in Brandenburg. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Alba lost its last friendly before the Bundesliga tip-off**

The city's basketball club — which plays at the Uber Arena — fell to Spain's Monbus Obradoiro in the final tune-up. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **India's central bank has ordered Tata Sons to list**

The Reserve Bank of India rejected the holding company's appeal against a forced public listing, opening what could be the country's largest IPO for Tata Sons — the unlisted parent of the Tata group. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 Forcing the house conglomerate onto the market is a regulatory power play, not a voluntary capital raise.

* **Fujitsu will export AI chips built on its supercomputer line**

The Japanese computing group is sending processors derived from its supercomputer technology to the United States and Asia — a hardware export, not another model-lab note. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Energy inflation is complicating the Fed's next rate move**

PNC's chief investment officer told Bloomberg that oil-driven prices are pushing markets toward higher-for-longer US rates, and that another tightening step could be a policy error while the Iran war drags. ([Bloomberg][12])
