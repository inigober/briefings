# News Briefing — 15 September 2026

Madrid sets a year-end clock on Ceuta, Berlin's districts reject a Senate street decree, and China writes exit controls while drought tightens the Panama Canal.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Sánchez has given himself until year-end to 'fix' Ceuta**

Pedro Sánchez, Spain's prime minister, told laSexta he now has until the end of 2026 to resolve the crisis in Ceuta — the North African enclave still holding last July's crossing — the first dated horizon after weeks of overflow camps, and he dismissed Pegasus-spyware fears as a "hoax" that should not drive Morocco policy. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Torres promised relief "in days"; the prime minister is selling a calendar that runs to December.

* **A Galician employment minister's family winery was fined for undocumented harvest labour**

Inspectors found two undocumented African workers at the Ribeiro vineyard of the family firm tied to José González Vázquez, Galicia's employment and migration minister; the company blames a temp agency and says it filed a complaint it will not produce. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Gallardón will testify as the Canal Isabel II 'Lezo' trial opens**

Alberto Ruiz-Gallardón, the former Madrid regional president, appears as a witness over the 2001 purchase of Colombian water firm Inassa by Canal de Isabel II, the region's public water company; prosecutors want prison for the directors who approved the deal. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Klingbeil sits down with UniCredit's Orcel over Commerzbank**

Lars Klingbeil, Germany's finance minister, meets Andrea Orcel as Italy's UniCredit still circles Commerzbank — the Frankfurt lender in which the Bund remains a shareholder — and Handelsblatt argues Berlin has only one sensible way still to come out ahead. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The first meeting is about whether the state still wants a German-controlled bank, not a courtesy call.

* **BMW's iX3 has 100,000 European orders in year one**

The electric iX3, first model of BMW's Neue Klasse architecture, has set a company record for first-year orders in Europe — a demand datapoint while much of German auto still talks about China and cuts. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **A satellite is being built to read plant stress from orbit**

Flex, a European Earth-observation mission, is meant to pick up the faint fluorescence that shows whether crops and forests are wilting under heat, pollution or disease — a remote-sensing play, not a farm subsidy. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Districts say Bonde's anti-bollard decree is unlawful**

Ute Bonde, Berlin's transport senator, wants bollards used only as a last resort to calm neighbourhood streets; most Bezirke say the Senate draft is unusable and legally offside — a fight over who controls Kiez traffic design. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

💡 Five days before the Abgeordnetenhaus vote, the Senate is picking a fight with the boroughs over street furniture.

* **Chialo will not be fined for staying silent in the grants inquiry**

Joe Chialo, the former culture senator already in the antisemitism-grants file, refused to answer the Untersuchungsausschuss; the committee wanted an Ordnungsgeld and has now been told it cannot levy one. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berlin treats children in bars as normal**

A Berliner feature argues the city lets kids into adult rooms — bars, clubs, galleries — in a way other capitals still police, and asks whether that is civic culture or a lapse. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Panama will cut canal transits again as El Niño drought deepens**

The Panama Canal, which carries about 5% of world seaborne trade and about 40% of US containers, will tighten daily slots further as reservoir levels fall — a climate limit on the shortcut between Asia and the US East Coast. ([The Guardian][10])

* **China has passed a sweeping law on who may travel abroad**

A new statute tightens exit controls in the name of state secrets, advanced technology and skilled workers — an administrative lock on people, not only on chips. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 Beijing is treating passports as a technology-security tool.

* **Trump is being read as having killed 'strategic ambiguity' on Taiwan**

Foreign Policy argues that after the Trump–Xi track, it is no longer unclear how Washington would defend Taipei — it is doubtful it would help at all, ending the old US habit of keeping Beijing guessing. ([Foreign Policy][12])
