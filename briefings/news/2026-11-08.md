# News Briefing — 08 November 2026

Spain's campaign pivots to economic homework and coalition arithmetic, Germany feels the AI boom in electronics prices and Patriot stockpiles, and from New Zealand to Israel the day's elections end without a clear governing majority.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo says a coalition with the far-right Vox "does not depend on me" and promises to repeal the government's housing decrees**

In an EL PAÍS interview the People's Party (PP) candidate, the front-runner for the 29 November election, also says he would not block a criminal proposal against Pedro Sánchez from the next attorney general. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Whoever wins on 29 November inherits a growing economy with falling unemployment and debt, but urgent structural gaps in housing, infrastructure, the budget and pensions**

EL PAÍS sets out the post-election economic to-do list for the party or coalition that manages to form a government. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **The Frente Amplio, the new left alliance fronted by former Barcelona mayor Ada Colau, targets 10% of the vote to double its seats**

Sources inside the alliance put current support at about 7.5% and argue it can climb during the three-week campaign, positioning itself as the guarantee of a progressive government. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Memory-chip shortages caused by the global AI build-out are pushing up the price of laptops, phones and other hardware just before Christmas**

Producers have throttled output while AI data centres soak up supply, so the cost is passing to German shoppers in the busiest retail season. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Defence minister Boris Pistorius says Germany has reached its limit on further Patriot air-defence deliveries to Ukraine and calls on allies to step up**

"Mehr geht gerade nicht" ("no more is possible right now"), he said, a sign that Berlin's own stockpiles constrain how much more it can send. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

* **A trial opens in Aachen over an April attack in which a man allegedly threw explosives in an ICE high-speed train near Siegburg, injuring ten passengers**

Two men intervened to prevent worse; the suspect's motive remains unclear. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Twenty-five years after Seeed's hit "Dickes B.", the Tagesspiegel measures what is left of the "Mama Berlin" the song celebrated**

The 2001 dancehall anthem from the Berlin band becomes a yardstick, line by line, for how the city has changed. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The English-language Berlin news show 20% Berlin devotes its latest edition to Left-party figure Elif Eralp and the "Gen Z Regelt" protest**

The episode, posted without video, also covers a sentencing; it is a recap rather than breaking news. ([20% Berlin][8])

* **A Cold War bunker in the Brandenburg forest where East Germany's interior ministry would have kept working in wartime is now open for guided tours**

This is a regional item, as Berlin-proper coverage was thin today; an organiser explains how the command post would have functioned. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **New Zealand faces weeks of uncertainty after an election too close to call**

Prime minister Christopher Luxon's National party is the largest, and on preliminary results could govern with the libertarian Act and populist New Zealand First parties, but only one seat separates his bloc from a majority, and uncounted votes and small parties could upend it. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Israel's president asks opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to form a government after the parliamentary election**

Eisenkot, a former army chief of staff, is 13 seats short of a majority and would need either Arab or ultra-Orthodox parties, each option carrying heavy political risk. ([Handelsblatt][11])

* **The slump in AI-linked stocks after the US midterms is sending investors back to China and India, which are seen as beneficiaries of easing inflation and trade tensions**

Bloomberg says out-of-favour Asian equities are being revived as the AI trade unwinds. ([Bloomberg][12])

* **Spain's Urda rail crash:** A former rail-accident investigator calls for more training of rail staff and an urgent audit of the system.
* **US politics:** Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff is emerging as the betting favourite for the 2028 presidential nomination.
* **Bird flu:** An outbreak killed about 6,000 turkeys in Lalendorf, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern.
