# News Briefing — 28 November 2026

On Spain's day of electoral silence, party finances and poll uncertainty frame Sunday's vote, while Germany's pension and far-right-cooperation fights, Berlin's security aftermath and Iran's shipping attacks show states struggling to hold their lines.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Sánchez closes his campaign in front of more than 13,000 supporters in Madrid's Caja Mágica**

The Socialist prime minister filled the arena, which the conservative Popular Party (PP) could not fill two days earlier, and told supporters "we will do it again" as the PSOE bets on a late comeback against polls that favour a PP–Vox majority. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Vox's economics spokesman puts €200bn of welfare cuts on the table while the PP proposes tax relief for top earners**

Vox, the far-right party that borrows Argentine president Javier Milei's chainsaw rhetoric, signalled the scale of its planned spending cuts three days before the vote, while the PP's proposed relief for high incomes would leave a roughly €50bn hole in public accounts. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Poll modeller Kiko Llaneras maps the surprises that could still upset the expected PP–Vox majority**

EL PAÍS's data columnist says a PP and Vox majority is the most likely result but that the margin of uncertainty also holds scenarios ranging from a Sánchez comeback to a rightward wave. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Labour minister Schweitzer calls his pension draft a "hard compromise", but the CDU/CSU says it is only a first step**

The unagreed draft from the Social Democrat minister, meant to settle a long coalition fight over Germany's state pension, drew no deal from the Union, so the dispute continues. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **The BSW party congress in Dresden faces its first major conflict over how to deal with the AfD**

The Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance (BSW), a left-populist party named after its founder, meets for its fourth federal congress, and its stance on the far-right AfD could affect government formation in Saxony-Anhalt. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Germany's Mittelstand turns more optimistic despite the Iran war, a new study finds**

The mid-sized family firms that form the backbone of German industry report brighter prospects in several sectors for the coming months, a sign that the economic outlook is improving. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's red–green–red coalition negotiators take over a row about recording racism against Palestinians and weight-based discrimination**

A proposal to add a new racism category, which alarmed even some Social Democrats a week ago, has been moved from the working group to the chief negotiators of the planned SPD–Green–Left government. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Ten years after the Christmas-market attack, about €16.5m has gone to those affected by terror attacks**

As the anniversary of the 2016 Islamist attack on Berlin's Breitscheidplatz approaches, many victims have received help, though the support system also drew criticism. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Berlin's Senate lags in dealing with a hack that stole millions of files from two departments**

The hacker group published the stolen files in early September, but the Senate has still not worked out how many people were affected or what damage was done. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Iran has hit at least six ships twice in two months to deter traffic through the Strait of Hormuz**

The "double tapping" tactic, a second strike on a vessel already hit, targets the narrow Gulf channel that carries much of the world's oil, according to the Financial Times. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Puma Shen, a China-sanctioned critic of Beijing, could become Taipei's next mayor**

Shen, a Taiwanese legislator sanctioned by China for efforts to prepare civilians for an invasion, could become a rising political star if he wins the capital's mayoral race. ([The New York Times][11])

* **Mexican airport operator GAP raises 7.8bn pesos for an energy and infrastructure trust, less than planned**

Grupo Aeroportuario del Pacífico raised about $398m for its Fibra vehicle, a listed trust that holds infrastructure assets, after months of delays and below its original target. ([Bloomberg][12])
