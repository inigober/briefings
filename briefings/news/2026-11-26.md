# News Briefing — 26 November 2026

Three days before Spain votes, parties fight over 47 undecided seats, German wage talks and a cobalt export block test industry, and new US chip curbs and an Asian rate hike unsettle the AI trade.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Forty-seven Congress seats remain in play three days before the election**

ElDiario.es's seat model rates 303 of the 350 seats as safe or probable, leaving 47 open across 36 provinces; since the last polls, the Socialists (PSOE) have gained about a point in the polling average and far-right Vox has lost half a point, leaving Vox on 52 safe-or-probable seats (four fewer) and PSOE on 98 (two more). ([eldiario.es][1])

* **A power struggle in the Spanish Catholic hierarchy surfaces over how to treat the 29 November vote**

A plenary assembly of the Episcopal Conference, the bishops' governing body, debated the Church's stance on the election and exposed rival camps of anti-Sánchez, reformist and moderate bishops, with staff describing "absolute misrule". Its president emerged "damaged" after failing to impose his line. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Andalusian nationalist leader "Er Gafa" bets on mass rallies to take Adelante Andalucía into Congress**

José Ignacio García, who leads the regional party Adelante Andalucía, is repeating the rally-driven strategy that lifted it in regional elections, aiming at young voters and those disillusioned with the PSOE. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Pay talks in the metal and electrical industry run through the night without a deal**

Negotiators for the industry, which employs millions of Germans and is the bellwether for wage settlements elsewhere, had still not reached an agreement by early morning, with workers waiting for a result. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Congo's block on cobalt exports, even for already-approved volumes, leaves German manufacturers exposed**

Congo, a leading supplier of the battery metal, has stopped approved shipments until further notice, and how hard German industry will be hit cannot yet be quantified, so attention turns to Berlin's response. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

* **Berlin start-up A11 launches a European alternative to Palantir's AI software**

Palantir, the US data-analytics group, is criticised in Germany in part for its work with US security agencies and the military; A11 now offers companies a European option. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Housing association chief says the Left-led coalition's new-build plan only works at €24 per square metre in rent**

After the property industry warned against a Left election win, the first plan on rents and construction is out, and the head of Berlin's largest housing association federation argues the numbers would require rents of €24 per square metre. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Deutsche Bahn widens its Siemensbahn test and announces tree clearing**

The railway is expanding trials on the Siemensbahn, the S-Bahn line meant to serve Spandau's Siemensstadt development, though the timetable for the first works remains tight. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Images of the Hera asteroid mission's impact site are presented in Berlin**

Hera follows a 2022 probe that was deliberately crashed into an asteroid to change its orbit, a test of planetary defence; its photographs of the impact site are now on show in the city. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Nvidia halts chip shipments to south-east Asia as the US tightens export controls**

Nvidia, the dominant maker of AI chips, stopped deliveries to the region after Washington stepped up rules meant to keep Chinese firms from reaching advanced AI computing power. ([Financial Times][10])

* **Bank of Korea raises its benchmark interest rate to 3.25% against a strong economy**

South Korea's central bank tightened policy again as growth stays robust, a counterpoint to the weakening mood in global tech stocks. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Australian firms expect inflation above 4% for a year despite five rate rises**

Businesses surveyed expect price growth to stay well above the 2–3% target band of the Reserve Bank of Australia, the central bank, which has raised rates five times this year. ([Bloomberg][12])
