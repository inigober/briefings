# News Briefing — 1 August 2026

Ceuta's mass return of nearly 50,000 crossers, a judge's move toward implicating the PSOE in a party-finance probe, and Germany's river drought frame a day when Berlin's state-election campaign begins and a record Ebola surge tests global health capacity.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Nearly 48,000 migrants voluntarily return to Morocco after Ceuta surge**

La Vanguardia reports that Interior says almost all of the roughly 50,000 people who crossed into the Spanish enclave this week have left, with Madrid describing departures as voluntary after the city was overwhelmed. The exodus eases immediate pressure on reception services but leaves unanswered how Spain will deter another breach at Europe's only land border with Africa. ([La Vanguardia][1])

* **Judge opens door to implicating PSOE in Leire Díez party-finance probe**

National Court magistrate Santiago Pedraz authorised Guardia Civil access to six Socialist Party bank accounts as part of the influence-peddling case centred on lobbyist Leire Díez, a step that could lead to formal charges against the party itself. The ruling widens a scandal that has already implicated senior officials and tests Pedro Sánchez's claim that donations were isolated misconduct. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 When judges green-light party-account scrutiny, corruption probes stop being about individual operators and start threatening the governing party's legal personhood — a threshold Spanish socialists have not faced in a decade.

* **Spotify chart manipulation draws bets on which song tops the platform**

EL PAÍS investigates how gambling markets now let punters wager on which track will reach number one on Spotify, creating incentives to inflate streaming counts fraudulently. The piece frames chart integrity as a regulatory blind spot as music platforms become financial instruments. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Historic Rhine low water throttles freight as sandbars strand barges**

Handelsblatt says drought has pushed river levels to record lows, forcing cargo vessels to sail partly empty through narrow channels and raising costs for chemicals, grain and industrial inputs that normally move by water. The bottleneck exposes how climate stress on inland shipping can hit factory supply chains faster than policymakers can reroute freight to rail. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Fatal storm triggers cleanup across Germany as emergency crews assess damage**

Tagesspiegel reports one person died when a tree fell during Thursday's severe weather and several others were seriously injured as squalls uprooted trees and flooded streets from the southwest to the northeast. Authorities are still cataloguing damage, but the episode adds to a summer of extreme-weather claims on insurers and infrastructure. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Subsidised pension depot from 2027 aims to turn young savers into equity investors**

Tagesspiegel cites ING Germany's chief Lars Stoy arguing that a state-backed "early-start pension" product launching in 2027 could broaden capital-market participation if banks simplify onboarding. The reform is part of a broader push to supplement pay-as-you-go pensions as demographics shrink the contributor base. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Prosecutors charge 70-year-old Berliner with coordinating two-tonne cocaine shipment**

The Berliner reports that investigators accuse a pensioner of pre-financing and organising the transport of 2.2 tonnes of cocaine, one of the largest alleged import plots linked to the capital in recent years. The case underscores how ageing facilitators can still anchor trans-European drug logistics. ([The Berliner][7])

* **State-election parties begin hanging tens of thousands of campaign posters**

Tagesspiegel says Berlin's parliamentary parties started mounting outdoor advertising on Sunday ahead of the Abgeordnetenhaus vote, blanketing streets with competing promises on housing, transport and security. The visual saturation marks the formal opening of a contest that will test whether the governing coalition can hold against a fragmented opposition. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Experts ask whether Brandenburg forests could face Mediterranean-scale wildfires**

Tagesspiegel interviews specialists on whether pine stands around Berlin could burn with southern-European intensity as heat and drought stress the capital's surrounding state. The question matters for a metro region that depends on nearby woodlands for recreation and whose fire services are already stretched by summer blazes. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Congo Ebola outbreak becomes fastest-growing in recorded history**

The Guardian reports confirmed cases have passed 3,360 across five eastern provinces with nearly 1,500 deaths and a case-fatality rate of about 45%, outpacing prior Central African waves. Health workers face the additional hurdle that a vaccine for the Bundibugyo strain may still be months away. ([The Guardian][10])

* **South Korean won rallies nearly 8% against the dollar despite tech sell-off**

The Financial Times says the won was one of July's best-performing major currencies even as Samsung and SK Hynix shares tumbled on AI-chip anxiety, suggesting capital is rotating within Korean assets rather than fleeing wholesale. The divergence complicates Seoul's efforts to manage export competitiveness and financial stability simultaneously. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Japan's NTT Data launches payments platform in India**

Nikkei Asia reports the IT services group has rolled out a digital payments product in India, betting on rising transaction volumes as the country digitises retail and government disbursements. The move typifies how Japanese firms are chasing growth in South Asia as domestic markets age. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
