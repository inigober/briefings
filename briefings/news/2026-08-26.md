# News Briefing — 26 August 2026

Spain is writing rules for data centres and asylum, Germany is arguing over courts and industry, Berlin's local map is shifting, and the world is reallocating AI talent amid violence in Haiti and the West Bank.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain wants a European licence for data centres — and 80% new renewable power**

A draft royal decree would make large data centres, the warehouses of the AI boom, meet a European operating certificate and cover 80% of their electricity with new renewable plants, turning a fashionable land grab into a grid-and-sovereignty permitting test. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The AI build-out is now being forced through Spain's electricity system as a licensing problem, not just a tech-park story.

* **Spain has 2.5 million unemployed and still cannot fill about 160,000 jobs**

Unfilled posts are stuck near 160,000 even with mass unemployment, a mismatch of low pay, ageing, weak candidates and a housing market that asks more than €1,500 in rent for jobs advertised at about €1,300 gross. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The vacancy paradox is a housing-and-pay constraint, not a shortage of people.

* **New asylum and immigration bills would speed expulsions and allow confinement**

Draft extranjería and asylum laws approved by the government tighten guarantees for children and other vulnerable migrants, but they also shorten removal timelines, restrict the right to asylum, and contemplate confinement and internal redistribution. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Germany's failed constitutional-court nominee is still waiting for an apology**

Frauke Brosius-Gersdorf, the jurist whose Constitutional Court bid collapsed, used a television encounter with Union floor leader Thorsten Frei to press for an apology he would not give, keeping the fight over judicial appointments inside party politics. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **France and Germany are colliding over how far to protect European industry**

Paris wants stronger "Made in Europe" rules for industry; Berlin warns that the same turn could start new trade fights, so the EU's green-and-competitive agenda is stuck on how much protection the single market can bear. ([Politico Europe][5])

* **Sachsen-Anhalt's 6 September vote is turning a thin eastern state into a political laboratory**

Before the Landtag election, Handelsblatt frames Sachsen-Anhalt as UNESCO heritage, pop export and a test bed for coalition arithmetic in a state where the usual western party map no longer fits. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg's next mayor will be a man — and from the East**

For 22 of the district's 26 years a woman has run the borough; this time every candidate is male and from the East, a representation break in a district that still sells itself as Kreuzberg's progressive brand. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **War munition is to be detonated at Borgsdorf, with 2,800 people in the blast radius**

Ordnance from the last war is being blown up in woodland at Borgsdorf, just north of the city, a civil-protection operation that will affect about 2,800 people in Berlin's commuter belt. ([rbb24][8])

* **A Berlin social worker has been promoted to Bundesliga referee**

Richard Hempel, 28, a trained social worker, is moving up to officiate in Germany's top flight and describes running a match as 90 minutes of social work — a small labour-market story from the city's amateur-to-elite pipeline. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **US visa policy is pushing the next AI founders away from America**

Tighter immigration rules are deterring international researchers from building AI companies in the United States, a talent leak Rest of World says other countries, including China, can use to catch up. ([Rest of World][10])

💡 Washington is treating visas as industrial policy — and handing the rejected pipeline to rivals.

* **A Haitian gang massacre has reached a once-quiet hillside above Port-au-Prince**

Armed men killed at least 47 people and kidnapped more than 50 in Kenscoff, a community in the hills above the capital; a gang leader has threatened to kill the hostages if the state kills any of his men while trying to retake the area. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Mike Huckabee is using the Bible against Israeli settlers**

Mike Huckabee, the US ambassador to Israel and a long-time defender of settlements, has now invoked scripture against settler violence in the West Bank, a rare public brake from inside Trump's camp. ([Foreign Policy][12])

💡 A settlement ally reaching for the Bible as a restraint is a crack in the usual US talking points, not a conversion.
