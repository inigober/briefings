# News Briefing — 19 September 2026

A Ceuta shelter inquiry, a Sahara alignment, Berlin's last campaign day under a police tally, and OpenAI's $280bn cash-burn number set the frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Prosecutors are investigating abuse in a Ceuta girls' shelter**

Spain's public prosecutor has opened a minors-court file on alleged beatings, threats and blocked healthcare at a state-run shelter for migrant girls in Ceuta, the North African enclave still holding July's crossing. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 After weeks of beach camps, the charge is now against the reception system itself.

* **Morocco has locked in US and Israeli backing for its Sahara claim**

Rabat has sealed an alignment with Washington and Israel that treats Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara as the only acceptable outcome — a squeeze on the file Spain still officially hedges. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **A hospital meeting has undone Ayuso's waitlist story**

Managers at Madrid's Ramón y Cajal hospital told surgeons they agreed to downgrade "preferente" surgery patients to "normal" — the list-shortening Isabel Díaz Ayuso, the regional premier, had pinned on a whistleblowing doctor. ([EL PAÍS][3])

💡 The minutes contradict the president's named culprit.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Most asylum claims in Germany now go to a fast track**

The new EU asylum rulebook puts a majority of applicants into accelerated procedures, and people who appeal a rejection can be deported before a court has ruled. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The right to appeal no longer pauses the plane.

* **Germany's cooperative banks are opening a crypto counter**

Volksbanken, the mutual-bank network that still holds a large share of German deposits, are preparing their own bitcoin and crypto offers to keep younger customers from fintechs. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **The EV sale is now a monthly rate, not a sticker price**

Germans rarely pay cash for an electric car; the decision is a lease or finance payment around €280 a month — and the contracts get expensive if you have a child or a dog. ([Die Zeit][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Police have logged about 500 incidents in the Berlin campaign**

As the Abgeordnetenhaus — Berlin's state parliament — votes on Sunday, police report some 500 cases of damaged posters and attacks on campaign helpers. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **The airport express will stop starting from Hauptbahnhof**

From 2027 the FEX airport train will no longer leave Berlin Hauptbahnhof, with weeks of services terminating at Südkreuz and a later S-Bahn cut to BER falling in the summer holidays. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **Katharina Günther-Wünsch is running Berlin's schools as a performance project**

The CDU education senator — a former athlete and medical student, and Mayor Kai Wegner's partner — has pushed a high-tempo, results-first overhaul of the city's classrooms. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **OpenAI says it will burn $280 billion by 2030**

The ChatGPT-maker has told counterparts it expects deeply negative cash flow through the decade as it builds infrastructure and prices come under pressure. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The lab's growth story now carries an explicit cash-hole number.

* **US deportees were bound and beaten in Equatorial Guinea**

Two men the Trump administration sent to Equatorial Guinea — one Egyptian, one Eritrean — were hooded, beaten and pushed down stairs at a hotel in view of other deportees, lawyers say; about 30 others remain trapped there. ([The Guardian][11])

* **India's tech boom is running ahead of Bangalore's pipes**

A Bloomberg week from India argues the IT capital's talent pool outpaced roads, power and planning, and asks whether Hyderabad can take the lead. ([Bloomberg][12])
