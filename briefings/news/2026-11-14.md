# News Briefing — 14 November 2026

Spain's campaign turns to pensions and last-minute turnout while Germany weighs record solar output and a stalled electric-car market, as a US air-traffic shutdown and a planned Trump–Xi–Putin meeting frame the wider world.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's cabinet has taken the first step towards raising pensions by 3–4% from 1 January 2027, approving fast-track processing of the decree that guarantees pensioners' purchasing power**

The move came on the first day of the official campaign for the 29 November election, with the final figure due on 27 November, and eldiario.es sets it against PP and Vox claims that the public pension system is bankrupt. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **The Socialist party (PSOE) has identified 18 priority provinces where it hopes to win almost 20 contested seats, appealing for tactical votes and betting that Vox's rise eats into the conservative PP**

Because Spain elects its parliament through provincial lists, a handful of marginal seats can decide whether the left or the PP–Vox bloc that polls favour comes out ahead. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Isabel Díaz Ayuso, the conservative president of the Madrid region, opened the campaign by attacking the prime minister's wife Begoña Gómez and calling strike pickets "union rubbish"**

eldiario.es says she has escalated her language against unions and the general strike for weeks and does not intend to tone it down. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Solar power is heading for a record share of German electricity generation, Handelsblatt reports, and asks what the growing contribution means for the energy transition**

Solar, which already covers a rising part of the country's power mix, is the fastest-growing source behind the shift away from fossil fuels. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Customers ordering an electric car in Germany now wait about a year, even as carmakers complain of weak demand**

Die Zeit suggests Volkswagen, Europe's largest carmaker, may not actually want to sell as many electric models as its order books would allow. ([Die Zeit][5])

* **A Tagesspiegel commentary argues that Chancellor Friedrich Merz needs leadership coaching, since pilots, anglers and taxi drivers need a licence but a chancellor does not**

The piece reads Merz's rocky start leading the CDU–SPD coalition as a skills gap rather than a matter of bad luck. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The first glass facades of the Siemens campus in Spandau, a new industrial and research district in west Berlin, are finished, and work on its school is about to start**

The hotel car park has been closed for the school site, and the S-Bahn has screened off a stretch of line beside the growing towers. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Steffen Krach, the Berlin SPD's parliamentary leader, can stay under investigation over a hospital sale even without lifting his immunity, the Abgeordnetenhaus told rbb**

Hanover prosecutors had asked for the immunity to be lifted; the finding frees them to resume a probe that had been on hold since Krach became a member of parliament. ([rbb24][8])

* **The Linke has broken off talks with the Greens on running the central Berlin district of Mitte, after a dispute over an antisemitism paper and a key post**

The stand-off, in which Linke deputy state chair Martha Kleedörfer is a central figure, adds strain to the parties' wider cooperation at state level. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **US air traffic was halted nationwide for more than four hours after a communications-system failure, grounding flights and cancelling about 6,000**

Transport Secretary Sean Duffy blamed the "outdated" air-traffic control system, said patches no longer work and asked Congress for at least $30bn to replace it. ([EL PAÍS][10], [EL PAÍS][11])

💡 Ageing safety-critical control systems, not one-off glitches, are the common thread with Spain's recent rail-safety debate.

* **The White House has announced a three-way meeting of Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin next week on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen**

APEC, the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum, is a regional trade body; Handelsblatt says Trump also wants to discuss cutting the world's nuclear arsenals, alongside the war in Ukraine. ([Handelsblatt][12])

* **US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has blamed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's department for failing to put the firm Megaspeed on a US blacklist**

The Financial Times says the attack risks stoking tensions inside the Trump administration over the scandal at the chipmaker Nvidia. ([Financial Times][13])
