# News Briefing — 14 September 2026

Ceuta's emergency contracts go to the president's circle, Lower Saxony's locals reward the AfD, and Trump swats away an AI pause as Berlin's election poll turns into a CDU–Left race.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Vivas steered emergency shelter contracts to relatives of his inner circle**

Juan Jesús Vivas, Ceuta's long-serving regional president, used emergency powers after the July crossing to award two unpublished contracts — €3 million and €2 million — for housing unaccompanied minors to the brother of a personal adviser and a first cousin of the port president. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The crisis is now also a procurement story: emergency law let the awards skip a public tender.

* **Internal Affairs says the 'walled millions' inspector's police network is real**

Óscar Sánchez Gil, the former UDEF anti-drugs chief who told a judge the force fakes the origin of big cocaine cases, had flagged an unread encrypted phone pointing at officers moving cocaine through Algeciras; Internal Affairs has now verified that the network he named exists. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Washington is stalling Spain's case against Venezuela's new oil broker**

A Spanish judge has waited more than nine months for US legal help on Alejandro Betancourt — the Venezuelan businessman Washington has now made its oil point-man — and says the file will be archived a second time if there is no answer by December. ([EL PAÍS][3])

💡 Shielding the broker in Madrid is a price of the Venezuela oil channel, not a clerical delay.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Lower Saxony's locals tripled the AfD and put the CDU ahead of the SPD**

About six million voters in Volkswagen's home state elected councils; first results show the AfD roughly tripling its score while the CDU finished ahead of the SPD — a western map Berlin will read as more than a local count. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 Yesterday was the campaign preview; today is a counted result.

* **SPD left-wingers want a harder line against the Union — and fear single digits**

Annika Klose and Hakan Demir, on the party's left, want some policies declared non-negotiable and attack three black-red coalition projects, even where Klose is in the room negotiating them. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **A night of real darkness is being sold as heart protection**

The body clock is sensitive to light; a new study, via Tagesspiegel, says keeping nights dark is a simple way to protect the heart — a public-health note, not a hospital reform. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A new poll has the CDU and the Left neck-and-neck — and the SPD behind the AfD**

With a week to the 20 September Abgeordnetenhaus vote, Tagesspiegel's survey leaves the winner open, puts the Social Democrats clearly behind the AfD, and finds Berliners split on who should govern with whom. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Night trains to BER are cancelled for construction**

Regional lines to Berlin Brandenburg Airport will not run tonight because of building work — a one-night cut on the airport rail, not a BER policy change. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Men in Mitte attacked a woman in an apparently antisemitic assault**

A woman was injured on Torstrasse after an attack police are treating as antisemitic; rbb24 reports a glass bottle and a Star of David in the account. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump has rejected the AI bosses' call for a slowdown**

After lab chiefs urged a slower build-out, the US president denounced regulation talk and treated existential fears about the technology as a political problem — not a reason to pause. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 He is answering a lab-led pause with a competitiveness line, not a safety one.

* **TDK will scale the parts that go into AI servers**

The Japanese electronics group is raising output of components used in AI servers — a supply-chain expansion, not another model-lab note. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **An Indonesian ferry capsized with six dead and about 130 still missing**

The Virgo Transport 8, sailing Java–Borneo with 243 people, went down in bad weather; rescuers have brought more than 100 ashore and are still searching. ([The Guardian][12])
