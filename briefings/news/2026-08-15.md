# News Briefing — 15 August 2026

Energy reprieves, ageing-state bottlenecks and softer forms of infrastructure failure run through Spain, Germany and Berlin today, while AI governance and healthcare oversight dominate the wider world.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Almaraz nuclear plant wins a reprieve until 2030**

Spain's ecological-transition ministry has formally extended the Almaraz reactor in Extremadura to June 2030, citing Middle East energy risks and buying peace in a long-running clash with the PP-run regional government over the closure timetable. The stopgap keeps the coalition's 2035 nuclear-exit plan nominally intact while inviting fresh pressure for further extensions. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Ageing is pushing Spain's primary-care model past its design limits**

With almost one in three Spaniards projected to be over 65 by 2045, doctors and patient groups say the GP-led primary-care system needs longer consultations and more staff if it is to manage chronic illness rather than episodic care. The demographic shift turns a staffing complaint into a broader state-capacity problem. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Eight Spanish universities fall out of the Shanghai ranking**

The Academic Ranking of World Universities now leaves only the University of Barcelona inside the global top 200, underlining how Spanish higher education is slipping in research-intensive league tables even as campuses keep expanding enrolment. The drop adds pressure on Madrid and the regions to treat research funding as industrial policy rather than prestige spending. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Wero remains a little-known answer to PayPal in Germany**

Wero, the European payments scheme meant to reduce dependence on US platforms, is still barely recognised by German consumers two years after launch, Handelsblatt reports. Weak uptake suggests that Europe's talk of payments sovereignty will stand or fall on merchant adoption and branding, not just regulatory ambition. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Blue-green algae warnings spread across the Baltic and northern lakes**

Health authorities are warning bathers away from stretches of Germany's Baltic coast and inland lakes as cyanobacteria blooms spread in hot, nutrient-rich water that can trigger rashes and other reactions. Summer heat is turning ordinary leisure water into a recurring public-health management problem. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Ritter Sport is covering up sustainability claims on chocolate bars**

German chocolatier Ritter Sport is masking environmental claims on many packs because the EU's anti-greenwashing EmpCo rules and a dispute over 75-gram bars have made old packaging legally risky, Berliner Zeitung reports. It is an early example of how tougher consumer-law enforcement is rewriting corporate language before shoppers notice the regulation itself. ([Berliner Zeitung][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin is seeing more gun crime involving illegal weapons and younger suspects**

Police and prosecutors say shootings in the capital are increasingly tied to illegal firearms and younger offenders, turning what were once episodic gang incidents into a broader urban-security problem. Their answer is more pressure on criminal networks, but the trend suggests enforcement is chasing a younger market. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **RE1 disruption will last another day because Fangschleuse works slipped**

Delayed construction at the new Fangschleuse station near Tesla's Gruenheide plant will keep the RE1 between Berlin and Frankfurt (Oder) shut a day longer than planned, extending disruption on one of the capital region's main eastbound rail corridors. The snag is another reminder that industrial expansion in Brandenburg keeps colliding with fragile commuter infrastructure. ([rbb24][8])

* **Berliners may wait years for fully accessible cash machines**

Germany is letting older ATMs delay speech output and tactile controls until 2040, a grace period that has now drawn an EU rebuke and will leave many Berlin street-corner machines unevenly usable for years. Disability compliance is still being subordinated to banks' hardware replacement cycles. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **Canada's private plasma-donation chain pauses collections after two donor deaths**

Grifols, the Spanish group that runs Canada's only private plasma-clinic network, has halted collections after two donor deaths and a new Health Canada investigation into safety practices. The case puts a fast-growing biotech supply business under scrutiny in a country that outsources part of its blood-product collection to a for-profit operator. ([The Guardian][10])

* **OpenAI's IPO ambitions are colliding with internal upheaval**

OpenAI, the ChatGPT maker, is dealing with executive departures and safety-team strain just as Sam Altman prepares an IPO push, the Financial Times reports, turning governance questions into a capital-markets issue. Investors are being asked to price not only model revenues but the durability of the lab's internal controls. ([Financial Times][11])

* **China is poised to lift travel restrictions on Manus founders**

Beijing is preparing to remove travel curbs on Manus founder Xiao Hong as the AI company nears resolution of its separation from Meta, according to the Financial Times, easing a political risk that had stranded the group's leadership. The move suggests regulators are willing to stabilise prized AI companies once ownership and security lines are clarified. ([Financial Times][12])
