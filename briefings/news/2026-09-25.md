# News Briefing — 25 September 2026

Spain's housing decree is now a Tuesday vote with Junts extracting tax concessions, German bond yields have reached a 2007 high, Berlin's Left decides who it will talk to, and Iran has offered a week that could reopen Hormuz.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The housing decree is now a Tuesday vote — and Junts wants tax concessions**

Pedro Sánchez's minority government is racing fiscal sweeteners to Junts, Carles Puigdemont's Catalan party, so a housing decree can pass on Tuesday: the nationalists want tax breaks or the state to cover rent that vulnerable tenants cannot pay, in exchange for not evicting them. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 A social-crisis photograph is being converted into the price of a parliamentary majority.

* **Spain still has not repaired 519 days of prison for an innocent woman**

Twenty-five years after the Wanninkhof case, the state has not made good the 519 days Dolores Vázquez spent in jail for a murder she did not commit — a media and courtroom lynching, laced with homophobia, that also left the real killer, Tony King, free to murder another young woman. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **A murdered woman's daughters had to bargain so her killer would not walk free**

The daughters of Carmen Ortiz, killed by her former partner in 2021, say they will never receive compensation and still do not know the whole truth, because they had to accept a plea deal to keep him in prison. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **German bond yields are at their highest since 2007**

Rising oil prices, inflation and growing supply bottlenecks have pushed yields on the government-bond market — the rate that sets the cost of German public and corporate borrowing — to levels last seen before the global financial crisis. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The cheap-debt backdrop that funded Germany's industrial model for a decade-and-a-half is no longer there.

* **Part-time work still taxes mothers twice — pay now, pension later**

In Germany it is still mainly mothers who cut hours, and they lose not only a slice of salary but also pension rights; Tagesspiegel walks through how couples can offset that loss inside the partnership. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Dresden has the first major Correggio show outside Italy**

The Gemäldegalerie is staging Antonio da Correggio — the Renaissance painter whose flesh-and-light studies have been copied for centuries — in a large exhibition that has not travelled as a body of work beyond Italy before. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Die Linke's congress will decide who it talks to about governing**

Having won the Abgeordnetenhaus election, Berlin's Left meets on Friday to choose its exploratory partners; the leadership wants talks with the Greens and the SPD and expects delegates to back that plan. ([rbb24][7])

* **A music-school director sued her way back onto the job**

Ina Finger is again head of the Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg music school after the district had removed her; a court forced the reversal, which Tagesspiegel calls a slap to the Bezirksamt. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Grünau residents are still waiting for a pedestrian light — after a child was hit**

Crossing Regattastraße is dangerous for children and older people; neighbours have long asked for a traffic light, and a ten-year-old was recently struck there. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Iran has offered the US a seven-day ceasefire that could reopen Hormuz**

Iran's foreign minister says a week-long pause could see the Strait of Hormuz — the oil-and-gas chokepoint whose transit trade had largely vanished — open again within seven days. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The offer is priced as a shipping and energy event, not only a diplomatic one.

* **A Tigrayan offensive has revived fears of Ethiopia's civil war**

Fighters from Tigray have pushed into neighbouring Afar and Amhara and joined a new rebel coalition aimed at overthrowing Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, raising the spectre of a return to a war that killed around 600,000 people. ([The Guardian][11])

* **China's lead in health tech is being sold as a global public good**

Rest of World argues that Chinese AI screening, biotech and drugs — on show from elderly-care expos to clinics — are a rare case where Beijing's industrial edge is good news for patients elsewhere, not only a strategic threat. ([Rest of World][12])
