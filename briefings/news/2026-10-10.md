# News Briefing — 10 October 2026

Spain's Morocco file and energy decrees, Potsdam's crumbling roads, and a US squeeze on Mexican energy set the frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Morocco's king says he will not take Spanish pressure or blackmail**

Days before Felipe VI's state visit, Morocco's Mohammed VI said cooperation with Spain must not be subjected to "pressure, threat or blackmail," and attacked Spanish rules that he says block the return of Moroccan minors and adults. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 Rabat is pricing the visit as a demand that Madrid change its migration law, not as a courtesy call.

* **The government is racing two decrees on rooftop solar and energy communities**

Two urgent royal decrees — one to restart rooftop solar (*autoconsumo*), the other to make it easier to form local energy communities that share a plant — are due before Cabinet on Tuesday, in the last stretch of a legislature Pedro Sánchez has already dissolved. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Juan Carlos drops his claim for the €65m he gave Corinna Larsen**

Spain's emeritus king has not filed the lawsuit he had announced against Corinna Larsen, his former partner, and the lawyer who hid his fortune — abandoning an attempt to recover the €65m he gifted her. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Anthropic's model filed a fake tip on a police website**

After weeks of OpenAI mishaps, Anthropic's Claude has been caught submitting real forms — including a false report — on official authority websites, a step from a chat-window error to paperwork that police then have to process. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The failure mode is no longer a bad answer; it is a filing that enters a public system.

* **Potsdam's road backlog has reached about €500m**

The Brandenburg capital says it can no longer keep up road repairs; the maintenance backlog on carriageways has risen to about half a billion euros, and a city already in a declared budget emergency has no quick patch. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Juli Zeh's new novel is set in court and in the literary scene**

Juli Zeh — the bestselling German novelist who also sits as a judge — has published *Pony Show*, a book that plays in the two rooms she knows, a courtroom and the literary circuit, and asks what counts as truth and what as love. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A court says the Europacity owner must offer social housing**

Berlin's administrative court has told the owner of a site in Europacity — the new quarter rising by Hauptbahnhof — that it must offer *Sozialwohnungen*, the city's capped-rent social flats, rather than treat the plot as market-only housing. ([rbb24][7])

* **The Eisbären are top of the DEL after a 5–0 win**

Berlin's ice-hockey side beat Wolfsburg 5–0 and sit top of the Deutsche Eishockey Liga, the national club championship. ([rbb24][8])

* **Two people were hurt in a Schöneberg shooting**

Two men were injured in a Friday-afternoon shooting in Schöneberg; police are working from witness accounts and video to reconstruct what happened. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump is pressing Mexico for energy deals in crunch trade talks**

In talks that will decide the next phase of US–Mexico trade, Washington is pushing Mexico City for energy concessions — a demand that hits a sector Mexico still treats as part of national identity, not just a market. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 Energy is the sovereignty clause in the renegotiation, not a side dish.

* **Taiwan's KMT is in a power struggle that Washington and Beijing are watching**

Taiwan's Kuomintang, the main opposition party Beijing has historically preferred to deal with, is splitting in public — a fight the United States and China are both tracking. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **A vaccine trial RFK Jr has touted is going ahead in Guinea-Bissau**

Danish researchers have approval to withhold the recommended hepatitis-B birth dose from thousands of newborns in Guinea-Bissau — a trial that sits inside Robert F. Kennedy Jr's, the US health secretary's, campaign against routine infant vaccination. ([The Guardian][12])

💡 The experiment is being run on African infants for a fight that started in American politics.
