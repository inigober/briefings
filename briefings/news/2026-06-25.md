# News Briefing — 25 June 2026

Regional graft probes widen from Andalucía to Madrid patronage networks, Germany's new conscription questionnaire yields barely 500 volunteers while Merz shuttles between Berlin fiscal summits and Danzig reconstruction talks, and a US–China AI espionage row collides with twin Venezuelan earthquakes — today's edition tracks how institutions strain under corruption, defence recruitment and technology rivalry.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Almería PP corruption parade nears end as ex-provincial chief and mayor testify**

eldiario.es reports that José Juan Martínez, mayor of Tíjola, and Javier Aureliano García, former PP provincial president and Diputación chief, are among the latest officials to appear before a judge in a long-running probe into alleged kickbacks on mask contracts and public-works awards. The Guardia Civil has already concluded the ex-Diputación president paid a mortgage with corruption proceeds — closing a judicial parade that has implicated dozens of regional conservatives. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Andalusian graft is no longer a peripheral subplot to Madrid's mascarillas verdict; prosecutors are reconstructing provincial procurement rings with the same forensic detail applied to ministerial cases.

🧩 Feijóo's national offensive gains ammunition from fresh PP scandals in a heartland the party treats as a fortress — complicating his claim that corruption is a uniquely socialist disease.

* **Canal Sur producer filed equality plan with forged union signature before CEO's sexual-assault charges**

eldiario.es reports that Andalucía Digital Multimedia — majority-owned by the Junta and Canal Sur's main production partner — registered its second equality plan in December using a union delegate's signature despite his four-year absence on excedencia, just before its chief executive was imputed for sexual assault. Labour ministry filings were a legal prerequisite to keep public contracts; the delegate warned his signature "on excedencia" should carry no weight. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 Public broadcasters' governance failures now intersect gender-policy compliance and criminal probes — a toxic mix for a regional government marketing itself as progressive.

🧩 If courts find the equality filing fraudulent, Canal Sur's procurement chain faces contract invalidation risks that could paralyse programming schedules.

* **Madrid PP tries to halt elDiario festival two days before opening**

eldiario.es reports that the regional PP filed a last-minute administrative challenge seeking to cancel the outlet's Ideas and Culture festival — after stages were built, artists had travelled and tickets were sold. Director Ignacio Escolar frames the move as censorship of a critical publisher; the party argues permit irregularities. The timing leaves the event in limbo hours before its Friday launch. ([eldiario.es][3])

💡 Press-freedom fights in Spain are shifting from courtroom libel battles to permit warfare that can bankrupt cultural events without banning journalism outright.

🧩 Ayuso and Almeida's Madrid machine is testing whether administrative law can achieve what criminal probes have not: silencing a hostile media platform.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Bundeswehr's new conscription questionnaire yields only 530 volunteers from 300,000 mailings**

Tagesspiegel reports the first balance sheet for Germany's revamped Wehrerfassung: roughly 300,000 questionnaires sent to young men and women, about 1,500 medical examinations conducted, and only 530 voluntary enlistments five months after launch. Defence officials call the pipeline a success; critics say the numbers expose how far voluntary models lag behind NATO manpower targets. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 Berlin's defence debate assumed questionnaires would surface latent patriotism — instead they quantify how few citizens will join without compulsion.

🧩 Merz's coalition must decide whether sub-thousand volunteer intakes justify maintaining a costly registration bureaucracy or pivot toward harder conscription triggers.

* **Merz hosts Länder leaders in Berlin for strained fiscal-relief talks**

Handelsblatt reports that Chancellor Friedrich Merz convened state premiers in Berlin as municipalities and Länder demand billions in federal relief — with Kommunen warning of insolvency risks and states resisting unfunded mandates. The summit sits between pension-reform battles and a Danzig trip devoted to Ukraine reconstruction pledges. ([Handelsblatt][5])

💡 Germany's federal bargain is fracturing on three fronts simultaneously: pensions, defence recruitment and subnational insolvency — each demanding cash Merz has not yet earmarked.

🧩 Berlin-hosted summits now function as triage wards where Länder extract side payments in exchange for not blocking Bundestag votes on unrelated reforms.

* **Merz travels to Danzig as Poland's Tusk backs Ukraine reconstruction conference**

Handelsblatt reports that Merz joins a Gdańsk meeting on rebuilding Ukraine even as Kyiv and Western capitals quarrel over funding formulas and oversight. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk is positioning himself as co-host to keep reconstruction on the agenda despite presidential tensions inside Ukraine. ([Handelsblatt][6])

💡 German industry needs export markets and energy corridors in a rebuilt Ukraine — but Merz arrives as domestic fiscal politics leave little room for open-ended reconstruction cheques.

🧩 Failure in Danzig would signal that European rebuild diplomacy is stalling just as Washington pressures allies to finance long-horizon security investments.

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Checkpoint rounds up Berlin's oddest data-protection breaches**

Tagesspiegel's morning column catalogues recent Datenschutz mishaps in the capital — from a hairdresser's Google-review reply that exposed a client's medical history to a university exam dispute that mishandled student records — alongside political items such as the ICC culture plan and a Neukölln SPD councillor's disciplinary reprimand. The digest underscores how everyday GDPR violations, not just hacker attacks, dominate Berlin caseloads. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

💡 Berlin's data-protection bureaucracy is drowning in petty breaches because schools, salons and borough offices digitised faster than they trained staff.

🧩 Senate digital-governance reforms will be judged on whether they simplify compliance for small actors — not only on high-profile cyber incidents.

* **ICC blueprint adds culture towers but financing model still missing**

rbb24 reports that the Senate's approved plan for the mothballed Charlottenburg congress centre envisions art, culture, gastronomy and research spaces plus two high-rise towers — yet officials acknowledge no financing concept is in place. The announcement follows Senate endorsement of a preferred operator path but leaves Westend residents facing years of scaffolding without budget clarity. ([rbb24][8])

💡 Berlin has political agreement on ICC reuse before it has a euro figure — repeating the pattern that delayed airport and museum megaprojects for a decade.

🧩 Investors will watch whether culture-led regeneration can attract private capital without the trade-fair rents originally promised — a test for post-industrial conference halls across Europe.

* **Berlin columnist's intercontinental trip undone by ten kilometres of Brandenburg rail**

Berliner Zeitung publishes a columnist's account of completing 10,000 kilometres across continents on schedule, only to fail the final ten-kilometre Deutsche Bahn stretch in Brandenburg en route home to Berlin. The piece satirises how national rail fragility eclipses long-haul reliability for capital residents. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

💡 After Tuesday's nationwide radio outage, Berlin media are personalising infrastructure failure as a daily quality-of-life tax — not an abstract network diagram.

🧩 Commuter ridicule keeps pressure on the Senate and federal government to deliver redundant signalling even when grand ICC and defence budgets absorb fiscal attention.

## World 🌐

* **Anthropic accuses Alibaba of 'largest known distillation attack' on Claude**

Nikkei Asia reports that Anthropic alleges Alibaba illicitly copied its frontier model through a distillation campaign — escalating US–China AI rivalry beyond export controls into corporate espionage claims. Alibaba shares fell to a 16-month low in Hong Kong as investors weighed regulatory and partnership risks. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

💡 Model-distillation fights will define whether Chinese labs can close capability gaps without access to Western weights — or whether litigation replaces chip bans as the primary chokepoint.

🧩 Cloud providers and enterprises licensing Claude must now audit supply chains for vendors accused of systematic IP theft — complicating procurement in both Washington and Brussels.

* **Twin earthquakes of magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 strike northern Venezuela**

EL PAÍS reports that two powerful tremors hit Venezuela's northern coast and Caracas, prompting acting president Delcy Rodríguez to declare a state of emergency while suspending flights, metro service and schools. Social-media footage showed collapsed buildings in affluent districts; official casualty figures remained unavailable as rescuers worked through the night. ([EL PAÍS][11])

💡 A double seismic shock tests an already fragile petrostate whose leadership faces US pressure and domestic economic collapse — with humanitarian response capacity unclear.

🧩 Caribbean seismic risk intersects with Venezuela's built-environment neglect; donors may condition aid on transparent damage assessments Rodríguez's government has not historically provided.

* **Micron forecasts sustained AI memory demand after 15-fold profit surge**

Financial Times reports that Micron Technology posted a fifteen-fold jump in quarterly profit and guided for continued strength as hyperscalers hoard high-bandwidth memory for AI clusters. Shares rallied in after-hours trading, lifting broader semiconductor sentiment after weeks of export-control anxiety. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 Memory chips are becoming the binding constraint in AI scaling — shifting geopolitical attention from GPU smuggling to HBM fabrication capacity concentrated in US and East Asian fabs.

🧩 European industrial policy debates that focused on lithography tools must now account for packaging and memory bottlenecks that can stall data-centre builds regardless of frontier logic-chip access.
