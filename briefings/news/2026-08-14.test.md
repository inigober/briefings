# News Briefing — 14 August 2026

Morocco and Spain harden the Ceuta frontier, a NRW wildfire forces evacuations, and Berlin's transit operator faces data-breach lawsuits — while AI price wars, Ebola's spread and open-source models frame the wider world.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Morocco deploys hundreds of police and troops as Madrid braces for another mass crossing**

Rabat has sent large security contingents to the Ceuta border after social-media calls for a fresh surge, while Spain says it has increased Guardia Civil and police numbers in the exclave by 42% and is trying to expel roughly 5,000 irregular migrants still inside the city. The mutual militarisation marks a new phase in the crisis that began with July's record entry, when Madrid had focused on reception tallies rather than pre-emptive border saturation. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **CEO of Ayuso government's ático-buying agency was sacked from prior public housing firm over contract oversight**

Pedro Corbalán, chief executive of Planifica Madrid — the Madrid regional entity that spent €6.3 million on Isabel Díaz Ayuso's controversial Chamberí penthouse — was dismissed in 2016 from a Las Rozas municipal housing company that auditors said had blocked proper scrutiny of public contracts, eldiario.es reports. The trail deepens questions about who controlled spending at PP-run housing bodies before the luxury purchase became a national scandal. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Orca matriarch "Gladis" found with shotgun pellet wounds in her dorsal fin**

Gladis, the 60-year-old leader of the Iberian orca group famous for ramming yachts, has appeared off Galicia with buckshot damage to her fin, prompting Spain's government to open an investigation and contact Portuguese authorities where initial reports suggest she was shot. The case turns a viral maritime-safety story into a cross-border wildlife-crime probe with implications for protected-species enforcement. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Forest fire near Hürtgenwald triggers district crisis team as flames close on village**

A large woodland blaze in western North Rhine-Westphalia has prompted officials to convene the district crisis staff and evacuate at least one suburb as shifting winds push flames to within a few hundred metres of homes. The emergency tests whether Germany's drought-stressed forests can be defended without the Mediterranean-scale resources that southern European states deploy every summer. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Private-equity giant Silver Lake in talks to buy US software firm Workday for $43 billion**

According to Handelsblatt sources, buyout firm Silver Lake is negotiating a takeover of cloud HR-software maker Workday that would rank among the largest software acquisitions on record, potentially enlisting additional financiers to fund the deal. For German corporate readers the rumour matters because Workday's European payroll and compliance tools underpin thousands of mid-market employers — any leveraged buyout could reshape pricing and product roadmaps on this side of the Atlantic. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Study finds jogging with a pram cuts runners' injury risk**

Researchers surveying parents who run with pushchairs report significantly fewer injuries than solo joggers, attributing the effect to slower pace, shorter stride and more cautious route choices rather than any mechanical advantage of the stroller itself. The finding is a rare piece of domestic health data in a week dominated by climate emergencies and capital-markets gossip. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Victims of BVG customer-data breach sue for compensation**

Eighteen months after hackers stole personal data from roughly 180,000 Berlin public-transport account holders, affected customers are taking the BVG to court seeking damages, while some state politicians argue the operator should offer payouts without litigation. The dispute keeps cybersecurity accountability on the ballot agenda weeks before Berlin's Abgeordnetenhaus election. ([rbb24][7])

* **Senate plans new S95 airport rail line with half-length trains only**

Berlin's planned S95 S-Bahn service between BER airport and Birkenwerder will run with shortened trainsets, rbb reports, drawing criticism from the Pro-Rail alliance that the capital is scaling back capacity before the line even opens. The decision feeds a wider pre-election argument over whether the city can expand airport links while its existing S-Bahn remains overcrowded. ([rbb24][8])

* **Brussels rebukes Germany over inaccessible cash machines**

Roughly 48,000 ATMs nationwide must be retrofitted with speech output and tactile keypads under disability law, yet Berlin has granted legacy devices protection until 2040 — prompting an EU infringement warning. Berliners who rely on street-corner cash dispensers face years of uneven access unless federal and state banking lobbies accelerate retrofits. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **DR Congo Ebola outbreak spreads to sixth province as toll tops 2,100 dead**

The Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ebola epidemic has reached Bas-Uele province after a man travelled from Haut-Uele, officials said, a day after the WHO chief warned the wave could become history's deadliest. More than 2,100 people have died among 4,500 recorded cases — a faster trajectory than the 2014–16 West Africa catastrophe that killed at least 11,000. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 Cross-border spread into a new province raises the odds that Kinshasa and donors will have to fund ring vaccination and treatment corridors across a country already fighting multiple armed groups.

* **China's top chip foundry says AI demand is lifting prices for older semiconductors**

SMIC, Beijing's leading contract manufacturer, told investors that "spillover" from AI build-outs is tightening supply and raising prices for mature-node chips used in cars, appliances and industrial gear. The comment shows how the frontier-model race is rippling into mundane hardware long before China closes the gap on cutting-edge processors. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **OpenAI and Anthropic slash prices as Chinese AI models undercut US leaders**

Silicon Valley's two best-known model labs released cheaper tiers this week after low-cost Chinese rivals challenged their trillion-dollar valuations, the Financial Times reports. The move signals that Washington's export controls have not stopped price competition from shifting to inference economics — where margins may compress even if frontier training stays concentrated in the US. ([Financial Times][12])
