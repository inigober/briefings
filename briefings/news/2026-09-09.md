# News Briefing — 9 September 2026

Spain's Supreme Court freeze on new citizens' votes, a shared PISA shock, and a US–Canada import ban set the frame as Berlin's culture-funding probe and a DeepSeek fundraising frenzy fill out the map.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The Supreme Court has frozen the vote for many new citizens under the 'grandchildren law'**

Spain's highest court, acting on challenges by Vox and Iustitia Europa, has issued a precautionary ban on electoral-roll entries for people nationalised under the ley de nietos — a 2022 route for descendants of exiles — unless they prove that exile ancestry through a long extra process; nationality itself is not revoked. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 Citizenship without a vote is now a live census fight, not only a paperwork backlog.

* **Spain's PISA drop is worse than its peers' — and sharpest among high earners**

The OECD's 2025 tests of 15-year-olds show Spain falling further than neighbouring systems in reading and maths, with an unusual slide among richer pupils and trouble with texts longer than about 400 words; researchers still tie much of the gap to child poverty, not only screens. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 Last week's German primer was that a new PISA round was due; this is the Spanish result, and the high-income fall is the surprise.

* **More than 2.4 million people have started grandchildren-law filings — and Cuba is the bottleneck**

Argentina accounts for the largest pile of applications, but the worst delays sit in the consular network, above all in Cuba, where the state cannot process the rush the 2022 law invited. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Centrists are being billed for a multi-trillion education miss**

Handelsblatt's morning briefing treats Germany's latest PISA-era learning slide as a location problem: mainstream parties neglected pupils, and the lost output is now counted in the trillions of euros. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **About 22% of commercial vehicles are failing their TÜV checks**

Germany has almost 3.9 million commercial vans and trucks on the road; as that fleet has aged, more of them are flunking the main inspection. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Weight-loss shots can cut the tax bill by up to €490 a month**

The injections and pills often cost several hundred euros a month; under set conditions those bills can be deducted, turning a private health spend into a fiscal one. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Ex-culture senator Chialo may have to testify against his successor**

Prosecutors are investigating a CDU-era scheme that paid out anti-antisemitism grants; Joe Chialo, who left the culture post, could incriminate himself if he gives evidence about Sarah Wedl-Wilson, who now holds it. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Police found twelve hand grenades in a Friedrichshain self-storage unit**

Officers secured the weapons in a commercial locker in the eastern district — a policing find, not a street attack. ([rbb24][8])

* **About 14,000 people protested at the Brandenburg Gate after the AfD's latest win**

The crowd turned out against the far-right party's success, in the run-up to Berlin's 20 September Abgeordnetenhaus election. ([The Berliner][9])

## World 🌐

* **Washington has banned Canadian dairy, motorcycles and most alcohol**

The import block landed a day after Ottawa imposed about $20 billion in retaliatory tariffs on US goods — a further turn in the trade fight Mark Carney had already warned was not being taken seriously. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 Yesterday's note was that Canadian counter-duties were due; this is the US reply, aimed at farms, bikes and drink.

* **DeepSeek's new funding round has spawned a costly shadow market**

Investors are piling into vehicles tied to the Chinese AI lab with rising fees and five-year lock-ups, a grey market around a company that still is not a straightforward public listing. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthis are sliding back toward open war**

Riyadh says an Iran-backed Houthi attack injured dozens and has promised to strike back, reopening a front that had been quieter while the US–Iran tanker fight ran in the Gulf. ([The New York Times][12])
