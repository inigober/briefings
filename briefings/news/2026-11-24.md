# News Briefing — 24 November 2026

Spain's final pre-election polls and a televised eight-way debate sharpen the left–right bloc contest, Germany's labour and supply dependencies resurface, Berlin's coalition hinges on one party vote, and the US–China AI rivalry shapes the world beat.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Eight-party TV debate exposes a PP–Vox bloc facing a divided left**

Parliamentary spokespeople from the eight main parties clashed on RTVE ahead of the 29 November general election, with Vox blaming immigration for the housing and wage squeeze and the conservative PP denying it froze pensions, in a debate EL PAÍS scrutinises for lies, inaccuracies and half-truths. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Last published poll gives the PP a narrow lead, with the right holding a majority**

The Ateneo del Dato survey for eldiario.es puts the PP on 32.1% against 30.8% for the Socialists (PSOE) and 17.6% for far-right Vox, the final poll allowed before Spain's pre-election publication blackout. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Former Socialist ministers rally behind Sánchez after Felipe González announces a blank vote**

A broad group of ex-ministers from past PSOE governments is publicly urging a vote for the party, isolating former prime minister Felipe González, who said he will cast a blank ballot and has again attacked Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Shortage of science and engineering skilled workers widens again after a cyclical dip**

The gap in so-called MINT jobs (mathematics, IT, natural sciences, technology) shrank during the downturn when postings fell, but Handelsblatt reports it is growing once more and describes it as worrying for industry. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Experts warn of a "protein shock" as Europe depends on imports, especially from China**

Europe's supply of protein for food and feed relies heavily on foreign suppliers, a dependence experts compare to that on oil and gas, though they say a way out exists. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **German true-crime documentary by two Berlin film students wins an International Emmy**

After their graduation film was stolen, the students documented their hunt for the thief in a true-crime documentary that has now won the international television award. ([Die Zeit][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's Left Party caucus votes on expelling Vanessa Emde, with a possible red-green-red coalition at stake**

The Linke's parliamentary group decides on Tuesday whether to throw out Emde, a vote Green co-chair Brantner calls necessary if the Greens are to govern with the Left alongside the Social Democrats (SPD). ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Gasag raises gas prices, adding about €32 a month to a typical heating bill**

Berlin's main gas supplier is lifting its tariff, and consumer advocates advise customers to compare alternative providers. ([Berliner Zeitung][8])

* **Hundreds of schools in Germany, including many in Berlin and Brandenburg, receive bomb threats**

Police say they see no concrete danger from the wave of threats. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Chinese military researchers fear AI makes China's nuclear forces easier to attack**

Researchers in Beijing are studying how to use artificial intelligence in warfare, but worry the same technology could expose the country's nuclear forces to a disabling strike, according to The New York Times. ([The New York Times][10])

* **US Justice Department admits in court it deported an American citizen to Mexico**

Brian José Morales García of Austin, Texas, was wrongly identified as undocumented and expelled, and the department conceded this while asking a federal court to dismiss his damages claim. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Ukraine's renewable energy push fell short as Russian strikes hit the grid this winter**

Foreign Policy argues that Kyiv's effort to build up renewable generation came too late to shield its power infrastructure from Russian attacks. ([Foreign Policy][12])
