# News Briefing — 25 October 2026

Elections in Brazil, Serbia and Israel test incumbents abroad, while Spain's campaign fights over trust in the postal vote and Berlin's red-green-red talks hinge on a single party's internal showdown.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Sánchez says a PP–Vox government would be "going back 50 years"**

In an interview with EL PAÍS, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, the Socialist (PSOE) candidate in the 29 November election, argues that a government of the conservative People's Party (PP) and the far-right Vox is not ordinary alternation of power, and names his biggest mistake as "not having seen Ábalos's corruption". ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **Correos defends the postal vote after Feijóo and Ayuso question it**

Olga García, operations director at the state postal operator Correos, says mail ballots are as safe as ballot-box votes because they travel sealed and under video surveillance, after PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo — a former Correos chairman — said he trusts the postmen, not the managers. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **A Quirónsalud executive has been a suspect for 30 months in the González Amador bribery strand**

The prosecutor says Fernando Camino, a senior manager at the hospital group Quirónsalud, has been "materially" under investigation since the strand on the alleged bribe involving Alberto González Amador, partner of Madrid's regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso, opened, yet Quirón has taken no action against him; his testimony is set for December, and an appeals court has authorised the UCO police unit to obtain the couple's full financial data. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Audi announces a US SUV plant on stage at a Trump campaign event**

Audi, Volkswagen's premium brand, used a Trump rally ahead of the US midterm elections to announce a new SUV factory in the United States, with a manager from Germany taking the stage, making a German carmaker part of the president's campaign. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **Berlin and the coastal states are still far apart on funding Germany's seaports**

The federal government pays €38.3m a year for all German seaports combined, while the coastal states and the port industry have long demanded much more, and the federal maritime coordinator now says he is optimistic about an agreement in 2026. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Germany will send staff officers and tanker aircraft to the first exercise of the Ukraine reassurance force**

Ukraine's allies, the so-called coalition of the willing, are building a force to secure a possible ceasefire with Russia, and in November it trains for the first time, with German participation. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A dispute over a Linke MP's Israel stance strains Berlin's red-green-red government talks**

The row over the Left party (Linke) deputy, whose critical line on Israel has drawn objections, overlaps with a Linke decision on whether to open formal coalition negotiations with the Social Democrats (SPD) and Greens, and the report asks how the two dates connect. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Four Berlin museums open free of charge today**

The Haus am Waldsee, Kunsthaus Dahlem, Georg Kolbe Museum and Brücke-Museum waive admission, echoing the free-museum Sunday, with guided tours and workshops on offer. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **BR Volleys beat Grizzlys Giesen 3–1**

Berlin's Bundesliga volleyball club won clearly in the rematch against Giesen. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **More than a million Lula supporters rally in Rio on the eve of Brazil's runoff**

Brazil's leftwing president Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and the far-right senator Flávio Bolsonaro are tied in the polls as nearly 159m Brazilians vote today, after Bolsonaro won the first round on 4 October by 47% to 45%. ([The Guardian][10])

* **Commodity trader Mercuria takes the Baltic Exchange to court over Hormuz losses**

Mercuria is challenging the Baltic Exchange, the London body whose freight benchmarks make it the "Dow Jones of shipping", over its decision to keep publishing price estimates for shipping oil through the Strait of Hormuz during the war. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 The case tests who bears the loss when a market benchmark keeps quoting a route that has effectively become a war zone.

* **AI models may be less of a black box than feared**

The Economist reports a glimmer of hope for those worried about AI safety: the large models that have long been dismissed as inscrutable "black boxes" may be easier to read than assumed. ([The Economist][12])
