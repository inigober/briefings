# News Briefing — 9 July 2026

Trump's trade threats against Spain and the collapse of the US–Iran ceasefire frame a day when court rulings, housing protest and alliance diplomacy expose how quickly domestic politics and Gulf escalation can unsettle Europe.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Trump threatens trade embargo on Spain, then claims Madrid "fully redeemed" after payment pledge**

EL PAÍS reports Washington is preparing a product list for possible commercial sanctions after Donald Trump called Spain a "horrible ally" over defence spending, only to say hours later that Madrid had committed to a major payment and "fully redeemed" itself. The whiplash leaves Sánchez's government calibrating a response as NATO leaders meet in Ankara. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 When a US president toggles between embargo threats and redemption within a single summit day, European capitals face bilateral commercial risk detached from formal alliance commitments.

* **Official data contradict Feijóo's claim of mass sick-leave fraud**

eldiario.es analyses statutory figures showing illness-related absences have risen across Spain and Europe alongside ageing workforces, pandemic after-effects and strained primary care — not the unfettered "fraud" the PP leader alleged in Bilbao. Editors argue the data undercuts his pledge to cut wages and benefits for absent workers. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **NATO leaders stage unity after Trump's attacks on Spain and Greenland**

eldiario.es says alliance chiefs are projecting Western cohesion at the Ankara summit despite Trump's public broadsides against Madrid and renewed pressure over Danish Greenland. Diplomats privately concede the display papers over intra-alliance friction rather than resolving it. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Google to count Android system backups against cloud storage quotas**

Handelsblatt reports Google will soon charge full-device backups against users' Google One allowances, ending a long-standing exemption that let Android owners archive photos and settings without eating into free tiers. The shift nudges millions of German smartphone users toward paid storage or slimmer backup habits. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Psychologists explain why adults regress to teenage family roles at home**

Tagesspiegel interviews clinicians on why middle-aged visitors to parental homes often slip back into adolescent dynamics — a pattern they link to unresolved childhood roles rather than mere nostalgia. Researchers caution the behaviour is common but can entrench conflict when caregiving or inheritance decisions loom. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Handelsblatt argues Trump's NATO theatrics unsettled Europe but changed little**

The paper's morning briefing concludes the US president dominated the Ankara summit with threats over Greenland, Iran and burden-sharing yet left few concrete policy shifts behind. Editors suggest European leaders may need to discount performative crises rather than treat each outburst as a structural break. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Ringbahn and S47 face overnight closures around Tempelhof**

Tagesspiegel warns travellers in southern Berlin to check schedules as the Ringbahn and S47 will not run through Tempelhof overnight because of engineering work. The disruption adds to a summer of patchy S-Bahn reliability as the BVG and Deutsche Bahn coordinate line repairs. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Berlin Young Union chief urges Wegner to abandon re-election bid**

Tagesspiegel says the head of the CDU youth wing in Berlin called on Mayor Kai Wegner to step aside as lead candidate, arguing his credibility is damaged ahead of September's Abgeordnetenhaus vote. The intervention widens internal conservative pressure three months before the ballot. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Neukölln tenants mount "Wall of Shame" over mould and disrepair at High-Deck estate**

Residents of the High-Deck-Siedlung published photographs of mould, burst pipes and chronic damp, warning a planned modernisation could precede steep rent hikes. A neighbourhood initiative says years of neglected maintenance have left households fearful of displacement. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **US carries out second day of strikes on Iran after Trump declares ceasefire over**

The Financial Times reports Washington resumed bombing as Donald Trump said Tehran had called to negotiate but cast doubt on any deal, ending a fragile truce struck in mid-June. Gulf bases and shipping lanes remain on alert as both sides trade strikes around Hormuz. ([Financial Times][10])

* **LG's $1bn investment shows Vietnam climbing the semiconductor value chain**

Nikkei Asia says the South Korean conglomerate is expanding packaging and testing capacity in Vietnam, adding to Hanoi's push to move beyond assembly into higher-value chip work. The bet underscores how Southeast Asia is capturing supply-chain steps once concentrated in North-east Asia. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

💡 When packaging investment follows final assembly, Vietnam's industrial policy graduates from low-wage magnet to a node governments must court in export-control diplomacy.

* **Liberia charges five over record 200kg cocaine shipment disguised as seasoning**

The Guardian reports authorities in Monrovia accused suspects after police found more than 200kg of cocaine falsely declared as Maggi cubes at the international airport — an estimated $19m haul among the country's largest drug seizures. Investigators say the case exposes growing transshipment risk through West African hubs. ([The Guardian][12])
