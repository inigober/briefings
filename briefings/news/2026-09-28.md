# News Briefing — 28 September 2026

On the eve of Spain's housing vote, Lower Saxony's runoffs shut the AfD out of three town halls, and the AI build-out is rewriting chips and debt.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The housing decree must now satisfy Junts, Podemos, and a demand for indefinite leases**

Spain's housing ministry is racing to close a text that can pass on Tuesday and still keep both Junts — Carles Puigdemont's Catalan party — and Podemos on side, while the tenants' union, in its second night camped in Madrid's Puerta del Sol, is demanding that rental contracts become indefinite, a demand that was not on the table. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The fight has moved from blocking evictions to rewriting the legal form of tenancy.

* **Clinicians are trying to explain a rise in cancer among the young**

Spanish specialists report more diagnoses in some cancers and age groups — including people in their twenties — and are tracing a mix of diet, gut flora, genetics and pollution rather than a single cause. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **The Supreme Court archived a Vox aide's case — and still called Ortega Smith sexist**

Spain's highest court has thrown out a complaint by a Vox employee against Javier Ortega Smith, a party founder, over how he treated her during maternity leave, but it still labelled his conduct "somewhat sexist" after he accused her of "lack of interest" for missing plenaries while she was off. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The AfD lost three mayoral runoffs in Lower Saxony**

In the runoffs after Lower Saxony's local elections, the AfD had a path to its first municipal top job in the state in three towns — and lost all three clearly; in Hanover, Green incumbent Belit Onay held the city. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

* **AI and defence money is minting more German unicorns — that still rent US models**

The Deutscher Startup Monitor finds more German firms crossing the billion-euro mark on the back of artificial-intelligence and defence investment, even as many of those same companies prefer to buy their models from US providers. ([Die Zeit][5])

💡 The boom is expanding Germany's start-up ledger without expanding its model stack.

* **Hamburg's finance senator has become the austerity general**

Andreas Dressel, the SPD politician who runs Hamburg's books, is now the city's cutter-in-chief — a role Die Zeit says does not fit a man who did not set out to be the one who says no. ([Die Zeit][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Kantstraße is now Berlin's busiest East Asian food mile**

The old Charlottenburg boulevard — long a cheap-goods and red-light strip — has become the city's densest stretch of East Asian kitchens, as crowded in the evening as Mitte. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **An ex-pastor is suing the BSR after a winter-service runaround**

Werner Alze fell on glazed January ice and spent months being passed between offices over who owed the winter grit; he is now taking Berlin's city cleaner, the BSR, to court. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **The marathon is still the day Berlin likes itself**

More than forty years after the first Berlin Marathon, rbb24 treats race day as the one date when the city forgets its grime and cheers the people willing to run through it. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **A TSMC affiliate's first Singapore plant has already sold out**

A company tied to Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company — the foundry that makes most of the world's advanced chips — says its first Singapore factory has no capacity left and is already looking at a further expansion. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **AI hyperscalers are rewriting how the world borrows**

The capital appetite of the large cloud-and-model companies is forcing both firms and governments to rethink debt — a financing problem, not only a chip-factory one. ([Financial Times][11])

💡 The constraint on the AI build-out is starting to show up in bond markets, not just in foundry queues.

* **Two mass shootings killed at least 27 people in South Africa**

Police say 17 people were killed near Johannesburg in what they treat as an illegal-gold-mining turf war, and 10 at a barbecue near Cape Town — a double blow in a country already living with one of the world's highest murder rates and a police service under investigation for corruption. ([The Guardian][12])
