# News Briefing — 16 October 2026

Spain's left closed a Canary Islands deal while a Madrid strike email blew up, Germany's banking and coalition both look fragile, and AI stress moved from equities into the debt that pays for the data centres.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **The Canary Islands left just closed a single ticket**

Unidad del Pueblo Canario is a joint list of Nueva Canarias, Podemos, Izquierda Unida and Sumar for the 29 November general election — the first of the snap-election left mergers to be signed, and the one Podemos is now holding up as a model for the rest of the country. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 A finished island deal does not unlock Madrid, where the same parties are still blocking each other.

* **Madrid's education department named the wrong school — then the threats started**

The Community of Madrid's education chief attributed a strike-week email to the only state school in Villarejo, a town east of the capital; the message had come from a private school and was misread, an official file was opened on the public-school head, and violent messages followed. ([eldiario.es][2])

💡 The strike is two days old; the new fact is a government false attribution that put a town's only state school in the firing line.

* **The government is preparing a roughly 5% rise in the minimum wage before the vote**

Spain's *salario mínimo interprofesional* is the statutory wage floor; the labour ministry is preparing an increase of about 5% before 29 November, after an experts' report, with unions asking for 7% and employers calling the timing a campaign move. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **The ECB has cleared Unicredit to buy Commerzbank**

Commerzbank is one of Germany's two large private banks; the European Central Bank has now given Unicredit, the Italian group that has been building a stake, regulatory room to take it over — a step the federal government has spent months trying to slow. ([Berliner Zeitung][4])

💡 The veto, if there is still one, is now political, not prudential.

* **The EU court says banks must repay phishing victims first**

The European Court of Justice has told banks they must refund customers who were tricked into handing over payment details even when the bank alleges gross negligence — the refund comes first, the fight over fault later. ([Berliner Zeitung][5])

* **Union and SPD can neither work together nor face an election**

Handelsblatt's morning note says the black-red coalition is heading for a *Beziehungstotalschaden* — a total breakdown of the relationship — but both parties still flinch from new elections, so the immediate test is whether MPs will stick with the reform course. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Greens will decide on Monday whether to open coalition talks**

After Berlin's election, the Greens, Die Linke and the SPD have been in exploratory talks; the Green party will now take the decision on formal coalition negotiations at a meeting on Monday. ([rbb24][7])

* **The Füchse are through in the Champions League**

Füchse Berlin, the city's handball club, beat FC Porto 36–33 and locked second place in their group, which puts them into the Champions League main round. ([rbb24][8])

* **Klaus Kinski would have been 100**

Kinski was the German screen actor whose rages and vampire-and-visionary roles still sit in the public head; a Tagesspiegel anniversary recap keeps the shadows in. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **AI data-centre debt is having its Darwin moment**

Bonds sold by the firms that build and rent AI compute — including "neoclouds" such as CoreWeave — have fallen to stressed levels as investors dump the paper, a shake-out the market is already calling Darwinian. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The AI scare has moved from the share price to the loans that pay for the buildings.

* **Indian software stocks are beating the chip names by the most in decades**

India's listed IT services firms — the outsourcing groups that write and run other companies' systems — are on course to outperform global semiconductor stocks by the widest gap in twenty years as the AI-chip rally unwinds. ([Bloomberg][11])

* **Senegal's new homosexuality law has put hundreds in cells**

After the law on "acts against nature" was toughened, Human Rights Watch counts at least 320 arrests since February, including forced HIV tests and charges of "voluntary transmission" based on status alone; people are going into hiding. ([The Guardian][12])
