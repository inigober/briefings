# News Briefing — 17 September 2026

Feijóo locks Ceuta's minors with Vox, Fraunhofer sells privacy as an industrial edge, a fuse-box fire stops Wittenbergplatz, and the Fed hikes as Huawei challenges Nvidia.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Feijóo has closed the door on moving Ceuta's minors to the peninsula**

Alberto Núñez Feijóo, who leads Spain's opposition PP, has aligned with Vox against sending any unaccompanied minors from Ceuta to mainland Spain — a lock the government says prolongs the enclave crisis rather than ending it. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 Peninsula relocation is now a Vox red line for the PP, not a relief valve for a city still holding July's crossing.

* **Ayuso's spokesman has to explain a luxury penthouse bought with public money**

Miguel Ángel García Martín, Madrid's presidency minister, appears before the regional assembly over Planifica Madrid's purchase of a luxury attic — a deal Más Madrid and the PSOE say the government is blocking the file on. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Anthropic has opened in Spain, pitching Madrid as a Spanish-speaking hub**

The Claude-maker has landed with former Google executive Cristina Pitarch and is already inside Santander, CaixaBank, BBVA, Telefónica and Indra — a commercial beach-head while the industry argues about slowing down. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Fraunhofer says Europe's privacy rules can be a competitive advantage**

A Fraunhofer study argues that data protection — the GDPR-era quality-over-hoarding model — can make European firms more competitive, not less, if they sell trust instead of maximum collection. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 Brussels' least-loved tech regulation is being recast as a product feature.

* **Berentzen is talking to a US whiskey giant about a takeover**

The Haselünne apple-korn distiller says Sazerac, the privately held US spirits group behind Buffalo Trace, is considering a bid — a German mid-cap drinks brand looking at an American owner. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Shorter days, colder air: what autumn actually does to the body**

Handelsblatt runs five claims about seasonal tiredness, weight and airways against the evidence — a physiology note, not a hospital reform. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A fuse-box fire has shut Wittenbergplatz U-Bahn station**

A technical fault set a Sicherungskasten alight; U1, U2 and U3 are skipping the Ku'damm interchange for the morning at least. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **Charité staff are being called out on strike from Thursday**

Verdi has summoned workers at Berlin's university hospital, with emergency cover — a new walkout two weeks after a three-day warning strike. ([rbb24][8])

* **A free heart-valve check is on offer at Hauptbahnhof**

Valve disease often hides; a Friday screening at the main station is offering free checks. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Trump's Fed pick has delivered a rate rise the White House did not want**

Kevin Warsh, less than four months in the chair, has led the US central bank to its first hike since 2023 — a reminder that even a loyalist can still move against the president. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The first test of Warsh was whether he would run a campaign bank; the answer, this week, is no.

* **Huawei has put 11 AI chips on the table against Nvidia, Intel and AMD**

The Shenzhen group is rolling out a full-ish accelerator stack — a hardware bid to run Chinese (and export) AI without US silicon. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **Sahel jihadist violence is on course for a record year**

Conflict monitor Acled says offensives by al-Qaida- and Islamic State-linked groups in the Sahel are heading for historic highs, 25 years after 9/11 — Africa's arid belt, not the Hindu Kush, is now the centre of that war. ([The Guardian][12])
