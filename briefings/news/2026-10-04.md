# News Briefing — 4 October 2026

Spain's voters and parties have split over the dead housing decrees, Merz is in Kyiv with a drone deal, and China is shutting record numbers of small banks.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **PP, Vox and Junts voters back the housing shield their parties just killed**

More than half of PP, Vox and Junts voters support the anti-eviction shield and the ban on vulture funds buying homes — the two measures those same parties voted down in Congress on Friday. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 The housing bloc in the electorate is wider than the housing bloc in parliament.

* **Most of the PSOE now wants Sánchez to call the election**

A majority of Pedro Sánchez's party and of his own government are telling the Socialist prime minister to dissolve now, calling Friday's defeat an opening; a minority still wants another housing decree first. ([EL PAÍS][2])

* **Junts is betting its recovery on Puigdemont's return**

Junts, the Catalan party whose votes sank the decrees, is writing its next phase around Carles Puigdemont coming back and is treating the housing backlash as background noise. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Merz turned up in Kyiv unannounced — with a drone deal in the bag**

Chancellor Friedrich Merz made a surprise morning arrival in Kyiv with economics minister Katherina Reiche, aiming to lock in a drone package and more military aid after Russia had warned against the trip. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Erfurt wants to be East Germany's other chip city**

When German semiconductor money is discussed, the map usually stops at Dresden; Erfurt, Thuringia's capital, is now pushing a large fabrication bet on a chip-making tradition of its own. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

💡 The East German chip story is no longer a single-city subsidy.

* **The AfD is at 30% — and the Union at a record low**

A new Insa poll puts the AfD, Germany's far-right party, at 30% and the CDU/CSU at 18%, with satisfaction in Chancellor Merz and his government equally thin. ([Berliner Zeitung][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **The Gorki is being turned into a listening membrane**

Nicole L'Huillier, a transdisciplinary artist, has wrapped the Maxim Gorki Theater — the Mitte house known for post-migrant political work — in *Membrana*, an installation that treats the building as a body that hears. ([The Berliner][7])

* **Hertha has kept its rhythm through the international break**

Hertha BSC, Berlin's 2. Bundesliga club, has not gone cold during the pause; a friendly at Bundesliga side Augsburg is the latest evidence the match tempo is still there. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **The Füchse scraped a win on the road in Bietigheim**

Berlin's Bundesliga handball club took a tight away game in Bietigheim, a result that mattered more than the scoreline. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **China shut a record 670 lenders last year**

Beijing closed more than 670 banks — a record — in a cleanup that Fitch says still leaves the smallest lenders as the weak point in the financial system. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 The purge is a map of which banks the state will no longer underwrite by default.

* **Kawasaki wants a fully autonomous humanoid robot by 2030**

Kawasaki Heavy Industries, the Japanese group behind trains and aircraft, says it is aiming for a fully autonomous humanoid AI robot by the end of the decade. ([Nikkei Asia][11])

* **The people who write with AI are telling everyone else not to**

Writers who already use AI as an "assistive tool" are the same cohort calling other people's AI drafts cheating — a hypocrisy that has become the new literacy argument. ([The Atlantic][12])
