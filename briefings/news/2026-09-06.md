# News Briefing — 6 September 2026

Spain's teachers are back on the streets as Madrid opens Ceuta's files, a Bavarian rocket has reached orbit, and Berlin's districts are refusing the Senate's hacker software while tanker war returns to the Gulf.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's 'teachers' legislature' is opening with strikes**

After the summer break, Spanish teachers are back on the streets as the last year of the government's self-styled "legislatura del profesorado" begins, while Education still owes promised cuts to class sizes and teaching hours — Catalunya starts with a one-day strike; Madrid has a start date but no end. ([eldiario.es][1])

* **Madrid will declassify files to show nobody saw the Ceuta jump coming**

The city's boycott of reception sites, including the fight over the port, has dragged out the late-July rush into the North African enclave; on Tuesday the government will declassify documents to argue that no one foresaw a crossing this large. ([EL PAÍS][2])

💡 The dump is a political defence: prove the rush was unforeseeable.

* **Spanish families recount Australia's asbestos 'Chernobyl'**

Two families who left Spain for Wittenoom in the 1960s describe the blue-asbestos town in Western Australia now likened to Chernobyl, where the mine has been linked to more than 2,000 deaths. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **A Bavarian startup has put satellites into orbit on its second try**

Isar Aerospace, founded only in 2018, has on its second test fired a rocket into space and delivered satellites to Earth orbit — a rare European launch success from a private German firm. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **1&1 is still blaming Vodafone for a rocky fourth-network launch**

Since 2023, 1&1 has been Germany's fourth mobile-network operator; its start was messy, and in the latest round of their dispute the newer firm is pointing at Vodafone. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **The federal government is lining up another tax rise on vape flavours**

Aroma mixes for e-cigarettes — honey-berry, watermelon-mint — already got more expensive at the start of 2026, and the federal government is expected to turn the duty screw again. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Lichtenberg is refusing the Senate's post-hack security software**

After the attack on Berlin's state network, the eastern district is blocking a Senate-hired firm from its servers — a fight between the city government and the Bezirke over who may lock down the IT. ([rbb24][7])

💡 The leak fight has moved from phishing warnings to which contractor may touch a district's machines.

* **Five departing deputies are closing their Abgeordnetenhaus years**

Five long-serving members of the city parliament, from Die Linke to the AfD, are not standing again before the 20 September vote and are looking back at the work — and at what comes next. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Police went to Berlin lidos 391 times this summer — mostly for theft**

Officers were called hundreds of times to the city's outdoor pools; most of the jobs were small crimes such as thefts, not violence. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **China's record graduate class is hitting an AI job market**

A record 12.7 million young people are looking for work as Beijing pushes artificial intelligence into the economy — and as the same technology starts to upend the jobs they trained for. ([The New York Times][10])

💡 The youth-jobs crisis and the AI rollout are now the same labour market.

* **The US has struck three Iranian oil tankers after attacks on its warships**

Military action over the Strait of Hormuz is escalating again: Washington says it hit three Iranian tankers in reply to attacks on US ships, after a pause in the six-month-old war. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Brazil's former air-force chief describes the meeting meant to stop Bolsonaro's coup**

Carlos de Almeida Baptista Júnior, who led the air force, recounts in a new book the 2022 session where Jair Bolsonaro's defence minister handed the service chiefs a document as the then-president still refused the election result — while Bolsonaro's son now vies with Lula. ([The Guardian][12])
