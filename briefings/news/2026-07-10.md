# News Briefing — 10 July 2026

A reopened Kitchen-corruption probe, coalition fights over housing expropriation and VW's stalled shrink plan, and a Berlin CDU leadership crisis frame a day when court rulings and export-control gaps expose how domestic institutions absorb external shocks.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Judge orders police back onto Rajoy–Cospedal trail in Kitchen case**

eldiario.es reports magistrate Antonio Piña has tasked Internal Affairs with recovering RAC1 recordings in which María Dolores de Cospedal allegedly discussed police "cleaning" compromising material from Luis Bárcenas at the request of "the president." With the Villarejo trial due to end on 30 July, investigators will return to a line of inquiry State lawyers say was meant to shield Mariano Rajoy. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 When a judge reopens an evidence trail days before trial closure, Spain's revolving-door corruption cases risk looking procedurally endless rather than finally adjudicated.

* **PP votes against 50% central funding for dependency care it long demanded**

eldiario.es notes regional governments run by Feijóo's party voted down a measure to raise Madrid's share of long-term care costs to half the total — despite years of demanding exactly that transfer. The initiative still advances with support from PSOE and Sumar partners, but the opposition's split vote undercuts a flagship regional complaint. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **At least 12 die in Los Gallardos wildfire, Andalucía's deadliest blaze**

EL PAÍS reports more than 200 firefighters are battling two simultaneous forest fires in Almería and Málaga, with at least a dozen people found dead inside burned vehicles near Los Gallardos. Regional authorities call it the most lethal wildfire episode in Andalucía to date this season. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **VW supervisory board shelves Blume's radical shrink plan — for now**

Handelsblatt says the carmaker's oversight body has not signed off on chief executive Oliver Blume's proposals for plant closures and deep job cuts, leaving Europe's largest automaker in limbo alongside a home market still debating industrial reform. Works councils and state shareholders appear to have slowed a restructuring blueprint presented as unavoidable. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Coalition's housing expropriation ban raises constitutional workaround questions**

Handelsblatt examines whether Schwarz-Rot can legally bar the forced purchase of large landlords when the Basic Law still allows socialisation in principle. Legal scholars quoted in the piece warn the draft risks a "sleight of hand" that could face immediate constitutional challenge. ([Handelsblatt][5])

💡 A federal ban on expropriation would pre-empt Berlin's voted path to socialise Deutsche Wohnen — shifting housing politics from city ballots to Bundestag majorities.

* **Economists split on whether agreed reform package can end Germany's slump**

In Handelsblatt's Economic Challenges podcast, Bert Rürup and Michael Hüther debate whether the coalition's newly agreed growth package contains enough supply-side and fiscal measures to pull Germany out of stagnation. Both agree the deal leans heavily on state-led industrial policy rather than market liberalisation. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Internal CDU letter puts Kai Wegner's candidacy in doubt**

Berliner Zeitung reports a confidential missive from within the Berlin CDU questions whether the governing mayor can credibly lead the party into September's Abgeordnetenhaus election after the blackout communications affair. Kreis chair leaders have scheduled emergency talks as polling shows the centre-right losing ground. ([Berliner Zeitung][7])

* **Legal scholars say federal expropriation ban could block Berlin's referendum mandate**

Tagesspiegel asks whether the Bund can override a capital-wide vote to socialise large landlords, citing constitutional experts who call the looming federal ban unprecedented. Berlin's senate argues voters' 2021 mandate cannot be voided by national legislation without a court fight. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Triathlete Laura Lindemann targets 2028 Olympics after overcoming motivation slump**

rbb24 profiles the Berlin-born athlete's return to World Cup podiums following a prolonged mental-health break, with Los Angeles 2028 now her focus for a first individual Olympic medal. Coaches say the comeback required restructuring training loads after years at the sport's elite level. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **OpenAI and Google supplied AI services to blacklisted Chinese groups via Singapore**

The Financial Times reports US technology firms have been selling model access to Singapore-based subsidiaries of Alibaba, Baidu and Tencent that remain on Washington's entity list. The routing highlights how export-control enforcement struggles to track cloud-delivered AI when intermediaries sit offshore. ([Financial Times][10])

💡 When frontier models reach sanctioned groups through third-country subsidiaries, US chip and software controls face a services loophole as large as the hardware gaps they were designed to close.

* **SK Hynix raises $26.5bn in largest foreign listing on US markets**

The Financial Times says the South Korean memory-chip maker completed the biggest-ever US debut by a foreign company, capitalising on AI-driven demand for high-bandwidth DRAM. The listing deepens Seoul's bet that packaging and memory — not only foundry logic — will capture AI infrastructure spending. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Fake federal agency in Nigerian budget triggers pre-election corruption storm**

The Guardian reports President Bola Tinubu ordered an investigation after a fictitious body was allocated 1.3bn naira in the 2026 budget, with officials alleging forged signatures inside the presidential complex. The scandal lands four months before a January general election in Africa's most populous democracy. ([The Guardian][12])
