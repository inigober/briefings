# News Briefing — 30 August 2026

A reconstruction cheque for Ceuta, Germany's record power prices, Berlin's leaked state files, and an AI race that looks the same in China as in Fukushima's ruins set today's frame.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Sánchez will put another €165 million into Ceuta this Tuesday**

Pedro Sánchez's government, a month into the enclave's migration crisis, will take an urgent cabinet package that adds €165 million for recovery — billed as 4% of Ceuta's output in aid — so the political year opens with a reconstruction cheque rather than tents. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The crisis is being refinanced as a city-recovery budget, which is how you end a news cycle you cannot police away.

* **Spain's last legislative year is also a jobs race for the ECB, the ILO and the FAO**

Labour minister Yolanda Díaz and agriculture minister Luis Planas are campaigning to run two UN agencies, and Madrid is watching a possible opening at the top of the euro — personnel bets that would outlast this Cortes. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Murcia is becoming Spain's other boat door, and the radar is thin**

The Guardia Civil still runs SIVE, the coastal surveillance system, off Murcia, but police unions say boats and staff are short as an Algerian route professionalises and regional president Fernando López Miras asks for soldiers on the shore. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **A Verivox study says Germany now has the G20's highest electricity prices**

The comparison site's ranking gives business lobbies the number they have been arguing from — that power costs are a location disadvantage, not just a household bill. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 Once the price is a league table, the energy transition is a competitiveness file, not an infrastructure one.

* **BYD is taking the hybrid price war to German showrooms**

A study says the Chinese carmaker is undercutting German brands on plug-in hybrids with low list prices and heavy discounts, and that the next argument may be tariffs again. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **IFA's boss says electronics fairs now have to be entertainment**

Germany's big consumer-electronics show is being recast as a stage because the industry's sales have been falling and a trade-fair floor no longer sells itself. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Extortionists are now threatening to publish Berlin's stolen files**

After the attack on the Landesnetz, the city-state's administrative network, criminals are threatening to leak what they took; a security expert walks through how stolen citizen and staff data can be used. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

💡 A ransom note is a negotiation; a leak threat is a market for other people's records.

* **Union Berlin is close to a Swiss midfielder, reports say**

Kastriot Imeri is said to be near a move to 1. FC Union, the Köpenick Bundesliga club, with coach Mauro Lustrinelli already knowing the player. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **Alba opened its season with two friendly wins**

Alba Berlin, the city's basketball club, started its campaign with two test-game victories. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **AI robots and drones are being sent into Fukushima's cleanup**

Japan is putting machines into the wrecked nuclear plant — work still too dangerous for people — as a test of whether the AI stack can do physical decommissioning, not just chat. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **The Indiana state fair is being read as a test of Trump's Midwest**

The FT asks whether the most stubborn conservative state in the Midwest will finally reject his politics, using the state fair as the stage. ([Financial Times][11])

* **China's AI future, seen up close, looks like America's**

A Rest of World reporter went to China expecting a different model of the AI race and found two countries building toward a similar stack. ([Rest of World][12])

💡 The useful finding is convergence: the hardware, the apps and the surveillance logic rhyme.
