# News Briefing — 28 August 2026

Ceuta's crisis is now a fight over food, Germany is arguing over AI jobs and coal, Berlin's hackers want a ransom, and the AI boom is claiming Indian land and American patients.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Ceuta's reception fight has become a blockade of food aid**

Madrid is asking the PP to help stop violence in Ceuta after a joint call from territorial minister Ángel Víctor Torres and Ceuta president Juan Jesús Vivas failed to prevent about a hundred people attacking a Red Cross delivery to migrants. ([EL PAÍS][1])

💡 The enclave argument has moved from which buildings to use to whether aid is allowed to arrive at all.

* **The counsellor Ayuso named over the Chamberí penthouse will go to the Assembly**

Miguel Ángel García Martín, the Madrid official Isabel Díaz Ayuso pointed to in the purchase of a Chamberí rooftop as office space, has asked to appear before the regional Assembly to discuss Planifica Madrid, the public housing company in the deal — though no date is set and the presidency says he has already explained himself. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **EL PAÍS has had a first look at GTA VI**

Spain's main newspaper was taken through Grand Theft Auto VI, the industry's most anticipated game, inside Rockstar, the studio behind the series; co-director Rob Nelson's line is that he hopes it meets the expectations. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **AI is hitting Germany's young workers, and a youth researcher thinks the AfD will collect the wreckage**

Mathias Albert, a youth researcher, says the labour-market shock from AI is landing hardest on young adults, that politics has not grasped the disruption, and that a "lost generation" would feed the AfD. ([Tagesspiegel][4])

💡 If entry-level work is the first thing models replace, the party that already owns young protest voters does not need a new programme — only a tighter labour market.

* **Sahra Wagenknecht will not call the AfD far-right — she will call parts of the government that**

The BSW leader, in a podcast on speech rules, declined to describe Alternative for Germany as extremist and said that, on one definition, parts of the federal government already qualify. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **A Union deputy wants coal plants back on while Iran keeps gas expensive**

Sepp Müller, deputy leader of the CDU/CSU group in the Bundestag, is pitching coal as a bridge until new gas plants are built, with the Iran war still holding gas prices up. ([Handelsblatt][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Berlin's state-network hackers have sent a ransom demand**

After the attack on the Landesnetz, the city-state's administrative network, a ransom note has arrived; officials are not yet sure it is from the original attackers, and a meeting with security agencies is set for Friday. ([rbb24][7])

💡 The outage is now a negotiation, whether or not the city intends to pay.

* **The AfD's Kristin Brinker is running for Berlin again, from behind the firewall**

Kristin Brinker is the AfD's lead candidate for the Abgeordnetenhaus for the third time, in a race where no other party will govern with her even as the party expects large gains. ([rbb24][8])

* **The Fabian trial will not reach a verdict before October**

Berlin's regional court has added six more hearing dates in the Fabian case after the defence filed new evidence motions, including an expert report meant to challenge the assumed sequence of the crime. ([Berliner Zeitung][9])

## World 🌐

* **India's data-centre boom is leaving the people it displaces with nothing**

As AI firms lock in tax breaks and cheap land from state governments, communities cleared for the warehouses of the compute build-out are not sharing in the proceeds. ([Rest of World][10])

💡 The same stack Spain is trying to license is, in India, already a land-and-compensation problem.

* **Cowboy clinics in the US are selling unproven extra years**

An FT investigation finds a thriving American market for stem-cell therapies sold as longevity medicine; specialists say the claims are dangerous. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Japan should not raid its foreign-exchange reserves to pay for Takaichi's food-tax cut**

Nikkei argues that using the country's FX hoard to fund Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's cut in food taxes would be the wrong fiscal tool for a cost-of-living promise. ([Nikkei Asia][12])
