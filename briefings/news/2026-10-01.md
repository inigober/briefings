# News Briefing — 1 October 2026

Spain's housing decrees now encode indefinite leases and a state arrears backstop, German car plants are squeezing the shop floor as Lusatia raises a record turbine, and Tokyo has given the human voice a legal shield against AI clones.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Automatic lease renewals now come with a year's rent as the exit fee**

Spain's second housing decree-law, due in the official gazette on Thursday, makes rental contracts roll on automatically — a de facto indefinite tenancy already used in Italy, France and the UK — and requires a landlord who breaks the chain to pay the tenant twelve months' rent. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 A year of rent as the price of ending a tenancy is how an emergency decree becomes a tenure regime.

* **The state will pay the arrears that keep vulnerable tenants in place past 2030**

Where there is no alternative home and the landlord is a company or owns more than two dwellings, Spain's regions will settle the unpaid rent and Madrid will reimburse them — a permanent eviction pause paid for by the Treasury, not only a date on a moratorium. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **Selling an empty home to the state now comes with a full tax write-off**

Owners who sell vacant homes to public housing companies get a 100% capital-gains exemption on properties under €200,000, tapering to a cap of €800,000, plus a further break if the proceeds go into an investment account. ([EL PAÍS][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **VW, Mercedes and BMW are tightening the shop floor**

Germany's three volume carmakers are stacking new demands on their workforces — longer flexibility, harder shifts, thinner buffers — as the auto slump that began in the order book reaches the line; at one of the three, workers can also take a larger share if they play along. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 The car slump is no longer only a factory-closure story; it is a rewrite of what a German autoworker can refuse.

* **The Deutschlandticket will cost €66.80 from January**

The national flat-rate local-transit pass — the Deutschlandticket that replaced a patchwork of regional fares — rises by €3.80 next year after Wednesday's price-setting council, a small number that hits every commuter who bought the climate-fare promise. ([rbb24][5])

* **The world's tallest wind turbine is up in Lusatia — and unproven**

A record-height turbine now stands in southern Brandenburg's coal country, a marker of the region's post-mine energy bet; the practical test of whether an XXL tower survives real wind and grid conditions is still ahead. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Neukölln's Ferat Kocak has quit the Bundestag interior committee**

Ferat Kocak, a Berlin Left MP for Neukölln, has given up his seat on the Bundestag's interior committee after reports he had contacts in clan networks — a personnel retreat by the party that just won the Abgeordnetenhaus. ([rbb24][7])

* **Die Linke wants Brandenburg's interior minister silenced in court**

Berlin's Left is seeking a cease-and-desist against Brandenburg interior minister Jan Redmann, and others, over Instagram attacks after the party's House win — a legal counterpunch where a political one has not landed. ([rbb24][8])

* **Ten Berlin scientists are rewriting how we see bodies and nature**

Tagesspiegel's annual list of the capital's hundred most important researchers highlights ten working from the cell to the rhinoceros — using living systems as both an invention template and a source of new disease. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **A Tokyo court has given the human voice the same rights as a face**

Tokyo District Court ruled that an AI tool which cloned actor Kenjiro Tsuda's "lustrous" baritone for TikTok videos violated his publicity rights — treating a voice as legally protectable in Japan, not merely a performance. ([The Guardian][10])

💡 The first serious constraint on voice clones in a major market is publicity law, not an AI statute.

* **Tencent is renting 100,000 chips from Oracle to catch up in AI**

The WeChat owner has leased about 100,000 processors from Oracle for the US group's South-East Asian data centres, a rented path to American compute in its race with ByteDance and Alibaba. ([Financial Times][11])

* **Lula is running on defence factories, not only social policy**

Brazil's president has put military-industrial spending at the centre of his re-election campaign — an unusual pitch for a left-wing incumbent, aimed at jobs and autonomy as much as at Washington. ([Foreign Policy][12])
