# News Briefing — 6 July 2026

Spain maps who wins and loses in a €28 billion annual rental transfer, Germany confronts an innovation deficit and democratic risk from the far right, and Berlin learns what January's blackout cost — while chip supply chains, religious politics and shipping's energy retreat show how global industry is rewiring outside Europe.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Annual rental flows now move €28 billion — roughly 1.6% of Spanish GDP**

eldiario.es maps how rent payments have doubled over a decade, tracing a large yearly transfer from millions of tenants to a much smaller pool of landlords and highlighting generational and income divides. The piece treats housing less as a monthly bill than as a macro redistribution mechanism reshaping household budgets. ([eldiario.es][1])

💡 Quantifying the cash flow makes the housing crisis legible as a structural transfer problem — harder for governments to dismiss as a supply tweak alone.

* **Audiencia Nacional sentences ex-PP minister Granados to 2.5 years in Púnica corruption case**

eldiario.es reports Francisco Granados was convicted for using his Madrid regional post to steer public contracts to a events firm that funded PP gatherings, including parties linked to Esperanza Aguirre. The ruling closes a decade-long probe into clientelist networks around the conservative establishment. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **PSOE schedules July primaries in five regions amid post-Ábalos judicial storm**

eldiario.es notes the party will hold first-round candidate votes on 19 July across Balearics, Cantabria, Valencia, Murcia and Madrid just days after the Supreme Court's 24-year sentence for José Luis Ábalos, with Sánchez insisting the party will "clean up and keep governing." The calendar tests whether internal renewal can outpace scandal headlines. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Handelsblatt warns Germany is researching too little to escape its growth trap**

The business daily argues innovation output is falling behind peers and that Schwarz-Rot's current reform package is unlikely to close the gap, leaving medium-term competitiveness at risk. Editors frame the problem as under-investment in future technologies rather than a cyclical downturn alone. ([Handelsblatt][4])

💡 A growth narrative built on legacy industry cannot self-fund the R&D pivot Berlin says it wants — the mismatch is becoming an explicit editorial verdict, not background noise.

* **Business leaders warn AfD strength poses a growing threat to German democracy**

Handelsblatt cites a new study showing many executives struggle to distance their firms from the far-right party as it gains ground, with some asking how long corporate non-engagement can hold. The report treats democratic stability as a boardroom risk, not only a ballot-box story. ([Handelsblatt][5])

* **Experts debate whether screen content — not just screen time — drives youth depression**

Tagesspiegel covers a German argument that rising adolescent depression may reflect what young people watch online, not merely how long they scroll, as politicians weigh tighter digital safeguards. Researchers caution evidence is incomplete but say content quality deserves equal scrutiny to usage caps. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Expert commission delivers report on January's citywide blackout**

rbb24 reports an independent panel has presented findings on the power failure that left much of Berlin without electricity in January, as the Senate faces questions over grid resilience and crisis communications. The publication lands after months of political dispute over what triggered the outage and who knew what when. ([rbb24][7])

* **Inclusion assistants for disabled pupils run short as costs rise and budgets tighten**

rbb24 explains Berlin families depend on paid school escorts so children with disabilities can attend mainstream classes, yet funding hours often fall below need as federal and local governments haggle over who pays. Advocates say a legal right is becoming a budget negotiation. ([rbb24][8])

* **CDU pulls Berlin election campaign video offline after criticism**

Tagesspiegel's Checkpoint reports the Christian Democrats temporarily removed a promotional clip from their state-election push following backlash, without detailing the specific complaint. The move comes three months before Berlin votes with the party trailing in recent polls. ([Tagesspiegel][9])

## World 🌐

* **Japanese AI chip startup partners with Malaysia's Oppstar to scale production**

Nikkei Asia reports the startup is tapping Malaysian semiconductor services firm Oppstar to move from design toward manufacturing, part of a wider push to build non-US AI silicon supply outside Silicon Valley's orbit. The deal underscores how East Asian fabrication networks are competing for niche AI hardware roles. ([Nikkei Asia][10])

* **Founder of prominent underground Chinese church released and reunites with family in US**

The Guardian says Ezra Jin, leader of Beijing's Zion Church, arrived in the United States after detention in China's October crackdown on unregistered congregations. His release is a rare concession in Beijing's tightening control over religious life. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Shipowners retreat from green shipping bets toward fossil fuels and nuclear**

The Financial Times describes an industry split as some carriers shelve alternative-fuel projects and explore nuclear propulsion or conventional bunkers, citing cost and technology delays. The shift complicates maritime decarbonisation targets written for a faster transition. ([Financial Times][12])

💡 When freight economics trump climate roadmaps, regulators lose leverage over a sector that accounts for a rising share of transport emissions.
