# News Briefing — 19 November 2026

Spain's only two-way campaign debate, Berlin's scramble over school data security and a hospital strike, and a record Anthropic listing alongside a looming ICJ genocide ruling and Trump's Iran claims set the frame for today's edition.

## Spain 🇪🇸

* **Spain's prime minister Pedro Sánchez and conservative opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo meet tonight in the campaign's only head-to-head debate, with the Socialists (PSOE) two points behind the People's Party (PP)**

The latest 40dB poll gives the PP 32.4% against the PSOE's 30.4% and the PP plus far-right Vox 192 seats, so Sánchez needs to show the race is open while Feijóo, who is not using the adviser who coached him in 2023, needs to avoid losing a contest he is not yet seen to have won. ([EL PAÍS][1])

* **The Basque National Party (PNV), long the kingmaker among Spain's regional parties, faces its hardest general election in years, with polls putting its own group in Congress and even all its senators at risk**

Its leader Aitor Esteban has been distancing the party from Sánchez, demanding to know "once and for all" whether the prime minister knew of the corruption cases around his circle. ([eldiario.es][2])

* **A National Court judge has opened an investigation into singer Julio Iglesias for alleged sexual violence, ruling that the detailed account of two former employees is enough to start the case**

Judge Antonio Piña stressed that he has not yet assessed the complainants' credibility and threw out several of the other offences alleged. ([eldiario.es][3])

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Rent affordability varies widely across Germany: a ranking by the property-owners' association Haus & Grund shows many districts where rents are modest relative to local incomes, even as big-city markets stay tight**

The ranking sets rents against incomes rather than looking at asking prices alone, which changes which regions look expensive. ([Handelsblatt][4])

* **Foreign minister Johann Wadephul spent the week in the South Caucasus talking up the region's rapprochement with Europe, but his flight home from Armenia to Berlin was held up**

The trip is part of Berlin's push to anchor Armenia and its neighbours closer to the EU. ([Tagesspiegel][5])

* **Saxony's conservative premier Michael Kretschmer and Greens co-leader Franziska Brantner clashed on the ARD talk show "Maischberger", with Kretschmer protesting he was "not in an inquisition"**

The row began over Christmas stollen and the Pope before escalating, a reminder of how raw relations between the CDU and Greens remain. ([Tagesspiegel][6])

## Berlin 🏙️

* **A nurses' strike at the Charité, Berlin's university hospital, is forcing up to 30 percent of treatments to be postponed, with patients of other hospitals affected too**

The pay dispute at Europe's largest university clinic shows how much of the city's hospital capacity depends on a single employer. ([Tagesspiegel][7])

* **A security flaw nicknamed "Java4Shell" has reached Berlin's schools, forcing central applications offline after a warning of an acute risk to sensitive pupil data**

Java4Shell is a vulnerability in widely used Java software that authorities have warned about nationwide, and the warning covers all Berlin schools. ([Tagesspiegel][8])

* **A largely unknown site on Pankow's northern edge is being pitched to rescue Berlin's bid to host the 2035 World Expo after Brandenburg declined to provide land**

The plan depends on the likely new SPD-Greens-Linke coalition agreeing to it; the three parties have said they will review the current government's Expo plans. ([rbb24][9])

## World 🌐

* **Anthropic, the US developer of the Claude AI assistant, raised $81bn in a record stock-market flotation that surpasses SpaceX's as the largest public listing ever**

Demand was strong enough that the company enlarged the offering, a sign of how much appetite remains for AI flotations. ([Financial Times][10])

* **The International Court of Justice, the UN's top court, rules today on whether Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, amid fears Washington could sanction its judges, including Nobel peace prize winner Navi Pillay**

The court has never before found a state responsible for genocide, and the judgment on how to prove "intent" could set a precedent for the case against Israel over Gaza. ([The Guardian][11])

* **Donald Trump says Iran appears to be working on biological weapons, after justifying the war against Iran with its alleged pursuit of nuclear arms and amid reports he is weighing a new strike**

The claim is unsubstantiated in the report, but it echoes the way the nuclear argument was used to build the case for the earlier war. ([Tagesspiegel][12])
