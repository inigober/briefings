## Spain 🇪🇸

* **BBVA forecasts 12% housing price growth in 2026 as construction accelerates**
  BBVA Research expects Spanish house prices to rise about 12% in 2026, close to 2025's 12.7% increase, with housing investment projected to grow 12.4%. The bank links demand to immigration-led household formation and rising participation by Spain-born buyers, but argues the price spiral will persist because supply remains structurally insufficient. ([El País][1])
  🧭 Spain's housing debate is shifting from whether prices will rise to whether construction can close the gap fast enough.
  🧩 Demographic demand is now a macro variable for the property market, not a secondary social story.

## Germany 🇩🇪

* **Employment fell by 486,000 in Q1 2026**
  Germany had 486,000 fewer people in employment in the first quarter of 2026 than in the previous quarter (−1.1%), according to official labour-market data reported by Handelsblatt. Manufacturing employment fell 2.1% and construction 1.1%, while the public sector added 181,000 jobs (+1.5%). ([Handelsblatt][2])
  🧭 The labour market is weakening in productive sectors even before a full recession narrative takes hold.
  🧩 Public-sector hiring is masking a broader industrial adjustment.

* **1.15 million job vacancies remain unfilled**
  Germany recorded 1.15 million open positions in Q1 2026, down 105,800 from the prior quarter but still implying 264 unemployed people per vacancy on average. Handelsblatt reports persistent mismatches between qualifications employers require and those jobseekers offer. ([Handelsblatt][3])
  🧭 Germany faces a paradox: rising unemployment alongside large unfilled demand.
  🧩 Skills policy may matter more than aggregate stimulus.

* **National workforce projected stable to 2045 — eastern regions lose ground**
  A federal spatial-planning projection (BBSR) suggests Germany's labour force will remain roughly stable through 2045, edging down only 0.5% from 2022 levels to about 43.3 million. The national headline hides sharp declines in eastern regions. ([Handelsblatt][4])
  🧭 Demographic policy will increasingly be regional, not federal.
  🧩 Aggregate stability can coexist with deepening east-west divergence.

## Berlin 🏙️

* **Experts accuse transport administration of blocking tram expansion**
  Specialists told Tagesspiegel that Berlin's transport authority is obstructing new tram routes during debate on the 2026–2028 public-transport plan, despite a planning horizon that runs to 2040. Critics argue near-term project lists are crowding out longer strategic corridors. ([Tagesspiegel][5])
  🧭 Berlin's sustainable-mobility rhetoric is running ahead of administrative follow-through.
  🧩 Tram networks are cheap capacity in a dense city — delays are cumulative.

* **Planned tram routes postponed or cancelled amid fiscal pressure**
  An updated city transport plan shows several tram extensions delayed or dropped as BVG consolidates costs and the Senate faces tight budgets. The emphasis is shifting toward maintaining existing infrastructure rather than expanding the network. ([Tagesspiegel][6])
  🧭 Fiscal stress is converting proactive network growth into reactive maintenance.
  🧩 Short-term savings risk higher long-term congestion and car dependence.
