# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of June 17–23, 2026

HAU programmes the week's strongest live work — ritual, migration, and participatory theatre — while ACUDkino stacks three new German-language features on one Sunday and KW keeps the Kyiv Biennial chapter open with a free-access evening.

## Top Picks

### Kyiv Biennial – A Bird That Cannot Land (Free Thursday)

**Venue:** KW Institute for Contemporary Art

**Date(s):** 11 June – 13 September 2026 (open through the week); **Free Thursday 18 June 2026**

**Time(s):** Wed–Mon 11:00–19:00 (closed Tue); Free Thursday 16:00–21:00

**Short Context:** The nomadic biennial still occupies KW with research-based work on exile, coloniality, and broken continuities across Eastern Europe and Central Asia. This Thursday's free evening lowers the threshold for a mid-week visit — useful if you skipped the opening fortnight ago.

**Why It Fits:** Politically urgent contemporary art with a concrete access hook this week; continues the city's most consequential exhibition without treating it as a repeat opening.

**Official Link:** [KW — Kyiv Biennial (plain-language info)](https://www.kw-berlin.de/en/info/plain-language)

### DER SOLDAT. Ein Übergangsritual

**Venue:** HAU2 (Hebbel am Ufer)

**Date(s):** Tuesday, 17 June 2026

**Time(s):** 19:00

**Short Context:** Julian Warner's world-premiere performance frames militarism as ritual transition — interdisciplinary theatre that treats the soldier's body as a site of ceremony and critique rather than spectacle.

**Why It Fits:** Experimental performing arts with explicit political framing; premiere-week timing and HAU's dramaturgical line make it the week's sharpest live commission.

**Official Link:** [HAU — schedule & tickets](https://www.hebbel-am-ufer.de/en/programme/schedule-tickets/)

### ACUDkino — Sunday feature triple bill

**Venue:** ACUDkino (Kino 1 & 2)

**Date(s):** Sunday, 21 June 2026

**Time(s):** 18:00 *Ich verstehe Ihren Unmut* (Kino 2); 18:15 *Gelbe Briefe (Sarı Zarflar)* (Kino 1); 20:00 *Rose* (Kino 2)

**Short Context:** Three contemporary German(-language) features in one evening: Kilian Armando Friedrich's workplace precarity drama, Ilker Çatak's migration-and-surveillance family story, and Markus Schleinzer's historical identity piece starring Sandra Hüller. A rare density of new political cinema in independent distribution.

**Why It Fits:** Socially engaged fiction with Berlin premiere energy; stacks three distinct auteur voices without leaving Mitte.

**Official Link:** [ACUDkino — *Ich verstehe Ihren Unmut*](https://acudkino.de/Programm/ich_verstehe_ihren_unmut/20467)
