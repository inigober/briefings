# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of September 23–29, 2026

The week is built from doubles and from records that will not stay still: Gob Squad’s German premiere turns the self into a party that cannot remember why it started, Nesterval walks the Scheunenviertel’s queer dead through the Sophiensæle, and Flora Yin-Wong occupies the Kuppelhalle with field recordings and ritual electronics. Around that triangle: a billboard of chance at Friedrichstraße, Arsenal’s archive festival, and a GDR feminist opening at Galerie im Turm.

## Top Picks

### Gob Squad — Doppelgänger/Doppelganger

**Venue:** HAU2, HAU Hebbel am Ufer, Hallesches Ufer 34

**Date(s):** Thursday, 24 September – Sunday, 27 September 2026 (German premiere Thursday)

**Time(s):** Thu 20:00; Fri 19:30; Sat 20:30; Sun 17:00; approx. 90 minutes. English live translation Thu/Sat; German Fri/Sun. Introduction Sun 16:15 with Sean Patten and Gábor Thury.

**Short Context:** Gob Squad’s German premiere is a hall of mirrors dressed as a birthday that nobody can quite place: cake, balloons, a photograph of people having a good time, and a collective that keeps splitting. Johanna Freiburg, Sean Patten, Tatiana Saphir, Sharon Smith, Berit Stumpf, Sarah Thom, Bastian Trost, and Simon Will work the usual Gob Squad contract — the audience is not decoration; it is the spare identity in the room. The piece asks where “we” begins once duplication, projection, and forgetting have done their work, and then lets that question tip into a party that will not resolve. Co-produced with Wiener Festwochen and &Espoo Theatre; about 90 minutes, in German and English with live translation depending on the night. Sunday’s early slot is the one if you want the introduction as well as the loop.

**Why It Fits:** Experimental theatre and participatory performance at a priority venue; identity as a structured problem rather than as a theme to be illustrated.

**Official Link:** [HAU — Gob Squad: Doppelgänger/Doppelganger](https://www.hebbel-am-ufer.de/en/programme/pdetail/gob-squad-doppelgaenger-doppelganger/)

### Nesterval’s Eldorado

**Venue:** Sophiensæle, Start: Kantine, Sophienstraße 18

**Date(s):** Thursday, 24 September – Sunday, 27 September 2026 (German-language premiere Thursday; English-language nights 1–4 October are the same production, not a second listing)

**Time(s):** 19:15; check-in 18:00–19:15, no late entry; approx. 3 hours 15 minutes. Box office from 17:00.

**Short Context:** Vienna’s queer Volks-theatre ensemble lands in Berlin for the first time with a house-wide, site-specific work on the Scheunenviertel around the Sophiensæle — the lives, loves, and murders of gay, lesbian, bisexual, and trans people before and under National Socialism, drawn from biographies and then split into parallel storylines you only ever see in part. You pick a figure, follow them through darkened rooms, and reconstruct the rest afterward; the conversation after the fact is part of the form, not a bonus Q&A. Martin Finnland directs; Teresa Löfberg wrote; the ensemble mixes actors, performers, and drag. It is long, immersive, and explicit about queerphobia, antisemitism, and violence — Kantine stays the awareness room. Four German nights this week; if you need English, hold 1–4 October rather than inventing a second festival.

**Why It Fits:** Queer history, memory, and experimental theatre with a named ensemble; a concrete four-night run through the building rather than a heritage panel.

**Official Link:** [Sophiensæle — Nestervals Eldorado](https://sophiensaele.com/de/stueck/nestervals-eldorado)

## Exhibitions Radar

### Dora Budor & Noah Barker. Monte Carlo Method

**Venue:** n.b.k. Billboard, intersection Friedrichstraße / Torstraße (screenings later at n.b.k. Erdgeschoss — not this week)

**Date(s):** On view 10 September – 29 November 2026 (billboard); film premiere 13 November 2026

**Time(s):** Outdoor billboard, always on view

**Short Context:** What you can see this week is the commission in the street, not the video it belongs to. Budor and Barker’s *Monte Carlo Method* (2026) takes the statistical technique that migrated from the Manhattan Project into financial forecasting and uses it to look at a city and a century built on chance; Lidiya Anastasova has placed the accompanying billboard a few steps from Neuer Berliner Kunstverein, at Friedrichstraße and Torstraße. The film itself premieres at n.b.k. on 13 November, with extra screening hours that weekend. Do not treat the November dates as this week’s appointment, and do not pretend the billboard is the exhibition in the galleries. It is a public-image placeholder for a moving-image argument that has not opened yet — which is still more specific than another indoor group hang with no end date.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary and research-based visual practice with a moving-image core at a priority institution; technology, chance, and public space rather than a seasonal cluster.

**Official Link:** [n.b.k. — Dora Budor & Noah Barker: Monte Carlo Method](https://vorschau.nbk.org/en/ausstellungen/billboard-budor-barker)
