# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of September 30–October 6, 2026

The week is built from family records that will not stay in the archive: Luanda and Pablo Casella turn a buried Brazilian scandal into a concert-theatre German premiere at HAU2, while HOUSE at Berghain opens a solar textile by Ala Roushan and Charles Stankievech. Around that: live-coded electronics in the Kuppelhalle, two ACUDkino documentaries, and a four-night Ballhaus run about kinship in transit.

## Top Picks

### Luanda Casella & Pablo Casella / NTGent — Trouble Score

**Venue:** HAU2, HAU Hebbel am Ufer, Hallesches Ufer 34

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 30 September – Thursday, 1 October 2026 (German premiere Wednesday)

**Time(s):** 19:00 both nights; approx. 100 minutes. English with English surtitles. Artist talk after the premiere (Wednesday) with Janez Janša, in English.

**Short Context:** The Casella siblings — Brazilian-born, Belgium-based, Luanda housed at NTGent — return to HAU for a fourth time with a piece that is storytelling, concert, and performance at once: an old family scandal, fragmentary crime stories, racial segregation and military dictatorship, then the whole thing transmuted into pop songs, hyperbolic analogies, and mantras. Pablo’s composition carries Lua’s language; pre-recorded voices (Quincy Gario, Nástio Mosquito, and others) sit inside live music; Lucius Romeo-Fromm is on direction assistance and choreography, Nick Verstand on light. The German premiere is Wednesday; Thursday is the second and last night this week. It is not a heritage panel with a guitar. It is a family record that has been scored until it can be stood in.

**Why It Fits:** Interdisciplinary performance and experimental theatre at a priority venue; memory, historical trauma, and a named NTGent collaboration rather than a workshop listing.

**Official Link:** [HAU — Luanda Casella & Pablo Casella / NTGent: Trouble Score](https://www.hebbel-am-ufer.de/en/programme/pdetail/luanda-casella-pablo-casella-ntgent-trouble-score/)

## Film & Screenings

### Roter Staub für weißes Gold

**Venue:** ACUDkino, Kino 1

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 30 September 2026

**Time(s):** 19:00; 60 minutes, German version. Film conversation and Q&A with director/film journalist Michael Enger after the screening.

**Short Context:** FIAN presents Michael Enger’s 2026 reportage in the *Alternative Rohstoffwoche*: almost three million tonnes of bauxite into Germany in 2024, 97 percent from Guinea, most of it from the CBG mine whose expansion Berlin underwrote with a €248 million federal guarantee. The “white gold” is aluminium for cars, packaging, and construction; the red dust is what the villages next to the pit actually get. Enger follows two NGO workers through settlements hit by the boom. One Wednesday night, then a talk — not a week-long repertory run. Do not invent extra dates from the rest of ACUDkino’s programme; this is the political-cinema appointment.

**Why It Fits:** Socially engaged documentary and political cinema with a post-screening discussion; resource extraction, borders, and German complicity rather than a generic nature film.

**Official Link:** [ACUDkino — Roter Staub für weißes Gold](https://acudkino.de/Programm/roter_staub_fur_weisses_gold/20518)

### Watching People Watching Birds

**Venue:** ACUDkino, Kino 2

**Date(s):** Thursday, 1 October; Friday, 2 October; Tuesday, 6 October 2026 (Berlin theatrical start)

**Time(s):** Thu 18:00; Fri 18:00; Tue 20:15; 92 minutes, original version with German subtitles

**Short Context:** Michael Loeken and Ulrike Franke turn the binoculars around. Bird numbers collapse; birdwatchers multiply; the film stays with the people who have decided that looking at what is left is a practice rather than a hobby — including Jonathan Franzen, who treats wild birds as a last usable connection to a natural world otherwise in retreat. Cineastic images, a dense sound world, humour next to the usual crisis register. Three slots this week, all Kino 2; Wednesday’s bauxite reportage is a different room and a different argument. Two ACUDkino listings is the cap.

**Why It Fits:** Hybrid documentary and observational cinema with ecology as structure rather than as caption; a dated Berlin start rather than a vague autumn documentary cluster.

**Official Link:** [ACUDkino — Watching People Watching Birds](https://acudkino.de/Programm/watching_people_watching_birds/20539)

### Your Baggage Unattended

**Venue:** Ballhaus Naunynstraße, Naunynstraße 27

**Date(s):** Saturday, 3 October – Tuesday, 6 October 2026

**Time(s):** Sat 20:00; Sun 19:00; Mon 20:00; Tue 20:00. German with English surtitles. 14€ / 8€.

**Short Context:** A family in a transit space — not a metaphor for “migration” so much as a room where mother, sister, daughter, brother, father, and son-in-law keep missing the words for what the Dort and the Hier did to them. Elona Beqiraj and Jenaba Samura wrote it; Thu Hoài Trần directs (her first production at the house after years as dramaturg); the cast is Elikem Anyigba, Neslihan Arol, Selin Kavak, Atilla Oener, Patrick Pinheiro, and Vanessa Yeboah. It premiered in June 2025 as the first play out of Ballhaus’s Extended Cooperation workshop; this week is a four-night return, not a new premiere, and that is still the performing-arts appointment once HAU2 has closed. Sunday’s 19:00 is the early slot if you want the week to end before Tuesday.

**Why It Fits:** Postmigrant theatre and experimental ensemble work at a priority venue; diaspora, care, and intergenerational expectation with named writers rather than an open call.

**Official Link:** [Ballhaus Naunynstraße — Your Baggage Unattended](https://ballhausnaunynstrasse.de/play/your-baggage-unattended/)
