# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of August 5–11, 2026

August opens with a concentrated mid-week cluster rather than a scatter of finissages: Radialsystem's SENSE programme runs two consecutive dance evenings, Silent Green pairs a geopolitically engaged exhibition opening with a stacked concert bill, and Wolf Kino hosts a Portuguese cinema week with director conversations. The visual-art calendar shifts toward Gropius Bau's institutional summer pair and PSM's late-summer gallery shows, while Georg Kolbe Museum anchors the week's major group opening west of the Spree.

## Top Picks

### THE PRESSING

**Venue:** Radialsystem

**Date(s):** Thursday, 6 August – Sunday, 9 August 2026

**Time(s):** Thu–Sat 20:30; Sun 19:30 (artist talk after Saturday performance)

**Short Context:** Dani Brown's solo performance returns to Radialsystem for a second August run within the SENSE festival frame — a vulva-centred choreographic proposition that reclaims the word 'CUNT' from vulgarity and treats the female sexual organ as a speculative artistic entity. Brown works from the neural connections between brain, clitoris, and carnal knowledge, accompanied by Justin F. Kennedy's immersive queer sound design and Jan Fack's Vanitas-inspired stage setting. The performance contains explicit nudity and runs approximately 80 minutes across four evenings; Saturday's show is followed by an artist talk moderated by Maja Zimmerman.

**Why It Fits:** Queer themes, gender, and body-political contemporary dance at a priority performance venue; interdisciplinary staging with named artists across choreography, sound, and scenography.

**Official Link:** [Radialsystem — THE PRESSING by Dani Brown](https://www.radialsystem.de/en/veranstaltungen/the-pressing-2026/)

### Islands of Time — Exhibition Opening with Pole, JakoJako & Rubén Nsue, Sunroof, Nicolas Bougaïeff

**Venue:** silent green Kulturquartier (Ateliers 2+3 and Kuppelhalle)

**Date(s):** Thursday, 6 August 2026

**Time(s):** Exhibition opening from 18:00 (free admission); concerts from 19:15 in Kuppelhalle (ticketed)

**Short Context:** Niccolò Masini's *Islands of Time* opens at silent green's Ateliers — a Miller-Zillmer Foundation collaboration exploring geographic belonging, memory, and deterritorialization across Western Sahara, the Italian-French border, Argentina, and the Aeolian Archipelago. The opening night concert programme in the Kuppelhalle stacks five leftfield electronic acts: Stefan Betke (Pole), JakoJako with dancer Rubén Nsue, Sunroof (Daniel Miller and Gareth Jones), and Nicolas Bougaïeff. Masini's research-based practice treats archives, spatial modalities, and oral knowledge as interconnected material; the exhibition remains on view through 16 August.

**Why It Fits:** Experimental electronic music, sound art, and research-based contemporary art at a priority music venue; named artists across ambient, modular synthesis, and geopolitical visual practice.

**Official Link:** [silent green — Islands of Time opening with Pole, JakoJako, Sunroof, and Nicolas Bougaïeff](https://www.silent-green.net/en/programme/detail/island-of-time-exhibition-and-concerts-feat-pole-jakojako-ruben-nsue-sunroof-nicolas-bougaeiff)

### Summer Special: Films from Portugal

**Venue:** Wolf Kino

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 5 August – Monday, 8 August 2026

**Time(s):** Screenings from 18:00–21:00 (individual start times vary by film; see programme)

**Short Context:** Wolf Kino hosts a five-day Portuguese cinema week in partnership with production companies Terratreme and Uma Pedra no Sapato — presenting young directors whose work has screened at Berlinale Forum, Rotterdam, Cannes, and Venice. The programme spans Cláudia Varejão's twin-study *Amor Fati*, Susana Nobre's 16mm autobiographical *Jack's Ride*, José Filipe Costa's participatory documentary *A Pleasure, Comrades!*, Maria Clara Escobar's co-production *Desterro*, and the omnibus *New Female Voices* with three short films by Sofia Bost, Leonor Teles, and Mariana Gaivão. Multiple screenings include post-film Zoom discussions with directors; students pay a reduced €4 fee throughout the summer break special.

**Why It Fits:** Socially engaged documentary, essay film, and contemporary international cinema at a priority independent cinema; artist-led discussion format with named directors.

**Official Link:** [Wolf Kino — Summer Special: Films from Portugal](https://wolfberlin.org/en/programme/events/summer-special-from-portugal)

### Marina Abramović — Balkan Erotic Epic. The Exhibition

**Venue:** Gropius Bau

**Date(s):** 15 April – 30 August 2026

**Time(s):** Mon 12:00–20:00; Wed–Fri 12:00–20:00; Sat–Sun 10:00–20:00 (Tue closed)

**Short Context:** Marina Abramović's first major Berlin institutional solo since the 1990s unites historical works and new pieces tracing her enduring engagement with ritual, eroticism, death, and the body as a site of political resistance. *Balkan Erotic Epic* draws on Balkan folk traditions and performance history to frame the body not as spectacle but as contested territory — work that connects to feminist practice, historical trauma, and interdisciplinary performance art. The exhibition closes at the end of August, making this week the final viewing window before Gropius Bau shifts toward autumn programming.

**Why It Fits:** Performance art history, gender, and body-political contemporary practice at a priority contemporary-art institution; closing-soon urgency with significant cultural importance.

**Official Link:** [Gropius Bau — Marina Abramović: Balkan Erotic Epic](https://www.gropiusbau.de/en/programme/marina-abramovic-balkan-erotic-epic-the-exhibition)

### Gabriele Stötzer — Dabei sein und nicht schweigen

**Venue:** Gropius Bau

**Date(s):** 19 June – 6 December 2026

**Time(s):** Mon 12:00–20:00; Wed–Fri 12:00–20:00; Sat–Sun 10:00–20:00 (Tue closed)

**Short Context:** Gropius Bau presents the largest institutional solo exhibition to date of Gabriele Stötzer — an East German artist whose five-decade practice centres justice, self-determination, and gender through experimental photography, film, and performance. With roughly 150 works, *Dabei sein und nicht schweigen* (*Being there and not keeping silent*) positions Stötzer's body as a site of feminist resistance rather than objectification, extending the institutional recovery of GDR-era artists whose work was marginalised during and after reunification. The show pairs naturally with Abramović's body-political survey in the same building.

**Why It Fits:** Feminism, gender, archives, and historical trauma through research-based artistic practice at a priority institution; significant institutional retrospective with named artist.

**Official Link:** [Gropius Bau — Gabriele Stötzer: Dabei sein und nicht schweigen](https://www.gropiusbau.de/en/programme/gabriele-stoetzer-dabei-sein-und-nicht-schweigen)
