# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of August 12–18, 2026

Mid-August thins into a Tanz im August cluster at the Haus der Berliner Festspiele while PSM and Crone Berlin bracket the visual-art calendar with paired finissages. Jazz returns to A-Trane for a sold-out Kurt Rosenwinkel guest week, and DOCK 11 sustains experimental performance through the August lull — a week dominated by dance and closing-soon gallery shows rather than cinema or expansive music programming.

## Top Picks

### Cloud Gate Dance Theatre of Taiwan / Cheng Tsung-lung — Sounding Light

**Venue:** Haus der Berliner Festspiele (Tanz im August)

**Date(s):** Saturday, 15 August – Monday, 17 August 2026

**Time(s):** Sat 19:00; Sun 18:00; Mon 19:00

**Short Context:** Cheng Tsung-lung's *Sounding Light* brings Cloud Gate Dance Theatre of Taiwan to Berlin for three evenings within Tanz im August — a meditation on forest ecology rendered through twelve dancers who generate much of the atmospheric soundtrack from their own bodies. Inspired by the artistic director's long walks through woodland, the piece stages scenes from a day in the life of a forest: bird mating rituals, rain through leaves, sunbeams breaking cloud cover. Lim Giong's score and Lulu W. L. Lee's lighting frame a work that treats dance and sound as inseparable material rather than accompaniment — characteristic of Cloud Gate's internationally influential synthesis of contemporary choreography and Asian movement traditions.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary dance and choreography at a priority festival; interdisciplinary sound-body practice with named artists across choreography, music, and lighting design.

**Official Link:** [Tanz im August — Cloud Gate Dance Theatre: Sounding Light](https://www.tanzimaugust.de/en/production/detail/cloud-gate-dance-theatre-sounding-light)

### Tobias Meinhart – Berlin People, Special Guest: Kurt Rosenwinkel

**Venue:** A-Trane

**Date(s):** Friday, 15 August 2026

**Time(s):** 20:30

**Short Context:** Saxophonist Tobias Meinhart brings his Berlin People project home for a five-night A-Trane residency — with Friday's concert featuring Kurt Rosenwinkel, one of contemporary jazz's most distinctive guitar voices. Meinhart has shaped New York's downtown scene for fifteen years while maintaining a Berlin-New York axis through this ensemble; JazzTimes calls Rosenwinkel "easily the most original guitar voice of the decade." The band — Ludwig Hornung on piano, Tom Berkmann on bass, Mathias Ruppnig on drums — draws from the recent album *Light & Shadow* and new compositions, blending driving unison lines with the open harmonic language that earned Meinhart four consecutive DownBeat Rising Star nominations. Note: this performance is sold out; remaining residency nights run 11–14 August.

**Why It Fits:** Leftfield jazz with named international artists at a priority club venue; contemporary composition and improvisation rather than mainstream entertainment programming.

**Official Link:** [A-Trane — Tobias Meinhart Berlin People with Kurt Rosenwinkel](https://a-trane.de/Events-Directory/a-trane-praesentiert-artist-in-summer-residence-day5tobias-meinhart-berlin-peoplespecial-guest-kurt-rosenwinkelfeat-ludwig-hornung-tom-berkmann-mathias-ruppnig/)

### Gökçen Dilek Acay — What time is it in the world

**Venue:** time is it in the world PSM

**Date(s):** 3 July – 14 August 2026 (closes Thursday)

**Time(s):** Tue–Sat 12:00–18:00

**Short Context:** Gökçen Dilek Acay's solo at PSM closes Thursday — a final viewing window for an exhibition that maps ecofeminist thought onto the body as territory and territory as body. Works incorporating batik, embroidery, soap-making, performance, and human hair weave traditional craft techniques into a meditation on transience, stillness, and the political clock of our climate moment. Acay invites viewers to "dance along" while watching time run down — a proposition that connects feminist material practice with environmental urgency at one of Berlin's most consistent independent gallery programmes.

**Why It Fits:** Feminism, ecology, and research-based contemporary art at an established gallery; closing-soon urgency with strong thematic match to identity and environmental justice priorities.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — What time is it in the world](https://www.indexberlin.com/exhibitions/list/44327/what-time-is-it-in-the-world)

### Empty Places, Crowded Hearts

**Venue:** Crone Berlin

**Date(s):** 27 June – 15 August 2026 (closes Friday)

**Time(s):** Wed–Sat 11:00–18:00

**Short Context:** Crone Berlin's summer group show closes Friday with a roster spanning generations of German and international practice: Hanne Darboven, Carola Dertnig, Hannah Sophie Dunkelberg, Judith Fegerl, Sylvie Fleury, Channa Horwitz, Sophie-Luise Passow, Charlotte Posenenske, Rosemarie Trockel, and Ruscha Voormann. The title signals tension between absence and affect — empty places against crowded hearts — within a programme that pairs conceptual rigour with material sensuality across drawing, sculpture, and installation. Works on view include Trockel's *Bush is a Bear* (1987), Horwitz's systematic drawings, and Voormann's recent *Aquarius II* (2026), making this finissage the week's essential Charlottenburg gallery appointment.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary art, archives, and conceptual practice at an established gallery; closing-soon urgency with multiple named artists across generations.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — Empty Places, Crowded Hearts](https://www.indexberlin.com/exhibitions/list/44376/empty-places-crowded-hearts)

### L2/are we listening

**Venue:** DOCK 11

**Date(s):** Friday, 14 August – Sunday, 16 August 2026

**Time(s):** 19:30 nightly

**Short Context:** Sara Shelton Mann and Jesse Zaritt continue their collaborative inquiry into belonging, beauty, mortality, joy, and loss — expanding last summer's *L/are we dreaming* with dramaturg and composer Niall Jones. *L2/are we listening* places dance movement at the centre of an interdisciplinary performance complemented by image, text, sound, and light, asking audiences to "see with our ears, hear with our eyes, and feel from knowing." The English-language work runs fifty minutes across three consecutive evenings at DOCK 11, sustaining the venue's August experimental performance calendar alongside Tanz im August's festival cluster at the Festspiele.

**Why It Fits:** Interdisciplinary performance and contemporary dance at a priority venue; participatory and sensory enquiry with named collaborators across choreography, dramaturgy, and composition.

**Official Link:** [DOCK 11 — L2/are we listening](https://dock11-berlin.de/en/theater/program/calendar/l2-are-we-listening)

### Wolfgang Haffner — Summer Special feat. Jakob Manz, Nils Kugelmann

**Venue:** A-Trane

**Date(s):** Tuesday, 18 August 2026

**Time(s):** 20:30

**Short Context:** Drummer Wolfgang Haffner marks fifty years on stage with a Summer Special at A-Trane — a career retrospective featuring shooting-star saxophonist Jakob Manz and bassist Nils Kugelmann alongside musical companions from decades of international collaboration. Haffner's CV spans more than 4,000 concerts across 100 countries and roughly 400 album credits, from Al Jarreau and Pat Metheny to Klaus Doldinger and Die Fantastischen Vier; two ECHO Jazz awards anchor a reputation built on groove, versatility, and compositional finesse. The Tuesday date closes the briefing week with a leftfield-jazz appointment at Charlottenburg's longstanding club — note tickets are sold out.

**Why It Fits:** Leftfield jazz with named artists at a priority venue; contemporary improvisation and European jazz tradition rather than mainstream entertainment.
