# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of June 24–30, 2026

Haus am Waldsee's eightieth-anniversary season anchors the visual-art calendar with sound and ecology on the lake, while DOCK11's Mash Dance festival and two major Wolf Kino openings carry the week's performance and cinema energy forward from KW's biennial chapter.

## Top Picks

### Resurrection

**Venue:** Wolf Kino

**Date(s):** from Thursday, 25 June 2026

**Time(s):** Check venue for daily screenings

**Short Context:** Bi Gan's third feature — Cannes 2025 Special Jury Prize — is a 160-minute dream odyssey through a world that has traded sleep for immortality. Jackson Yee leads a nameless wanderer preserving dreams from oblivion in imagery the director builds with the same long-take intoxication as *A Long Day's Journey into Night*. Wolf Kino opens the German run this week.

**Why It Fits:** Experimental international cinema at its most formally audacious; diaspora, memory, and sensory cinema aligned with essay-film and hybrid documentary interests.

**Official Link:** [Wolf Kino — Resurrection](https://wolfberlin.org/de/programm/filme/resurrection)

### Mash Dance Berlin 2026 – Victims & Images: Vol 2

**Venue:** DOCK11

**Date(s):** Sunday, 28 June 2026

**Time(s):** 19:30

**Short Context:** Roni Chadash's solo closes the Mash Dance festival with a performance that interrogates how bodies are photographed, surveilled, and remembered — bodily image as political subject rather than aesthetic object. The festival's final evening situates Israeli contemporary choreography within Berlin's post-October 7 discourse without reducing movement to statement. Earlier festival evenings include Hillel Kogan's *THISISPAIN* (25 June) and Iris Erez & Elad Vazana's *The War Within* (26 June) at the same address.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary dance, choreography, and identity politics at a priority performance venue; festival centerpiece with strong artistic match.

**Official Link:** [DOCK11 — Victims & Images Vol 2](https://dock11-berlin.de/en/theater/program/calendar/victims-images-vol-2)

### Ayumi Paul – Fischmond am Waldsee

**Venue:** Haus am Waldsee (Sculpture Garden)

**Date(s):** 14 June 2026 – 23 May 2027

**Time(s):** Sculpture garden hours; confirm with venue

**Short Context:** Berlin-based artist Ayumi Paul installs a sound-and-sculpture work on the Waldsee grounds as part of the villa's eightieth-anniversary programme. The piece treats the lakeside garden as acoustic architecture — listening as much as looking — extending the institution's long engagement with experimental sound practices beyond the white cube.

**Why It Fits:** Sound art, sculpture, and research-based installation at a priority contemporary-art venue; multispecies and environmental sensibility in an outdoor setting.

**Official Link:** [Haus am Waldsee — 80th anniversary programme](https://hausamwaldsee.de/en/anniversary-80-years-haus-am-waldsee/)

## Exhibitions Radar

### Haus am Waldsee — Wo ich wohne & Green Sanctuary

**Venue:** Haus am Waldsee

**Date(s):** 14 June – 27 September 2026

**Time(s):** Wed–Sun 11:00–18:00 (closed Mon–Tue); confirm with venue

**Short Context:** Two anniversary exhibitions share the villa's domestic scale: *Wo ich wohne* gathers Nigin Beck, Hannah Höch, Ayumi Paul, Richard Venlet, and others reflecting on eight decades of exhibition history through spatial intervention; Veit Laurent Kurz's *Green Sanctuary (C.O.T.B)* transforms the café and garage into an ecological installation probing multispecies coexistence. Together they treat the institution's full footprint — gallery, café, peripheral rooms — as sites for historical and ecological imagination.

**Why It Fits:** Research-based artistic practice, institutional history, and ecology through contemporary installation at a priority venue.

**Official Link:** [Haus am Waldsee — Wo ich wohne](https://hausamwaldsee.de/en/wo-ich-wohne)

## Film & Screenings

### Night and the City

**Venue:** Kino Arsenal

**Date(s):** Friday, 26 June 2026

**Time(s):** 20:00

**Short Context:** Jules Dassin's 1950 postwar noir follows a small-time hustler navigating London's underground wrestling circuit — urban dislocation, crime, and the precarity of immigrant life rendered in expressionist shadows. Arsenal's repertory screening recovers a classic of global city cinema whose themes of displacement resonate with the week's migration-focused programming elsewhere.

**Why It Fits:** Political cinema and urbanism through classic international repertory; post-screening context at a priority film institution.

**Official Link:** [Arsenal June 2026 calendar (PDF)](https://www.arsenal-berlin.de/assets/Kino/PDFs/26-06_Kalendarium-Plakat_arsenal_A1_cmyk_Web.pdf)

**Venue:** ACUDkino

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 24 June 2026

**Time(s):** 19:00

**Official Link:** [ACUDkino — Scherbenland](https://acudkino.de/Programm/)

### Donkey Days

**Venue:** Wolf Kino

**Date(s):** from Thursday, 25 June 2026

**Time(s):** Check venue for daily screenings

**Short Context:** Rosanne Pel's feature follows sisters Anna and Charlotte returning to their childhood home, where buried family secrets — including their mother's love for a donkey — force a reckoning with loyalty and autonomy. Dutch-German co-production with a psychologically precise, darkly comic register; Berlin premiere run opens alongside Bi Gan's *Resurrection*.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary international cinema exploring family, identity, and diaspora experience; complements the week's experimental feature programming at the same address.

**Official Link:** [Wolf Kino — Donkey Days](https://wolfberlin.org/de/programm/filme/donkey-days)
