# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of September 9–15, 2026

Berlin Art Week arrives as sound, labour, and architecture rather than as a booth crawl: Sakamoto’s first major European retrospective opens in the Rieckhallen, Trisha Brown’s wall-walkers return to Mies’s glass hall, and Tanzfabrik opens its season on migrant care work. The through-line is bodies under pressure — caregivers, historical reconstructions, and instruments that refuse to stay in the background.

## Top Picks

### Ryuichi Sakamoto — seeing sound, hearing time

**Venue:** Hamburger Bahnhof – Nationalgalerie der Gegenwart

**Date(s):** Opening Thursday, 10 September 2026; exhibition 11 September 2026 – 20 June 2027

**Time(s):** Opening 19:00; exhibition hours Tuesday–Wednesday and Friday 10:00–18:00, Thursday 10:00–20:00, Saturday–Sunday 11:00–18:00 (Monday closed)

**Short Context:** This is the first major European retrospective of Ryuichi Sakamoto as a composer-artist, not as a soundtrack brand: seven works in five large installations across the Rieckhallen, opening into Berlin Art Week and the museum’s thirtieth year. *IS YOUR TIME* (2017) turns a piano damaged in the 2011 tsunami into a sounding object fed by seismological data; further rooms come out of the album *async* (2017) with Shiro Takatani and Apichatpong Weerasethakul, and two Carsten Nicolai films use music from Sakamoto’s last album *12*. Ingrid Buschmann and Sachiko Namba keep the argument on the intersection of sound and visual art that Hamburger Bahnhof has claimed since it opened — which is the right frame, provided the show does not collapse into memorial piety. Sakamoto died on 28 March 2023; the dates that matter this week are the Thursday opening and the Friday public start.

**Why It Fits:** Sound art, audiovisual installation, and experimental electronic practice at a priority institution; named collaborators across composition, moving image, and installation rather than a greatest-hits corridor.

**Official Link:** [Hamburger Bahnhof — Ryuichi Sakamoto: seeing sound, hearing time](https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/hamburger-bahnhof/exhibitions/detail/ryuichi-sakamoto/)

### Trisha Brown: Walking on the Wall (1971)

**Venue:** Neue Nationalgalerie

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 9 September 2026; Tuesday, 15 September 2026

**Time(s):** Wed 17:00; Tue 18:30. Kianí Del Valle’s *Instrucciones para existir* follows in the same building (Wed 17:30; Tue 19:00)

**Short Context:** Trisha Brown’s *Walking on the Wall* — bodies in harnesses moving sideways across a museum wall as if gravity had been rotated ninety degrees — is restaged in Mies van der Rohe’s glass hall, which is still one of the few rooms in Berlin where a 1970s dance work looks like a problem rather than a replica. The harnesses, the wall, and the public looking up from the floor are the whole argument: how a modernist interior organises bodies, and what happens when choreography refuses the floor it was given. Kianí Del Valle’s *Instrucciones para existir* shares the same early evening both nights; treat them as one architecture-and-body appointment, not as two festival splits.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary dance history and experimental performance at a major modernist site; a named reconstruction with enough weight to plan Wednesday and the following Tuesday around.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — Trisha Brown: Walking on the Wall](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/45811/trisha-brown-walking-on-the-wall-1971)

### Jasna L. Vinovrški — SKRB (CARE) - three shift system

**Venue:** Uferstudios 1, Tanzfabrik Berlin

**Date(s):** Saturday, 12 September 2026; Sunday, 13 September 2026 (German premiere)

**Time(s):** Sat 19:00 (artist talk with Felicitas Zeeden after the performance); Sun 16:00 (family-friendly environment); approx. 55 minutes

**Short Context:** Vinovrški’s German premiere treats care as embodied labour rather than as a theme to be illustrated: three performers whose lives already combine artistic, educational, administrative, and family work become a living archive of what Europe prefers not to count. The piece is explicit about women from former Yugoslavia working as caregivers in Germany — ageing populations, labour shortages, and a care system that runs on undervalued migrant time. Choreography, storytelling, and humour keep it from becoming a policy briefing with dancers; Koraljka Begović, Melita Spahić Bezjak, Larisa Lipovac Navojec, and Ana Novković perform. Spoken English, German, and Croatian; pay what you can.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary dance with a direct hit on migration, labour, and gender; a named German premiere at a priority performance venue rather than a general season opener.

**Official Link:** [Tanzfabrik — Jasna L. Vinovrški: SKRB (CARE)](https://www.tanzfabrik-berlin.de/en/events/3473/2026-09-12-19-00)

### July Weber — ILLUSIONS_bones&keys

**Venue:** Uferstudios 14, Tanzfabrik Berlin

**Date(s):** Friday, 11 September 2026; Saturday, 12 September 2026

**Time(s):** Fri 19:00; Sat 16:00; open continuously ~180–360 minutes, free entry, first come first served. Tuce Alba concert Saturday 20:30–21:00 inside the installation

**Short Context:** July Weber’s season-opening installation is a sculptural concert you can walk through: metal clinking against metal, bones on the floor, wind machines turning electric guitars, strobe and fog in the mix. **Why It Fits:** Interdisciplinary performance-installation and choreographic sculpture at a priority venue; named artists across installation, light/sound, and a guest concert rather than a generic season trailer.

## Music

### Katokye

**Venue:** silent green Kulturquartier, Kuppelhalle

**Date(s):** Thursday, 10 September 2026

**Time(s):** Doors 19:00; start 20:00

**Short Context:** John Katokye brings ekyeshongoro — improvised, overlapping vocal phrases from the Banyankore of western Uganda, sometimes running past ten minutes — into the Kuppelhalle the same week Sakamoto’s rooms open across town. Cattle-herding songs about family, regional history, and landscape are not a world-music garnish here; the Nyege Nyege Tapes album *Obuhangwa bwa Banyankore na Bahororo* (2025) already made the case that this is a hypnotic, structurally specific practice. Backup singers stacking his lines into a continuous flow is the form. Global experimental music, not a heritage showcase.

**Why It Fits:** Global experimental and traditional-contemporary vocal practice with a named artist at a priority music venue; a concrete Thursday concert rather than a generic DJ listing.

**Official Link:** [silent green — Katokye](https://www.silent-green.net/en/programme/detail/katokye?tx_news_pi1%5Bday%5D=10&tx_news_pi1%5Bmonth%5D=9&tx_news_pi1%5Byear%5D=2026&cHash=25a19d35ea995ac5c08085791c21b6f5)

### Transgressive Memory Festival: Resynthesising the Traditional

**Venue:** silent green Kulturquartier, Betonhalle

**Date(s):** Saturday, 12 September 2026

**Time(s):** 19:30–22:30; admission free

**Short Context:** CTM, invited by the Körber-Stiftung, threads its long-term *Resynthesising the Traditional* project into one Saturday evening rather than a sprawl of sub-events. Stefan Fraunberger’s *Quellgeister #4* works dilapidated Transylvanian pipe organs as nature-culture wreckage, not as heritage conservation; Dania’s *Replica – Relic* reanimates 3D-scanned Mesopotamian objects from the British Museum as instruments, imagining a southern-Iraqi sound world without colonial interference; Marcin Pietruszewski and Lukas de Clerck’s *Oto Aulos* rebuilds the extinct Greco-Roman double reed as live computer space. Memory, distortion, and the refusal of tradition as a closed object — one festival listing with named artists, not a weekend of sub-events.

**Why It Fits:** Experimental electronic and contemporary composition with explicit work on memory, archives, and colonial afterlives; CTM curation and named artists rather than a generic festival umbrella.

**Official Link:** [silent green — Transgressive Memory Festival: Resynthesising the Traditional](https://www.silent-green.net/en/programme/detail/transgressive-memory-festival-resynthesising-the-traditional?tx_news_pi1%5Bday%5D=12&tx_news_pi1%5Bmonth%5D=9&tx_news_pi1%5Byear%5D=2026&cHash=c15e233becfde6837f8828a6907acefa)
