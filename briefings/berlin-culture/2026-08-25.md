# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of August 26–September 1, 2026

Late August turns from festival residue to a sharper kind of looking: a bat-eyed dance at HAU, essay cinema at Arsenal, and Berlinische Galerie’s survey of GDR architecture. The through-line is perception and public form — how bodies, cameras, and buildings organise what can be seen, and what gets remembered.

## Top Picks

### Bat Dances

**Venue:** HAU3, HAU Hebbel am Ufer (Tanz im August)

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 26 August – Saturday, 29 August 2026

**Time(s):** Wed 20:00 (premiere); Thu–Fri 18:00; Sat 17:00; ~60 minutes

**Short Context:** Kareth Schaffer and Jonas Hauer’s *Bat Dances* is a danced monologue from a bat’s point of view, built to be equally usable by sighted, blind, and visually impaired audiences rather than described after the fact. Leah Katz performs the solo while echolocation, sound dramaturgy, and a cautious humour replace the usual add-on audio description; the piece asks how another species perceives the world, and whether dance can answer without turning ecology into costume. Co-produced with Tanz im August at HAU3, it is the week’s clearest choreographic proposition — conceptually tight, formally unusual, and more interesting than most remaining festival traffic.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary dance and interdisciplinary performance at a priority venue; multispecies thinking, access-led form, and named collaborators across choreography, composition, and performance.

**Official Link:** [Tanz im August — Kareth Schaffer & Jonas Hauer: Bat Dances](https://www.tanzimaugust.de/en/production/detail/kareth-schaffer-jonas-hauer-bat-dances/)

### News from Home

**Venue:** Kino Arsenal (Arsenal 1)

**Date(s):** Monday, 31 August 2026

**Time(s):** 19:30; introduction by Birgit Kohler

**Short Context:** Chantal Akerman’s *News from Home* (1976) remains one of the essential essay films: long static takes of 1970s New York set against her mother’s letters from Brussels, read in a voice that refuses to perform filial warmth. Street noise sometimes drowns the correspondence; intimacy and distance occupy the same soundtrack. Birgit Kohler’s introduction should keep the screening on the side of form and family politics rather than heritage piety — Arsenal doing what it does best, which is treating cinema as a thinking tool rather than a canon checklist.

**Why It Fits:** Essay film, political cinema, and post-screening framing at a priority film institution; exile, gender, and the politics of looking rather than repertory filler.

**Official Link:** [Arsenal — News from Home](https://www.arsenal-berlin.de/en/cinema/film-screening/news-from-home-2395/)

### Josef Kaiser — Building for the GDR

**Venue:** Berlinische Galerie

**Date(s):** Opening Thursday, 27 August 2026; exhibition 28 August 2026 – 1 February 2027

**Time(s):** Opening 19:00; exhibition hours Wednesday–Monday 10:00–18:00

**Short Context:** Berlinische Galerie opens a research exhibition on Josef Kaiser, the architect behind some of East Berlin’s most ideologically loaded public buildings — Kino International, Hotel Berolina, Restaurant Moskau — and therefore behind the visual grammar of Karl-Marx-Allee. This is architecture as state form: how a regime built leisure, hospitality, and cinema as arguments in stone, glass, and procession. In a week otherwise short on major institutional openings, it is the one exhibition with genuine historical weight rather than late-summer inventory.

**Why It Fits:** GDR history, urbanism, and political memory at a priority contemporary-art museum; architecture treated as ideology rather than nostalgic décor.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — Josef Kaiser. Bauen für die DDR](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/44561/josef-kaiser-bauen-fur-die-ddr)

## Film & Screenings

### La Jetée + On vous parle de Paris: Maspero, les mots ont un sens

**Venue:** Kino Arsenal

**Date(s):** Sunday, 30 August 2026

**Time(s):** 18:00

**Short Context:** Arsenal pairs Chris Marker’s *La Jetée* with *On vous parle de Paris: Maspero, les mots ont un sens* — memory-politics in twenty-eight minutes of still photographs, then a film about François Maspero’s radical publishing house, where words were treated as a political instrument rather than a lifestyle accessory. The double bill is almost too on-the-nose for this briefing’s interests, which is precisely why it belongs here: time, trauma, and the left’s print culture in one sitting. See it as the weekend hinge before Monday’s Akerman.

**Why It Fits:** Essay film, political cinema, and intellectual history at a priority cinema; Marker’s memory machine plus radical publishing rather than generic repertory.

**Official Link:** [Arsenal — La Jetée + Maspero](https://www.arsenal-berlin.de/en/cinema/film-screening/5547/)

### Ruba Al-Sweel, Arvin Arta, Dana Dawud, Noura Tafeche and Zein Majali — Short Film Screening

**Venue:** Galerie im Turm, Frankfurter Tor 1

**Date(s):** Thursday, 27 August 2026

**Time(s):** 20:00–21:30

**Short Context:** A named-artist short-film evening at Galerie im Turm is the week’s best alternative to Arsenal’s canonical pair: Ruba Al-Sweel, Arvin Arta, Dana Dawud, Noura Tafeche, and Zein Majali in one programme, which at least promises a multi-voice moving-image night rather than an open-call slurry. Arta’s *Cloud Domination* has already been around the independent circuit this summer; seeing that practice next to other artist films is more useful than another institutional retrospective. It also pulls cinema east of the Spree on the same night as the Kaiser opening.

**Why It Fits:** Artist-led short film and experimental moving image; diasporic and contemporary-image practices outside the repertory house.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — Short Film Screening](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/44942/short-film-screening)

## Wildcards

### Thomas Billhardt — Die Augen weit offen

**Venue:** Fotografiska, Oranienburger Straße 54

**Date(s):** Thursday, 27 August 2026

**Time(s):** 19:00 (opening)

**Short Context:** Thomas Billhardt’s *Die Augen weit offen* opens at Fotografiska on the same evening as Kaiser — a GDR photojournalism opening that belongs next to the architecture show rather than in a separate “photography” ghetto. Billhardt’s camera was one of the East’s public eyes; the title’s wide-open gaze is either a method or an alibi, and the interesting question is which. Use it as a compact visual-archive counterpoint to Berlinische Galerie’s buildings, not as a lifestyle museum outing.

**Why It Fits:** Archives, GDR memory, and the politics of documentary looking; a hybrid opening that connects photography to this week’s architectural argument.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — Die Augen weit offen](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/45392/die-augen-weit-offen)

### Heinz Mack — Spectrum Mundi

**Venue:** St. Matthäus-Kirche, Matthäikirchplatz

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 2 September 2026

**Time(s):** 19:00 (opening)

**Short Context:** Heinz Mack’s *Spectrum Mundi* opens at St. Matthäus-Kirche on the Kulturforum — ZERO-movement light, colour, and cosmic scale inside a postwar church rather than a white cube. Mack can slide into monument-of-modernism piety; the setting at least forces the work to argue with sacred architecture instead of floating as brand sculpture. Flag it now: it is the first major September colour-and-light appointment west of Potsdamer Straße.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary composition of light and colour at a significant Kulturforum site; a named artist with enough historical weight to plan around.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — Spectrum Mundi](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/45274/spectrum-mundi)

### HAUS AUS GLAS — Vom Bauen und Niederreißen

**Venue:** Schinkel Pavillon

**Date(s):** Thursday, 3 September 2026

**Time(s):** 20:00

**Short Context:** A performative portrait of Philip Johnson — *Haus aus Glas*, on building and tearing down — is almost too well placed after this week’s Kaiser show. Johnson’s glass house is the Western twin of the problem: modernism as transparency, power, and demolition dressed up as progress. Schinkel Pavillon is the right room for that argument if the piece can hold the architecture without becoming a TED-talk with props.

**Why It Fits:** Architecture, power, and experimental performance at a priority contemporary-art venue; a direct continuation of this week’s GDR building story into American modernism.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — HAUS AUS GLAS](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/45281/haus-aus-glas-vom-bauen-und-niederreissen-ein-performatives-portrat-des-amerikanischen-architekten-philip-johnson)
