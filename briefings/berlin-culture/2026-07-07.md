# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of July 8–14, 2026

A post-gallery-week lull concentrates the week's strongest material into three overlapping currents: feminist histories on screen and stage (Barbara Loden's *Wanda*, Heroines of Sound), geopolitical sound programming through the Kyiv Biennial's Concert-Week at KW, and a pair of closing exhibitions that map migration, borders, and queer spatial politics before Friday's finissage rush.

## Top Picks

### Heroines of Sound Festival 2026

**Venue:** Radialsystem

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 9 July – Friday, 11 July 2026

**Time(s):** Day 1 from 17:30; festival hours vary by day — confirm with venue

**Short Context:** Berlin's flagship feminist new-music festival returns to Radialsystem with three evenings of contemporary composition, sound art, and interdisciplinary performance. Day 1 opens with Kyoka, Ensemble Spółdzielnia Muzyczna, Miako Klein, and Jia Lim; subsequent nights extend the programme across electroacoustic experiment, choreographed sound, and expanded performance formats. Heroines of Sound treats gender not as a marketing frame but as a curatorial lens on who gets heard in experimental music — a rare sustained platform for composers and performers working at the edges of notation, electronics, and live art.

**Why It Fits:** Feminist programming, new music, sound art, and experimental performance at a priority venue; strong artist names and interdisciplinary format.

**Official Link:** [Radialsystem — Heroines of Sound Festival 2026](https://www.radialsystem.de/de/programm/programm/)

### Wanda (Barbara Loden)

**Venue:** Wolf Kino

**Date(s):** From Wednesday, 8 July 2026 (ongoing run)

**Time(s):** Screening times vary — confirm with cinema

**Short Context:** Barbara Loden's 1970 landmark — which she wrote, directed, and stars in — receives a rare Berlin theatrical run at Wolf Kino. *Wanda* follows a woman drifting through rural Pennsylvania after leaving her family, joining a small-time criminal in a portrait of female existence rendered without redemption arcs or moral scaffolding. Loden's near-documentary image-making anticipated decades of independent cinema; the Criterion restoration has kept the film's quiet radicalism alive. Wolf pairs the screening with an online talk by Barbara Loden scholar Elena Gorfinkel.

**Why It Fits:** Feminist cinema, gender, and politically engaged moving image; rare theatrical opportunity for a canonical independent film at a priority arthouse venue.

**Official Link:** [Wolf Kino — Wanda](https://wolfberlin.org/de/programm/filme/wanda)

### Etwas Ganz Besonderes (Eva Trobisch)

**Venue:** Wolf Kino

**Date(s):** From Thursday, 9 July 2026 (ongoing run)

**Time(s):** Screening times vary — confirm with cinema

**Short Context:** Eva Trobisch's third feature — which premiered in Berlinale 2026 competition — follows Lea, a young woman auditioning for a casting show while her east German hometown renovates a museum with EU funds as her family's pension struggles to survive. The ensemble spans three generations navigating separation, provincial cultural ambition, and the question of which story counts as authentically one's own. Trobisch treats Ostalgie and post-reunification economic precarity without condescension, finding dark comedy inside a family unable to agree on a single homestory.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary German cinema addressing migration-adjacent identity, urbanism, and labour; socially engaged drama at a priority film venue.

**Official Link:** [Wolf Kino — Etwas Ganz Besonderes](https://wolfberlin.org/de/programm/filme/etwas-ganz-besonderes)
