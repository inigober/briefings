# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of June 10–16, 2026

## Top Picks

### Who does the earth belong to while painting the wind?!

**Venue:** ChertLüdde

**Date(s):** 2 May – 25 July 2026

**Time(s):** Tue–Sat 11:00–18:00

**Short Context:** Petrit Halilaj transforms the gallery with a deconstructed shipping container — charred remains of props attacked before his opera *Syrigana* premiered — alongside childhood drawings buried during the Kosovo War, qilim gathering spaces, and sculptures responding to racist caricature. The exhibition threads personal archive, collective trauma, and queer re-readings of national myth.

**Why It Fits:** Research-based, politically charged installation practice at the intersection of diaspora, decolonial thought, and performance — precisely the kind of work Halilaj has pursued in Berlin for years.

**Official Link:** [ChertLüdde exhibition page](https://chertluedde.com/exhibition/51332/)

### In Other Realms

**Venue:** Capitain Petzel

**Date(s):** 1 May – 13 June 2026 (closes Friday)

**Time(s):** Tue–Sat 11:00–18:00

**Short Context:** Rodney McMillian's first show with the gallery centres on a new film drawing from an Ida B. Wells-Barnett speech at the NAACP's founding conference (1909), surrounded by vinyl "Black Paintings" and sculptural assemblages. The work refuses pure abstraction, insisting that material history and racial politics are inseparable.

**Why It Fits:** Politically engaged moving image and object practice — socially conscious, anti-spectacular, and closing imminently.

**Official Link:** [Capitain Petzel — In Other Realms](https://www.capitainpetzel.de/exhibitions/145-rodney-mcmillian-in-other-realms-as-part-of-gallery-weekend-berlin-1/)

### Werckmeister Harmóniák

**Venue:** Arsenal – Institut für Film und Videokunst

**Date(s):** 13 June 2026

**Time(s):** 20:00

**Short Context:** Béla Tarr's 145-minute allegory of social collapse plays on 35mm with German subtitles — a travelling circus and a mysterious prince destabilise a Hungarian town in winter. Arsenal's repertory programming remains one of the few reliable places in Berlin to see this scale of slow cinema on celluloid.

**Why It Fits:** Political cinema at full length, celluloid presentation, post-screening culture — core Arsenal territory.

**Official Link:** [Arsenal programme](https://www.arsenal-berlin.de/en/)

### Festival Never Work

**Venue:** Sophiensæle

**Date(s):** 12–27 June 2026 (multiple events this week)

**Time(s):** Various; see individual listings below

**Short Context:** An international performance festival occupying all Sophiensæle stages — and unconventional workspaces — to examine labour, precarity, and working conditions in the arts. The building's history (craft guild, workers' movement hub, forced-labour site under Nazism, GDR theatre workshops) frames a programme of choreography, participatory performance, and talks.

**Why It Fits:** Interdisciplinary performance with explicit politics of labour and institution — rare festival-scale coherence in Berlin's independent scene.

**Official Link:** [Never Work festival](https://sophiensaele.com/en/festival/never-work)

## Exhibitions Radar

### Zip: 01-01-2024 – 12-31-2024

**Venue:** Galerie Buchholz

**Date(s):** until 20 June 2026 (closing soon)

**Time(s):** Tue–Sat 11:00–18:00

**Short Context:** Yuji Agematsu compresses a calendar year of daily collected debris into sculptural "zips" — pocket-sized archives of urban encounter. The work rewards slow looking; conceptually rigorous though visually understated.

**Why It Fits:** Research-based conceptual practice; time, archive, and the everyday as artistic material.

## Film & Screenings

### Dhakira al Khasba (Fertile Memory)

**Venue:** Arsenal

**Date(s):** 15 June 2026

**Time(s):** 20:00

**Short Context:** Michel Khleifi's 1981 feature — among the first films directed by a Palestinian — weaves memory, gender, and resistance on 16mm. Introduction and discussion with Juan González Monroy.

**Why It Fits:** Politically engaged cinema with post-screening discourse; archival format.

**Official Link:** [Arsenal programme](https://www.arsenal-berlin.de/en/)

### What's Underground About Marshmallows?

**Venue:** Arsenal

**Date(s):** 11 June 2026

**Time(s):** 20:00

**Short Context:** An evening of texts by and about Jack Smith, with editor Marc Siegel in person. Essential for anyone tracking the genealogy of queer underground cinema and its influence on contemporary moving-image art.

**Official Link:** [Arsenal programme](https://www.arsenal-berlin.de/en/)

### Xiao Wu

**Venue:** Arsenal

**Date(s):** 13 June 2026
