# Berlin Culture Briefing — Week of September 2–8, 2026

September starts with a tight cluster rather than a scatter: the Jewish Museum’s anniversary exhibition looking for a liveable present, a live-scored fairy tale about patriarchal violence at silent green, and a documentary on the Sonderkommando revolt at ACUDkino. Thursday’s openings — Mack’s colour in a church, a sound-minded group show in Wilmersdorf, a Philip Johnson portrait at Schinkel Pavillon — do more than the thinner performance and music calendars around them.

## Top Picks

### The Opposite of Now

**Venue:** Jewish Museum Berlin

**Date(s):** Opening Thursday, 3 September 2026; exhibition on view thereafter (Tue–Sun)

**Time(s):** Opening 18:30; exhibition hours Tuesday–Sunday 10:00–18:00 (Monday closed)

**Short Context:** For its twenty-fifth anniversary the Jewish Museum asks twelve artists to look for an opposite to the present — not a slogan about hope, but a set of probes into how individuals and communities imagine a different order. Yael Bartana, Andrea Büttner, Chto Delat International, Arnold Dreyblatt, William Forsythe, Dana Kavelina, Alexander Kluge, Daniel Laufer, Dor Zlekha Levy, Ari Benjamin Meyers, Alona Rodeh, and Eran Schaerf draw on Jewish tradition and on Bloch and Adorno rather than on anniversary décor. In a week otherwise short on major institutional openings, this is the one exhibition with real civic weight: crisis as material, not as mood lighting.

**Why It Fits:** Contemporary art, memory, and political imagination at a major Berlin institution; named artists across research-based, moving-image, and interdisciplinary practice rather than commemorative filler.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — The Opposite of Now](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/45823/the-opposite-of-now)

### Midori Hirano x The Juniper Tree (1990) — Heroines of Sound / Heroines Edition #I

**Venue:** silent green Kulturquartier, Kuppelhalle

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 2 September 2026

**Time(s):** Doors 19:00; start 20:00

**Short Context:** Midori Hirano’s German-premiere film concert re-scores Nietzchka Keene’s *The Juniper Tree* (1990) — Björk’s first screen role, a black-and-white Icelandic fairy tale of witchcraft, misogyny, and patriarchal violence that has sat too long in the arthouse basement. Hirano works from piano, electronics, and carefully held space rather than from gothic volume; the point is to keep the film’s fragility audible without sanding off its force. Wouter Vanhaelemeesch introduces a Heroines of Sound commission originally made for Videodroom and Film Fest Gent, now landing in the Kuppelhalle before the soundtrack’s release on VIERNULVIER Records.

**Why It Fits:** Ambient and experimental electronic practice with a named composer; audiovisual crossover, feminist material, and contemporary composition rather than a generic silent-film-with-laptop night.

**Official Link:** [silent green — Midori Hirano x The Juniper Tree](https://www.silent-green.net/en/programme/detail/midori-hirano-x-the-juniper-tree-1990-im-rahmen-von-heroines-of-sound-festival-heroines-edition-i?tx_news_pi1%5Bday%5D=2&tx_news_pi1%5Bmonth%5D=9&tx_news_pi1%5Byear%5D=2026&cHash=9a1ff49457c71afd345fb29fb7a22211)

### Aufstand in Auschwitz

**Venue:** ACUDkino

**Date(s):** Tuesday, 8 September 2026

**Time(s):** 19:00; Q&A with the film team

**Short Context:** Gabi Schlag’s 2026 documentary reconstructs the 7 October 1944 Sonderkommando revolt at Auschwitz-Birkenau — the camp’s only armed uprising — in graphic-novel images built from recent historical research rather than from the usual archive loop. Andreas Kilian co-wrote; Andrea Rudorff, Gideon Greif, and Marcello Pezetti appear among the on-screen voices. The film team is present for the Tuesday screening, which is the right way to show a work that treats testimony, coercion, and the ethics of picturing the killing process as an argument, not as heritage atmosphere.

**Why It Fits:** Socially engaged documentary and political-historical cinema with a post-screening discussion; archives, historical trauma, and named filmmakers rather than repertory solemnity.

**Official Link:** [ACUDkino — Aufstand in Auschwitz](https://acudkino.de/Programm/aufstand_in_auschwitz/20530/58889)

### Heinz Mack — Spectrum Mundi

**Venue:** St. Matthäus-Kirche, Matthäikirchplatz

**Date(s):** Opening Wednesday, 2 September 2026

**Time(s):** 19:00 (opening)

**Short Context:** Heinz Mack’s *Spectrum Mundi* opens at St. Matthäus-Kirche on the Kulturforum — ZERO-movement light, colour, and cosmic scale inside a postwar church rather than a white cube. Mack can slide into monument-of-modernism piety; the setting at least forces the work to argue with sacred architecture instead of floating as brand sculpture. It is the week’s first colour-and-light appointment west of Potsdamer Straße, and it belongs in the body of the briefing now that the opening has arrived.

**Why It Fits:** Light and colour as contemporary composition at a significant Kulturforum site; a named artist with enough historical weight to plan around.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — Spectrum Mundi](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/45274/spectrum-mundi)

### Ingeborg Bachmann – Jemand, der einmal ich war

**Venue:** ACUDkino

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 2 September 2026

**Time(s):** 20:30

**Short Context:** ACUDkino opens the week with a documentary on Ingeborg Bachmann — *Jemand, der einmal ich war* — which is to say on language, afterlife, and the problem of a voice that keeps being quoted into piety. ## Performing Arts

### HAUS AUS GLAS — Vom Bauen und Niederreißen

**Venue:** Schinkel Pavillon

**Date(s):** Thursday, 3 September 2026

**Time(s):** 20:00

**Short Context:** A performative portrait of Philip Johnson — *Haus aus Glas*, on building and tearing down — lands the week after Berlinische Galerie’s Josef Kaiser survey and makes the comparison unavoidable. Johnson’s glass house is the Western twin of the problem: modernism as transparency, power, and demolition dressed up as progress. Schinkel Pavillon is the right room for that argument if the piece can hold the architecture without becoming a TED-talk with props; it is also the only verified in-window performance with this kind of conceptual pressure.

**Why It Fits:** Architecture, power, and experimental performance at a priority contemporary-art venue; a direct continuation of last week’s GDR building story into American modernism.

**Official Link:** [INDEX Berlin — HAUS AUS GLAS](https://www.indexberlin.com/events/list/45281/haus-aus-glas-vom-bauen-und-niederreissen-ein-performatives-portrat-des-amerikanischen-architekten-philip-johnson)

**Date(s):** Wednesday, 9 September 2026

**Time(s):** 17:00; Kianí Del Valle’s *Instrucciones para existir* follows at 17:30 in the same building

**Short Context:** Trisha Brown’s *Walking on the Wall* — bodies in harnesses moving sideways across a museum wall as if gravity had been rotated ninety degrees — is restaged at the Neue Nationalgalerie the day after this briefing week.
