# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-11-05

Cold-weather eating this week: dumplings, Georgian khinkali, Syrian eggplant and yogurt, and a Nanjing-leaning Chinese menu. Most of it is cheap and casual, spread from Wedding and Moabit out to Charlottenburg.

### WeiDaoJia — Prenzlauer Berg — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 12:00–22:30; Fri–Sat 12:00–23:00; Sun 12:00–22:30

**Rating:** 4.8 (360)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3105457945904461337&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The Nanjing dishes are what set this place apart from the usual Sichuan-and-noodles Chinese menu: crispy rice, wok-fired clams and handmade jiaozi are the orders to build a meal around. The menu is broad, so it takes some deliberate ordering to find the regional cooking. The lion's-head meatball is the signature, and it may need to be ordered ahead, so ask before you go.

### Papaya Mum — Charlottenburg — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.4 (1637)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=15424834162655154313&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

This Thai kitchen makes its own fermented sausages, curry pastes and dried fish, which is more preparation than the usual Kantstraße curry assembly. Shrimp-paste fried rice, grilled fish and the dipping sauces are the sensible places to start. It is a place to go with several people and order across the menu, since a single plate will not show you much. Thai food without one narrow regional focus, but with real work behind the condiments.

### Malakeh — Schöneberg — €€

**Rating:** 4.8 (1841)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=8367060389816948939&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Syrian home-style cooking with Damascus and Aleppo roots, and not another kebab-and-shawarma stop. The fatteh with fried eggplant, bread, tomato and yogurt is the dish to order, and the kibbeh in warm yogurt sauce is a close second. Be ready for richness: much of the menu is fried or yogurt-heavy, so balance it with something fresh. Check the changing specials before settling on the standards.

### Salhino — Charlottenburg — €€

**Rating:** 4.7 (1005)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=522893128756490706&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Khinkali are the benchmark here, with a proper dumpling texture and well-judged seasoning, and the rest of the traditional Georgian menu is built around them. Amber wines go well with the food, but a few shared plates plus a bottle turn this into a bigger bill than the €€ tier suggests. It is dinner only on most days and closed on Mondays. Same family of cooking as Gotxa, with dumplings as the reason to book.

### Örnek Lahmacun Evi — Gesundbrunnen / Wedding — € (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 09:00–00:00; Fri–Sat 09:00–01:00; Sun 09:00–00:00

**Rating:** 4.5 (1265)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=9582617472781194209&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A lahmacun specialist: thin, freshly baked flatbread with a meat topping, and not much else you need to order. Keep it narrow, because the grill menu around the lahmacun is not the draw. It is a cheap, focused stop rather than a full evening out, and the long opening hours, late into the night, make it easy to fit in.

### Panda Meister Moabit — Moabit — € (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 11:30–21:30

**Rating:** 4.8 (484)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=12405272178246059722&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Handmade dumplings in several fillings, vegan ones included, and the value for the money is what gets this place mentioned. Don't expect refined regional distinctions; this is straightforward, well-made dumpling eating. Prices on offer can change, so look at the current menu before you plan around any figure you have heard. Good for a quick, inexpensive dinner in Moabit.

### Li.Ke Thai Vegan — Friedrichshain — €

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (2462)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=12740184214125134837&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Vegan Thai cooking with full flavors, and the pad thai and yellow lemongrass curry are the dishes to order. The heat is mild, so this is not the place if you want fierce chili or fermented seafood funk. Judged on flavor balance and low prices, it holds up well against any Thai place regardless of the vegan label. A calmer counterpoint to a week of spicier food.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Salhino — for khinkali and a bottle of amber wine on a cold evening.
2. WeiDaoJia — for Nanjing dishes you will not find on most Chinese menus; order the crispy rice and clams and ask about the lion's-head meatball.
3. Malakeh — for fatteh and kibbeh when you want something comforting and different from a kebab night.
