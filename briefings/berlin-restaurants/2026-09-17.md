# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-09-17

This week moves off the usual east-side loop: dumpling-and-noodle cooking in Steglitz, two Thai kitchens with different jobs, charcoal grill work on Skalitzer Straße, and a Charlottenburg pair of product-led European rooms. CODA is the one expensive Neukölln outlier — a dessert tasting-menu rather than another canal-side luxury dinner.

### Fu Li Lai — Steglitz — € (good value)

**Hours:** Mon 12:00–22:00; Wed–Sun 12:00–22:00; (closed Tue)

**Rating:** 4.4 (758)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=9315743648325898124&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

An Erlenstraße Chinese room built around dumplings and Xi'an-style noodles rather than a pan-Chinese greatest-hits list — home-style cooking in a part of the city this briefing rarely reaches. The case is comfort and repeatability, not ambition: it reads more canteen than destination, and the published write-up trail is thinner than for the city's better-documented Chinese specialists. Against Holly China's tighter Xi'an street-food format in Charlottenburg, Wok Show's dumpling-only Prenzlauer Berg counter, or last week's broader northern table at Shan Shan, Fu Li Lai is the south-west budget option — less of a dish-by-dish briefing, more of a useful cheap specialist if you are already in Steglitz or willing to travel for noodles and dumplings at €. Tuesday closure is the only real scheduling friction; skip it if you want a composed evening rather than a focused bowl-and-plate meal.

### Khao Taan — Friedrichshain — €€€

**Hours:** Tue–Sat 18:00–23:00 (closed Mon, Sun)

**Rating:** 4.7 (459)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1368696323655479600&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Gryphiusstraße Thai kitchen that treats dinner as a family-style composition: seasonal recipes, house-pressed coconut milk, and spice handling that is closer to home cooking with research behind it than to a curry-house template. The price and the shared/set structure are the tax — you give up à la carte freedom, Monday–Sunday closure plus dinner-only hours make it a planned meal, and it sits a tier above the usual €€ Thai weeknight. Against last week's heat-first Larb Koi a few streets away, or Dao's broader Kantstraße institution two weeks ago, Khao Taan is the more authored option — less casual, more technically specific, and less useful if you mainly want a fiery walk-in. Worth booking when you want Thai cooking with a point of view; skip it if Papaya's blunt canteen heat is the job.

### Papaya — Schöneberg — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–16:00 & 17:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.3 (633)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=2456362811345032783&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The Winterfeldtstraße Thai canteen, not the Kantstraße Papaya Mum kitchen from late July: Isaan salads, grilled meats, and curries that do not get dialed down for timid tables, in a room that still feels more business than destination. A 4.3 across a large review base is competent rather than ecstatic, the multi-location footprint can flatten personality, and it is conceptually less distinct than Khao Taan on this list. Against Larb Koi's fiercer, smaller-room specialist act last week or Papaya Mum's pantry-depth Kantstraße ecosystem, this is the straightforward heat-and-breadth option — easier to drop into, less of a thesis. Useful when you want punchy regional Thai without a set menu or a booking ritual; not the week's most interesting kitchen if you already have a spicy-Thai default.

### Adana Grillhaus — Kreuzberg — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 14:00–02:00; Fri–Sat 12:00–03:00; Sun 12:00–02:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (1687)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=13852046494429029825&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The Skalitzer Straße grill house, not the Audre-Lorde-Straße room from early July: Adana, Beyti, lamb ribs, mezze, and künefe in a charcoal sequence built for a full table rather than a snack stop. It is a known name, which is both the point and the limit — popularity costs spontaneity, and this is institution cooking rather than a discovery. Against Tadım Ocakbaşı's smaller, more obsessive skewer craft from late July, or Fes on the same Kreuzberg grill map, the Skalitzer Adana is the group-meal benchmark: more range, later hours, less of a specialist whisper. Strong when you want charcoal work and a proper grill-house sequence; skip it if you already treat the other branch as a regular and do not need a second room doing the same job.

### Il Calice — Charlottenburg — €€€

**Hours:** Tue–Fri 12:00–00:00; Sat 13:00–00:00; (closed Mon, Sun)

**Rating:** 4.6 (560)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3662233515076542285&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Walter-Benjamin-Platz Italian room whose case is product and technique — olive oil, charcuterie, pasta, grill work, seasonality — plus a wine program that is more serious than most Berlin trattoria branding. It drifts above a cheap weeknight, leans destination rather than standby, and Monday–Sunday closure plus a late Saturday start require planning. Against Gotxa's Basque snack format from late August, Common's bakery-to-pizza dual identity two weeks ago, or merold's product-led German room last week, Il Calice is the more classical European craft pick: ingredient fundamentals instead of a concept. Worth crossing west for when you want Italian cooking that is actually about product; less compelling if this week's specialists already cover the meal you want.

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 17:00–23:00 (closed Sat–Sun)

A Leibnizstraße Austrian kitchen with Michelin-selected recognition and a price tier that still belongs in a weekly logic, which is rarer than another €€€€ Central European tasting menu. The published case here is thinner than for Il Calice: cuisine and reputation more than a dish-by-dish argument, and weekend closure makes it a weekday-only plan. Against Jolesch's more straightforward Kreuzberg schnitzel-and-goulash room from mid-August, or Horváth's canal-side tasting-menu autobiography two weeks ago, Nußbaumerin is the west-side sit-down Austrian option — more formal than Jolesch, far more usable than Horváth. Include it when you want Central European craft without an occasion budget; it is not the week's strongest evidence base if you need named dishes before you go.

The week's only fine-dining entry, and the odd one: dessert-led tasting-menu cooking on Friedelstraße, counter seating, kitchen in view, Michelin-recognized, and firmly outside the €–€€ logic that should drive most meals here. The format is the attraction and the problem — technically specific, easy to polarize, and a weak value argument if what you actually wanted was a savory specialist dinner. Against Rutz's sauce-driven three-star benchmark from mid-August, Bieberbau's herb-and-spice set menu last week, or Horváth's Austrian-rooted canal room, CODA is the more conceptual Neukölln outlier — closer to home geographically, less universally recommendable. Worth considering only if the dessert-dining idea itself is the point; skip it if the rest of this list already has the food you want to eat.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Khao Taan — the clearest culinary thesis this week, with family-style Thai craft that is more specific than another spicy canteen.
2. Fu Li Lai — the most useful cheap specialist, and the one that actually expands the map instead of circling the same central addresses.
3. Il Calice — the strongest European product case here, with enough technique to justify a west-side booking without becoming a tasting-menu night.
