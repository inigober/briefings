# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-09-24

This week is a short specialist list: weekend-only northern Thai noodles on Weserstraße, a seafood-and-dim-sum Chinese room near Fasanenstraße, an ocakbaşı on Hermannstraße, and cheap Mexican sandwiches in Friedrichshain. No tasting menu — the useful meals are the focused ones.

### Khao Soi — Neukölln — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Fri 18:30–22:00; Sat 18:00–22:00; (closed Mon–Thu, Sun)

**Rating:** 4.5 (33)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=9604530647532537398&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Weserstraße Thai kitchen built around one job: handmade khao soi, unsoftened heat, and sides that reportedly outrun the namesake bowl. The constraint is availability — Friday–Saturday dinner only, a 33-review sample that is too small to treat as settled, and a pop-up-ish format that can make consistency less predictable than a standing canteen. Against last week's family-style authored dinner at Khao Taan, Papaya's broader Winterfeldtstraße heat, or Larb Koi's fiercer Friedrichshain room two weeks ago, this is the narrower craft play: less of a full Thai menu, more of a single-dish argument. Worth planning when you specifically want Chiang Mai-style noodles with real chili; skip it if you need a weeknight table or a survey dinner.

### Meet You — Charlottenburg — €€€

**Hours:** Mon 11:30–22:00; Wed–Fri 11:30–22:00; Sat–Sun 11:00–22:00; (closed Tue)

**Rating:** 4.3 (1086)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=17428014459240498453&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Fasanenstraße Chinese room whose case is seafood, dumpling range, and weekend dim sum rather than another noodle counter — jiaozi, xiao long bao, and a brunch window that is more useful than most Kantstraße dinner defaults. The 4.3 across a large review base is competent rather than pointed, the menu is broader than a single-region specialist, and €€€ puts it above the usual weeknight Chinese spend. Against Shan Shan's northern table last week, Shaniu's Shanghainese-leaning noodles three weeks ago, or Ming Dynastie's Mitte institution, Meet You is the west-side occasion lane: less chili-and-comfort, more seafood and dim sum for a group. Book it when that format is the point; it is not the value play if you mainly want a bowl.

### Mira — Neukölln — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 08:00–01:00; Fri–Sun 08:00–02:00

**Rating:** 4.8 (1473)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=13164128406963470813&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Hermannstraße ocakbaşı whose published case is veal kebap, grilled meats, and a generous mezze/dip spread rather than a single obsessive skewer. The write-up trail is thinner than for the city's best-documented grill rooms, so this reads more as a flexible group table than as the sharpest technical specialist, and two Neukölln entries in one short list is already enough of that neighborhood. Against last week's Skalitzer Adana Grillhaus, Tadım's smaller Zossener Straße craft from late July, or Jemenitisches Restaurant three weeks ago, Mira is the local charcoal option — later hours, easier from home, less of a destination argument. Useful when you want a full grill-house sequence nearby; not the week's most specific kitchen if you already have a Kreuzberg ocakbaşı default.

### Sabor a mí — Friedrichshain — € (good value)

**Hours:** Tue–Thu 17:00–22:00; Fri–Sun 12:00–22:00; (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.7 (654)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=12374683917197371925&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Grünberger Straße Mexican counter whose concrete product case is the torta, with tacos as the second lane — specialization instead of another broad Tex-Mex menu. The format is the limit: more truck-and-counter than a sit-down dinner, weekday evenings only until Friday, and less of a composed meal than the rest of this list. Against Mama Van's bánh mì focus two weeks ago or the reader's usual taco-and-grill references, this is the cheap singular option that actually expands the map rather than adding another Asian or Turkish room. Best as a low-cost stop when sandwich construction is the point; ignore it if you want a table and a longer evening.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Khao Soi — the clearest culinary thesis this week, even with the weekend-only window: handmade noodles and heat that is not sanded down.
2. Meet You — the most useful Chinese differentiation here, if you want seafood and dim sum instead of another noodle bowl.
3. Sabor a mí — the strongest cheap specialist, with a named sandwich and a price that supports actually going.
