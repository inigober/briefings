# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-08-27

This week's most useful meals have a narrow job: dumplings in Prenzlauer Berg, chef-driven Thai in Friedrichshain, Lebanese-Persian cooking in Moabit, a Persian lunch counter in Schöneberg, and Basque tapas in Kreuzberg. Ming Dynastie is back as the broad Chinese institution for groups, and UUU is the one expensive outlier — a Chinese tasting menu in Wedding rather than another Mitte luxury room.

### Wok Show — Prenzlauer Berg — € (good value)

**Hours:** Mon 17:00–22:00; Wed–Thu 17:00–22:00; Fri–Sun 12:30–22:00; (closed Tue)

**Rating:** 4.3 (1531)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=10950842182876736015&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Prenzlauer Berg dumpling specialist whose case is simple: handmade jiaozi, a long-running neighborhood reputation, and a price that makes it a default rather than an occasion. The menu does not pretend to cover regional Chinese cooking at large, so this is a weak choice if you want a survey dinner with mixed appetites. Against Ming Dynastie's encyclopedic spread or last week's more snackable Sichuan small plates at Hi! Chili, Wok Show is the narrower craft play — more utilitarian than destination-coded, and better as a focused dumpling stop than a full evening. One of the clearer cheap specialists in the current pool when execution matters more than room or range.

### Ming Dynastie — Mitte — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (3653)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=261247375087039031&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Still the most reliable large-menu Chinese kitchen in Mitte when you want Sichuan heat, off-menu specials, and enough range to feed a mixed table without collapsing into generic pan-Chinese cooking. The breadth is also the weakness: it is less focused than a single-region specialist, and the room is more institution than neighborhood secret. Against Wok Show's dumpling-only logic or Chuan Garden's tighter Sichuan thesis from earlier in the summer, Ming Dynastie is the group-dining benchmark — less intimate, more technically confident across a bigger map. Worth returning to after a stretch away if you want range and chef-level execution rather than a newly fashionable small room.

### Nutts Thai Kitchen — Friedrichshain — €€

**Hours:** Mon 03:30–22:00; Thu 04:30–22:00; Fri–Sat 03:30–22:00; Sun 03:30–21:00; (closed Tue–Wed)

**Rating:** 5.0 (1099)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=2970083855136947493&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A small Friedrichshain Thai kitchen that gets praised at the dish level — fish cakes, khao soi, papaya salad — rather than for a polished concept. The 5.0 rating across a large review base is striking, but the room has historically been tiny and hard to book, and some of the appeal may sit as much in personality as in a clearly framed regional thesis. Against Hanuman's curry-specialist focus or Chon Thong's Kantstraße insider reputation, Nutts sounds more chef-led and intimate, and less obviously pinned to one subregion. The posted hours look unreliable (early-morning opens), so confirm before you go; the cooking case is still one of the strongest in this week's pool if you care about flavor more than comfort.

### El Reda — Moabit — € (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 11:00–00:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (3385)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=7673253916481811750&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Moabit Lebanese-Persian restaurant that is useful because it is rooted and cheap rather than because it is a tight technical specialist. The large menu and community reputation suggest a kitchen built for regulars, which is also how unevenness creeps in: breadth without a single signature can blur. Against Tadım's charcoal-grill craft or Rocket & Basil's more precise Persian lunch plates, El Reda is the everyday sit-down option — more institution than thesis, more value than precision. A strong neighborhood pick when you want substantial Middle Eastern food without crossing the city for a grill specialist.

### Rocket & Basil — Schöneberg — €€

**Hours:** Tue–Fri 11:30–15:00; Sat–Sun 10:00–16:00; (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.6 (834)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=2358804007255459580&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Schöneberg daytime counter with a clearer Persian identity than most Berlin lunch cafés: sabzi kuku and mirza ghasemi are the kind of dishes that signal a point of view rather than a generic salad-and-sandwich menu. It is not a dinner destination, and the format is lighter than El Reda's sit-down spread. That is also the point — a useful specialist for lunch when you want savory Iranian cooking without committing to a full restaurant evening. Better as a weekday stop than as the week's main event, but more specific than the usual deli-café blur.

### Gotxa — Kreuzberg — €€€

**Hours:** Wed–Thu 18:00–23:00; Fri–Sat 18:00–00:00; Sun 01:00–22:00; (closed Mon–Tue)

**Rating:** 4.9 (398)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1105756903453319625&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Kreuzberg Basque-leaning tapas kitchen with Spanish-chef credibility and concrete strengths in tortilla, gildas, croquetas, and cheesecake — more regionally convincing than the city's generic small-plates bars. The risk is the format: a social snack room where the bill climbs quickly, and popularity can start to outrun the food case. It is the sharper identity play if you want product-driven Spanish cooking rather than a hybrid concept, but value depends on ordering into that Basque lane instead of treating it as an unstructured graze. Stronger on craft and specificity than on weeknight practicality.

### UUU — Wedding — €€€€

**Hours:** Fri–Sat 17:30–22:00; Sun 12:00–17:00; (closed Mon–Thu)

**Rating:** 4.7 (103)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1304440578310595723&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The week's only fine-dining entry, and the interesting one because it treats Chinese cooking as a tasting-menu craft problem rather than another European luxury room. Wedding is a better neighborhood for this audience than Mitte prestige dining, but the price and limited Friday–Sunday hours make it an occasion, not a habit. Against Rutz's sauce-driven European benchmark from last week or Ming Dynastie's flexible mid-range Chinese format here, UUU offers more conceptual rarity and less weeknight usefulness. Include it only when you specifically want a high-end Chinese tasting menu — distinctive, not a value argument.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Nutts Thai Kitchen — the most interesting cooking case this week, with dish-level praise and enough intensity to justify the booking hassle.
2. Wok Show — the clearest cheap specialist, doing one thing well at a price that supports repeat visits.
3. Gotxa — the strongest European craft pick, with Basque identity and specific plates that rise above generic tapas branding.
