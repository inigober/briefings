# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-11-12

A week for garlic, sambal and sourdough: a Greek taverna that does not hold back, a Malaysian kopitiam on Lausitzer Platz, and a few cheap, useful lunches scattered from Kreuzberg out to Reinickendorf. Cold weather suits all of it.

### Ma-Makan — Kreuzberg — €€

**Hours:** Wed–Fri 12:00–16:00 & 17:30–22:00; Sat 11:00–16:00 & 17:30–22:00; Sun 11:00–16:00; (closed Mon–Tue)

**Rating:** 4.4 (548)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=673259701163278563&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The sambal and curry pastes are made in-house, and it shows in the nasi lemak: coconut rice, crisp anchovies, peanuts, pickles and a sambal that ties them together, with a real contrast of textures on the plate. The tahu goreng in soy-tamarind-peanut sauce is a good meat-free order. Already a favorite of yours, and still the benchmark for Malaysian-Singaporean cooking in town. The room is small and fills up, and specials like laksa rotate, so do not count on a particular one.

### Taverna Ousia — Schöneberg — €€

**Hours:** Mon 17:00–01:00; Wed–Sat 17:00–01:00; Sun 17:00–00:00; (closed Tue)

**Rating:** 4.6 (2103)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=9655664065910324608&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Garlic is the point here. The fried anchovies come with a skordalia that is properly assertive, and the vegetable plates and fish are seasoned with the same confidence. Build the meal from shared starters rather than defaulting to a mixed grill and you get the best of it. Tables sit close together and the room is loud, so this is a lively dinner, not a quiet one, and booking ahead is wise.

### Salami Social Club — Friedrichshain — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 12:00–22:00; Fri–Sat 12:00–23:00; Sun 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.6 (1950)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=16689718780361449351&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Better on toppings than on dough. The black-pudding pizza is the one that gives the place an identity, and seasonal pies like the pumpkin one, with tangy cheese, olives and chili honey, show a kitchen that thinks about balance. If you want strict Neapolitan tradition, look elsewhere. As a reliable, inventive pizza stop on Frankfurter Allee, it earns its crowds.

### Ferro Canteen — Reinickendorf — € (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 09:00–16:00 (closed Sat–Sun)

**Rating:** 3.9 (54)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=288347911868847309&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A workday canteen in the Wilhelm Hallen with changing mains at roughly €9–16 and house-made sourdough with a proper crust. Meat, fish, vegetarian and vegan options rotate, so there is no signature dish to plan around. This is a useful lunch if you are in the north, not a reason to cross the city, and the modest rating reflects a small, new following rather than a problem. Weekdays only, and it closes at 16:00.

### Kírkē Deli — Kreuzberg — €

**Rating:** 4.9 (192)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=4890278196409092073&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A small Greek-leaning deli where the sandwiches carry the weight: tuna, pastrami and truffle-goat-cheese are the ones people order, with Greek cheeses, olives and spreads behind them. It is more a focused pantry than a traditional Greek menu, so expect good ingredients rather than regional orthodoxy. Add wine and a few deli plates and the bill climbs past sandwich money. Check the opening times before you go.

### Barra — Neukölln — €€€

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 18:00–23:15 (closed Sat–Sun)

**Rating:** 4.5 (807)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=15461423447321715949&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The week's one step up in price, and a relaxed one. Michelin singles out the house-rolled pasta and the choice of ingredients, and you can order à la carte or go for the sharing menu, which keeps it from feeling like a formal evening. Order carefully, because a full shared meal adds up quickly. The room is lively rather than hushed, and it is only open on weekday evenings.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Ma-Makan — for the nasi lemak and the sambal, on a weekend lunch when you want something with real depth.
2. Taverna Ousia — for a loud, garlicky night out with friends and plenty of starters.
3. Barra — for the pasta, on a weekday evening when you want a good meal without a tasting-menu commitment.
