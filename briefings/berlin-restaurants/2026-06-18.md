# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-06-18

This week’s verified pool skews heavily toward Thai and Turkish cooking, with no new regional Chinese candidates in the inbox. The selections favor specialists and institutions over broad menus: two Friedrichshain Thai kitchens with different ambitions, a Schöneberg mainstay for heat seekers, Kreuzberg grill work at both sit-down and snack scale, and two Prenzlauer Berg options that lean polished without crossing into fine dining.

### Papaya — Schöneberg — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–16:00 & 17:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.3 (628)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=2456362811345032783&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A long-running Schöneberg address for regional Thai cooking that does not dial back chili for timid palates. Isaan salads, grilled meats, and curries arrive with consistent intensity in a no-frills room that feels more canteen than destination. The multi-location footprint can make it feel less personal than a single-owner kitchen, and the 4.3 rating against six hundred reviews suggests competent rather than transcendent execution. Still one of the more reliable options in Berlin when you want genuine heat and breadth across Thai regions without fine-dining pricing — better as a dependable neighborhood meal than a once-a-year splurge.

### Khao Taan — Friedrichshain — €€€

**Hours:** Tue–Sat 18:00–23:00 (closed Mon, Sun)

**Rating:** 4.7 (447)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1368696323655479600&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

An intimate set-menu Thai restaurant built around family recipes, house-pressed coconut milk, and careful sourcing — a step above casual curry-house cooking in both price and craft. The format limits flexibility: you eat what the kitchen has composed for the evening, which rewards trust more than picky ordering. Natural wine pairings and premium ingredients push the bill into splurge territory for Thai food in Berlin, though the 4.7 rating reflects genuine enthusiasm from diners who value technique over volume. Worth planning ahead when you want Thai cooking with real personal vision; less compelling if you mainly want fiery street-food bluntness or à la carte freedom.

### Larb Koi — Friedrichshain — €€

**Hours:** Wed–Thu 18:00–22:00; Fri–Sat 18:00–23:00; Sun 03:00–22:00; (closed Mon–Tue)

**Rating:** 4.6 (686)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1837714750978964420&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Among the fiercest Thai kitchens in the city, Larb Koi trades polish for bold, home-style flavor — whole fish, oyster dishes, and curries that assume you can handle serious spice. Some items require advance ordering, and the limited hours plus small-room format mean it rewards planning rather than spontaneous drop-ins. Compared with Boii Boii’s more refined, ingredient-forward approach in Kreuzberg, Larb Koi is rawer and more heat-forward; compared with Papaya’s broader regional menu, it feels more chef-driven and selective. Not for everyone, but one of the stronger bets in Berlin when intensity and regional specificity matter more than comfort.

### Hasir Ocakbaşı — Kreuzberg — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 12:00–00:00; Fri–Sat 12:00–01:00; Sun 12:00–00:00

**Rating:** 4.5 (1910)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=11943487455487135675&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Kreuzberg institution with deep roots in Berlin’s Turkish dining history — charcoal-grilled meats, house breads, and mezze executed with the confidence of a kitchen that has done this for decades. Fame brings crowds and a slightly tourist-facing energy that can dilute the neighborhood-imbiss feel, and the room itself is functional rather than atmospheric. None of that undermines the core proposition: this remains a dependable mid-range ocakbaşı with genuine technique and portions that justify the price tier. Better value and more culinary substance than most döner-adjacent spots on Oranienstraße, even if it will never feel like a hidden gem.

### Toros Tantuni — Kreuzberg — €

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 12:00–21:30; Sun 12:00–21:30; (closed Sat)

**Rating:** 4.6 (741)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1543942635703526120&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A narrow specialist focused on Mersin-style tantuni — spiced entrecôte rolled into handmade dürüm with technique that punches well above its snack-price tier. The menu is essentially one thing done repeatedly, so it suits a quick, focused meal rather than a leisurely dinner, and the street-food format means limited seating and no broader Turkish grill repertoire. Google lists it under a pizzeria name, which is confusing on paper but does not affect what you actually get at the counter. One of the better-value specialist picks in Kreuzberg when you want regional Turkish street food rather than a full ocakbaşı spread.

### Osmans Töchter — Prenzlauer Berg — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 17:00–00:00; Fri–Sat 17:00–01:00; (closed Sun)

**Rating:** 4.7 (1964)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=13278237169014365985&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

An elevated Anatolian mezze restaurant in the Bötzow Areal that blends Turkish regional cooking with Mediterranean polish — creative small plates, refined presentation, and a dining room with more design intent than a typical neighborhood ocakbaşı. The stylized interior and sharing format push prices upward relative to simpler Turkish grills, and some diners may find the atmosphere more sceney than substance-first. The cooking itself shows real regional depth in the mezze program, making it a solid choice when you want Turkish flavors with sit-down comfort in Prenzlauer Berg. Less raw and value-driven than Hasir, but stronger on variety and ambiance for a group evening.

### ITA Bistro — Prenzlauer Berg — €€

**Hours:** Mon 18:00–23:30; Thu–Sat 18:00–23:30; Sun 01:00–21:30; (closed Tue–Wed)

**Rating:** 4.5 (325)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=11092233695097909376&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A wood-oven bistro on Lettestraße serving a seasonal, globally influenced sharing menu in an open-kitchen setting that feels convivial rather than formal. The eclectic concept — pulling from multiple traditions without anchoring to one cuisine — gives the kitchen room for creativity but can make consistency harder to predict visit to visit. Technique and product quality are clearly priorities, and the mid-range pricing fits a neighborhood dinner with friends more than a special-occasion tasting menu. Interesting when you want craft-driven cooking without committing to a single regional identity; less compelling if you prefer specialists with a clear culinary through-line.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Larb Koi — the most distinctive Thai cooking here, with real intensity and chef-level ambition at a still-reasonable price tier.
2. Hasir Ocakbaşı — the safest bet for a full Turkish grill meal with proven technique and strong value.
3. Toros Tantuni — best specialist on the list for a quick, high-quality bite that most Berlin diners overlook.
