# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-07-09

This week's verified pool is unusually thin — only four candidates passed Google Places checks — but the geography and cuisines spread well: Sichuan in Mitte, Korean vegan in Wedding, Indonesian in Moabit, and Syrian Levantine in Friedrichshain. After two weeks of heavy Sichuan and Turkish coverage, the inbox shifts toward Southeast Asian and Middle Eastern specialists with clearer regional identity. All four selections sit at € or €€; no fine-dining entry in the pool.

### Chuan Garden 川园 — Mitte — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (341)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3614477287672776716&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Torstraße Sichuan kitchen where chefs from Himmelspagode bring serious regional technique — rabbit dishes, hot pot, and bold málà cooking in a format that feels more destination than canteen. The large terrace suits summer dining, though the address has seen high turnover; past tenants failed quickly, which is worth noting before treating it as a proven long-term fixture. Against Liu Nudelhaus's noodle-focused Chengdu work or Wei House's Friedrichshain dim sum, Chuan Garden offers a broader Sichuan menu with genuine spice depth at a mid-range price. One of the more interesting honest Sichuan options in East Berlin when you want full-menu regional cooking rather than a single-dish specialist — though not quite at the level of the city's most established institutions.

### Dal Tokki — Wedding — € (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Tue 12:00–15:00 (closed Wed–Sun)

**Rating:** 4.7 (102)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=15991654687140121839&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A family-run Prinzenallee address serving a fixed vegan Korean lunch set — balanced components, simple execution, and pricing that undercuts almost anything comparable in Berlin. The discipline of a single set menu keeps the kitchen focused, but it also limits flexibility: lunch only, two days a week, no à la carte freedom. Compared with broader Korean options in the city, Dal Tokki is a specialist bet on value and consistency rather than variety or evening dining. Worth planning around the narrow hours when you want a thoughtful vegan meal without fine-dining markup — one of the stronger value picks in Wedding when the schedule aligns.

### Nusantara — Moabit — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–21:30

**Rating:** 4.4 (1707)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=6608885631759338744&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

One of the few Indonesian specialists in Berlin, founded by Indonesians and built around regional dishes that most pan-Asian restaurants here ignore entirely. The 4.4 rating across seventeen hundred reviews signals broad rather than ecstatic consensus — competent and culturally grounded, but not a hidden-gem discovery story. Moabit's Turmstraße location keeps it off the usual Kreuzberg–Neukölln dining circuit, which can be an advantage for locals and a friction point for cross-town visits. Against Southeast Asian generalists or Thai-heavy briefings from recent weeks, Nusantara offers genuine Indonesian specificity and freshness; less fiery and street-food blunt than Larb Koi, but more distinctive in its own category.

### Aleppo Supper Club — Friedrichshain — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 11:00–23:00; Fri–Sat 11:00–23:30; Sun 11:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.9 (1251)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=16433895107034430658&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A deeply personal Syrian kitchen on Wühlischstraße where maqluba, kabsa, and metabel arrive with regional authenticity and a compelling backstory — low-priced but richly expressive Levantine cooking that outshines trendier Middle Eastern options in the city. The takeaway-forward format at one location and the less formal setting mean it suits a focused meal or pickup more than a leisurely sit-down dinner, and the dual-address history (Rigaer Straße / Wühlischstraße) can confuse first-time visitors. Compared with Shaam Restaurant's broader Yemeni-Gulf menu in Neukölln or Osmans Töchter's polished Anatolian mezze, Aleppo Supper Club is rawer and more home-style — stronger on personal Syrian identity than ambiance. One of the more distinctive Levantine bets in Friedrichshain when flavor and cultural specificity matter more than dining-room polish.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Dal Tokki — the clearest value proposition here, with disciplined vegan Korean cooking at a price tier that is hard to match elsewhere in the city.
2. Aleppo Supper Club — the highest-rated entry, with rare Syrian regional depth and a 4.9 consensus that reflects genuine enthusiasm rather than hype.
3. Chuan Garden 川园 — the strongest Sichuan full-menu option in this week's pool, with real technique from experienced chefs and daily hours that ease planning.
