# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-08-06

This week's verified pool is the deepest yet — twenty-one candidates passed Google Places checks — with strong representation across regional Chinese, Thai, Turkish grills, French bistro cooking, Italian craft, and one Michelin-starred splurge. Lao Xiang, Chon Thong, Shaniu's House of Noodles, Banh Xeo Saigon, Et Dünyası, Papaya Mum, Khao Taan, and Loumi were excluded as repeats within the ten-week window. Seven selections span Prenzlauer Berg, Schöneberg, Neukölln, Kreuzberg, Charlottenburg, and Mitte, with tulus lotrek as the single fine-dining entry and a Neukölln pair that pairs a charcoal-grill institution with an affordable döner benchmark.

### Wei Dao Jia — Prenzlauer Berg — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 12:00–22:30; Fri–Sat 12:00–23:00; Sun 12:00–22:30

**Rating:** 4.8 (299)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3105457945904461337&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Nanjing-leaning Chinese kitchen on Oderberger Straße where dumplings, crispy rice dishes, clams, and Mapo Tofu carry more regional specificity than the Sichuan-and-pan-Chinese shorthand that still dominates much of Berlin's Chinese landscape. The menu breadth rewards repeat visits and informed ordering — Lion's Head Meatball by request signals ambition, but wandering into generic territory dilutes the kitchen's sharper work. Against Lao Xiang's Northern Chinese dim sum brunch from last week or Holly China's Xi'an street-food focus, Wei Dao Jia is the clearer eastern-side bet when you want dish-level regional identity rather than a broad all-purpose Chinese dinner. Not the cheapest casual Chinese meal in the neighborhood, but one of the more interesting regional-Chinese specialists in Prenzlauer Berg when culinary point of view matters more than trend value.

### You Mi — Schöneberg — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.9 (375)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=15420434776594381727&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Nürnberger Straße Chinese restaurant with a large menu that reads more like a kitchen built for Chinese-speaking regulars than a simplified export format — tofu skin, bamboo salad, garlic shoots with Chinese sausage, chili beef, and yuxiang eggplant are the called-out strengths rather than a single viral dish. The breadth can feel uneven if you order without guidance, and the regional specialization is less sharply defined than at the strongest Nanjing or Northern Chinese specialists elsewhere in the city. Against Good Friends' Cantonese banquet tradition or Wei Dao Jia's tighter regional lane, You Mi is broader and more exploratory — better for group ordering sessions in West Berlin when you want depth across dishes rather than a single culinary thesis. Promising rather than fully pinned down, but the cited plates suggest real kitchen confidence when you treat it as a targeted ordering exercise.

### Mardin — Neukölln — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 09:00–03:00; Fri–Sat 09:00–04:00; Sun 09:00–03:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (1545)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=15343297613053954740&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Kottbusser Damm charcoal-grill institution where juicy lamb kebabs, lahmacun, and homemade ayran carry the meal with the credibility of a kitchen backed by the M47 offshoot culture rather than tourist-facing branding. The room can be boisterous and crowded, and ordering broadly — which is how the grill shows best — can push the spend above snack-tier expectations. Against Adana Grillhaus's blunt Kreuzberg tradition or Tadım Ocakbaşı's craft-focused Beyti from recent weeks, Mardin is louder and more institution-scale around Hermannplatz — less hidden-gem narrative, more heavyweight reference point for serious Turkish grilling. One of the stronger mid-range grill bets in southern Berlin when charcoal technique and specialist identity matter more than a quiet evening out.

**Rating:** 4.0 (1729)

A Karl-Marx-Straße döner benchmark where above-average meat handling and bread quality justify the long-running reputation across multiple city branches — the Neukölln address is among the cited strong locations. The proposition narrows to one format rather than broader menu exploration, and branch quality can vary citywide, which keeps expectations grounded. Against Prizreni's qebape grill counter or Kalaja's Balkan bakery hybrid from last week, Imren is the pure affordable-Turkish specialist bet — less variety, more concentration on what a serious döner should taste like at snack pricing. Not novel, but exactly the kind of low-ticket, high-flavor neighborhood address this audience tends to appreciate when execution is strong rather than image-driven.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Wei Dao Jia — the clearest regional-Chinese discovery here, with Nanjing-leaning dish specificity and strong value on the eastern side of the city.
2. Mardin — the most dependable grill institution on the list, with charcoal technique and lahmacun credibility that rewards broad ordering around Hermannplatz.
3. Imren — the strongest affordable specialist bet, with döner execution at a price tier that is hard to argue with on Karl-Marx-Straße.
