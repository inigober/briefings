# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-07-02

This week's verified pool is almost entirely Sichuan Chinese and Turkish grill work, with one Vietnamese pho counter and a Neukölln fish specialist rounding out the mix. Regional Chinese finally returns after three weeks of Thai- and Turkish-heavy coverage, and two reader favorites — Liu Nudelhaus and Adana Grillhaus — make their first appearance in these briefings. Boii Boii and Hasir Ocakbaşı were excluded as repeats within the ten-week window. No fine-dining entry in the pool; all six selections sit at € or €€.

### Liu Nudelhaus — Mitte — €

**Hours:** Mon–Tue 11:30–15:00; Wed–Fri 11:30–15:00 & 17:00–19:30; (closed Sat–Sun)

**Rating:** 4.5 (2419)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=5978374952795598817&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Chengdu noodle specialist on Kronenstraße that does one thing with unusual focus: hand-pulled noodles in genuinely spicy, numbing Sichuan broths at a price tier where most Mitte lunch spots trade flavor for foot traffic. The menu is narrow, the room is functional, and the weekend closure limits spontaneity — check hours before crossing town on a Saturday. Against Berlin's broader Chinese landscape, Liu competes with single-dish counters rather than full-menu institutions like Ming Dynastie; the noodle technique and regional spice work are the entire proposition. One of the stronger affordable specialists in the city when you want Chengdu authenticity without a canteen-scale menu diluting the kitchen's intent.

### Adana Grillhaus — Kreuzberg — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 12:00–03:00; Fri–Sat 12:00–04:00; Sun 12:00–03:00

**Rating:** 4.8 (2289)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3901475484461481539&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A decades-old charcoal grill on Audre-Lorde-Straße that has outlasted trendier neighbors by executing Turkish ocakbaşı fundamentals with consistency rather than reinvention. Adana kebab, lahmacun, and mixed grill plates arrive with the blunt, meat-forward confidence of a kitchen that has grilled the same way for years — no tasting menus, no fusion gestures. The late hours and meat-centric format suit a hearty dinner more than a light lunch, and the menu offers little for vegetarians. Compared with Hasir's broader institution-scale operation or Toros Tantuni's single-dish snack format, Adana sits in the dependable mid-range grill category — less discovery-oriented than a specialist, but more satisfying for a full sit-down Turkish meal when technique and portion size matter.

### Wei House (Chuan House) — Friedrichshain — €€

**Hours:** Tue–Sat 17:00–22:30; Sun 17:30–22:30; (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.6 (524)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=17257470551723575126&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Listed on Google Maps as Chuan House, this Landsberger Allee address delivers Sichuan cooking with genuine málà heat and handmade dim sum in a setting that feels more neighborhood canteen than destination dining room. The smaller review base relative to Tian Fu or Ming Dynastie suggests it remains under the radar, which can be an advantage for diners who want regional specificity without tourist-facing energy. Evening-only hours and a Friedrichshain location that is not central may limit casual drop-ins. Against Willing's cozy Schöneberg spice work or Tian Fu's hot-pot tradition, Wei House leans toward bold, balanced Sichuan flavors and dim sum craft — a worthwhile Friedrichshain bet when you want technique-forward regional Chinese without Mitte crowds.

### Tian Fu — Wilmersdorf — €€

**Hours:** Tue–Fri 12:00–15:00 & 17:30–22:30; Sat–Sun 12:00–22:30; (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.3 (1225)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=9725218145076602110&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A long-running Uhlandstraße address for Sichuan hot pot and dim sum that trades niche discovery for dependable, technically grounded regional cooking. The 4.3 rating across twelve hundred reviews signals solid rather than ecstatic consensus — this is a reliable workhorse, not a hidden gem — and the Wilmersdorf location draws a mix of locals and visitors that can make it feel less intimate than smaller specialists. Hot pot and dim sum are the strengths; diners wanting fiery, single-dish noodle work may find Liu Nudelhaus more focused. Still one of the more trustworthy Sichuan options in western Berlin when you want a full-menu restaurant with real regional identity rather than a pan-Chinese catch-all.

### PHO – Noodlebar Kreuzberg — Kreuzberg — € (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 11:30–23:00

**Rating:** 4.4 (1407)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=16055962147337026381&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A compact Adalbertstraße pho counter built around fresh broth, fast service, and a street-food format that keeps prices low without pretending to be a full Vietnamese restaurant. The daily hours and high review volume suggest consistent neighborhood demand, though the 4.4 rating against fourteen hundred reviews places it in competent rather than exceptional territory. Compared with Kotti Dang's deeper broth work in Neukölln, PHO Noodlebar is more utilitarian — better for a quick, satisfying bowl near Kottbusser Tor than a destination pho pilgrimage. At this price tier, the value proposition is the main draw: dependable flavor without the markup of a sit-down dining room.

### Ergün's Fischbude — Neukölln — € (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 04:00–23:00; Fri–Sat 16:00–00:00; Sun 04:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (958)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=11959334156022895069&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Rudow fish shack where Turkish grilling technique meets fresh seafood in an unpretentious, counter-service format — no tablecloths, no wine list, just fish cooked with conviction. The unusual early-morning hours and remote Neukölln location mean it rewards planning rather than spontaneous Kreuzberg-adjacent dining, and the basic comfort limits its appeal as an evening destination. Against full-service Turkish grills like Adana or Hasir, Ergün's is a specialist bet: narrower scope, stronger focus on freshness, and pricing that reflects the snack-scale format. One of the better-value fish options in southern Berlin when you want Turkish flavors applied to seafood rather than lamb and beef.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Liu Nudelhaus — the most focused specialist here, with genuine Chengdu noodle technique at an unbeatable price tier.
2. Adana Grillhaus — the safest bet for a full Turkish grill meal, with a 4.8 rating that reflects decades of consistent execution.
3. Wei House (Chuan House) — the strongest under-the-radar Sichuan pick, with real málà depth and dim sum craft in Friedrichshain.
