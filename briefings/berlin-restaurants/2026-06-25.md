# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-06-25

This week's verified pool is thin — only five candidates passed Google Maps checks — but the mix covers useful ground: a Vietnamese pho counter, a Thai curry specialist, a Turkish breakfast bakery, and two Italian options at opposite ends of the price spectrum. No new regional Chinese made the cut without repeating recent coverage, and there is no fine-dining entry. The selections favor focused kitchens and neighborhood value over breadth.

### Kotti Dang — Neukölln — € (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 11:30–23:00

**Rating:** 4.8 (1508)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1366842234553923275&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Kottbusser Damm pho specialist built around homemade broth and a tight menu that does not pretend to be a full Vietnamese restaurant. The depth of the stock and the vegan-friendly options stand out at a price tier where most Berlin pho feels thin or sweetened for a broad audience. Quirky decor and a single-dish focus limit its appeal as a destination dinner, and the room reads more casual canteen than serious dining room. Still one of the stronger value bets in southern Neukölln when you want pho with real flavor rather than a generic Asian noodle bowl — better as a reliable neighborhood meal than a cross-city pilgrimage, though the pricing makes that easy to justify.

### Hanuman Thai Curry House — Kreuzberg — €€

**Hours:** Tue–Thu 12:00–15:30 & 17:00–22:00; Fri–Sun 12:00–22:00; (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.7 (360)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=10805004978977801651&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A curry-focused Thai kitchen on Skalitzer Straße that narrows its ambition to regional Thai curries rather than the encyclopedic menus common at Berlin's broader Thai restaurants. That focus is the main draw: when the kitchen is on, the spice work and sauce balance show more intent than a generic pad thai-and-curry-house spread. The smaller review base and limited non-curry visibility mean consistency is harder to judge at a glance, and diners wanting grilled meats, salads, or street-food bluntness may find the scope narrow. Compared with Boii Boii's ingredient-forward refinement or Larb Koi's heat-first intensity, Hanuman sits in a useful middle ground — a solid Kreuzberg option when curry specificity matters more than menu breadth.

### Simitdchi Cafe und Backhaus — Kreuzberg — €

**Hours:** Mon–Sat 06:30–20:00; Sun 07:00–20:00

**Rating:** 4.2 (449)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=14131623560540591311&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

An Adalbertstraße bakery and breakfast spot where simit, menemen, and Turkish morning staples are the point — not a full grill or dinner restaurant. The technique in the baked goods and the neighborhood-embedded feel give it more authenticity than most café-breakfast hybrids near Kottbusser Tor. The 4.2 rating suggests competent rather than exceptional execution by Google standards, and the format limits you to breakfast and bakery hours rather than a leisurely evening meal. Worth knowing if you want genuine Turkish morning flavors without crossing town; less relevant when you are planning a full ocakbaşı dinner — for that, Hasir remains the stronger Kreuzberg bet from recent weeks.

### Malafemmena — Schöneberg — € (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 04:00–23:00; Sat–Sun 12:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.4 (4122)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=13383657543209065497&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Hauptstraße Neapolitan pizzeria with a licensed oven and Italian-sourced ingredients, operating at a price tier that undercuts most sit-down Italian in Schöneberg. The pizza-only menu keeps the kitchen focused: cornicione, sauce balance, and oven work are the entire proposition, and the volume of positive reviews suggests the fundamentals land consistently. A 4.4 across four thousand ratings is respectable but not ecstatic — some diners will find the room functional and the scope too narrow for anything beyond pizza night. Among Berlin's Neapolitan options, it competes on technical authenticity and value more than atmosphere; a stronger specialist bet than most neighborhood Italian places when you want one thing done properly.

### Lavanderia Vecchia — Neukölln — €€

**Hours:** Wed–Fri 12:30–23:00; Sat–Sun 17:30–23:00; (closed Mon–Tue)

**Rating:** 4.4 (912)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3264297671270047681&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A converted laundromat in a Neukölln courtyard serving honest Italian cooking on a fixed-menu format that trades flexibility for a sense of place. The room has character without feeling designed for Instagram, and the kitchen leans regional and heartfelt rather than trend-driven — a different proposition from Malafemmena's pizza-only focus or ITA Bistro's globally eclectic sharing plates. Limited hours and a set menu mean it rewards planning and trust in whatever the kitchen has composed that day; picky eaters or spontaneous drop-ins may find the constraints frustrating. A worthwhile Neukölln dinner when you want Italian with neighborhood soul rather than polished Mitte dining — interesting more for atmosphere and sincerity than for technical fireworks.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Kotti Dang — best value on the list, with pho broth quality that punches above its price tier.
2. Malafemmena — the most technically focused specialist here, and hard to beat for Neapolitan pizza at this cost.
3. Hanuman Thai Curry House — the strongest bet for regional Thai curry work without fine-dining pricing.
