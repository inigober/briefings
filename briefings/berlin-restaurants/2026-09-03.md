# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-09-03

This week is strongest on specialists with a job: Shanghainese-leaning noodles in Wilmersdorf, hand-pulled bowls in Friedrichshain, curry craft on Kantstraße, Yemeni cooking on Karl-Marx-Straße, Bosnian grill in Wedding, and a Neukölln bakery that becomes a pizza room at night. Horváth is the one expensive outlier — Austrian-rooted tasting-menu cooking on the canal rather than another Mitte luxury room.

### Shaniu's House of Noodles — Wilmersdorf — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.5 (2272)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=2072006413172091597&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Pariser Straße noodle kitchen with a clearer house identity than most west-side Chinese rooms: cold noodles, braised pork belly with homemade noodles, chili wontons, and ribs instead of another Sichuan greatest-hits list. The Shanghainese-and-Korean lean is the point and the limit — less regionally pure than a strict specialist, and popular enough that lines and tactical ordering are part of the deal. Against Lon Men's more canonical Kantstraße noodle-and-dim-sum standby or Mr. Noodle Chen's Lanzhou-style Friedrichshain lane, this Wilmersdorf address is the more idiosyncratic west-side bet. Useful when you want noodle craft with a specific house flavor rather than chili-forward northwestern defaults.

### Wen Cheng — Friedrichshain — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Tue–Sat 17:00–22:00; Sun 12:00–15:00 & 17:00–22:00; (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.6 (1872)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=17373268780583117555&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Simon-Dach-Straße hand-pulled noodle specialist whose appeal is concentration: one craft, a citywide reputation, and an east-side location that keeps it in the reader's practical orbit. Crowds are the tax on that reputation, and the published case here is thinner on named dishes than at Shaniu's. Against Mr. Noodle Chen two weeks ago — also Friedrichshain, also noodle-led, but with more soup-and-skewer breadth — Wen Cheng is the narrower evening play, and Monday closure plus dinner-only weekdays make it less of a spontaneous lunch default. Still one of the clearer craft matches for anyone who already treats Liu Nudelhaus as a reference point.

### Dao — Charlottenburg — €€€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.5 (1204)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=11289346261669056027&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A longstanding Kantstraße Thai kitchen whose reputation sits on curry execution and ingredient seriousness rather than a newly fashionable concept. The menu is broad rather than tightly regional, and the room's polish is a larger part of the package than at humbler specialists, which is why it lands a tier above the usual €€ Thai weeknight. Against Tossakan's Prenzlauer Berg all-rounder or Nutts' smaller chef-led Friedrichshain room, Dao is the more institutional west-side option — less scrappy, more established, and easier to book for a mixed table. Include it when curry handling and durability matter more than discovering a new regional lane.

### Jemenitisches Restaurant — Neukölln — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.5 (7593)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=12409537892763432544&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The Karl-Marx-Straße Yemeni room this reader already treats as a reference: rice, spice, and braised meat with a specialist identity that most Levantine all-rounders never attempt. The editorial write-up trail is thinner than the review volume, so this is not a newly argued discovery so much as a reminder that the cuisine is still underserved in Berlin coverage. Against last week's broader Lebanese-Persian spread at El Reda or Mardin's charcoal-grill institution around Hermannplatz, this is the tighter regional kitchen — less grill-theatre, more Gulf-and-Yemeni comfort cooking at a price that supports repeat visits. One of the week's more useful meals if you care about specificity more than novelty.

### Sarajevo — Wedding — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 08:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.8 (2526)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=11185880011603264770&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Triftstraße Bosnian neighborhood restaurant whose case is rootedness rather than a fashion cycle: grilled meats, robust comfort cooking, and a review base large enough to suggest regulars rather than a one-season crowd. Dish-level reporting is thinner than at the best-documented Balkan rooms, so this is a confidence-and-identity pick more than a plate-by-plate briefing. Against Stari Most, Kalaja, and Prizreni from earlier in the summer, Sarajevo is the Wedding counterweight — less Neukölln-corridor convenience, more local-institution energy. A strong everyday European regional bet when you want flavor and hospitality without another polished central-European dining room.

### Common — Neukölln — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Tue 08:00–16:00; Wed–Fri 08:00–16:00 & 18:00–22:30; Sat 09:00–16:00 & 18:00–22:30; Sun 09:00–16:00; (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.6 (241)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=4148914317838078783&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Karl-Marx-Straße bakery by day and pizza project by night, which is a clearer specialist identity than another all-day café with a token evening menu. The dual format is also the weakness: it is not a full restaurant if you want a long savory dinner beyond dough, and it is still new enough that longevity is unproven. Against Malafemmena's livelier Schöneberg Italian energy or the more conventional neighborhood bakery-café blur, Common is the craft-first local option — fermentation and baking ambition in an accessible format rather than a hype opening. Best as a neighborhood stop when dough work is the point; skip it if you want a broader kitchen.

### Horváth — Kreuzberg — €€€€

**Hours:** Wed–Sat 18:30–20:00 (closed Mon–Tue, Sun)

**Rating:** 4.5 (663)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=11873083102904049717&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The week's only fine-dining entry, and the one that still has a culinary autobiography: Austrian foundations, canal-side Kreuzberg, and a long reputation for precision rather than generic luxury signaling. The price and the narrow Wednesday–Saturday seating window make it an occasion, not a habit, and it sits far above the €–€€ logic that should drive most weeks. Against Rutz's sauce-driven Mitte benchmark, tulus lotrek's Kreuzberg tasting menus, or last week's Chinese tasting-menu experiment at UUU, Horváth is the more personally authored European high-end option — regional grounding instead of status décor. Worth planning only when you specifically want that Austrian-influenced tasting-menu craft.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Jemenitisches Restaurant — the clearest specialist on the list, with a cuisine Berlin still under-covers and a price that supports actually going.
2. Shaniu's House of Noodles — the most specific Chinese noodle case this week, with a house identity that is not just another Sichuan template.
3. Wen Cheng — the strongest east-side craft pick for hand-pulled noodles, even if popularity is part of the cost.
