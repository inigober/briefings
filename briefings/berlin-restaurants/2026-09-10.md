# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-09-10

This week is strongest on kitchens with a specific job: Northern Chinese dishes that reward calling ahead in Prenzlauer Berg, unapologetic Thai heat in Friedrichshain, pho in Lichtenberg, a bánh mì counter near Alexanderplatz, Serbian home cooking in a Moabit market hall, and a Neukölln German room built around Brandenburg product. Bieberbau is the one set-menu outlier — herb-and-spice cooking in Wilmersdorf rather than another Kreuzberg luxury tasting menu.

### Shan Shan — Prenzlauer Berg — €€

**Hours:** Wed–Sun 04:00–22:00 (closed Mon–Tue)

**Rating:** 4.6 (436)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=2813619000083273545&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Gleimstraße Northern Chinese room whose case is repertoire rather than a single noodle format: dumplings, lion's head meatballs, squirrel fish, mapo tofu, and cold starters that reward diners who order with intent. The published 04:00 open looks like a data quirk, Monday–Tuesday closure is real, and the stronger dishes may need a call ahead — this is not a walk-in greatest-hits kitchen. Against last week's Shanghainese-leaning Shaniu's in Wilmersdorf or Wen Cheng's hand-pulled Friedrichshain focus, Shan Shan is the broader northern-table option — less specialized than a dumpling-only stop like Wok Show, more useful if you want a sit-down meal with labor-intensive plates. Best when you treat it as a dish-by-dish exercise rather than a default Chinese dinner.

### Larb Koi — Friedrichshain — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Wed–Thu 18:00–22:00; Fri–Sat 18:00–23:00; Sun 03:00–22:00; (closed Mon–Tue)

**Rating:** 4.6 (705)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1837714750978964420&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Krossener Straße Thai kitchen that still treats "original" spice as a warning, not a garnish: octopus curry and papaya salad with mini octopus are the dishes that get named first, and the heat can be punishing if you order on pride rather than appetite. Popularity and a small room make it less of a secret than it once was, Monday–Tuesday closure plus dinner-only weekdays require planning, and the Sunday 03:00 open is almost certainly a listing error. Against last week's broader Kantstraße curry institution at Dao, or Nutts' chef-led Friedrichshain room two weeks ago, Larb Koi is the fiercer specialist — less polish, more seasoning accuracy, and closer to the heat-first Thai this reader already uses as a reference. One of the week's clearest flavor bets if you want intensity over comfort.

### Thành Koch — Lichtenberg — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 11:00–00:00

**Rating:** 4.0 (792)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=13453267633157062630&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Herzbergstraße Vietnamese kitchen whose published case rests on pho quality and a less generic second lane in goat sausage, which is a better reason to travel than another central-city noodle soup. The 4.0 rating across a large review base is solid rather than ecstatic, Lichtenberg sits outside the usual orbit, and the write-up trail is more enthusiastic than analytical. Against Co Do Hue's tighter Hue identity in Charlottenburg or Duc Anh Quan's Dong Xuan hall context from mid-August, Thành Koch is the sit-down east-side pho room — more convenient than a market excursion if you are already in Lichtenberg, less of a weeknight default from Neukölln. Worth the trip when broth and a mixed table matter more than neighborhood convenience; skip it if you only want a nearby Vietnamese lunch.

### Sljiva — Moabit — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 04:00–22:00; Sat 12:00–22:00; (closed Sun)

**Rating:** 4.5 (232)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=397963443234687770&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Serbian kitchen inside Arminiusmarkthalle that leans into home-style cooking — sarma, stews — rather than another cevapcici-and-grill template. The market-hall setting is the limit as much as the point: less of a dinner-room evening than last week's Wedding sit-down at Sarajevo, and the weekday 04:00 open looks unreliable even if Saturday noon is plausible. Against Stari Most, Kalaja, and Prizreni from late July, Sljiva is the Moabit counterweight with a narrower, stew-led identity — more specific than a generic Balkan grill, less documented dish-by-dish than the best-covered rooms. A strong food-first stop when you want regional comfort in Moabit; not the place if you need table-service atmosphere or a long, composed dinner.

### Mama Van — Mitte — € (good value)

**Hours:** Daily 11:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.5 (1005)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3098666772816247662&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Karl-Liebknecht-Straße bánh mì specialist whose concrete product case is the classic sandwich with pâté — a clearer job than most Alexanderplatz-adjacent Vietnamese rooms that try to be a full restaurant. Range is the weakness: this is lunch craft, not a survey of regional Vietnamese cooking, and the location pulls more transient footfall than a neighborhood regulars' counter. Against Thành Koch's sit-down pho on this list or Co Do Hue's Hue repertoire, Mama Van is the targeted cheap specialist — useful when you want one thing done properly, not a dinner destination. Best as a low-cost stop when sandwich construction is the point; ignore it if you want a broader kitchen.

### merold — Neukölln — €€ (good value)

**Hours:** Wed–Thu 19:00–23:00; Fri–Sat 19:00–23:30; (closed Mon–Tue, Sun)

**Rating:** 4.7 (238)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=6099182812103138905&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Pannierstraße German kitchen that keeps the cooking pared back and product-led — Berlin/Brandenburg ingredients, a house-made bratwurst that changes monthly — without sliding into a tasting-menu night. The polish is real: this is closer to bistronomic craft than to a cheap neighborhood canteen, and the Wednesday–Saturday dinner window plus Sunday closure make it a planned meal, not a spontaneous one. Against last week's bakery-to-pizza dual format at Common, or Barra's seasonal Okerstraße room from late July, merold is the more ingredient-rigorous Neukölln option at still-approachable pricing. Strong when you want technique and a clear product thesis without paying Horváth money; less satisfying if you mainly want a regional-ethnic specialist with a single cuisine identity.

### Bieberbau — Wilmersdorf — €€€ (good value)

**Hours:** Mon–Tue 17:30–00:00; Thu–Fri 17:30–00:00; (closed Wed, Sat–Sun)

**Rating:** 4.5 (663)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=16347767220074598492&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The week's only fine-dining entry, and the one that still argues for itself on value: set-menu cooking with herbs and spices as the through-line, on a west-side street rather than a prestige canal address. The format removes à la carte freedom, weekend closure plus Wednesday off make it awkward to book around a normal social week, and the international framing is less regionally distinct than last week's Austrian-rooted Horváth. Against Rutz's three-star Mitte benchmark or UUU's Chinese tasting-menu experiment in Wedding, Bieberbau is the more practical upscale slot — technique without €€€€, less hype-bound than Kreuzberg's conceptual rooms. Worth planning when you want a composed European evening in Wilmersdorf; skip it if the week's €€ specialists already cover what you actually want to eat.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Larb Koi — the clearest flavor case this week, with real heat, named dishes, and enough kitchen conviction to justify the booking friction.
2. merold — the strongest neighborhood craft pick, with product focus and a la carte pricing that still belongs in a weeknight logic.
3. Shan Shan — the most useful Chinese table here if you want Northern repertoire and labor-intensive plates rather than another noodle-only format.
