# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-10-15

This week leaves the usual Thai-and-noodle loop: a Wedding butcher-grill that is better on a plate than in bread, house-made pasta in Tempelhof and at Gleisdreieck, Motzstraße schnitzel, and a Kreuzberg vegan set that still costs like a careful mid-range dinner.

### Et Dünyasi by Ehli-Kebap — Wedding — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 08:00–19:00; Sat 08:00–20:00; (closed Sun)

**Rating:** 4.1 (123)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3563538702923663257&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Gerichtstraße meat counter whose useful argument is the yaprak skewer — beef with lamb and fat — plus Beyti-style wrapped Adana and köfte, not another charcoal theatre on Hermannstraße or Skalitzer. It is a butcher as much as a restaurant: the review base is small and split, the sandwich bread is heavily toasted, service can lose orders, and weekdays are done by early evening. Against last week's Akçaabat köfte sandwich, Mercan's Wiener Straße lokanta, Mira's Hermannstraße ocakbaşı, or the Skalitzer Adana grill-house sequence, this is the north-side meat-craft stop — less of a sit-down mezze night, more of a plate or İskender order. Go for the skewers and köfte; skip the toasted dürüm, and skip it entirely if you wanted a late table.

### Trattoria Lauretta — Tempelhof — €€

**Hours:** Tue–Sun 17:00–23:00 (closed Mon)

**Rating:** 4.5 (530)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=1808302106466398168&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Burgemeisterstraße Italian room whose selection reason is house-made pasta in a neighborhood this briefing almost never reaches. The published case is thin: pasta-and-pizza rather than a named regional osteria, little dish-by-dish criticism, dinner-only, Monday closed. Against last week's Muskauer Straße pasta at Zum Heiligen Teufel, La Bolognina's Donaustraße ravioli, or Il Calice's product-led Charlottenburg table, Lauretta is the south-west craft lead — less documented, less of a destination argument, more of a local manufactory if you are already in Tempelhof or willing to travel for pasta without another west-side evening. Worth a first visit when that geography is useful; it is not the week's strongest evidence base.

### Ripieno — Schöneberg — €€

**Hours:** Mon 04:00–21:30; Tue 12:00–21:30; Thu–Sun 12:00–21:30; (closed Wed)

**Rating:** 4.6 (678)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=4405641568888698269&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

House-made pasta at Am Lokdepot, after a 2023 move off the old address: gricia and oxtail ragù are the named dishes, sauces as the job rather than a full Italian survey. The format is the ceiling — little for a mixed table that does not want pasta — and the tasting notes support those plates rather than the whole menu; Wednesday is closed. Against Lauretta above, last week's Zum Heiligen Teufel, or La Bolognina three weeks ago, Ripieno is the more specific pasta stop: less neighborhood-unknown, more of a craft claim, still not Nini e Petirosso as a personal Italian default. Go when pasta is the meal; skip it if you want a broader menu or a composed wine-and-product night.

### Sissi Restaurant — Schöneberg — €€€

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 17:00–23:00; Fri–Sat 17:00–23:30; Sun 17:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (1377)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=6400383924715615295&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Motzstraße Austrian kitchen whose case is veal schnitzel with crisp breading, warm potato-cucumber salad, and Wiener Saftgulasch — technique on the classics, not a contemporary small-plates room. A fuller meal sits above the cheap-specialist logic of this list, the interior is themed in a way that is not the food argument, and the supported dishes are substantial rather than light. Against Nußbaumerin's weekday-only Leibnizstraße Austrian from late September, Jolesch's more straightforward Kreuzberg schnitzel-and-goulash, or Horváth's canal-side tasting-menu autobiography, Sissi is the Schöneberg classic: more tourist-facing than Nußbaumerin, far more usable than Horváth, still a planned dinner. Include it when schnitzel execution is the point; it is not the week's most interesting kitchen if you already have a Central European default.

### HAPPA — Kreuzberg — €€€ (good value)

**Hours:** Tue–Sat 18:00–22:00 (closed Mon, Sun)

**Rating:** 4.8 (462)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=946052033712709760&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The week's only set-menu entry: seasonal vegan cooking on Schlesische Straße, Alpine-Mediterranean lean, low-waste kitchen, a three-course format around €40 rather than a luxury tasting. Fully vegan and menu-led, so there is no à la carte escape, Tuesday–Saturday dinner only, and this is not a substitute for Horváth-level technique. Against last week's fermentation-led Otto, Matthias's product-led French room, or CODA's dessert tasting-menu, HAPPA is the cheaper chef-led evening — less theatre, more a complete meal at mid-range money, still a planned sitting. Book it if a vegan set at that price is the night you want; the rest of this list is the better weekly food if you came for pasta or grill.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Ripieno — the clearest craft case this week: house-made pasta with named dishes, not another generic Italian room.
2. Et Dünyasi by Ehli-Kebap — the most useful change to the Turkish picture after weeks of ocakbaşı, lokanta, and köfte sandwiches: butcher-counter meat, ordered on a plate.
3. HAPPA — the only complete chef-led sitting here that still behaves like a mid-range dinner rather than a luxury tasting.
