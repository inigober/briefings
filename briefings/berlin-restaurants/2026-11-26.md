# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-11-26

Cold-weather eating this week: roast meats, dumplings, schnitzel and flatbread straight from the oven, spread from Steglitz to Prenzlauer Berg. Mostly casual, a couple of sit-down dinners, and a lot of dishes worth ordering by name.

### Good Friends — Charlottenburg — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.4 (2733)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=7076436058078775098&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Kantstraße has plenty of Chinese restaurants, but this is the Cantonese one to know for roast meats and seafood. Bring a group and order selectively: the roast meats, the turbot, and the crispy noodles with fish cakes are the dishes people point to. The menu is enormous and the kitchen is not equally good at all of it, and a seafood-heavy table pushes the bill past the usual €€ range.

### Lahmacun Kebab Haus / Küsleme — Wedding — €

**Hours:** Mon–Thu 08:00–00:00; Fri–Sat 08:00–02:00; Sun 08:00–23:50

**Rating:** 4.8 (975)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=8096592926846417793&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Two specialties carry this place: lahmacun and kelle paça, the tripe-and-head soup that few Berlin kitchens bother with. Add a fresh ayran and you have a cheap, properly Turkish meal on Luxemburger Straße. The repertoire is narrow by design, so come for those dishes rather than a mixed grill. The restaurant is listed under two names, so look for the lahmacun sign.

### Fleischerei Ayoub — Moabit — €

**Hours:** Mon–Sat 08:00–20:00 (closed Sun)

**Rating:** 4.9 (72)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=567808258898969351&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Lebanese butcher with a manakish bakery attached, which is a better idea than it sounds. The za'atar flatbread is the thing to order, and the hot dishes and butcher specialties set it apart from an ordinary sandwich counter. This is a shop, not a sit-down restaurant, so treat it as a stop for a flatbread and maybe a hot plate. Consistency across the hot food is still unproven, and it is closed on Sundays.

### Casalot — Mitte / Europacity — €€

**Hours:** Daily 11:30–00:00

**Rating:** 4.7 (7994)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=2715981627122034909&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Palestinian and wider Arabic cooking, best approached through mansaf and the yogurt kibbeh rather than the usual mezze run. It works for a group sharing several plates. The room and the long menu make it feel more like a dining hall than a specialist kitchen, and a full shared meal can climb above €€. Good, not the strongest of its kind in the city.

### Knödelwirtschaft — Neukölln — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Fri 17:00–23:00; Sat–Sun 12:00–23:00

**Rating:** 4.6 (1349)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=10657343154959969986&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Savory dumplings and almost nothing else, which is the appeal on a cold night. Mix and match fillings such as bacon, mountain cheese, spinach-ricotta and beetroot, and finish with the semolina dumpling dessert. There is a vegan tofu-and-mushroom option too. If you are not in the mood for a dumpling-centered meal, there is little else to fall back on.

### Jolesch — Kreuzberg — €€€

**Hours:** Daily 17:00–00:00

**Rating:** 4.3 (1799)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=14401300538729835448&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Austrian classics done in the expected way: Wiener schnitzel, Tafelspitz, Käsespätzle and Kaiserschmarrn. The schnitzel is what draws the praise, so start there. A full dinner with several courses costs more than a typical weeknight meal, and the classics are more convincing than the newer additions like the tempeh schnitzel, which is mainly there for mixed-diet groups.

### Kokio — Prenzlauer Berg — €€

**Rating:** 4.5 (2461)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=18334790395386684159&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Korean fried chicken with a menu built around crunch and a range of spicy sauces. It does one thing and the format suits that. The press coverage is more enthusiastic than technical, so treat it as a solid casual chicken stop rather than a destination. Worth it if you live nearby, less so across town.

### Udagawa / 宇田川 — Steglitz — €€€

**Hours:** Mon 18:00–22:30; Wed–Sun 18:00–22:30; (closed Tue)

**Rating:** 4.4 (335)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=240047763774104311&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A traditional Japanese neighborhood restaurant far from the inner-city circuit. Order the sashimi, unagi or tempura; the sukiyaki is the one dish reviewers steer people away from. The menu is long, so choose with care, and a fish-focused dinner will cost well above a casual meal. Dinner only, and closed on Tuesdays.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Lahmacun Kebab Haus / Küsleme — lahmacun, kelle paça and ayran for very little money, when you want something specific and Turkish.
2. Fleischerei Ayoub — za'atar manakish from a working butcher's bakery, an easy daytime stop in Moabit.
3. Knödelwirtschaft — the right dinner for a cold evening, with dumplings you assemble yourself from bacon, cheese or beetroot fillings.
