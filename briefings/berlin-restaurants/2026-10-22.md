# Berlin Restaurant Briefing — Week of 2026-10-22

Noodles and charcoal lead this week, from belt noodles in Prenzlauer Berg to an Adana grill in Kreuzberg, with Indonesian home cooking, Campanian Italian and a serious bagel to round it out. Most of it sits well under 35 EUR and spreads from Tegel to Friedrichshain.

### Kongfu Chili — Prenzlauer Berg — €€

**Hours:** Daily 17:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.6 (514)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=12949376634428473489&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Belt noodles get plenty of attention for their chew, but here the seasoning is just as considered. The three-in-one bowl brings oil-splashed noodles, stewed beef, vegetables and pressed tofu together, and it is the dish to order. It is a noodle specialist, so treat the rest of the menu as supporting cast. Open evenings only, which makes it a dinner plan rather than a lunch stop.

### You Mi — Schöneberg — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–22:00

**Rating:** 4.8 (399)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=15420434776594381727&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Come with a few people and share. Tofu skin, bamboo salad, garlic shoots with Chinese sausage and fish-fragrant eggplant are the dishes worth pointing at, with Sichuan leanings throughout. The menu is large and quality ranges from solid to exceptional, so order selectively rather than at random. A one-person main would sell the place short.

### Nusantara — Moabit — €€

**Hours:** Daily 12:00–21:30

**Rating:** 4.4 (1759)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=6608885631759338744&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Indonesian home cooking is still thin on the ground in Berlin, which is reason enough to know this place. Gado-gado and bakmie goreng anchor the menu, and the daily-changing dishes are where the kitchen shows its hand. Tofu and tempeh are treated as real options, not afterthoughts. Because the specials rotate, you will not know exactly what you are getting until you sit down.

### Thai Art — Charlottenburg — €€

**Hours:** Mon–Sat 11:30–21:30 (closed Sun)

**Rating:** 4.5 (668)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=6298924624623143780&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Noodle soup is the reason to come: tom yum noodles with a sweet-sour-salty balance and contrasting pork preparations, plus boat noodles with a broth tradition most Berlin Thai kitchens skip. The restaurant has moved to Kantstraße, so quality is worth confirming on a first visit rather than assuming. Sundays are closed.

### Doyum Grillhaus — Kreuzberg — €€

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=11413251185972459168&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The Adana kebab is the draw: well seasoned, juicy inside, charred outside, cooked over charcoal. This is a grill place first, not a döner stop, and it is a meat-centric menu with little to offer vegetarians. Close in spirit to Adana Grillhaus. Expect prices to be higher than older write-ups suggest.

### Trattoria da Silvana & Renato — Tegel — €€

**Rating:** 4.5 (492)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=10111132630863960935&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

A Campanian trattoria far out in Reinickendorf, with daily-changing dishes rather than a fixed Italian greatest-hits menu. The pizza and the aubergine casserole are the ones to look for, though the casserole can sell out, so go earlier rather than late. It is a long trip for most of the city, so this one suits people already in the north-west. Check opening days before setting out.

### Sironi – La Pizza — Schöneberg — €€

**Hours:** Daily 08:30–23:00

**Rating:** 4.2 (1470)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=3410053893751303197&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

Sironi is a bakery first, and the pizza shows it: the dough is the point, with different styles rather than one interchangeable base. That makes it a good place to compare crusts instead of chasing the newest Neapolitan opening. It is a dough specialist, not a full Italian restaurant, so order pizza and leave it there. This is the Goltzstraße pizzeria, not the market-hall bakery.

### Fine Bagels — Friedrichshain — €

**Hours:** Daily 08:00–19:00

**Rating:** 4.2 (1190)

**Maps:** https://maps.google.com/?cid=5879610426525979150&g_mp=Cidnb29nbGUubWFwcy5wbGFjZXMudjEuUGxhY2VzLlNlYXJjaFRleHQQAhgEIAA

The bagels are hand-rolled, proofed overnight and boiled before baking, and you can taste it in a resilient crust and a properly chewy middle, not the cakey texture of weaker versions. It is a breakfast and early-lunch stop, closing at 19:00. Fillings can push the bill past the bargain bracket, so keep the order simple.

### This week's strongest bets

If I were choosing only three from this list:

1. Kongfu Chili — for a bowl of belt noodles and stewed beef on an evening when you want real seasoning, not just texture.
2. You Mi — for a shared-table dinner with friends where you can order across the menu.
3. Doyum Grillhaus — for charcoal-grilled Adana kebab, the closest thing to a sure bet here for a grill fan.
